## Hook
Imagine a junior secondary student who secretly takes a classmate's expensive geometry set during break time. At first, he feels clever, hiding the set in his locker. But as the afternoon wears on, seeing his classmate in tears and unable to complete her technical drawing assignment, a heavy ache settles into his chest. He realizes that what he did was deeply wrong. He doesn't just hide his face; he walks up to the teacher, confesses truthfully, returns the geometry set, and apologizes to his friend. That inner transformation and outward restitution is what the Bible calls **repentance**. In this lesson, you will discover that God never leaves us trapped in our wrongdoing. From the royal palace of King David to the sycamore tree of Zacchaeus the tax collector in Jericho, God calls every human being to turn away from sin and step into forgiveness and restoration!

## Key Terms
- Term: Repentance
- Definition: A genuine sorrow and remorse for wrongdoing, accompanied by an intentional decision to turn away from sin and return to God in obedience.
- Term: Metanoia
- Definition: The Greek biblical word for repentance, signifying a complete change of mind, perspective, heart, and life direction.
- Term: Restitution
- Definition: The act of restoring, returning, or making amends for what was unjustly taken, damaged, or defrauded from another person.
- Term: Prophet Nathan
- Definition: The bold prophet sent by God to confront King David after his sins of adultery with Bathsheba and the murder of Uriah the Hittite.
- Term: Zacchaeus
- Definition: A chief tax collector in Jericho who was wealthy, short in stature, and radically transformed after hosting Jesus in his home.
- Term: Parable of the Ewe Lamb
- Definition: The judicial story told by Prophet Nathan about a rich man with many flocks who stole a poor man's single beloved pet lamb to entertain a traveler.
- Term: Contrition
- Definition: Deep, heartfelt sorrow for having offended God, characterized by humility and a desire for reconciliation.
- Term: Confession
- Definition: Openly and honestly acknowledging one's faults, sins, and transgressions before God and those harmed.

## Core Concept
### The Foundations of Biblical Repentance
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Call to Repentance I" caption="Call to Repentance I: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

Biblical repentance is far more than merely feeling afraid of being caught or dreading punishment. It involves a three-fold dimension:
1. **Intellectual Awareness:** Realizing and admitting that one's actions violate God's moral law.
2. **Emotional Contrition:** Experiencing godly sorrow that breaks pride rather than defending it.
3. **Volitional Transformation (Change of Will):** Actively turning around, seeking forgiveness, and making amends (restitution) wherever possible.

### King David's Call to Repentance (2 Samuel 12:1–13; Psalm 51:1–19)
- **The Transgression:** King David committed adultery with Bathsheba, wife of Uriah the Hittite. To conceal the pregnancy, David orchestrated Uriah's death on the frontline of battle and took Bathsheba as his wife.
- **The Divine Confrontation:** God sent the Prophet Nathan to tell David a parable: A wealthy man owning countless sheep and cattle received a guest. Instead of taking an animal from his own herds, he seized the single, beloved ewe lamb of a poor neighbor—a lamb that ate from his cup and slept in his arms—and prepared it for the visitor.
- **David's Outrage and Conviction:** Burning with indignation, David declared, "As the Lord lives, the man who has done this deserves to die! And he shall restore the lamb fourfold!" Nathan then delivered God's piercing judgment: **"You are the man!"**
- **The Broken Confession:** Stripped of self-justification, David did not blame anyone else. He confessed: *"I have sinned against the Lord."* In Psalm 51, David poured out his heart: *"Have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love... Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me."* God forgave his sin, though David faced painful disciplinary consequences.

### Zacchaeus Responds to the Call to Repentance (Luke 19:1–10)
- **The Social Context:** Zacchaeus was the chief tax collector in the wealthy commercial city of Jericho. In Roman-occupied Judea, tax collectors (*publicans*) were despised as traitors and extortioners who overcharged citizens to amass private fortunes.
- **The Desire to See Jesus:** Hearing that Jesus was passing through Jericho, Zacchaeus wanted to see Him. Because he was short of stature and the crowd was dense, he set aside his executive pride and ran ahead to climb into the branches of a sycamore-fig tree.
- **The Divine Notice and Hospitality:** Looking up, Jesus called him by name: *"Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for I must stay at your house today."* Zacchaeus came down with haste and received Jesus joyfully, despite the crowd murmuring that Jesus had gone to be the guest of a notorious sinner.
- **Radical Transformation and Restitution:** Overwhelmed by Jesus' unconditional grace and love, Zacchaeus stood before the Lord and made a revolutionary pledge:
  1. *"Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor."*
  2. *"And if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold."*
- **The Savior's Declaration:** Jesus pronounced salvation over his household: *"Today salvation has come to this house... For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost."*

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Equating repentance with regret. Regret is simply feeling bad about negative consequences; repentance is actively forsaking the wrong behavior and turning toward God.
- **Mistake 2:** Thinking King David justified his actions because he was king. David humbly confessed, "I have sinned against the Lord," proving that God's moral law applies equally to rulers and commoners.
- **Mistake 3:** Believing Zacchaeus earned salvation through money. Zacchaeus' generous fourfold restitution was not a payment for forgiveness, but the outward fruit of an inwardly transformed heart.
- **Mistake 4:** Assuming restitution is unnecessary if one has prayed. Both biblical accounts emphasize that genuine repentance involves rectifying damage done to others whenever possible.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Differentiating Worldly Sorrow from Godly Repentance
*Question:* A student named Emeka gets caught copying answers during an exam. He cries loudly because he is disqualified from the exam hall and will face detention. Is this true repentance?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Analyze the motivation:** Emeka is distressed because he got caught and suffers immediate disciplinary penalties (disqualification, detention).
2. **Compare with biblical definition:** Biblical repentance focuses on grief over having violated honesty and offended God and teachers, not just the pain of getting caught.
3. **Determine the remedy:** Emeka demonstrates true repentance when he acknowledges his dishonesty, accepts his penalty without bitterness, resolves never to cheat again, and commits to honest study.

### Example 2: Calculating Restitution under the Zacchaeus Standard
*Question:* If Zacchaeus had unlawfully extorted 250 silver denarii from a merchant in Jericho, how much money did he pledge to restore to that merchant under his restitution promise?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the restitution formula:** In Luke 19:8, Zacchaeus pledged: *"If I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold."*
2. **Multiply the stolen sum by 4:** $250 \times 4 = 1,000$ silver denarii.
3. **Pedagogical Meaning:** Zacchaeus exceeded even the standard civil requirement of the Mosaic Law (which typically required the principal plus one-fifth, or 20%), demonstrating overwhelming gratitude and radical ethical renewal.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Discussion:** Discuss with your child the importance of owning up to mistakes. Ask: "When you do something wrong, is it easier to make excuses or apologize? What did King David do when Nathan confronted him?"
- **Family Action:** Encourage restitution in daily family life. If a child damages a sibling's book or toy, guide them to apologize and replace or repair the item with their own pocket money or effort.
