## Hook
Imagine your parents suddenly waking you up tomorrow morning and saying: "Pack your school bags and say goodbye to your neighborhood, your friends, and everything familiar. We are moving to a new land. We do not have an address yet, but God has told us to pack up and start walking, and He will guide our steps!" How would you feel? You would probably feel nervous, full of questions, and reluctant to leave your comfort zone. Yet, over four thousand years ago, an elderly man named Abram heard that exact call from the living God. Without arguing, hesitating, or demanding a GPS map, he packed his tents, took his family, and set out into the unknown. In this lesson, you will discover the transformative power of biblical obedience through the inspiring call of Abraham, the father of faith!

## Key Terms
- Term: Obedience
- Definition: Willingly, promptly, and completely submitting to the lawful commands and guidance of God and rightful authorities.
- Term: Faith
- Definition: Confident trust and reliance on God's character, promises, and word, even when circumstances cannot be physically seen.
- Term: Abraham (Abram)
- Definition: The founding patriarch of Israel, originally named Abram ("exalted father"), whom God renamed Abraham ("father of a multitude").
- Term: Haran
- Definition: The northern Mesopotamian commercial city where Abram lived with his father Terah before God called him to depart for Canaan.
- Term: Canaan
- Definition: The land of promise flowing with milk and honey, located in the Levant, which God swore to give to Abraham and his descendants.
- Term: Covenant
- Definition: A solemn, binding sacred agreement between God and man established with sovereign promises and ethical conditions.
- Term: Altars of Shechem and Bethel
- Definition: Stone worship altars erected by Abram in Canaan to thank God, call upon His name, and dedicate the land to the Lord.
- Term: Sarai (Sarah)
- Definition: The devoted wife of Abraham who accompanied him on his journey of faith and was later renamed Sarah ("princess").

### The Call of Abram (Genesis 12:1–9)
- **The Setting:** Abram was living comfortably in Haran, a thriving ancient city, after migrating from Ur of the Chaldeans with his father Terah. When Terah died at age 205, God spoke directly to Abram.
- **The Radical Mandate:** God commanded Abram: *"Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you"* (Genesis 12:1). This command required three costly separations:
  1. Separation from his geographical homeland (*country*).
  2. Separation from his extended clan and social safety net (*kindred*).
  3. Separation from his ancestral heritage and domestic security (*father's house*).
- **The Sevenfold Covenant Promise:** God accompanied this daunting command with extraordinary sovereign promises:
  1. *"I will make of you a great nation."*
  2. *"I will bless you."*
  3. *"And make your name great."*
  4. *"So that you will be a blessing."*
  5. *"I will bless those who bless you."*
  6. *"And him who dishonors you I will curse."*
  7. *"And in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed."* (The ultimate Messianic promise pointing to Jesus Christ).
- **Abram's Unhesitating Obedience:** Scripture records the decisive simplicity of faith: *"So Abram went, as the Lord had told him; and Lot went with him. Abram was seventy-five years old when he departed from Haran"* (Genesis 12:4).
- **Journey into Canaan:** Abram took Sarai his wife, Lot his brother's son, all their accumulated wealth, and the persons they had acquired in Haran, and they journeyed toward Canaan.
- **Erecting Altars of Worship:**
  - Upon reaching the oak of Moreh at **Shechem**, the Lord appeared to Abram and said: *"To your offspring I will give this land."* Abram built an altar there to the Lord.
  - Moving east of **Bethel**, he pitched his tent between Bethel on the west and Ai on the east; there he built another altar and called upon the name of the Lord.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Thinking Abraham was a young teenager when God called him. Scripture explicitly notes that Abram was seventy-five years old when he departed from Haran, showing that God can call and use people at any stage of life.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming Abraham knew the map of Canaan beforehand. Hebrews 11:8 states that Abraham "went out, not knowing where he was going." His compass was God's voice, not human cartography.
- **Mistake 3:** Believing obedience means having no fears or doubts. Obedience is choosing to trust and act on God's word in spite of fear, uncertainty, or discomfort.
- **Mistake 4:** Forgetting the Messianic scope of the promise. The promise that "in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed" was fulfilled through Jesus Christ, the seed of Abraham, offering salvation to all mankind.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Calculating Abraham's Faithful Milestones
*Question:* A student is asked to calculate how old Abram was when he left Haran, and what relatives accompanied him on his journey of obedience.
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the biblical text:** Genesis 12:4-5.
2. **Determine his age:** The text explicitly states: *"Abram was seventy-five years old when he departed from Haran."*
3. **Identify accompanying companions:**
   - His wife: Sarai.
   - His nephew: Lot (son of Abram's deceased brother Haran).
   - Their servants and households.
4. **Conclusion:** At an age when most people retire into comfort, Abram stepped out into pioneering faith and total obedience to God.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Altars at Shechem and Bethel
*Question:* Why did Abram build altars at Shechem and between Bethel and Ai upon entering Canaan?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the culture of Canaan:** The land was inhabited by pagan Canaanites who worshipped idols on high places.
2. **Analyze Abram's purpose:**
   - At **Shechem** (Oak of Moreh): God appeared and promised the land to his offspring. The altar was a physical marker of thanksgiving and claiming the divine promise.
   - At **Bethel and Ai**: Abram pitched his tent and called upon the name of the Lord, establishing public worship of the one true God in a pagan land.
3. **Application for students:** Wherever a Christian goes—new school, hostel, or workplace—they should establish habits of prayer and dedication to God.

## Guardian Sync
- **Family Discussion:** Read Genesis 12:1-4 together. Ask your child: "What makes it difficult to obey parents or teachers immediately? How did Abraham show that he trusted God completely?"
- **Faith Challenge:** Encourage your child to identify one area where they have been delaying obedience (such as cleaning their room, completing holiday projects, or waking up on time) and challenge them to practice prompt obedience this week.
