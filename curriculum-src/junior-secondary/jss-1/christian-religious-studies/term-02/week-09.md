## Hook
Imagine you volunteer to clean up the school chapel or science laboratory after school every Friday. You scrub the desks, sweep the floors, and pack the equipment until everything shines. But instead of praising you, some students sneer: "Why are you stressing yourself? Are you the only one in this school? You are just trying to show off to the teachers!" Have you ever felt that stinging discouragement when trying to do good? If so, you are walking in the very footsteps of Joseph, who was thrown into a dungeon for doing what was right, and Ruth, who spent scorching days gleaning in foreign fields! In this lesson, you will draw together the inspiring life lessons of Joseph and Ruth, examine 1 Peter 2:18–25 on enduring hardship in service, discover how Christian youth serve church, school, and community today, and conquer your Term 2 80% Mastery Gate!

## Key Terms
- Term: Christian Service
- Definition: Selflessly dedicating one's abilities, time, and heart to meet the needs of others as unto the Lord Jesus Christ, without seeking worldly applause.
- Term: Diligence
- Definition: Consistent, thorough, and careful effort applied to any task, whether small or great, seen or unseen.
- Term: Moral Integrity
- Definition: Steadfast adherence to honesty, purity, and righteous principles, regardless of personal temptation or social pressure.
- Term: Servanthood
- Definition: The Christ-like posture of placing the welfare of others ahead of personal comfort and ego.
- Term: Patient Endurance
- Definition: Bearing unfair treatment, misunderstandings, or hardships without bitterness, trusting in God's righteous judgment (1 Peter 2:19-20).
- Term: Vocation
- Definition: A person's calling or life occupation through which they glorify God and serve their fellow human beings.
- Term: Obstacles in Service
- Definition: Discouragements, mockery, envy, physical fatigue, and false accusations encountered when doing good.
- Term: Term 2 80% Mastery Gate
- Definition: The academic and pedagogical standard requiring students to demonstrate at least 80% competency across Term 2 concepts before sitting for terminal examinations.

### Living in God's Service (1 Peter 2:18–25)
The Apostle Peter explains that serving God in a fallen world often brings unfair treatment:
1. **Enduring Injustice Graciously:** *"For this is a gracious thing, when, mindful of God, one endures sorrows while suffering unjustly. For what credit is it if, when you sin and are beaten for it, you endure? But if when you do good and suffer for it you endure, this is a gracious thing in the sight of God"* (1 Peter 2:19–20).
2. **Jesus Christ as the Ultimate Archetype:** *"For to this you have been called, because Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example, so that you might follow in his steps. He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth. When he was reviled, he did not revile in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but continued entrusting himself to him who judges justly"* (1 Peter 2:21–23).
3. **The Redemptive Purpose:** Christ bore our sins in His body on the tree that we might die to sin and live to righteousness; by His wounds we are healed.

### Ways Christians are Called to Serve Today
Christian youth are called to serve across three distinct environments:
- **1. Service in the Home and Family:**
  - Honoring, respecting, and promptly obeying parents and guardians (Ephesians 6:1–3).
  - Cheerfully undertaking household chores: sweeping, washing dishes, caring for younger siblings or aging grandparents, just as Ruth cared for Naomi.
  - Fostering peace, avoiding sibling rivalry and jealousy, unlike Joseph's brothers.
- **2. Service in the School and Peer Community:**
  - Assisting teachers with classroom materials, cleaning chalkboards, and organizing desks.
  - Tutoring struggling classmates who find mathematics, languages, or sciences challenging.
  - Standing up against school bullying, cultism, and exam malpractice with Joseph-like integrity.
- **3. Service in the Church and Broader Society:**
  - Participating actively in youth choir, ushering, sanitation, and technical ministries in church.
  - Engaging in community sanitation, visiting orphanages, and helping the physically disabled.
  - Practicing professional and civic honesty in future careers as model Nigerian citizens.

### Problems and Obstacles Encountered in Christian Service
When young people decide to serve God faithfully, they face real-world challenges:
1. **Misunderstanding and False Accusations:** Just as Potiphar's wife falsely accused Joseph, students doing good may be misunderstood or accused of bad motives.
2. **Mockery, Peer Pressure, and Jealousy:** Jealous peers may tease or ostracize a diligent student as an "eye-servant" or "teacher's pet."
3. **Physical Exhaustion and Burnout:** Serving others without proper rest, balanced study, and prayer can lead to weariness.
4. **Lack of Appreciation or Ingratitude:** Serving people who never say "thank you," like the royal cupbearer who forgot Joseph in prison for two full years.
5. **The Christian Response:** Never lose heart! Galatians 6:9 promises: *"And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up."*

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Thinking Christian service is limited to ordained pastors and priests. Every Christian believer is called to be a servant of God in their school, home, and profession.
- **Mistake 2:** Believing that if you serve God, you will never experience suffering or betrayal. Both Joseph and Ruth experienced deep bereavement, false accusation, and poverty before their elevation.
- **Mistake 3:** Retaliating when treated unfairly. 1 Peter 2 explicitly commands Christians not to retaliate with insults or threats, but to commit their case to God who judges righteously.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking menial work is shameful. Ruth's humble gleaning and Joseph's prison duties prove that all honest labor done for God carries great dignity.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Resolving False Accusation in the School Environment
*Question:* A student named Chidi finds an envelope containing examination fees on the school walkway and turns it over to the vice principal. Later, a rumor spreads that Chidi stole money from the envelope before submitting it. How should Chidi respond using the models of Joseph and 1 Peter 2?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Analyze the emotion:** Chidi feels deeply hurt, angry, and discouraged from ever doing good again.
2. **Apply the example of Joseph:** Joseph was falsely accused by Potiphar's wife and jailed. He did not curse God or become bitter; he continued serving faithfully in prison.
3. **Apply 1 Peter 2:23:** Jesus was reviled but did not revile in return; He committed His case to God who judges righteously.
4. **Actionable guidance:** Chidi should maintain a clear conscience, speak the truth calmly to authorities without trading insults, and trust that his integrity will be vindicated by God.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Cupbearer's Ingratitude (Genesis 40:23)
*Question:* After Joseph accurately interpreted the cupbearer's dream and asked him to remember him to Pharaoh, Genesis 40:23 states: *"Yet the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph, but forgot him."* How does this teach resilience in service?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the human flaw:** People frequently forget kindness and fail to show gratitude.
2. **Examine Joseph's duration in waiting:** Joseph waited two additional years in prison after being forgotten.
3. **Identify God's timing:** God brought Joseph out of prison at the exact strategic moment when Pharaoh needed a Prime Minister, not merely a released slave.
4. **Lesson for youth:** Serve others without banking on human gratitude; your ultimate reward and promotion come from God in His perfect time.
