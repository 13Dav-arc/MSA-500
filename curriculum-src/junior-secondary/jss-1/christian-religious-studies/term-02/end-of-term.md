## Key Terms
- Term: Metanoia
- Definition: The Greek biblical word for repentance, signifying a complete transformation of mind, heart, purpose, and direction toward God.
- Term: Restitution
- Definition: The moral and practical act of restoring, repaying, or making amends for what was wrongfully taken or defrauded from others.
- Term: Sackcloth and Ashes
- Definition: Traditional ancient symbols of profound sorrow, mourning, contrition, and humble repentance before God.
- Term: Ichabod
- Definition: "The glory has departed from Israel," the tragic name given to Phinehas' son when the Ark of God was captured by the Philistines.
- Term: Covenant Obedience
- Definition: Wholehearted, prompt submission to God's divine commands and promises, demonstrated by Abraham's journey to Canaan and test on Moriah.
- Term: Jehovah Jireh
- Definition: "The Lord will provide," the compound Hebrew divine name commemorating God's provision of the substitutionary ram on Mount Moriah.
- Term: Integrity in Service
- Definition: Steadfast commitment to moral purity, honesty, and excellence, exemplified by Joseph in Potiphar's house and in prison.
- Term: Kinsman-Redeemer (Goel)
- Definition: A close relative legally empowered under Mosaic law to redeem family property and marry a widow to preserve the deceased kin's lineage.
- Term: Patient Endurance
- Definition: Bearing unjust suffering, insults, and hardships without retaliation, following the footsteps of Jesus Christ (1 Peter 2:18-25).
- Term: Servanthood
- Definition: The Christ-like disposition of humbly dedicating one's skills, time, and heart to the welfare of others and the glory of God.

1. **The Call to Repentance:**
   - **David (2 Samuel 12; Psalm 51):** Convicted by Nathan's ewe lamb parable; confessed without making excuses; cried out for a clean heart and a right spirit.
   - **Zacchaeus (Luke 19:1–10):** Short chief tax collector who climbed a sycamore tree; welcomed Jesus joyfully; pledged half his wealth to the poor and fourfold restitution to the defrauded.
   - **The City of Nineveh (Jonah 3:1–10):** Pagan capital warned by Jonah; king and citizens observed a national fast, covered themselves in sackcloth, sat in ashes, and turned from violence; God relented of disaster.
   - **John the Baptist (Luke 3:1–14):** Wilderness forerunner preaching baptism of repentance; demanded practical ethical fruits: crowds share extra coats and food; tax collectors collect no more than legal rates; soldiers avoid violence, false accusations, and greed.

2. **The Consequences of Impenitence:**
   - **Eli, Hophni, and Phinehas (1 Samuel 2; 3; 4):** Priests who defiled holy sacrifices with the three-pronged fleshhook, demanded raw meat before the fat was offered, and committed sexual immorality at the sanctuary.
   - Eli's fatal passivity: warned verbally but failed to restrain his sons, honoring them above God.
   - The disaster at Aphek: Israel treated the Ark as a magic lucky charm; 30,000 soldiers died; the Ark was captured; Hophni and Phinehas died; Eli fell and broke his neck; the dying mother named her son *Ichabod* ("The glory has departed").

3. **The Call to Obedience:**
   - **The Call of Abraham (Genesis 12:1–9):** Commanded at age 75 to leave country, kindred, and father's house for Canaan; received the sevenfold covenant promise ("in you all families of the earth shall be blessed"); erected altars of worship at Shechem and Bethel.
   - **The Supreme Test on Mount Moriah (Genesis 21–22):** Birth of Isaac at age 100; commanded to offer his only beloved son; obeyed promptly early in the morning; prophesied "God will provide the lamb"; angel intervened; substitutionary ram caught in thicket; named place *Jehovah Jireh*; received God's sworn oath of multiplication and universal blessing.

4. **The Call to Service and Christian Endurance:**
   - **Joseph's Faithful Service (Genesis 37–41):** Beloved son with coat of many colors; betrayed and sold for 20 silver pieces; faithful in Potiphar's house ("The Lord was with Joseph"); fled sexual temptation ("How can I do this great wickedness and sin against God?"); managed prison with empathy; interpreted Pharaoh's dreams of 7 fat and 7 lean cows; promoted to Prime Minister at age 30; saved millions from famine and forgave his brothers.
   - **Ruth's Devoted Service (Ruth 1–4):** Widowed Moabite who clung to destitute Naomi ("Your people shall be my people, and your God my God"); gleaned humbly in the fields of Boaz; recognized as a woman of noble character; married Boaz as kinsman-redeemer; mother of Obed, great-grandmother of King David, and ancestor of Jesus Christ.
   - **Living in God's Service (1 Peter 2:18–25):** Enduring unjust suffering patiently; following Christ who committed no sin, did not revile when insulted, and entrusted Himself to God who judges righteously.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Confusing sorrow over punishment with true repentance. Saul wept when he lost his kingdom, but David wept over offending God. Repentance is directed toward God and changes behavior.
- **Mistake 2:** Thinking God allowed Isaac to be harmed on Mount Moriah. God intervened and provided a ram, making it clear that human child sacrifice is an abomination.
- **Mistake 3:** Assuming Joseph's brothers sold him because they needed money. The root cause was bitter envy and jealousy over Jacob's love and Joseph's prophetic dreams.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking Ruth's Moabite ancestry disqualified her from God's blessings. God honors heart faith and righteous character over ethnic origin, bringing Ruth into the messianic genealogy of Jesus Christ.
- **Mistake 5:** Believing Christian endurance means tolerating evil without speaking truth. Jesus and the apostles spoke truth boldly while enduring physical mistreatment without hatred or revenge.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Synthesizing the Models of Restitution
*Question:* Compare the actions of King David, Zacchaeus, and the King of Nineveh. How does each prove their repentance through tangible actions?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **King David:** David did not execute Nathan or defend his royal prerogative. He fasted, lay on the ground, confessed his guilt, and accepted God's disciplinary chastening while penning Psalm 51.
2. **Zacchaeus:** Zacchaeus took immediate financial action: he gave half of his total possessions to the poor and pledged fourfold restitution to anyone he had cheated.
3. **The King of Nineveh:** The king took civic and national action: he stepped down from his royal throne, wore coarse sackcloth, sat in ashes, decreed a total national fast, and commanded all citizens to stop their violence.
4. **Pedagogical Takeaway:** True repentance is never an invisible secret; it is validated by public humility, restitution, and turning away from wickedness.

### Example 2: Correlating Mount Moriah with Calvary
*Question:* How do the events on Mount Moriah in Genesis 22 foreshadow the crucifixion of Jesus Christ on Calvary?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **The Beloved Only Son:** Isaac was the beloved, miraculous "only son" of Abraham; Jesus is the only begotten Son of the Father (John 3:16).
2. **Bearing the Wood:** Isaac carried the wood for his sacrifice up Mount Moriah; Jesus carried His wooden cross up Golgotha (John 19:17).
3. **Willing Submission:** Isaac submitted to his elderly father without violent resistance; Jesus submitted to the Father's will in Gethsemane ("Not my will, but yours be done").
4. **The Divine Provision:** Abraham prophesied *"God will provide for himself the lamb"*; John the Baptist declared of Jesus: *"Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!"* (John 1:29).
5. **The Substitution:** The ram was sacrificed in place of Isaac; Jesus died as our substitutionary sacrifice on the cross.

A) Pharaoh took off his royal signet ring and put it on Joseph's hand (correct)
B) Joseph was dressed in garments of fine linen and given a gold chain (correct)
C) Joseph rode in the second chariot as second-in-command over all Egypt (correct)
D) Pharaoh commanded Joseph to bow down and worship the Egyptian sphinx
Feedback: Pharaoh gave Joseph his signet ring, fine linen robes, a gold chain, and the second chariot (Genesis 41:42-43). Joseph was never commanded to worship idols.

### Q45 [multi-select]
Stem: Which of the following virtues were demonstrated in the life and conduct of Ruth in Bethlehem? (Select all that apply)
A) Selfless love and steadfast loyalty to her widowed mother-in-law Naomi (correct)
B) Humble, tireless diligence gleaning in the barley harvest fields (correct)
C) Noble character and modesty recognized by Boaz and the community (correct)
D) Demanding high wages from Naomi before helping her
Feedback: Ruth demonstrated sacrificial loyalty, humble hard work, and noble moral character (Ruth 1:16; 2:7; 3:11). She never demanded money from Naomi.

### Q46 [matching]
Stem: Match each biblical figure with their specific confession or cry of repentance/obedience:
- King David: "I have sinned against the Lord" (2 Samuel 12:13)
- Zacchaeus: "If I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold" (Luke 19:8)
- Ruth: "Where you go I will go... your people shall be my people, and your God my God" (Ruth 1:16)
- Joseph: "How then can I do this great wickedness and sin against God?" (Genesis 39:9)

### Q47 [matching]
Stem: Match each place with its significance in the Second Term curriculum:
- Nineveh: Great Assyrian city that averted destruction through national repentance and fasting
- Shiloh: Location of the Tabernacle and Ark where Eli and his sons ministered
- Mount Moriah: Mountain region where Abraham offered the ram in place of Isaac
- Bethlehem: "House of Bread" where Ruth gleaned in the fields of Boaz

### Q48 [matching]

### Q49 [matching]

### Q50 [matching]
Stem: Match each life event of Joseph with the corresponding stage of his service:
- Dothan: Stripped of his coat of many colors and sold into slavery for 20 silver pieces
- Potiphar's House: Managed domestic estate with integrity and fled sexual temptation
- Pharaoh's Prison: Interpreted dreams of the cupbearer and baker while managing prisoners
- Egyptian Palace: Stored grain during seven years of plenty and saved nations in famine
Feedback: Dothan was the pit; Potiphar's house was domestic management/purity; prison was dream interpretation/care; and palace was Prime Minister and grain administrator.
