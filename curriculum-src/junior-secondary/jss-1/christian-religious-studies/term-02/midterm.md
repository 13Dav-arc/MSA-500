## Hook
Congratulations on reaching the midpoint of your Second Term in JSS 1 Christian Religious Studies! Over the past four weeks, you have explored some of the most dynamic, transformative, and solemn accounts in biblical history. You examined the true nature of repentance—not merely feeling bad about getting caught, but turning completely around with a humble heart and making things right. You saw King David convicted by Nathan's parable and Zacchaeus restoring fourfold from his sycamore encounter. You witnessed an entire heathen empire, Nineveh, fall to its knees in sackcloth and ashes, and heard John the Baptist's trumpet call to bear fruits worthy of repentance. You walked through the sobering halls of Shiloh to witness the tragic ruin of High Priest Eli and his sons, Hophni and Phinehas, who despised God's holy sacrifices and brought the departure of God's glory (*Ichabod*). Finally, you marveled at the courage of Abraham, who at seventy-five years old left everything familiar to follow God's voice into the land of Canaan. Now, it is time to consolidate your knowledge and demonstrate your mastery in this Midterm Continuous Assessment!

## Key Terms
- Term: Metanoia
- Definition: The Greek term for genuine repentance, signifying a complete transformation of mind, heart, purpose, and behavior toward God.
- Term: Restitution
- Definition: Making practical amends, repairing damages, or returning stolen or defrauded goods to their rightful owners.
- Term: Prophet Nathan
- Definition: God's courageous messenger who convicted King David with the parable of the stolen ewe lamb.
- Term: Zacchaeus
- Definition: The wealthy chief tax collector in Jericho who demonstrated genuine repentance by giving half his wealth to the poor and restoring fourfold.
- Term: Nineveh
- Definition: The imperial Assyrian city that averted divine destruction within forty days by humbling itself in universal fasting, sackcloth, and prayer.
- Term: John the Baptist
- Definition: The wilderness prophet and forerunner to Christ who baptized for repentance and gave practical ethical instructions to crowds, tax collectors, and soldiers.
- Term: Hophni and Phinehas
- Definition: The corrupt sons of High Priest Eli whose impenitence, sacrilege, and immorality led to defeat at Aphek, the capture of the Ark, and their deaths.
- Term: Ichabod
- Definition: The name given by Phinehas' dying wife to her son, meaning "The glory has departed from Israel."
- Term: Abrahamic Covenant
- Definition: God's sovereign covenant with Abram (Genesis 12), promising a great nation, great name, divine protection, and blessing to all families of the earth.
- Term: Shechem and Bethel
- Definition: The early worship locations in Canaan where Abram erected stone altars and called upon the name of the Lord.

1. **The Meaning and Practice of Repentance (Weeks 01–02):**
   - Repentance is an inward change of heart resulting in outward fruits of ethical righteousness.
   - King David models genuine confession without excuse: *"I have sinned against the Lord."*
   - Zacchaeus models radical restitution: fourfold refund to the defrauded and 50% given to the poor.
   - Nineveh proves that no society is too wicked for God's mercy when leaders and citizens genuinely fast, put on sackcloth, and turn away from violence.
   - John the Baptist highlights practical social righteousness: sharing extra food and coats, collecting only lawful taxes, and avoiding military extortion.

2. **The Peril of Impenitence and Parental Neglect (Week 03):**
   - Hophni and Phinehas abused the sacred priesthood by forcibly taking sacrificial meat before the fat was offered and committing immorality at the sanctuary.
   - Eli failed to restrain them, honoring his sons above God.
   - Treating the Ark of God like a magical talisman did not save Israel from military slaughter at Aphek.
   - Persistent rebellion against God leads to the tragedy of *Ichabod*—the departure of divine presence and glory.

3. **Faith and Radical Obedience (Week 04):**
   - God called Abram at age 75 to abandon country, kindred, and father's house.
   - Abram obeyed immediately without knowing the full map, trusting God's sevenfold covenant promises.
   - Wherever Abram pitched his tent in Canaan (Shechem, Bethel), he erected stone altars to worship the living God.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Thinking feeling guilty is identical to repentance. Guilt is simply an emotional signal; repentance is the decisive turning away from sin toward God and making restitution.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming religious status protects against divine judgment. Hophni and Phinehas were sons of the High Priest and carried the holy Ark, but were slain because of their unrepentant sins.
- **Mistake 3:** Believing Abraham was forced to leave Haran. God invited Abram through promise; Abram exercised free will and faith by actively choosing to obey.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking John the Baptist required people to become monks or hermits. John told citizens to remain in their professions (soldiers, tax collectors) but to conduct them with honesty, non-violence, and generosity.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Comparing David's Repentance and Eli's Sons' Impenitence
*Question:* Contrast King David's reaction when confronted by Prophet Nathan with Hophni and Phinehas' reaction when warned by their father Eli.
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Analyze David's reaction (2 Samuel 12:13):** As supreme monarch, David could have executed Nathan. Instead, he humbled himself immediately, took full responsibility, confessed *"I have sinned against the Lord,"* and pleaded for mercy in Psalm 51.
2. **Analyze Hophni and Phinehas' reaction (1 Samuel 2:25):** When warned about their sacrilege and immorality, they refused to listen or change their behavior, hardening their hearts in stubborn arrogance.
3. **Conclusion:** David received forgiveness and spiritual restoration; Hophni and Phinehas faced sudden destruction on the battlefield.

### Example 2: Analyzing Abraham's Covenant Faith (Genesis 12)
*Question:* What makes Abraham's obedience in Genesis 12:4 a gold standard for Christian discipleship?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Evaluate his age and security:** Abram was 75 years old, wealthy, and deeply settled in Haran with his extended family.
2. **Examine the cost:** He had to separate from his country, his clan, and his inheritance without seeing the destination first.
3. **Evaluate his execution:** He did not delay, make excuses, or demand proof. He packed his tents, took Sarai and Lot, and walked into Canaan, immediately dedicating the land through worship altars.
4. **Pedagogical Takeaway:** True faith is demonstrated by active, trusting, and prompt obedience to God's word.

## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the primary meaning of biblical repentance (*metanoia*)?
A) Feeling sorrow for wrongdoing, turning away from sin, and obeying God (correct)
B) Blaming others for your mistakes so you avoid punishment
C) Paying money to a temple to purchase forgiveness
D) Doing penance in public while continuing the bad habit
Feedback: Biblical repentance is a complete change of mind, heart, and purpose, resulting in turning away from sin toward God and righteous living.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: Which prophet did God send to confront King David after his sins involving Bathsheba and Uriah?
A) Prophet Elijah
B) Prophet Nathan (correct)
C) Prophet Isaiah
D) Prophet Samuel
Feedback: God sent the Prophet Nathan to confront King David through the convicting parable of the poor man's ewe lamb (2 Samuel 12:1-7).

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: In which psalm did King David pour out his broken heart and plead for a clean heart after his sin was exposed?
A) Psalm 23
B) Psalm 51 (correct)
C) Psalm 91
D) Psalm 121
Feedback: Psalm 51 contains David's famous penitential prayer: "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me."

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: What was Zacchaeus' profession in the city of Jericho?
A) A fisherman
B) A chief tax collector (correct)
C) A Roman soldier
D) A carpenter
Feedback: Luke 19:2 states that Zacchaeus was a chief tax collector (*architelones*) and was very wealthy.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: What tree did Zacchaeus climb in order to see Jesus above the crowd in Jericho?
A) An olive tree
B) An oak tree
C) A sycamore-fig tree (correct)
D) A cedar tree
Feedback: Luke 19:4 records that Zacchaeus ran ahead and climbed up into a sycamore tree to see Jesus.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: How did Zacchaeus demonstrate that his repentance was genuine when Jesus visited his home?
A) He gave half his goods to the poor and promised fourfold restitution to anyone he had defrauded (correct)

C) He became a Roman tax commissioner
D) He moved his house outside of Jericho

D) "Build stone walls against the Assyrians!"

### Q8 [single-choice]

C) He laughed and threw a grand banquet
D) He fled the city on horseback
Feedback: Jonah 3:6 describes the king's humility: he stepped down from his throne, took off his royal robe, put on sackcloth, and sat in ashes.

A) Abandon tax collecting completely
B) Collect no more than what you are authorized to collect (correct)
C) Increase tax rates by twenty percent
D) Pay taxes only to the temple
Feedback: Luke 3:13 records John's instruction: "Collect no more than you are authorized to do."

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: What counsel did John the Baptist give to soldiers regarding their conduct?
A) Use weapons to seize food from markets
B) Do not extort money by threats or false accusations, and be content with your wages (correct)

### Q11 [single-choice]

### Q12 [single-choice]
