## Hook
Have you ever heard someone in your town or village say: *"Ah, look at that boy or girl! You can tell immediately that they come from a good home"*? Why do people say that? It isn't because the student is wearing gold jewelry or riding in a luxury car. It is because of their polite greetings, honest speech, quiet humility, and readiness to help others. In Nigeria and throughout biblical history, one of the greatest treasures a person can inherit or pass down is not a bank account, but a **good family name**. King Solomon declared in Proverbs 22:1: *"A good name is to be chosen rather than great riches, and favor is better than silver and gold."* In this lesson, you will explore the specific daily roles and responsibilities that make a household flourish, discover what it truly means to protect your family's moral reputation, and learn how to remember your Creator in the days of your youth!

## Key Terms
- Term: Good Family Name
- Definition: A household's earned reputation for moral integrity, godliness, honesty, respect, and peaceful coexistence within the community.
- Term: Reputation
- Definition: The overall estimation, character assessment, and social esteem in which a person or family is held by the public.
- Term: Moral Integrity
- Definition: Consistent soundness of moral character, practicing truthfulness and virtue both in private and in public.
- Term: Domestic Responsibilities
- Definition: Daily practical duties (cooking, cleaning, security, finances, childcare) required to sustain a healthy, functional household.
- Term: Paternal Leadership
- Definition: The God-given duty of a father to provide spiritual direction, physical protection, financial sustenance, and moral example for his family.
- Term: Maternal Nurture
- Definition: The loving, diligent care, emotional support, domestic management, and character formation provided by a mother (Proverbs 31).
- Term: Filial Responsibility
- Definition: The active contribution of children toward home harmony through chores, academic effort, obedience, and moral uprightness.
- Term: Ecclesiastes 12:1
- Definition: The sacred biblical exhortation commanding youth: *"Remember also your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come."*

### Specific Roles of Family Members
1. **The Father's Responsibilities:**
   - **Spiritual Leadership:** Leading daily family prayers, reading scripture, and taking the family to church (Joshua 24:15: *"As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord"*).
   - **Economic Provision:** Working diligently to provide food, shelter, clothing, medical care, and school fees (1 Timothy 5:8: *"If anyone does not provide for his relatives, and especially for members of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever"*).
   - **Protection and Discipline:** Guarding the home against physical danger and negative moral influences, correcting children with firmness and love.
2. **The Mother's Responsibilities:**
   - **Nurturing and Teaching:** Instilling early moral values, hygiene, manners, and faith in the children (Proverbs 1:8: *"Do not forsake your mother's teaching"*).
   - **Home Administration:** Managing household resources, meal preparation, cleanliness, and domestic comfort with the diligence of the Proverbs 31 woman.
   - **Emotional Anchor:** Providing empathetic listening, comfort, peacemaking, and encouragement to husband and children.
3. **The Children's Responsibilities:**
   - **Domestic Support:** Performing daily chores—sweeping the compound, washing dishes, fetching water, running errands, and tending gardens—without grumbling.
   - **Academic Seriousness:** Focusing on school studies, completing homework, and striving for academic excellence to justify parents' sacrifices.
   - **Family Ambassadorship:** Behaving with modesty, honesty, and politeness outside the home, knowing their conduct reflects directly on their family name.

### The Concept of a Good Family Name (Proverbs 22:1; Ecclesiastes 12:1)
- **The Value of a Name over Riches:** In biblical thought, a "name" represents character, honor, credibility, and moral standing.
  - *"A good name is to be chosen rather than great riches, and favor is better than silver and gold"* (Proverbs 22:1).
  - Money can be lost, stolen, or devalued overnight, but a reputation for honesty, righteousness, and dependability endures across generations.
- **The Youth's Divine Mandate:** King Solomon instructs young people in Ecclesiastes 12:1:
  - *"Remember also your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come and the years draw near of which you will say, 'I have no pleasure in them.'"*
  - Youth is the golden season of vitality, memory, energy, and opportunity. Dedicating one's youth to God prevents decades of moral regret, addiction, and dishonor.

### How Children Protect or Destroy the Family Name
- **Actions that Protect and Elevate the Family Name:**
  1. Truthfulness and honesty in school and community interactions.
  2. Academic diligence and refusal to engage in examination malpractice.
  3. Respectful speech toward elders, teachers, and peers.
  4. Generosity and willingness to help elderly neighbors and the needy.
- **Actions that Tarnish and Destroy the Family Name:**
  1. Stealing, fraud (*yahoo-yahoo*), or cybercrime.
  2. Joining secret cults, street gangs, or drug-abusing peer groups.
  3. Arrogance, rudeness, and street fighting.
  4. Academic laziness, truancy, and cheating.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing only adults are responsible for the family reputation. A child's bad behavior in school or on the street instantly reflects on their parents and family name.
- **Mistake 2:** Thinking a "good name" means coming from a rich or famous dynasty. Even the poorest family can possess a sterling, illustrious name through unbending honesty, hard work, and godly character.
- **Mistake 3:** Assuming youth is the time to live recklessly before repenting in old age. Ecclesiastes 12:1 warns youth to remember God *now*, because bad habits formed in youth often become lifetime chains.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking boys should not do domestic chores like cooking or washing. Every child, male or female, has a moral duty to contribute to household chores and cleanliness.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Applying Proverbs 22:1 to Modern Peer Pressure
*Question:* A classmate offers to sell a leaked examination paper to a student named Ngozi for ₦2,000, promising she will score an 'A' without studying. How does Proverbs 22:1 guide Ngozi's choice?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Analyze the temptation:** Ngozi wants a high grade and is tempted by an easy shortcut.
2. **Evaluate the risk to her "name":** If caught, she faces exam cancellation, suspension, and public disgrace that will humiliate her parents and tarnish her family name.
3. **Apply Proverbs 22:1:** *"A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches."* An honest 'B' or 'C' achieved with integrity preserves her honor and clear conscience before God, which is infinitely more valuable than an unearned 'A' acquired by fraud.
4. **Action:** Ngozi firmly rejects the offer and reports the misconduct, preserving her family's reputation.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Warning of Ecclesiastes 12:1
*Question:* Why does King Solomon specifically command young people to remember their Creator *in the days of their youth*?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Examine the phrase "days of your youth":** Youth is when habits, character, worldviews, and physical health are formed.
2. **Examine the phrase "before the evil days come":** Old age brings physical weakness, fading eyesight, memory loss, and reduced strength, making it harder to establish new spiritual disciplines.
3. **Synthesize the moral lesson:** Serving God in youth safeguards a person from destructive choices, laying a foundation of righteousness that blesses their future children and community.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Discussion:** Read Proverbs 22:1 and Ecclesiastes 12:1 together. Ask your child: "What does our family name stand for in this community? What values do we want people to think of when they hear our surname?"
- **Responsibility Audit:** Review your child's weekly chore roster at home. Affirm their contributions to the family's welfare and encourage pride in domestic cleanliness and mutual support.
