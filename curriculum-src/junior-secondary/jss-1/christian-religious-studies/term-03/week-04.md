## Hook
Suppose your best friend's father is the supreme military ruler of the country, and one day, the ruler becomes furiously jealous of you and declares: "This boy must die!" What would your friend do? Would he take his father's side to guarantee his own future as the next ruler? Or would he risk his own life, face his father's flying spear, and sneak into the countryside to warn you and help you escape? That was the life-and-death test faced by Prince Jonathan in 1 Samuel 20! At the same time, Jesus told a famous parable about a friend who pounded on his neighbor's locked door at midnight to ask for three loaves of bread to feed an unexpected traveler. In this lesson, you will dive deep into the legendary covenant friendship of David and Jonathan, discover Jesus' teaching on persistence and friendship in the Parable of the Friend at Midnight, and equip yourself with sharp biblical wisdom to resist the seductive traps of bad companions!

## Key Terms
- Term: Covenant Friendship
- Definition: A solemn, inviolable commitment of mutual loyalty, love, protection, and shared destiny entered into before God.
- Term: King Saul
- Definition: The first king of Israel, whose jealous obsession with David drove him to repeatedly attempt to kill him, despite Jonathan's interventions.
- Term: Ezel Stone
- Definition: The agreed secret meeting place in the field where Jonathan tested King Saul's intentions and signaled David with three arrows.
- Term: The Arrow Signal
- Definition: The covert code used by Jonathan: shooting arrows beyond a young boy to secretly signal to hidden David that Saul was determined to kill him.
- Term: Parable of the Friend at Midnight (Luke 11:5–13)
- Definition: Jesus' teaching illustrating persistence, boldness, and hospitality in friendship, and how God generously answers earnest prayer.
- Term: Impunity / Persistence (*Anaideia*)
- Definition: Bold, shameless persistence and unyielding determination in asking for what is needed, refusing to give up.
- Term: Proverbs 1:10
- Definition: The timeless moral warning: *"My son, if sinners entice you, do not consent."*
- Term: Discernment
- Definition: The spiritual ability to judge wisely between good and bad character, truth and falsehood, genuine friends and dangerous deceivers.

## Core Concept
### The Supreme Test of David and Jonathan's Friendship (1 Samuel 20:1–42)
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Relationship in the School II" caption="Relationship in the School II: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

- **Saul's Murderous Envy:** As David's military fame grew, King Saul burned with insane jealousy, hurling spears at David and ordering his death. David fled and confided in Jonathan: *"What have I done? What is my guilt, and what is my sin before your father, that he seeks my life?"*
- **Jonathan's Covenant Loyalty:** Jonathan refused to believe his father would act without telling him, but David warned: *"Your father knows well that I have found favor in your eyes, and he thinks, 'Do not let Jonathan know this, lest he be grieved.' But truly, as the Lord lives and as your soul lives, there is but a step between me and death"* (1 Samuel 20:3).
- **The Secret Test at the New Moon Feast:**
  - David hid in the field while Jonathan attended the royal feast. On the second day, Saul noticed David's empty seat and asked why the son of Jesse was missing.
  - Jonathan explained that David had earnestly asked permission to attend a family sacrifice in Bethlehem.
  - Saul's anger blazed against Jonathan: *"You son of a perverse, rebellious woman! Do I not know that you have chosen the son of Jesse to your own shame... For as long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth, neither you nor your kingdom shall be established. Therefore send and bring him to me, for he shall surely die!"*
  - When Jonathan defended David, asking: *"Why should he be put to death? What has he done?"*, Saul hurled his spear at Jonathan to strike him. Jonathan rose from the table in fierce anger, knowing his father was determined to murder David.
- **The Covenant Signal with the Arrows:**
  - The next morning, Jonathan went out to the field near the **Stone Ezel** with a small boy.
  - Jonathan shot three arrows and called out to the boy: *"Is not the arrow beyond you? Hurry! Make haste! Do not stay!"* This was the agreed secret signal to hidden David: *Flee immediately; Saul seeks your blood!*
  - After sending the boy back to town with his weapons, David came out from behind the stone. They embraced, wept bitterly—David weeping the most—and Jonathan said: *"Go in peace, because we have sworn both of us in the name of the Lord, saying, 'The Lord shall be between me and you, and between my offspring and your offspring, forever.'"*

### The Parable of the Friend at Midnight (Luke 11:5–13)
Jesus taught this parable to illustrate bold persistence in friendship and prayer:
- **The Dilemma:** A man receives an unexpected traveler at midnight. In ancient Middle Eastern culture, failing to show hospitality to a guest was a grave social disgrace. The host had no bread in his cupboard.
- **The Bold Appeal:** He runs to his friend's house and calls through the door: *"Friend, lend me three loaves, for a friend of mine has arrived on a journey, and I have nothing to set before him."*
- **The Initial Refusal:** The neighbor answers from within: *"Do not bother me; the door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed. I cannot get up and give you anything."*
- **The Power of Persistence (*Anaideia*):** Jesus reveals: *"I tell you, though he will not get up and give him anything because he is his friend, yet because of his impudence [shameless persistence] he will rise and give him whatever he needs."*
- **The Spiritual Application:**
  - *"Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you."*
  - If earthly human beings, being imperfect, know how to give good gifts to their children and friends, how much more will the heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!

### Discerning Good Friends vs. Bad Friends (Proverbs 1:10–19)
- **The Enticement of Sinners:** King Solomon gives an urgent warning: *"My son, if sinners entice you, do not consent"* (Proverbs 1:10).
- **The Seductive Tactics of Bad Friends:**
  1. *"Come with us, let us lie in wait for blood; let us ambush the innocent without reason."* (Recruiting into gangs or cults).
  2. *"We shall find all precious goods, we shall fill our houses with plunder."* (Promises of fast money, fraud, or stolen goods).
  3. *"Throw in your lot among us; we will all have one purse."* (False loyalty and equal sharing of criminal loot).
- **The Inevitable End:** Scripture warns: *"Their feet run to evil... these men lie in wait for their own blood; they set an ambush for their own lives. Such are the ways of everyone who is greedy for unjust gain; it takes away the life of its possessors"* (Proverbs 1:16–19).

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing Jonathan betrayed his country by helping David. David was an innocent national hero being persecuted unjustly by an unstable king; Jonathan upheld divine justice and righteousness.
- **Mistake 2:** Thinking the neighbor gave bread because he liked midnight noise. Jesus explicitly stated that while friendship alone was not enough to wake him, the man's bold, shameless persistence compelled him to give the bread.
- **Mistake 3:** Assuming "just hanging out" with cult members won't affect you. Proverbs 1:15 commands: *"My son, do not walk in the way with them; hold back your foot from their paths."* Complete avoidance of criminal circles is the only safe boundary.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking David forgot Jonathan after he became king. In 2 Samuel 9, King David sought out Jonathan's crippled son, Mephibosheth, restored all Saul's family land to him, and had him eat at the royal table for the rest of his life.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Deconstructing the "One Purse" Seduction in Modern Schools
*Question:* A group of older boys in the hostel invites a JSS 1 student named Dayo, saying: *"Join our secret campus squad. We protect each other, share stolen exam questions, and have one common purse. Nobody will ever touch you in this school!"* How does Proverbs 1:10–19 dismantle this trap?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the parallel:** The invitation matches Proverbs 1:14: *"Throw in your lot among us; we will all have one purse."*
2. **Examine the hidden consequence:** Proverbs 1:18 reveals that criminals *set an ambush for their own lives*. Secret cults and cheating rings bring expulsion, police arrest, criminal records, and physical violence.
3. **Apply Proverbs 1:10:** *"My son, if sinners entice you, do not consent."* The word "consent" means saying 'yes' or cooperating.
4. **Action:** Dayo must say a firm, immediate "NO!", walk away, and report the solicitation to the school guidance counselor or principal.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Cultural Principle of Hospitality in Luke 11
*Question:* Why was the host in Luke 11 so desperate that he risked waking up his neighbor's entire sleeping family at midnight?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify ancient Near Eastern culture:** Welcoming travelers and providing fresh bread was a sacred moral obligation. Sending a hungry traveler to sleep without food brought shame on the entire village.
2. **Analyze the host's humility:** The host was unashamed to admit his poverty ("I have nothing to set before him") to serve his guest.
3. **Connect to prayer:** Jesus uses this human desperation to show that if an annoyed neighbor will give bread due to persistence, our loving heavenly Father will never turn away those who pray with persistent faith.

## Guardian Sync
- **Family Devotion Discussion:** Read Proverbs 1:10-15 together. Ask your child: "Have you ever been invited by classmates to do something you knew was wrong—like mocking a teacher, vandalizing desks, or copying homework? How did you respond?"
- **Covenant Loyalty Lesson:** Discuss the story of David and Jonathan, emphasizing that real friends protect each other from danger and uphold righteousness, rather than covering up crimes.
