## Hook
Step outside your front gate on a Saturday morning in any Nigerian town or neighborhood. What do you see? You see market women setting out trays of fresh tomatoes, elders sitting under shade trees discussing community affairs, young men clearing drainage ditches during environmental sanitation, children playing football on the sand pitch, and neighbors greeting one another with warm smiles: *"E kaaro sir!"*, *"Bawo ni?"*, *"Sannu da aiki!"*, *"Ndewo daada!"* That living, breathing network of people sharing the same geographical space, resources, and civic destiny is your **community**. But a community only survives when its members respect leaders, obey public laws, contribute to joint projects, and care for those who cannot care for themselves. Jesus declared in Matthew 25 that whatever kindness we show to the poorest, hungriest, and most vulnerable neighbor, we do directly unto Him! In this lesson, you will discover the biblical foundations of community life, explore the role of traditional and civic leaders, and learn how Christian youth become powerful catalysts for community harmony!

## Key Terms
- Term: Community
- Definition: A group of people living together in the same geographical locality, town, village, or neighborhood sharing common facilities, interests, rules, and cultural heritage.
- Term: Community Leaders
- Definition: Respected figures entrusted with authority to guide, arbitrate, and administer community affairs, including traditional rulers, chiefs, elders, youth leaders, and ward councilors.
- Term: Traditional Rulers
- Definition: Custodians of culture, customary law, and local peace, such as Obas, Emirs, Obis, and Chiefs in Nigerian societies.
- Term: Community Development Projects
- Definition: Cooperative civic initiatives organized by residents (e.g., grading roads, building health clinics, drilling boreholes, cleaning gutters) to improve local living conditions.
- Term: Civic Duty
- Definition: The moral and legal obligations of citizens to obey laws, pay local levies, participate in sanitation, and promote public peace.
- Term: Vulnerable Groups
- Definition: Persons in the community requiring special social support, care, and protection, such as orphans, motherless babies, the elderly, widows, and people living with disabilities.
- Term: Matthew 25:40
- Definition: Jesus' sacred judgment principle: *"Truly, I say to you, as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me."*
- Term: Harmonious Coexistence
- Definition: Living together in mutual respect, peace, tolerance, and constructive cooperation despite differences in ethnicity, background, or social status.

### Community Leadership Structures
In Nigerian communities, multiple tiers of leadership collaborate to sustain order:
1. **Traditional Rulers and Chiefs:**
   - Obas (Yoruba), Emirs (Hausa/Fulani), Obis/Ezes (Igbo), and other royal titleholders.
   - Act as sacred custodians of ancestral heritage, arbitrate family and land disputes, and maintain communal peace.
2. **Council of Elders and Community Development Associations (CDAs):**
   - Respected senior citizens and neighborhood leaders who plan security rosters, street lighting, and sanitation days.
3. **Religious and Educational Leaders:**
   - Pastors, Imams, school principals, and teachers who provide moral instruction and mentor youth.
4. **Youth and Women Leaders:**
   - Organize communal labor, cultural festivals, security surveillance, and market welfare.

### Ways of Sustaining Harmonious Community Relationships
Peace in a neighborhood does not happen by accident; it requires conscious, daily ethical practices:
- **1. Respect for Traditional and Civic Authority:**
  - Submitting to community rules and respecting elders creates social stability. When citizens despise authority, fighting, chaos, and crime proliferate.
- **2. Obedience to Law and Order:**
  - Laws are enacted to govern behavior, protect lives, and preserve property. Citizens must respect traffic rules, avoid public disturbances, and report suspicious activities to lawful authorities.
- **3. Participation in Community Development Projects:**
  - Governments alone cannot build every facility. Responsible citizens pay community security levies, contribute toward transformer repairs, drill communal boreholes, and participate in monthly environmental cleanups.
- **4. Peaceful Conflict Resolution:**
  - Settling boundary disputes, noise complaints, or disagreements through dialogue and arbitration rather than violent physical clashes or police litigation.

### Caring for the Poor, Disabled, and Motherless Babies (Matthew 25:31–46)
The definitive Christian measure of community health is how it treats its most vulnerable members:
- **The Judgment of the Sheep and Goats:**
  - When the Son of Man comes in His glory, He will separate all nations as a shepherd separates sheep from goats.
  - To the righteous on His right, the King will say: *"Come, you who are blessed by my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry and you gave me food, I was thirsty and you gave me drink, I was a stranger and you welcomed me, I was naked and you clothed me, I was sick and you visited me, I was in prison and you came to me"* (Matthew 25:34–36).
  - When the righteous ask when they did this, the King answers: **"Truly, I say to you, as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me"** (Matthew 25:40).
- **Visiting Orphanages and Motherless Babies Homes:**
  - Homes where children who have lost their parents are nurtured. Churches, schools, and families visit these centers with food, clothing, baby formula, and love, reminding them they are treasured by God.
- **Honoring and Assisting Persons with Disabilities:**
  - Providing physical ramps, assistive devices, and emotional inclusion, never mocking physical challenges or isolating disabled persons from social participation.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Thinking community development is solely the government's job. Governments provide macro infrastructure, but local maintenance, security vigilance, and sanitation require active citizen participation.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming charity only counts when giving large millions of Naira. Sharing a cup of rice, offering a bottle of clean water, or helping a blind neighbor cross a busy road are profoundly valued by God (Matthew 10:42).
- **Mistake 3:** Believing traditional rulers oppose Christian faith. Most traditional rulers serve as civic peacemakers and guardians of law and order; Christians are commanded to honor and pray for them.
- **Mistake 4:** Ignoring the plight of motherless babies and orphans. James 1:27 defines pure religion before God as visiting orphans and widows in their affliction.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Applying Matthew 25:35–40 to a School Outreach
*Question:* A JSS 1 Christian Fellowship club raises ₦15,000 and two cartons of noodles to visit a local motherless babies' home. How does Matthew 25:40 interpret their outreach?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the recipients:** The children in the motherless babies' home represent the *"least of these"*—defenseless, vulnerable orphans who cannot repay the favor.
2. **Examine Christ's identification:** In Matthew 25:40, Jesus does not say "you did it *like* you did it to me"; He says *"you did it TO ME."* Jesus takes personal ownership of how the vulnerable are treated.
3. **Pedagogical Meaning:** The students' modest gift of noodles and love is counted by God as direct worship and service to Jesus Christ Himself.

### Example 2: Resolving Neighborhood Drainage Blockages
*Question:* During rainy season, rainwater overflows into a school compound because a commercial shopkeeper dumps trash into the street drainage gutter. How should community relationship principles resolve this?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the violation:** Dumping trash into public gutters violates community sanitation laws and creates health hazards (floods, malaria).
2. **Apply non-violent civil procedure:** Students or school leaders should not smash the shopkeeper's goods.
3. **Engage community leadership:** Report the matter to the Community Development Association (CDA) chairman, local environmental health officers, or the market chief.
4. **Conclusion:** Peaceful dialogue and enforcement by recognized community leaders preserves both public hygiene and neighborhood harmony.

## Guardian Sync
- **Family Community Action:** Plan a family visit with your child to a local orphanage or motherless babies' home. Have your child present a small food item or used clothing to experience the joy of Matthew 25 firsthand.
- **Civic Awareness Discussion:** Discuss with your child the importance of monthly environmental sanitation, paying neighborhood security dues, and treating security guards and street cleaners with dignity and respect.
