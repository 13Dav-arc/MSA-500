## Key Terms
- Term: Imago Dei
- Definition: The image and likeness of God bestowed uniquely upon human beings, imparting inviolable dignity, moral conscience, and spiritual purpose.
- Term: Filial Obedience
- Definition: The scriptural duty of children to honor, respect, and obey their parents in the Lord with promptness and sincerity (Ephesians 6:1–3).
- Term: A Good Family Name
- Definition: A household's earned reputation for godliness, integrity, truthfulness, and civic honor, more precious than silver and gold (Proverbs 22:1).
- Term: In Loco Parentis
- Definition: The legal and moral principle whereby school teachers and administrators act in the place of parents to guide, protect, and instruct students.
- Term: Covenant Friendship
- Definition: A deep, selfless, and inviolable bond of mutual loyalty and protection entered into before God, modeled by David and Prince Jonathan.
- Term: The Least of These (Matthew 25:40)
- Definition: The poor, hungry, sick, imprisoned, orphans, and vulnerable persons with whom Jesus Christ intimately identifies His own presence.
- Term: Body of Christ (Ekklesia)
- Definition: The universal spiritual community of all Christian believers functioning as an interdependent organism under the supreme headship of Jesus Christ.
- Term: Conscientious Submission (Romans 13:5)
- Definition: Obeying lawful civil authorities and paying taxes not merely out of fear of punishment, but for the sake of a clear conscience before God.
- Term: Acts 5:29
- Definition: The supreme higher-allegiance mandate: *"We must obey God rather than men"* when civil commands directly force a believer to sin.
- Term: The Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12)
- Definition: Jesus' universal ethical standard: *"Whatever you wish that others would do to you, do also to them."*

1. **Foundations of Creation and Covenant (Term 1):**
   - God is the eternal, sovereign Architect who spoke reality into existence *ex nihilo*.
   - Man was formed from the dust and animated by God's breath, given dominion as a steward.
   - Marriage was instituted in Eden for companionship, mutual support, and procreation ("one flesh").
   - Disobedience brought spiritual death, shame, guilt, and judgment, balanced by God's promise of redemption (*Protoevangelium*, Genesis 3:15) and protective coats of skins.
   - Human corruption brought the Flood; Noah's faithful obedience preserved life in the Ark; God established the universal rainbow covenant affirming the sanctity of human life.

2. **Repentance, Obedience, and Faithful Service (Term 2):**
   - True repentance (*metanoia*) requires honest confession (David, Psalm 51) and practical restitution (Zacchaeus, Luke 19).
   - Entire cities can be saved through genuine contrition (Nineveh, Jonah 3), while persistent impenitence and weak parenting destroy dynasties (Eli, Hophni, Phinehas, *Ichabod*).
   - Biblical obedience means stepping out into the unknown at age 75 (Abraham, Genesis 12) and yielding one's dearest treasure on the altar of testing (Isaac on Moriah, *Jehovah Jireh*).
   - True greatness is servanthood: Joseph maintained purity in Potiphar's house and fed nations from prison, while Ruth gleaned humbly in Bethlehem to enter the royal lineage of Jesus Christ.

3. **Relationships in Family, School, Community, and Nation (Term 3):**
   - **Family:** Children honor and obey parents for long life; fathers lead without provoking; spouses love and respect each other; a good family name is chosen above great riches (Proverbs 22:1); youth remember their Creator (Ecclesiastes 12:1).
   - **School:** School is an extended family; teachers act *in loco parentis*; friends must be chosen for character and diligence ("Iron sharpens iron"); Jonathan models selfless covenant loyalty; the midnight host models bold persistence; youth reject the seductive "one purse" gang trap (Proverbs 1:10).
   - **Community and Church:** Leaders (traditional, civic, pastors, bishops) preserve order; true religion cares for the vulnerable (Matthew 25:40; James 1:27); the Church is the unified Body of Christ, overcoming evil with good (Romans 12:21).
   - **Civil State and Multi-Faith Society:** Governing authorities are ordained by God; citizens pay taxes and obey laws for conscience' sake (Romans 13:5; Matthew 22:21); when forced to sin, believers obey God rather than men (Acts 5:29); religious tolerance and peaceful coexistence flourish through the Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12) and Good Samaritan mercy (Luke 10).

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing religion is only about rituals performed on Sunday morning. Christian Religious Studies teaches that true faith transforms how we treat parents, classmates, government officials, and neighbors of other faiths every day of the week.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming a good family name comes from expensive designer clothes. A good family name is built on unbending moral integrity, truthfulness, and godliness.
- **Mistake 3:** Thinking the Good Samaritan was an orthodox Jewish priest. The Samaritan was from a rival group; Jesus used him to show that genuine love crosses all ethnic and sectarian barriers.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking civil obedience is absolute without limits. Christians obey lawful government, but when a human ruler commands sin or forbids worship, Acts 5:29 applies: *"We must obey God rather than men."*
- **Mistake 5:** Believing religious tolerance means believing all religions are identical. Tolerance means defending your neighbor's right to live in peace and practice their faith without hatred or violence, while keeping your own Christian convictions clear and firm.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Integrating Sessional Ethics in a National Crisis
*Question:* During an ethnic and religious clash in a Nigerian town, a displaced family of another faith seeks refuge in a Christian church compound. How should the church respond using the combined lessons of Matthew 25:35, Luke 10:33–35, and Romans 12:20?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Apply Matthew 25:35:** *"I was a stranger and you welcomed me."* Welcoming displaced, vulnerable people is counted by Jesus as welcoming Christ Himself.
2. **Apply the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:33):** Love is not restricted by religious borders; the Samaritan showed mercy to a wounded man from a rival faith.
3. **Apply Romans 12:20:** *"If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him something to drink; for by so doing you will heap burning coals on his head. Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good."*
4. **Action:** The church provides food, water, medical aid, and physical shelter to the displaced family, demonstrating the radical, transformative love of the Gospel to the entire nation.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Contrast Between Jonathan and Saul
*Question:* Contrast King Saul's treatment of David with Prince Jonathan's treatment of David. What does this contrast teach about jealousy versus love?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Saul's attitude:** Driven by insecurity, envy, and pride; saw David as a threat to his dynasty; hurled spears at David and Jonathan; broke covenant with God.
2. **Jonathan's attitude:** Driven by selfless covenant love; stripped his royal robe to honor David; defended David at the risk of his life; made a multi-generational covenant of peace.
3. **Core lesson:** Envy destroys leaders and households; true Christian love rejoices in others' gifts, puts others first, and establishes lasting peace.
