## Hook
Imagine waking up in Nigeria one morning to discover that there are no traffic lights, no police officers at intersections, no judges in courtrooms, and no government laws of any kind. What would happen? Within thirty minutes, reckless drivers would cause colossal gridlock, armed robbers would invade banks and markets without fear, and the strong would brutally oppress the weak. Life would degenerate into terrifying chaos! Have you ever wondered why civil governments exist, and what God expects of Christians living under secular rulers, presidents, and governors? In Romans 13 and 1 Peter 2, the Apostles Paul and Peter wrote to Christians living under the mighty Roman Empire. They declared that civil authority is instituted by God to maintain public order, punish wrongdoers, and reward good citizenship. In this lesson, you will discover the biblical foundations of government, learn why paying taxes and following laws is a Christian duty, and see how youth can become shining model citizens of Nigeria!

## Key Terms
- Term: Civil Authority
- Definition: The lawful government officials, agencies, courts, and institutions established to maintain public order, administer justice, and protect citizens within a sovereign state.
- Term: Rule of Law
- Definition: The fundamental constitutional principle that all persons, institutions, and leaders are accountable to laws that are publicly promulgated and equally enforced.
- Term: Romans 13:1
- Definition: The apostolic command: *"Let every person be subject to the governing authorities. For there is no authority except from God, and those that exist have been instituted by God."*
- Term: Model Citizen
- Definition: A resident who actively obeys the law, pays taxes faithfully, protects public property, respects fellow citizens, and contributes positively to national progress.
- Term: Taxes / Tariffs / Revenue
- Definition: Compulsory financial contributions levied by the state on citizens' income, goods, and property to fund public services (roads, schools, security, healthcare).
- Term: Civic Duty
- Definition: The moral, patriotic, and legal obligations required of citizens, including voting, obeying traffic rules, reporting crimes, and respecting national symbols.
- Term: Conscientious Submission
- Definition: Obeying government laws not merely out of dread of physical punishment, but for the sake of a clear conscience before God (Romans 13:5).
- Term: Acts 5:29
- Definition: The supreme higher allegiance principle: *"We must obey God rather than men"* when civil commands directly force a believer to commit sin or violate God's law.

### The Core Teachings of Romans 13:1–7
The Apostle Paul outlines four fundamental truths regarding civil government:
1. **Divine Ordination of Authority:** *"Let every person be subject to the governing authorities. For there is no authority except from God, and those that exist have been instituted by God. Therefore whoever resists the authorities resists what God has appointed, and those who resist will incur judgment"* (Romans 13:1–2).
2. **The Purpose of Rulers:** Rulers are not a terror to good conduct, but to bad. The magistrate is *"God's servant for your good. But if you do wrong, be afraid, for he does not bear the sword in vain. For he is the servant of God, an avenger who carries out God's wrath on the wrongdoer"* (Romans 13:4).
3. **Submission for Conscience' Sake:** Christians obey the law not merely to escape police arrest or court fines, but out of personal moral reverence for God: *"Therefore one must be in subjection, not only to avoid God's wrath but also for the sake of conscience"* (Romans 13:5).
4. **Civic Financial Responsibility (Paying Taxes):** *"For because of this you also pay taxes, for the authorities are ministers of God, attending to this very thing. Pay to all what is owed to them: taxes to whom taxes are owed, revenue to whom revenue is owed, respect to whom respect is owed, honor to whom honor is owed"* (Romans 13:6–7).

### The St. Peter Directive on Good Citizenship (1 Peter 2:13–17)
The Apostle Peter reinforces Paul's message with practical civic clarity:
- *"Be subject for the Lord's sake to every human institution, whether it be to the emperor as supreme, or to governors as sent by him to punish those who do evil and to praise those who do good."*
- *"For this is the will of God, that by doing good you should put to silence the ignorance of foolish people."*
- Peter issues four timeless golden rules for Christian citizens:
  1. **Honor everyone:** Treat every human being with basic dignity and courtesy.
  2. **Love the brotherhood:** Maintain deep, affectionate fellowship with fellow believers.
  3. **Fear God:** Worship, revere, and place God's holy commandments above all else.
  4. **Honor the emperor / president:** Respect lawful political rulers and the offices they hold.

### Jesus' Teaching on Paying Taxes (Matthew 22:15–22)
When the Pharisees and Herodians tried to trap Jesus by asking, *"Is it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar, or not?"*, Jesus asked for a Roman coin (denarius) and asked:
- *"Whose likeness and inscription is this?"* They answered, *"Caesar's."*
- Jesus delivered His profound verdict: **"Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's"** (Matthew 22:21).
  - The coin bore Caesar's image, so it belonged to the civil state.
  - Human beings bear God's image (*Imago Dei*), so our ultimate worship and souls belong to God.

### The Boundary: When Civil Laws Conflict with God's Law (Acts 5:29)
Does biblical submission mean obeying evil commands? **No!**
- When the Sanhedrin strictly charged the Apostles to stop preaching in the name of Jesus, Peter and the apostles answered boldly: **"We must obey God rather than men"** (Acts 5:29).
- Examples of righteous civil disobedience in scripture:
  - The Hebrew Midwives (Exodus 1) refused Pharaoh's order to kill newborn male babies.
  - Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego (Daniel 3) refused to bow down to King Nebuchadnezzar's golden image.
  - Daniel (Daniel 6) continued praying three times a day despite King Darius' decree.
- **The Standard:** Christians obey all civil laws faithfully, unless a law explicitly commands them to sin or forbids them from worshipping God. Even then, believers accept the consequences with peaceful courage, never resorting to violent terrorism or armed rebellion.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing Christians should be anarchists who ignore the government because "our citizenship is in heaven." Philippians 3:20 confirms our heavenly citizenship, but Romans 13 commands us to be exemplary earthly citizens.
- **Mistake 2:** Thinking paying taxes is optional if the government has corrupt officials. When Paul wrote Romans 13, the pagan emperor Nero was ruling Rome; Paul still commanded Christians to pay taxes.
- **Mistake 3:** Believing Christians must obey every single government command, even if it orders you to commit murder or worship idols. Acts 5:29 establishes the higher authority: *"We must obey God rather than men."*
- **Mistake 4:** Equating civil disobedience with violent rioting. Biblical figures who practiced civil disobedience (Daniel, Peter) acted peacefully, endured arrest without violence, and trusted God.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Applying Matthew 22:21 to Modern Nigerian Taxation
*Question:* A Christian merchant in Onitsha or Lagos argues: *"The government officials mismanage road funds, so I will forge my financial books to pay zero taxes."* How does Jesus' teaching in Matthew 22:21 correct this businessman?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify Jesus' explicit command:** *"Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's."*
2. **Examine the historical context:** Rome was an imperial occupying power that spent tax revenue on pagan temples and military conquests; yet Jesus commanded people to pay their lawful taxes.
3. **Analyze Romans 13:6-7:** Christians pay taxes for conscience' sake before God, not based on whether politicians are perfect.
4. **Action:** The businessman must pay honest taxes, keep transparent accounting books, and advocate for good governance through lawful civic channels.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Boundary of Civil Obedience in Acts 5:29
*Question:* A school principal commands a Christian student to lie to an inspecting government education panel, claiming the school has thirty functional computers when it only has five. How should the student handle this situation?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the command:** The principal is commanding the student to speak a deliberate falsehood (bear false witness).
2. **Check God's supreme law:** The Ninth Commandment (Exodus 20:16) states: *"You shall not bear false witness."* God's law explicitly forbids lying.
3. **Apply Acts 5:29:** *"We must obey God rather than men."* Human authority has limits; when an authority commands sin, God's law takes absolute precedence.
4. **Action:** The student must respectfully decline to lie, speaking only the truth calmly and accepting whatever academic pressure may follow.
