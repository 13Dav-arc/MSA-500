## Hook
Imagine a vibrant classroom in a secondary school in Abuja, Jos, or Ibadan. In the first row sits Emeka, a devoted Christian who plays the keyboard in his church youth band. Right beside him sits Amina, a devout Muslim girl wearing a neat white hijab, who is the top student in Basic Technology. Across the aisle sits Babatunde, whose grandfather is a revered custodian of African traditional customs. When break time arrives, Emeka shares his lunch with Amina, Babatunde helps Emeka solve a difficult algebra problem, and all three laugh together on the playground. That beautiful, peaceful reality is what makes Nigeria extraordinary! Nigeria is a rich multi-religious nation where **Christianity**, **Islam**, and **African Traditional Religion (ATR)** live side by side. But what happens when religious intolerance, bigotry, and prejudice arise? Communities burn, lives are lost, and national progress halts. Jesus gave us the supreme standard for human society in the Golden Rule: *"Whatever you wish that others would do to you, do also to them."* In this lesson, you will discover the biblical foundation of religious tolerance, explore the common moral grounds we share, and conquer your Sessional 80% Mastery Gate!

## Key Terms
- Term: Religious Tolerance
- Definition: The capacity and willingness to permit, respect, and coexist peacefully with people whose religious beliefs, worship practices, and doctrines differ from one's own.
- Term: Peaceful Coexistence
- Definition: Living together in mutual safety, social harmony, and constructive civic cooperation without violent conflict, religious discrimination, or hatred.
- Term: Pluralistic Society
- Definition: A diverse nation or community (such as Nigeria) encompassing multiple ethnic groups, languages, cultures, and religious faiths coexisting under a shared constitutional framework.
- Term: The Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12)
- Definition: Jesus' timeless ethical principle: *"So whatever you wish that others would do to you, do also to them, for this is the Law and the Prophets."*
- Term: Common Moral Grounds
- Definition: Shared ethical virtues recognized across Christianity, Islam, and Traditional Religion, including the sanctity of human life, honesty, charity to the poor, respect for elders, and justice.
- Term: Religious Bigotry / Fanaticism
- Definition: Narrow-minded, hostile intolerance and blind prejudice toward persons of other faiths, often inciting hatred and violence.
- Term: The Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37)
- Definition: Jesus' masterclass parable demonstrating that true love crosses ethnic, religious, and sectarian boundaries to rescue a hurting neighbor in need.
- Term: Sessional 80% Mastery Gate
- Definition: The rigorous annual benchmark requiring students to demonstrate at least 80% comprehensive mastery across all three terms before promotion to JSS 2.

### The Multi-Religious Landscape of Nigeria

Nigeria is blessed with rich cultural and religious diversity. The three recognized religious traditions in our society are:
1. **Christianity:** Centered on faith in the Triune God, salvation through Jesus Christ, the Holy Bible, and active love for neighbor.
2. **Islam:** Centered on faith in Allah (the One God), the prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), the Holy Qur'an, prayer, and charity (*Zakat*).
3. **African Traditional Religion (ATR):** The indigenous ancestral heritage centered on the Supreme God (Olodumare, Chukwu, Ubangiji), veneration of ancestors, moral taboos, and respect for nature.

### Three Common Elements Across Religions in Nigeria
As highlighted in official NERDC curriculum studies, religious tolerance is grounded in recognizing three fundamental shared convictions:
1. **Belief in the Supreme Being:** All three religions acknowledge one Supreme Creator who made the heavens and the earth and sustains human existence.
2. **The Reality of Moral Breakdown:** All three recognize that human beings often fall into sin, disobedience, selfishness, and moral failure, damaging their relationship with God and fellow humans.
3. **The Necessity of Reconciliation and Moral Restoration:** All three emphasize that broken relationships between humanity and God must be restored through prayer, repentance, righteous conduct, and peaceful living.

### Biblical Principles for Living with People of Other Faiths
The New Testament provides clear, non-negotiable guidelines for Christian engagement in a diverse society:
- **1. The Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12):**
  - *"So whatever you wish that others would do to you, do also to them, for this is the Law and the Prophets."*
  - If you wish Muslims or traditional believers to respect your church services and Christian holidays, you must respect their mosques, prayer times, and cultural rights.
- **2. The Parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37):**
  - When a Jewish man was beaten by armed robbers on the Jericho road, the priest and the Levite (his own religious leaders) walked past him on the other side.
  - A **Samaritan**—a man from a despised, rival ethnic and religious group—stopped, felt deep compassion, bandaged his wounds with oil and wine, set him on his own beast, brought him to an inn, and paid all expenses.
  - Jesus asked: *"Which of these three, do you think, proved to be a neighbor to the man who fell among the robbers?"* The lawyer replied: *"The one who showed him mercy."* And Jesus said: **"You go, and do likewise."**
- **3. Pursuing Universal Peace (Romans 12:18; Hebrews 12:14):**
  - *"If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all"* (Romans 12:18).
  - *"Strive for peace with everyone, and for the holiness without which no one will see the Lord"* (Hebrews 12:14).
- **4. Gentle, Respectful Witness (1 Peter 3:15):**
  - *"Always be prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect."*
  - Christians share their faith through godly character, loving service, and respectful dialogue—never through insults, violence, or coercion.

### Practical Steps to Promote Religious Peace in Schools and Communities
1. **Never Mock or Insult Other Faiths:** Avoid insulting the sacred texts, prayers, or clothing of other religions.
2. **Collaborate on Common Community Projects:** Work together in school study clubs, neighborhood environmental sanitation, and sports teams regardless of faith.
3. **Stand Together Against Social Injustice:** Christians and Muslims in Nigeria can jointly oppose drug abuse, cultism, exam malpractice, corruption, and kidnapping.
4. **Practice Hospitality During Festivals:** Share meals and send goodwill greetings during Christmas, Easter, Eid al-Fitr, and Eid al-Kabir.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Thinking religious tolerance means compromising your personal Christian beliefs. Tolerance does not mean adopting Islamic or traditional rituals; it means respecting the human rights and freedom of others to practice their faith without violence or hatred.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming Christians should hate people who do not go to church. Jesus commanded us in Matthew 5:44 to love our enemies and pray for those who persecute us.
- **Mistake 3:** Believing the Samaritan in Luke 10 was an orthodox Jew. The Samaritans were a rival, despised religious sect; Jesus deliberately used him to show that love and mercy transcend religious and ethnic divides.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking religious violence can defend God. God is the sovereign Creator who does not need human weapons or arson to defend His glory; Christians are called to be peacemakers (Matthew 5:9).

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Applying the Good Samaritan Principle in a Nigerian School
*Question:* During a school inter-house sports competition, an athlete named Farouk (a Muslim student) collapses on the running track with a sprained ankle. Emeka, a Christian student from a rival house, sees Farouk in agony while other runners sprint past. How should Luke 10:33–37 guide Emeka?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Identify the situation:** Farouk is in physical distress and cannot walk.
2. **Identify the barrier:** They belong to different religions and competing sports houses.
3. **Apply the Samaritan example (Luke 10:33-34):** The Samaritan saw a wounded man across religious lines and had compassion, bandaging his wounds.
4. **Action:** Emeka immediately stops, helps Farouk up, supports him to the Red Cross first-aid tent, and waits until the nurse attends to him. Emeka proves that Christian love knows no religious barriers.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Golden Rule in Neighbor Relations
*Question:* A family living in an apartment compound plays loud gospel music at 1:00 AM, waking up their Muslim neighbor whose child has an exam the next morning. How does Matthew 7:12 evaluate this action?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Analyze the Golden Rule:** *"Whatever you wish that others would do to you, do also to them."*
2. **Reverse the situation:** Would the Christian family appreciate a neighbor playing loud music at 1:00 AM while they are trying to sleep or pray? Absolutely not.
3. **Evaluate the conduct:** Blasting loud music late at night—even religious music—violates the Golden Rule and disturbs community peace.
4. **Action:** The Christian family should lower their volume, use headphones, and respect their neighbor's right to peaceful sleep.
