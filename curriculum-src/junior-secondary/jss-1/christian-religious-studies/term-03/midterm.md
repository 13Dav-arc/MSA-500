## Hook
Congratulations on reaching the Midterm Continuous Assessment milestone of your final term in JSS 1 Christian Religious Studies! Over the past four weeks, you have examined the human heart in its two most immediate environments: the **family home** and the **school community**. You explored God's divine blueprints for peaceful homes in Ephesians 6, Colossians 3, and 1 Peter 3, discovering the sacred balance of loving parental instruction, filial obedience, and mutual honor between husbands and wives. You uncovered the priceless treasure of a good family name in Proverbs 22:1 and listened to King Solomon's urgent charge to remember your Creator in the days of your youth (Ecclesiastes 12:1). In the school environment, you walked beside young David and Prince Jonathan, watching a royal heir risk his father's flying spear to protect his covenant brother, and marveled at the bold persistence of the Friend at Midnight in Luke 11. Now, it is time to synthesize this wisdom, demonstrate your mastery, and shine in your Midterm Continuous Assessment!

## Key Terms
- Term: Nuclear Family
- Definition: A domestic household consisting of a father, mother, and their biological or adopted children.
- Term: Extended Family
- Definition: A broader multi-generational kinship network encompassing grandparents, uncles, aunts, cousins, and in-laws.
- Term: Filial Obedience
- Definition: The moral and scriptural duty of children to honor, respect, and obey their parents in the Lord (Ephesians 6:1–3).
- Term: Proverbs 22:1
- Definition: The timeless wisdom declaring that a good name is to be chosen rather than great riches, and favor is better than silver and gold.
- Term: Ecclesiastes 12:1
- Definition: The divine command urging youth: *"Remember also your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come."*
- Term: In Loco Parentis
- Definition: The legal and pedagogical doctrine where teachers and school authorities act in the place of parents to instruct, guide, and protect students.
- Term: Covenant Friendship
- Definition: A solemn, inviolable bond of mutual loyalty, selfless love, and protection entered into before God, modeled by David and Jonathan.
- Term: Stone Ezel
- Definition: The secret field location where Jonathan used a three-arrow code to warn David of King Saul's deadly intentions.
- Term: Parable of the Friend at Midnight
- Definition: Jesus' teaching in Luke 11:5–13 showing that bold, shameless persistence in asking brings generous provision from God.
- Term: Proverbs 1:10
- Definition: The cardinal moral command for youth: *"My son, if sinners entice you, do not consent."*

1. **Biblical Family Structures and Roles (Weeks 01–02):**
   - Children must honor and obey parents in the Lord; this is the first commandment with an attached promise (long life and prosperity).
   - Fathers must provide for household needs (1 Timothy 5:8) and train children in God's instruction without provoking them to bitterness.
   - Husbands must love their wives sacrificially as Christ loved the Church; wives must respect and submit to their husbands in the Lord.
   - The extended family acts as an anchor of oral wisdom, cultural heritage, and communal solidarity.

2. **The Sanctity of a Good Family Name (Week 02):**
   - Character and moral reputation far outweigh silver, gold, and material riches (Proverbs 22:1).
   - Serving God in youth (Ecclesiastes 12:1) preserves a young person from destructive habits, addictions, and early regrets.
   - Children protect their family name through academic honesty, polite speech, and resisting cybercrime (*yahoo-yahoo*), cultism, and truancy.

3. **Friendship in the Academic Community (Weeks 03–04):**
   - The school functions *in loco parentis*; teachers provide mentorship, and fellow students collaborate as academic brothers and sisters.
   - Choosing friends based on moral character, diligence, and fear of God elevates a student's future ("Iron sharpens iron"; Proverbs 27:17).
   - Prince Jonathan models selfless covenant love: he stripped his royal robe for David, defended him before Saul, endured a spear, and signaled him at the Stone Ezel.
   - The Parable of the Friend at Midnight teaches bold persistence in prayer and hospitable love.
   - Proverbs 1:10 gives a direct mandate: when sinners entice you with false brotherhood and quick plunder, do not consent!

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing children have no impact on the family's public reputation. A student's misconduct in school or on the street directly shames their parents and damages the family name.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming Jonathan was disloyal to Israel by helping David. Jonathan defended an innocent servant of God against an unlawful, murderous tyrant, upholding divine righteousness.
- **Mistake 3:** Thinking the neighbor in Luke 11 gave bread because he enjoyed being woken up. He gave bread because the man's bold persistence would not let him go back to sleep.
- **Mistake 4:** Believing you can belong to a bad peer group without being influenced. Proverbs 13:20 and 1 Corinthians 15:33 warn that bad company inevitably corrupts good morals.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Integrating Ephesians 6 and Proverbs 22:1 in School Life
*Question:* A student named Ifeoma is asked by classmates to join an unauthorized Friday evening party where alcohol and smoking will be present. Her parents have explicitly told her to return home immediately after school. How do Ephesians 6:1 and Proverbs 22:1 direct her action?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Analyze Ephesians 6:1:** *"Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right."* Sneaking to an unauthorized party violates direct parental authority and divine commandment.
2. **Analyze Proverbs 22:1:** *"A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches."* Attending the party risks moral harm, police raids, and public disgrace that will ruin her reputation and family name.
3. **Synthesize the response:** Ifeoma firmly declines the invitation, walks home immediately after dismissal, and shares her day with her parents, preserving her integrity and family honor.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Jonathan Model of Leadership
*Question:* What makes Prince Jonathan's relationship with David in 1 Samuel 18–20 a masterclass in selfless leadership?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Consider his natural right:** By royal blood and succession laws, Jonathan was next in line for the crown of Israel.
2. **Consider his spiritual discernment:** Jonathan recognized that God's sovereign spirit was on David to lead the nation.
3. **Examine his sacrificial actions:** He gave David his own royal armor, defended David at the risk of his life, and wept with him in covenant blessing.
4. **Conclusion for students:** True greatness does not cling selfishly to titles or envy gifted peers; it rejoices in others' gifts and serves God's bigger purposes.
