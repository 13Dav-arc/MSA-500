## Hook
Picture this real-life scenario: You are walking home from school along an asphalt road. A sleek, tinted-glass saloon car slows down beside you, matching your exact walking pace. The electric window rolls down smoothly, and a smiling, well-dressed adult leans out, waving a crispy thousand-naira note and a box of chocolates. *"Hello young scholar! Your uncle asked me to pick you up quickly because there is an emergency at home. Hop into the back seat!"* At that precise split-second, your heart starts pounding rapidly, your stomach feels strangely cold, and every instinct in your body screams that something is terribly wrong! What do you do? Do you smile politely and climb into the air-conditioned car, or do you step back, run towards a crowded fuel station, and scream for help? The ability to recognize these deadly traps in seconds is the difference between freedom and tragedy! Welcome to **Avoiding Suspicious Situations and the 80% Term 1 Mastery Gate**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Suspicious Movement
- Definition: Any unusual, erratic, or stealthy physical action, loitering, or observation that deviates noticeably from normal social behavior and signals criminal intent.
- Term: Red Flag
- Definition: A clear warning indicator or behavioral cue that alerts an individual that a person, environment, or interaction carries high danger or deception.
- Term: Stranger Danger
- Definition: A vital security principle that teaches young people to exercise caution, maintain physical distance, and refuse unsolicited gifts, rides, or private invitations from unfamiliar adults.
- Term: Tricky Person Concept
- Definition: An advanced child safety rule stating that dangerous adults do not always look scary or mean; they often disguise evil intentions behind polite smiles, gifts, or requests for help.
- Term: Intuition (Gut Feeling)
- Definition: The brain's subconscious early-warning system that processes hidden environmental threats and triggers physiological alarm bells (such as a racing heartbeat or stomach knots).
- Term: Safe Haven
- Definition: A well-populated, secure public place (such as a police station, commercial bank, busy fuel station, or supermarket) where an individual can seek immediate sanctuary from pursuers.
- Term: Loitering
- Definition: Standing, lingering, or waiting around a facility, gate, or school compound for an extended period without any legitimate business or clear lawful purpose.
- Term: "No, Go, Yell, Tell"
- Definition: A gold-standard four-step emergency self-protection protocol: Say NO loudly, GO away immediately, YELL for help, and TELL a trusted adult immediately.
- Term: Secret Code Word
- Definition: A confidential, pre-arranged password shared strictly between parents and children to verify whether an unfamiliar messenger was truly sent by family.
- Term: 80% Mastery Gate
- Definition: The formal academic standard requiring students to demonstrate at least 80% comprehension of Term 1 security topics before sitting for the Terminal Examination.

### The "Tricky Person" Rule vs The "Monster" Myth
Children often imagine that kidnappers look like frightening, ugly movie villains with eye patches and scars. In reality:
- Predatory adults usually look clean, well-spoken, respectable, and polite.
- **The Universal Adult Rule:** Normal, responsible adults **never ask a strange child for help** with adult tasks (e.g., asking a 10-year-old to help find lost puppies, carry boxes into a house, or guide them through lonely alleys). Responsible adults ask other adults for help!
- If an unfamiliar adult asks you for help, that is an immediate **Red Flag**! Step away immediately.

### The "No, Go, Yell, Tell" Protocol
When confronted with an aggressive, predatory, or suspicious situation, execute these four actions in rapid sequence:
1. **NO:** Shout a firm, loud, and confident "NO!" Do not apologize or hesitate.
2. **GO:** Turn and run immediately in the opposite direction towards safety. (Vehicles cannot reverse as fast as a person can run back around a corner).
3. **YELL:** Scream loudly: *"HELP! THIS IS NOT MY PARENT! HE IS TRYING TO TAKE ME!"* Screaming specific words alerts the public that a crime is occurring, rather than ordinary childish tantrums.
4. **TELL:** Run directly into a **Safe Haven** and report the incident immediately to an adult in uniform (police officer, security guard, teacher, or bank teller).

### Identifying Safe Havens Along Daily Routes
Students should map out reliable Safe Havens between home and school:
- Commercial bank premises guarded by security personnel.
- Busy petrol filling stations with staff and bright lighting.
- Registered supermarkets, pharmacy stores, and government offices.
- Active police stations or civil defence posts.
- **Never seek shelter in:** Uncompleted buildings, abandoned parked vehicles, dense thickets, or dark dead-end alleys!

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Ignoring Your "Gut Feeling" to Avoid Being Rude
Many well-mannered students feel something is deeply wrong with an adult's behavior, but they stay and listen because they fear being scolded as "disrespectful."
> **Correction:** Your personal safety and life are infinitely more important than politeness! If an adult makes you feel uncomfortable or unsafe, ignore politeness: step back, run away, and seek help immediately.

### Mistake 2: Running into Lonely, Hidden Alleys When Followed
Believing that ducking into a deserted shortcut or uncompleted building will help you shake off a pursuer.
> **Correction:** Running into an isolated alley corners you without witnesses or escape routes! Always run towards bright lights, busy main roads, crowds, and commercial shops.

### Mistake 3: Believing Kidnappers Only Target Children in the Dark
Assuming that daylight and sunny afternoons are automatically safe from criminal abduction.
> **Correction:** Kidnappings and abductions frequently occur in broad daylight between 2:00 PM and 5:00 PM when schools close and roads are crowded with pedestrians. Maintain high alertness at all hours.

### Mistake 4: Believing Anyone Who Knows Your Name is a Family Friend
Assuming that if a stranger calls you by your first name, they must have been sent by your mother.
> **Correction:** Predators often read embroidered names on backpacks, lunch boxes, or social media posts! Never trust someone just because they call your name. Require the pre-arranged **Family Secret Code Word**.

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: The Vehicle Approach
**Situation:** While walking home on a Friday afternoon, 11-year-old Musa notices a blue sedan driving very slowly behind him for over two blocks. As Musa approaches a lonely stretch of road, the car accelerates, pulls across the curb in front of him, and the passenger door pops open.
- **Wrong Response:** Musa walks closer to the open door to look inside or tries to reason with the passenger.
- **Right Response:** 
  1. Musa recognizes the immediate danger signal: a vehicle blocking his path with an open door.
  2. Musa does not run forward where the car can follow. He immediately does an about-face (180-degree turn) and sprints back towards the busy market junction he just passed.
  3. The car cannot easily reverse across oncoming traffic lanes. Musa reaches the security guard at a commercial bank and reports: *"A blue car tried to block me down the road!"*

### Scenario 2: The Lost Puppy / Assistance Trick
**Situation:** While playing near her estate playground, a friendly, well-dressed lady approaches Ifeoma with tears in her eyes: *"My little white puppy ran into that abandoned warehouse behind the fence! Can you please crawl through that hole and help me hold him? I am too big to fit through!"*
- **Wrong Response:** Ifeoma feels sorry for the lady and crawls through the hole into the dark, abandoned structure.
- **Right Response:**
  1. Ifeoma remembers the "Tricky Person" rule: **Adults never ask children for help with difficult or dangerous tasks**.
  2. Ifeoma steps back five paces and says firmly: *"No! Go and ask the estate security men at the gate to help you!"*
  3. Ifeoma immediately jogs back to her home and tells her father what happened.
