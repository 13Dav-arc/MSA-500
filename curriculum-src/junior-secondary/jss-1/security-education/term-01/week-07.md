## Hook
Have you ever wondered why banks build thick reinforced concrete walls, install bullet-resistant double-door security mantraps, and station armed guards at their entrances, while an abandoned kiosk on a lonely street gets broken into every single weekend? Or why a house with bright solar perimeter lights, sharp security wire, and a sturdy dog is bypassed by midnight prowlers in favor of an unlit bungalow with an open wooden gate? The secret lies in a famous law of crime: **criminals are rational opportunists who look for the easiest, fastest, and lowest-risk targets**! If committing a crime requires too much effort, takes too much time, and carries a high risk of getting caught, the criminal will walk away. This powerful science of stopping crime *before* it happens is known as **Crime Prevention and Target Hardening**! How can you turn your home, your route to school, and your daily habits into an unbreakable shield? Let us discover the rules of self-protection!

## Key Terms
- Term: Crime Prevention
- Definition: Any proactive strategy, policy, or measure implemented to eliminate, deter, or reduce the opportunities, causes, and risks of criminal behavior before a crime occurs.
- Term: Target Hardening
- Definition: The practical security technique of physically strengthening a building, property, or person to make unlawful entry, theft, or attack substantially more difficult and risky.
- Term: Situational Crime Prevention
- Definition: A crime-reduction approach that focuses on altering the immediate physical environment to increase the effort and risks of committing a crime while reducing its anticipated rewards.
- Term: Natural Surveillance
- Definition: Designing physical spaces, trimming overgrown hedges, and placing lighting so that residents and passersby can easily see and observe all entry points and activities clearly.
- Term: Access Control
- Definition: Physical or technological barriers (such as deadbolts, security gates, biometric scanners, and identity badges) that restrict entry strictly to authorized individuals.
- Term: Perimeter Security
- Definition: The outermost protective ring of a property (including boundary fences, walls, anti-climb spikes, security gates, and perimeter floodlights).
- Term: Deadbolt Lock
- Definition: A heavy-duty, bolt-activated door lock that cannot be pushed back or retracted by a knife blade or plastic card, providing superior resistance against forced entry.
- Term: Defensible Space
- Definition: A residential design concept where physical environments are arranged to create clear ownership and territorial boundaries that deter trespassers and intruders.
- Term: Neighborhood Watch
- Definition: An organized community security initiative where residents actively collaborate, monitor suspicious movements, and alert law enforcement to protect their neighborhood.
- Term: Situational Awareness
- Definition: The conscious, continuous mental habit of remaining alert, paying attention to your surrounding environment, and identifying potential hazards or threats early.

### The Concept of Target Hardening
Criminals operate under the **Rational Choice Theory**: they weigh the *effort* required, the *risk* of detection, and the *reward* gained. Target hardening alters this balance in favor of the victim:

1. **Increasing the Effort:** Installing heavy iron burglary proofing on windows, five-lever mortise deadbolts on exterior doors, and concrete boundary walls topped with concertina razor wire.
2. **Increasing the Risks:** Illuminating dark yards with motion-activated LED security lights, trimming thick shrubs that conceal prowlers, installing CCTV cameras, and owning trained security dogs.
3. **Reducing the Rewards:** Not leaving cash, expensive jewelry, laptops, or car keys in plain view through window blinds; using hidden, bolted steel wall-safes.
4. **Removing Excuses:** Posting clear "Private Property — Trespassers Will Be Prosecuted" warning signs and keeping perimeter access strictly gated.

### Personal Security Precautions for Students
No security device can replace personal vigilance. Students must practice basic personal protection rules every day:
- **Variable Travel Routines:** Avoid walking the exact same isolated shortcut every day. Walk on well-lit, busy streets with other students whenever possible.
- **Eliminate Walking Distractions:** Never walk along busy streets wearing noise-canceling headphones or staring down at a smartphone screen, which blinds you to oncoming vehicles and snatch thieves.
- **Guarding Personal Information:** Never reveal your family's travel plans, daily routine, or financial details to strangers or on social media platforms.
- **The "No Stranger Pick-Up" Rule:** Never accept rides, gifts, drinks, or money from unfamiliar persons, even if they claim that your parents sent them to pick you up. Always confirm with school authorities first.

### Community Vigilance and Neighborhood Watch
In many Nigerian residential communities and estates, residents form Landlord and Tenant Associations (CDAs — Community Development Associations) that operate Neighborhood Watch teams. These associations:
- Restrict estate access gates after 10:00 PM to registered residents and identified visitors.
- Screen incoming commercial tricycle (Keke) and motorcycle (Okada) riders.
- Fund community security guards (vigilante personnel registered with the Police or Civil Defence).
- Maintain rapid communication channels (such as community broadcast groups) to alert neighbors during security emergencies.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Relying Exclusively on Spring-Latch Locks
Many residents believe locking the round doorknob (spring latch) is sufficient to secure an exterior entrance door. 
> **Correction:** A standard spring-latch knob can be slipped open in seconds using a sturdy plastic card or pocket knife ("loiding"). Exterior doors must always feature a deadbolt lock that extends at least one inch into a heavy steel strike plate anchored into the door frame.

### Mistake 2: Hiding House Keys in Obvious Outdoor Spots
Leaving a spare key under a welcome doormat, on top of the door frame, or inside a decorative flowerpot next to the front door.
> **Correction:** Burglars know every common hiding place and check them first! Never leave keys outside your house. Leave emergency duplicate keys with a trusted neighbor, family member, or locked inside a digital key safe.

### Mistake 3: Neglecting Natural Surveillance Around Windows
Allowing overgrown trees, dense hibiscus hedges, or high flower bushes to block the view of ground-floor windows and entryways from the main street.
> **Correction:** Criminals love overgrown foliage because it provides total concealment while they work on prying open window grilles. Hedges near windows should be trimmed below two feet, and tree branches pruned above six feet.

### Mistake 4: Believing Crime Prevention is Exclusively the Police's Duty
Assuming that because police officers exist, citizens do not need to lock gates, secure valuables, or report suspicious movements.
> **Correction:** The police cannot be everywhere at every second. Crime prevention is a shared civic responsibility: citizens provide target hardening and intelligence, while law enforcement provides emergency response and legal prosecution.

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: The Vulnerable Home Audit
**Situation:** Mr. Emeka’s family lives in a rented apartment on a quiet street. Over the past three months, two houses on the street experienced burglary. Mr. Emeka asks his JSS 1 child to identify three major security vulnerabilities around their home and propose solutions.
- **Observation A:** The light bulb on the front porch has been burned out for four weeks, leaving the front door in pitch darkness.
- **Observation B:** The front wooden door has only a small spring lock, and the strike plate screws are short (half-inch) nails driven into soft wood.
- **Observation C:** A portable aluminum ladder is left lying on the grass beside the compound wall.
- **Step-by-Step Security Correction:**
  1. *Fixing Visibility:* Replace the burned-out bulb immediately with a solar-powered LED floodlight with a motion sensor to eliminate dark concealment zones.
  2. *Hardening the Door:* Install a five-lever deadbolt mortise lock and reinforce the strike plate with 3-inch steel screws that anchor directly into the wall framing.
  3. *Eliminating Intruder Tools:* Move the aluminum ladder into a locked storage shed or chain and padlock it to a permanent pillar so intruders cannot use it to climb into upper balconies.

### Scenario 2: Student Route Safety Planning
**Situation:** Amina closes from school at 3:30 PM. She has two options to reach home: Option 1 is a quiet, deserted footpath through an uncompleted building site that takes 10 minutes. Option 2 is a bustling commercial street with market stalls and heavy pedestrian traffic that takes 20 minutes.
- **Evaluation:** Option 1 offers total privacy to criminals; if someone ambushes Amina, there are no witnesses, no lighting, and nowhere to run for help. Option 2 provides maximum **Natural Surveillance**—shopkeepers, drivers, and fellow pedestrians can see and intervene if danger arises.
- **Safe Decision:** Amina must choose Option 2. Personal safety and survival always take priority over saving ten minutes of walking time.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Security Checklist:** Walk around your compound with your child this weekend. Test all exterior locks, inspect window burglary bars for rust or loose mountings, and check that compound gates latch securely.
- **Family Emergency Drill:** Ensure your child memorizes the National Emergency Toll-Free Number (**112**) and at least two phone numbers of immediate family members.
- **Key Safety Rule:** Teach children never to carry house keys visibly displayed on backpacks with address tags attached, as losing the bag exposes the family home to immediate burglary risk.
