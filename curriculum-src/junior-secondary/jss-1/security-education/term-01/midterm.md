## Hook

You learned why security is the primary purpose of government under Section 14(2)(b) of our Constitution and explored its four dimensions—personal, home, community, and national. ## Key Terms
- Term: Security
- Definition: The state of being free from danger, threat, harm, fear, injury, or loss; measures taken to protect people, assets, and nations.
- Term: Situational Awareness
- Definition: The active mental habit of observing, understanding, and evaluating one's immediate surroundings to anticipate and avoid potential dangers.
- Term: Security Hazard
- Definition: Any biological, chemical, physical, or environmental condition with the potential to cause injury, death, or property destruction.
- Term: Carbon Monoxide (CO)
- Definition: The deadly, odorless, and colorless poisonous gas emitted by running generators, car exhausts, and fuel combustion.
- Term: Theft (Stealing)
- Definition: The unlawful taking of another person's property without their consent, with intent to permanently deprive the owner.
- Term: Burglary
- Definition: The serious criminal offense of breaking and entering into a dwelling house with intent to commit a felony, traditionally under cover of night.
- Term: Target Hardening
- Definition: Strengthening the physical defenses of a building (locks, bars, perimeter lighting) to deter and delay criminal entry.
- Term: Assault
- Definition: An intentional, unlawful act that causes another person to reasonably fear imminent violent physical contact without physical touch.
- Term: Battery
- Definition: The actual intentional, unlawful application of physical force or violent bodily contact upon another person without consent.
- Term: Grievous Bodily Harm (GBH)
- Definition: Severe bodily injury causing broken bones, permanent disfigurement, or loss of organ function, carrying heavy prison sentences.

### Core Review Summary
1. **Dimensions of Security:** Security spans personal alertness, home compound defenses, community neighborhood watches, and national sovereignty. Under Section 14(2)(b), citizen safety is the supreme duty of the state. Nigeria's emergency line is **112**.
2. **Neutralizing Deadly Hazards:**
   - *Generator Safety:* Operate generators strictly outdoors at least 20 feet (6 meters) away from windows to prevent fatal Carbon Monoxide asphyxiation.
   - *Gas Leaks:* Never strike matches or turn on light switches when smelling gas; ventilate the room and use the soapy water bubble test.
   - *Electrical Fires:* Never use water on live electrical equipment; use an ABC dry chemical extinguisher.
3. **Property Crimes and Target Hardening:**
   - Theft is non-violent taking; Burglary is breaking and entering at night; Housebreaking occurs by day; Armed Robbery uses deadly weapons.
   - Defend homes using heavy deadbolts, welded window bars, motion-sensor lighting, and social media discretion.
4. **Crimes of Violence:**
   - Assault is the threat of imminent violence causing fear (no physical contact required).
   - Battery is the actual unlawful physical strike or contact.
   - Aggravated assault and Grievous Bodily Harm (GBH) attract severe sentences (7 to 14 years) under the Criminal Code and the VAPP Act 2015.

## Comparative Matrix
| Crime / Hazard | Key Defining Element | Primary Preventative Measure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CO Poisoning** | Invisible, odorless gas emitted by generators | Operate generator outdoors at least 20 feet from windows |
| **Cooking Gas Leak** | Heavy hydrocarbon gas pooling near floors | Never flip switches or light matches; ventilate gently |
| **Residential Burglary**| Breaking into buildings at night to steal | Install solid deadbolts, welded bars, and motion lights |
| **Assault** | Threat causing reasonable fear of imminent harm | Maintain physical distance, escape, report to authorities |
| **Battery** | Actual unlawful physical blow or strike on body | Proportional self-defense; seek medical injury report |

## Common Mistakes
- **Assuming Carbon Monoxide Has a Warning Smell:** CO is completely odorless and tasteless; victims fall asleep and die painlessly without smelling anything.
- **Throwing Water on Grease or Electrical Fires:** Water conducts electricity (causing shock) and causes burning oil to explode in violent steam eruptions.
- **Believing Assault Requires Bruises or Bleeding:** Assault is the threat or attempt that causes terror; the moment bruises or physical contact occur, it becomes battery!

## Worked Examples
### Diagnostic Problem 1: Evaluating Domestic Hazards
- **Scenario:** A household in Warri places their generator on the covered balcony right outside the children's bedroom during a midnight downpour. By 3:00 AM, the parents feel dizzy and find the children unresponsive.
- *Diagnosis:* Severe Carbon Monoxide (CO) poisoning caused by exhaust fumes seeping through the bedroom louvers.
- *Immediate Emergency Action:* Evacuate everyone into fresh open air immediately, call emergency line **112**, perform rescue breathing, and transport victims to a hospital with emergency oxygen facilities.

### Diagnostic Problem 2: Categorizing a Street Altercation
- **Scenario:** A motorist in Lagos jumps out of his car during a traffic dispute, brandishes an iron tire lever, and screams at a pedestrian: "I will crack your skull open!" The pedestrian dodges, and the driver kicks him in the ribs, cracking two bones.
- *Phase 1 (Waving the iron lever and threatening death):* **Assault with an offensive weapon**.
- *Phase 2 (Kicking and cracking ribs):* **Battery causing Grievous Bodily Harm (GBH)** under Section 355 of the Criminal Code.
