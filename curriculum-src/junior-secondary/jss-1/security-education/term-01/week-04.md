## Hook
Imagine walking through a crowded school hallway or bus stop when an aggressive stranger suddenly charges toward you, screaming violent threats, waving a jagged broken bottle inches from your face, and yelling, "I will cut your throat right now!" Even if the bottle never touches a single millimeter of your skin, your heart hammers, cold terror grips your spine, and you jump back in pure fear. Did a crime just occur? Yes! In criminal law, that terrifying threat is called an **Assault**! And the very second that bottle, a flying fist, or a heavy slap actually makes physical contact with your body, the crime escalates into **Battery**! Why does the law strictly forbid not just physical violence, but even the threat of violence? Why are school bullies and domestic abusers facing long prison sentences in Nigerian courts today? Let us explore the law of personal safety!

## Key Terms
- Term: Assault
- Definition: An intentional, unlawful act that causes another person to reasonably apprehend or fear imminent harmful, offensive, or violent physical contact.
- Term: Battery
- Definition: The actual intentional, unlawful application of physical force or violent bodily contact upon another person without their consent.
- Term: Aggravated Assault
- Definition: An assault committed with the intent to commit a serious felony or executed using deadly weapons (such as knives, broken bottles, iron rods, or firearms), causing severe bodily harm.
- Term: Bullying
- Definition: Unwanted, aggressive, and repeated behavior among school-aged youths that involves a real or perceived power imbalance, causing physical injury or psychological torment.
- Term: Grievous Bodily Harm (GBH)
- Definition: Any serious, severe, or life-threatening bodily injury that causes permanent disfigurement, broken bones, loss of organ function, or long-term disability.
- Term: Domestic Violence
- Definition: Violent, aggressive, or abusive behavior occurring within the home, typically involving the physical battery or emotional abuse of a spouse, child, or domestic partner.
- Term: VAPP Act 2015
- Definition: The Violence Against Persons (Prohibition) Act, a landmark Nigerian law that strictly prohibits and severely penalizes all forms of physical violence, battery, and domestic abuse.
- Term: Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
- Definition: A severe mental and emotional condition triggered by experiencing or witnessing terrifying, violent events, characterized by nightmares, flashbacks, and severe anxiety.
- Term: Self-Defense
- Definition: The legal justification permitting an individual to use reasonable, proportional force to protect themselves or another person from imminent unlawful bodily harm.
- Term: Medical Evidence
- Definition: Formal clinical documentation, photographs, medical reports, and forensic records prepared by a licensed physician documenting physical injuries for court prosecution.

## Core Concept
### Distinguishing Assault from Battery
While people in everyday conversation say "assault and battery" as if it were a single event, in Nigerian criminal jurisprudence (Criminal Code and Penal Code), they are two distinct, sequential criminal offenses:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        ASSAULT VERSUS BATTERY: THE LEGAL DIFFERENCE                    │
├────┬─────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ S/N│ Criminal Offense    │ Legal Meaning & Action Required                             │
├────┼─────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1  │ **Assault**         │ The *threat or attempt* to inflict bodily harm that puts the│
│    │                     │ victim in reasonable fear. No physical contact is required! │
│    │                     │ *(e.g., Cocking a gun, raising an iron rod threateningly).* │
├────┼─────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2  │ **Battery**         │ The *actual physical impact or strike* on the victim's body │
│    │                     │ without consent. Physical contact is mandatory!             │
│    │                     │ *(e.g., Slapping, punching, kicking, stabbing, or beating).*│
├────┼─────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3  │ **Combined Offense**│ When an attacker threatens the victim (Assault) and then    │
│    │                     │ immediately strikes them (Battery).                         │
└────┴─────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Common Forms of Assault and Battery in Nigeria:
1. **School Bullying and Hazing:** Senior students extorting food or money from junior students, beating juniors during nocturnal dormitory hazing, or forcing students into dangerous manual labor.
2. **Domestic Violence and Child Battery:** Spousal abuse, domestic servants beaten with electrical cables or burning irons, and excessive, brutal corporal punishment.
3. **Street Brawls and Public Fighting:** Commercial bus drivers (Danfo drivers), conductors, and touts (Agberos) fighting with iron clubs, broken bottles, or charms in public motor parks.
4. **Assault with Offensive Weapons:** Attackers brandishing machetes, daggers, or broken bottles during street quarrels.
5. **Indecent or Sexual Assault:** Unwanted, non-consensual sexual touching, groping, or molestation.

---

### Severe Consequences of Assault and Battery:
- **Physical Consequences:**
  - Fractured jawbones, broken ribs, and shattered skulls.
  - Blindness caused by facial strikes or shattered glass bottles.
  - Permanent facial scarring, disfigurement, and amputation.
  - Fatal internal bleeding, organ rupture, or death.
- **Psychological Consequences:**
  - Chronic fear, paranoia, and persistent panic attacks.
  - Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) and severe clinical depression.
  - School dropout or academic failure caused by intense school phobia.
  - Low self-esteem and suicidal thoughts among bullied adolescents.

---

### Legal Penalties in Nigeria
Under the **Criminal Code (Sections 351 to 356)**, the **Penal Code**, and the **VAPP Act 2015**:
- **Common Assault:** Punishable by **one year imprisonment**.
- **Assault Occasioning Harm:** Assault resulting in physical bleeding, bruises, or cuts is a felony punishable by **three to five years imprisonment**.
- **Grievous Bodily Harm (GBH):** Assault causing permanent organ damage, broken bones, or severe disfigurement carries **seven to fourteen years imprisonment**!
- Under the **Child Rights Act 2003**, subjecting a child to physical torture or cruel punishment attracts mandatory jail terms.

---

### Immediate Reporting Procedures for Victims
If you or someone you know is assaulted or battered:
1. **Escape to Safety:** Immediately get away from the attacker into a crowded area, staff room, or public building.
2. **Seek Immediate Medical Attention:** Go to a hospital or clinic immediately. Treating injuries is paramount, and the doctor will issue a formal **Medical Injury Report** documenting the wounds.
3. **Report to Authorities:**
   - *In School:* Report immediately to the School Counselor, Disciplinary Committee, or Principal.
   - *In Society:* Report to the nearest Police Station (request the **Gender/Family Support Unit**) or contact the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC).
4. **Preserve Physical Evidence:** Do not wash torn or bloodstained clothing, take clear photographs of bruises, and record names of eyewitnesses.

## Comparative Matrix
| Dimension | Assault | Battery |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical Contact** | **Zero contact required;** strictly the threat/fear | **Mandatory physical contact;** strike or touch |
| **Core Element** | Reasonable mental apprehension of imminent harm | Actual unlawful application of physical force |
| **Classic Example** | Brandishing a broken bottle yelling, "I will stab you!"| Stabbing the victim with the bottle or slapping them |
| **Legal Classification**| Misdemeanor or felony depending on weapon used | Felony when resulting in bodily harm or wounds |
| **Psychological Harm** | Acute terror, shock, anxiety, and panic | Physical pain, injury, combined with trauma |

## Common Mistakes
- **"It Was Only a Joke / Play":** Claiming that slapping a classmate or twisting their arm was "just play." In law, applying physical force to someone without their clear consent is battery, regardless of whether you claim it was a joke!
- **Believing Assault Requires Physical Touch:** Many people think assault means being beaten up. Raising a clenched fist, pointing a dagger, or threatening to kill someone within arm's reach is already a full criminal assault!
- **Retaliating with Disproportionate Violence:** If someone pushes your shoulder, you do not have the legal right to smash a wooden chair over their head. Self-defense allows only **reasonable, proportional force** to protect yourself, not revenge or excessive brutality!

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Differentiating an Assault from a Battery
**Scenario:** During an argument on the school sports field, Chuka screams at Segun, picks up a heavy stone, and throws it directly at Segun's head:
- *Phase 1:* The moment Chuka raises the stone and throws it, creating terror in Segun: **Assault**.
- *Phase 2 (Outcome A):* Segun ducks swiftly, and the stone flies past his ear, hitting a wall: **Assault only** (no physical contact occurred).
- *Phase 2 (Outcome B):* The stone strikes Segun on the forehead, cutting his skin and drawing blood: **Assault and Battery Occasioning Harm**!

### Example 2: Legal Consequences of School Bullying
**Scenario:** A senior student, Jide, corners a JSS 1 junior student in the dormitory, demands his pocket money, and beats him with an iron belt buckle when the junior refuses, fracturing the junior's arm.
- **Offenses Committed:** Extortion, assault with an offensive weapon, and battery causing Grievous Bodily Harm (GBH).
- **Consequences:** Jide is not merely suspended; the victim's parents file a formal police report. Jide is arraigned before the Juvenile Court under the Child Rights Act, facing custodial remand in a correctional center, and pays restitution for medical bills.

## Guardian Sync
- **Open Communication on Bullying:** Ask your child directly: "Has anyone at school or in the neighborhood threatened you, called you cruel names, or hit you?" Create an open, trusting space where your child feels safe confiding in you.
- **Anger Management Coaching:** Teach your child how to manage frustration without resorting to violence. Emphasize that throwing punches or picking up weapons is a sign of weakness, not strength.
- **Reporting Protocol:** Make sure your child knows where to go if they feel unsafe at school—identifying trusted teachers, prefects, or school administrators.
