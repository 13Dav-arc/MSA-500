### Essential Takeaways for Midterm Mastery
1. **The Nigeria Police Force (Week 01):** Supervised by the Police Council and headed by the Inspector-General of Police (IGP). Operates through Zonal Commands (AIGs), State Commands (CPs), and local Divisions (DPOs). Under the Police Act 2020, suspects must be brought before court within 24–48 hours, torture is prohibited, and bail is strictly free.
2. **The Armed Forces of Nigeria (Week 02):** Governed by Section 217 of the Constitution; commanded by the President (Commander-in-Chief) and coordinated by the Chief of Defence Staff (CDS). Comprises the Nigerian Army (*"Victory is from God Alone"*), Nigerian Navy (*"Onward Together"*), and Nigerian Air Force (*"Willing... Able... Ready"*). Strictly subordinate to civilian authority.
3. **Paramilitary Agencies I (Week 03):** 
   - *NSCDC:* Protects critical national infrastructure (petroleum pipelines, electrical grids, railways), regulates Private Guard Companies, and operates the Agro-Rangers.
   - *FRSC:* Headed by the Corps Marshal; reduces road crashes, enforces the Highway Code and speed limiters, issues Driver's Licenses, and operates emergency toll-free line **122**.
4. **Paramilitary Agencies II (Week 04):**
   - *NIS:* Manages people and migration; produces Nigerian passports; monitors international airports, seaports, and land borders; issues CERPAC permits.
   - *NCS:* Manages goods and fiscal revenue; assesses import/export duties; intercepts smuggled contraband; protects local manufacturing from unfair dumping.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Confusing NPF and Paramilitary Mandates
Assuming all uniformed agencies have identical powers to investigate ordinary domestic theft or arrest pickpockets.
> **Correction:** The NPF is the universal civil police force. Paramilitary agencies have specialized statutory jurisdictions (e.g., FRSC handles roads; NSCDC handles infrastructure; Customs handles cargo).

### Mistake 2: Believing the Military Can Be Deployed Without Presidential Authority
Assuming a state governor can deploy the Nigerian Army to fight local crime independently.
> **Correction:** Under Section 218 of the Constitution, the power to determine the operational use of the Armed Forces belongs exclusively to the **President of Nigeria** as Commander-in-Chief.

### Mistake 3: Paying for Police Bail
Believing that an investigating police officer can legally demand money before releasing a detained relative.
> **Correction:** Bail is unconditionally **Free**. Demanding or paying money for bail constitutes criminal extortion and bribery under Nigerian law.

## Worked Examples

### Problem 1: Multi-Agency Border Incident Analysis
**Scenario:** At a maritime seaport terminal in Port Harcourt, a foreign container ship docks. During inspection, officers find twenty undocumented foreign stowaways hiding in the engine hold, while two cargo containers contain prohibited automatic firearms declared as "used motor vehicle spare parts."
- **Agency Action Plan:**
  1. *Human Migration Breach:* The **Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS)** takes custody of the undocumented foreign stowaways to process them for immigration violations and repatriation.
  2. *Contraband & False Declaration:* The **Nigeria Customs Service (NCS)** seizes the container of firearms, impounds the false manifest, arrests the clearing agent for smuggling, and hands the lethal weapons over to military or police authorities for ballistics tracking.

### Problem 2: Infrastructure Sabotage vs Traffic Enforcement
**Scenario:** A speeding pickup truck loaded with stolen copper transmission cables from a national power grid transformer collides with a street barrier on an expressway and tries to speed away.
- **Inter-Agency Coordination:**
  1. *Traffic Intervention:* The **Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC)** halts the reckless driver, secures the crash site, and prevents highway obstruction.
  2. *Infrastructure Crime Handover:* Upon inspecting the bed of the truck and identifying stolen power grid cables, the FRSC immediately hands over the suspects and stolen cables to the **Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC)** or the Police for economic sabotage prosecution under the Miscellaneous Offences Act.

A) The Inspector-General of Police

Feedback: Under the Constitution, the democratically elected President of Nigeria is the Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces.
