## Key Terms
- Term: Midterm Assessment
- Definition: A formative diagnostic evaluation administered midway through an academic term to test comprehension of preceding modules before advancing to advanced topics.
- Term: Emergency Management
- Definition: The organized, continuous process of analyzing, preparing for, responding to, and recovering from catastrophic hazards and emergencies.
- Term: NEMA
- Definition: The National Emergency Management Agency, the apex federal body responsible for disaster mitigation, search-and-rescue, and relief coordination in Nigeria.
- Term: The Golden Hour
- Definition: The critical first sixty minutes following severe trauma or emergency during which prompt medical care gives the highest probability of survival.
- Term: "Turn Around, Don't Drown"
- Definition: The universal flood safety rule warning motorists and pedestrians never to walk, swim, or drive through moving floodwaters of unknown depth.
- Term: Smoke Inhalation
- Definition: Breathing in toxic carbon monoxide, hydrogen cyanide, and superheated soot particles, which is the leading cause of death in building fires.
- Term: Kidnapping
- Definition: The unlawful, forcible seizure, transportation, and confinement of a person against their will, typically to extract an exorbitant financial ransom.
- Term: Route Variation
- Definition: The security practice of altering daily departure times and travel routes to prevent criminals from planning predictable transit ambushes.
- Term: Cultism
- Definition: Membership, initiation, and activities within an illegal, secretive group bound by blood oaths, characterized by intimidation and criminal violence.
- Term: Renunciation
- Definition: The formal, public, and legal process through which a repentant cult member severs their allegiance to a secret cult and returns to peaceful society.

### Essential Midterm Takeaways
1. **Emergency Management (Week 01):** NEMA coordinates national disaster relief, airlifts, and IDP camp logistics under the Presidency; SEMA handles state-level emergencies and flood shelters. Natural hazards include river overflows (Niger and Benue) and gully erosion; man-made hazards include building collapses caused by substandard cement and quack builders. Families should maintain an emergency grab-bag with documents and a whistle.
2. **First Response & Survival (Week 02):** The Golden Hour dictates survival. In floods, never cross moving water (15 cm knocks down an adult; 30 cm floats cars) and watch for Electric Shock Drowning. In fires, smoke inhalation is the #1 killer; crawl low beneath smoke and never use elevators. For fresh burns, cool under clean running water for 10–20 minutes (never use raw eggs or palm oil!).
3. **Kidnapping & Abduction (Week 03):** Over 60% of targeted kidnappings involve inside collusion. Prevent attacks through routine variation, digital silence on social media, and staff vetting. If captured, remain calm, obey commands, avoid aggressive eye contact, and eat provided food. During a commando rescue assault, **immediately drop flat to the floor with hands over your head** to avoid accidental friendly fire!
4. **Cultism and Gangs (Week 04):** Founded peacefully in 1952 by Prof. Wole Soyinka and the "Magnificent Seven" at UCI, cults degenerated into violent, armed criminal syndicates in the 1980s. Recruiters use deceptive promises of protection and connection to trap students into brutal "blending" hazing and blood oaths. Cultism leads to violent death, expulsion, and 10 to 21 years imprisonment under state anti-cultism laws.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Treating First Aid Folk Remedies as Valid Medicine
Smearing raw eggs, engine grease, or kerosene on a fresh burn because "an elder recommended it."
> **Correction:** These substances trap intense heat inside the tissue and introduce deadly bacteria into open skin, leading to severe sepsis. Cool running water is the only medically verified burn remedy.

### Mistake 2: Running Toward Armed Rescuers During a Commando Breach
Jumping up and sprinting toward tactical soldiers shouting for joy when a hostage rescue raid begins.
> **Correction:** In high-speed commando assaults, soldiers shoot any standing figure in the room. You must **drop flat on the floor, cover your head, and stay completely still** until soldiers clear the room.

### Mistake 3: Believing Cult Initiation Can Be Undone Secretly Without Official Help
Thinking that if you simply stop attending cult meetings, the gang will leave you alone.
> **Correction:** Secret cults hunt down deserters. You must undergo the **Official Renunciation Protocol** through school administrators, security agencies, and parental protection.

## Worked Examples

### Problem 1: Analyzing Flood Evacuation Decisions
**Scenario:** Following 24 hours of non-stop rain, floodwater rises to knee level on a residential street. A father wants to drive his family across a submerged bridge to reach an aunt’s house. His 12-year-old child reminds him of the "Turn Around, Don't Drown" rule.
- **Evaluation:** Water rushing across a bridge easily washes away the road surface underneath without visible surface signs. Thirty centimeters (1 foot) of water floats a car, carrying the family into the river.
- **Safe Decision:** The father turns the car around, parks on high ground, and moves the family to the nearest **SEMA Emergency Shelter**.

### Problem 2: Deconstructing a Cult Recruitment Approach
**Situation:** A senior student corners a JSS 1 student, gives him an expensive wristwatch, and says: *"You are a smart boy. Come to our exclusive brothers' meeting tonight at 9:00 PM behind the abandoned waterboard tank. Don't tell your mom or dad."*
- **Analysis:** This has three undeniable indicators of cult recruitment: an unearned expensive gift (bribery/grooming), an isolated night venue (initiation trap), and a demand for secrecy from parents.
- **Action:** The student politely refuses the gift, walks away, and reports the incident immediately to the School Principal and Guidance Counselor.
