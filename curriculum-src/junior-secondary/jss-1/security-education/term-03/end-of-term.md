## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Believing National Security Depends Exclusively on Weapons
Assuming that buying more military hardware is the only way to secure a country.
> **Correction:** Weaponry is only one component. Real national security requires social justice, economic opportunity, transparent governance, clean public institutions, disaster preparedness, and active, patriotic citizen intelligence.

### Mistake 2: Joining Street Mobs in Lynching Suspected Criminals
Thinking that participating in mob violence ("jungle justice") makes you a brave crime-fighter.
> **Correction:** Jungle justice is an act of cold-blooded Murder under Section 316 of the Criminal Code. It murders innocent people, eliminates vital forensic intelligence, destroys public peace, and results in mandatory death penalties for the mob participants.

### Mistake 3: Confusing the Specific Mandates of Specialized Security Agencies
Assuming any security agency can perform any duty without legal authorization.
> **Correction:** Every agency in Nigeria operates under strict statutory boundaries enacted by the National Assembly. Passports belong to NIS; road traffic to FRSC; pipeline defense to NSCDC; fiscal tariffs to Customs; economic fraud to EFCC; and civil order to the Police.

## Worked Examples

### Problem 1: Full-Spectrum Emergency Management Analysis
**Scenario:** A massive explosion rocks an industrial fuel depot in a commercial city, triggering a raging chemical fireball. Several workers are severely burned, electrical transmission lines collapse into flooded gutters, and looters attempt to break into nearby stores.
- **Integrated Multi-Agency Protocol:**
  1. *Fire Suppression (FFS):* The **Federal Fire Service** deploys high-expansion chemical foam trucks and sets up water monitors from municipal hydrants to contain the hydrocarbon fire.
  2. *First Aid Response (Term 3):* First responders cool thermal burns immediately with clean running cold water for 15 minutes, rejecting raw eggs or palm oil, and treat for traumatic shock during the Golden Hour.
  3. *Infrastructure & Electrical Hazard Defense (NSCDC):* The **NSCDC** cordons off the breached power lines, preventing civilians from entering energized water (Electric Shock Drowning) and protecting transformer coils from looters.
  4. *Civil Order & Anti-Looting (Police):* The **Nigeria Police Force** establishes a security perimeter, arrests looters, and maintains public order under Section 214.
  5. *Disaster Relief Coordination (NEMA/SEMA):* **NEMA** and **SEMA** mobilize mobile clinics, emergency food, and shelter for displaced local residents.

### Problem 2: Digital Identity and Transit Defense Analysis
**Scenario:** A student traveling home from boarding school receives an urgent SMS: *"Your student debit card has been suspended, click this link to verify your PIN."* Ten minutes later, while waiting at an unlit bus stop, an unpainted private car with no interior door handles offers a cheap ride home.
- **Security Assessment:**
  1. *Cyber Threat Defense (Week 06):* The student recognizes a **Phishing Attack**, refuses to click the link, and protects their account with Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).
  2. *Transit Security Defense (Week 07):* The student spots the classic **"One Chance" Trap** (unpainted vehicle, missing interior door handles, isolated night location, suspicious discount).
  3. *Action:* The student walks safely into the official municipal motor park, boards a registered commercial bus, and texts the license plate to their parents.
