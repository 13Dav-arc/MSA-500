Imagine walking through a bustling, crowded international market in Lagos or Kano. Would you write your bank card PIN on your forehead? Would you hand your house keys to a total stranger wearing a mask who whispers, *"Give me your keys and I will give you a bag of gold tomorrow"*? Of course not! Yet, every single day on the Internet, millions of smart teenagers fall victim to online impostors because they leave their digital front doors wide open! ## Key Terms
- Term: Digital Citizenship
  Definition: The norms of appropriate, responsible, ethical, legal, and empowered behavior regarding technology use, digital communication, and online community participation.
- Term: Cyber Safety (Online Safety)
  Definition: The collection of habits, protective practices, and technical measures used by individuals to protect their personal privacy, digital devices, and emotional well-being from online hazards.
- Term: Phishing
  Definition: A deceptive social engineering cyber-attack where criminals impersonate trustworthy institutions (such as banks, schools, or email providers) via fraudulent emails, SMS, or fake websites to steal passwords, PINs, or financial details.
- Term: Malware (Malicious Software)
  Definition: Any destructive software program developed to intentionally infiltrate, damage, disrupt, spy on, or gain unauthorized control over a computer system (e.g., viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware).
- Term: Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) / Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
  Definition: A cybersecurity verification process that requires a user to provide two separate forms of identification before gaining account access (e.g., a secret password plus an instant OTP code sent to a mobile phone).
- Term: Personally Identifiable Information (PII)
  Definition: Any sensitive personal data that can be used to distinguish, trace, or identify an individual’s identity (such as full name, home address, National Identification Number, date of birth, and biometric data).

### Core Rules of Cyber Safety

#### 1. Protecting Personally Identifiable Information (PII)
Never publicly broadcast sensitive personal details on public social media platforms, gaming chat rooms, or unverified websites:
- **Private Data to Guard:** Full legal name, residential home address, current physical location, school name and uniform pictures, personal telephone number, parent bank details, and your 11-digit **National Identification Number (NIN)**.
- **Why It Matters:** Cybercriminals compile scattered public details to execute **identity theft**, answer security questions, or track a student's physical location.

#### 2. The Golden Rules of Password Security
Your password is the digital key to your online life:
- **Length & Complexity:** Make passwords at least **10 to 12 characters** long, combining uppercase letters (`A–Z`), lowercase letters (`a–z`), numbers (`0–9`), and symbols (`! @ # $ % ^ & *`).
- **Avoid Guessable Information:** Never use birthdays, telephone numbers, your pet's name, or sequential numbers (e.g., `password123` or `ade12345`).
- **Never Reuse Passwords:** Use unique passwords for different accounts so that if one website suffers a data breach, your other accounts remain secure.
- **Activate Two-Factor Authentication (2FA):** Always enable 2FA on email, school portals, and messaging apps. Even if a thief steals your password, they cannot log in without the second secret code sent to your phone.

#### 3. Recognizing Phishing Scams
Phishing is the most widespread cybercrime targeting everyday citizens:
- **How It Works:** You receive an urgent email or SMS stating: *"URGENT: Your bank account will be blocked within 24 hours due to BVN failure! Click this link immediately to verify your details!"*
- **The Red Flags:**
  - Creates artificial panic and urgency (*"Act within 10 minutes or lose your account!"*).
  - Comes from suspicious, misspelled email addresses (e.g., `support@zen1th-bank-alert.com` instead of the genuine official bank domain).
  - Asks for secret credentials (banks NEVER ask for your ATM PIN or password via email or phone!).
  - Contains poor spelling, awkward grammar, and suspicious clickable links.

#### 4. Understanding Malware Types
- **Computer Virus:** Malicious code that attaches itself to legitimate program files and replicates when the program is run.
- **Worm:** Standalone malware that self-replicates across computer networks automatically without requiring user intervention.
- **Trojan Horse:** Deceptive malware disguised as a harmless, attractive file (such as a free video game or screen saver) that secretly installs backdoors or spyware once opened.
- **Ransomware:** Highly dangerous malware that encrypts a victim’s personal files, locking them until the victim pays extortion money to the cybercriminals.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1: Believing that anti-virus software protects you from phishing scams.**
  *Correction:* Antivirus software stops malicious software files from executing on your hard drive, but it cannot stop you from voluntarily typing your secret password into a fake phishing website! Phishing defense requires human awareness and critical thinking.
- **Mistake 2: Thinking that using "Password@1" is a super-strong, unhackable password.**
  *Correction:* Automated cybercrime dictionary tools test thousands of common variations like "Password@1" in seconds. Passwords should be unpredictable passphrases combining unrelated words and symbols (e.g., `Blue$Turtle#Jumping92!`).
- **Mistake 3: Assuming that cyberbullying is just "harmless online joking."**
  *Correction:* Cyberbullying causes severe emotional trauma and is legally prohibited under the Nigerian Cybercrimes Act of 2015, carrying serious legal consequences for perpetrators.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Evaluating a Suspicious Message for Phishing Indicators
A student receives an SMS message on their phone:
> *"URGENT ALERT: Central Bank of Nigeria has selected your telephone number to receive an educational grant of ₦100,000! Click here immediately to claim: http://cbn-grant-reward.free-hosting.org/login and enter your bank account number and ATM PIN!"*

**Critical Cyber Analysis:**
1. **Urgency & Flattery:** Promises free money and demands immediate action to bypass logical thinking.
2. **Deceptive URL:** The address is hosted on a free hosting site (`free-hosting.org`), NOT the authentic official government domain (`cbn.gov.ng`).
3. **Sensitive Credential Request:** Requests an **ATM PIN**—something no genuine banking authority ever asks for.
4. **Action:** The student recognizes this as a malicious phishing scam, deletes the SMS immediately, and alerts their parents.

---

### Example 2: Designing a Secure Password with 2FA Protection
Help a secondary school student create an uncrackable password for their national examination portal account:
- **Weak Attempt:** `kemi2010` (easily cracked in milliseconds using public birth year data).
- **Strong Master Passphrase:** `Pencil#River*84Lagos!` (19 characters, combines uppercase letters, lowercase letters, symbols, numbers, and unrelated concepts).
- **Enabling 2FA:** The student links their portal account to their mobile phone so that whenever anyone attempts to log in, a 6-digit One-Time Password (OTP) is sent via SMS. Even if someone guesses the password, they cannot breach the account!
