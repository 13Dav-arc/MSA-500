## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: What is information transmission fundamentally defined as in information technology?
A) The systematic process of conveying, transferring, or broadcasting meaningful messages from a sender across a medium to a receiver (correct)
B) The mechanical manufacturing of plastic computer mouse casings
C) The permanent formatting of a computer's secondary storage drive
D) The physical cleaning of cooling fan dust in a computer lab
Feedback: Information transmission is the process of conveying structured, meaningful data and messages from a source across a channel to a destination.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: What are the three foundational elements of the communication transmission model?
A) Sender (Source), Transmission Channel (Medium), and Receiver (Destination) (correct)
B) Keyboard, Monitor, and Mouse
C) Motherboard, CPU, and Power Supply
D) Vacuum Tube, Transistor, and Microchip
Feedback: Every communication link requires a Sender to formulate the message, a Medium to convey it, and a Receiver to decode it.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: What mode of communication requires both the sender and receiver to participate simultaneously in real time, synchronized by an electronic clock?
A) Synchronous transmission (correct)
B) Asynchronous transmission
C) Offline batch processing
D) Latent storage transmission
Feedback: Synchronous transmission occurs in real time with continuous two-way communication between parties (such as live phone calls or video conferences).

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following communication channels is an everyday example of Synchronous Transmission?
A) A live interactive video call on Zoom or Google Meet (correct)
B) An email sent with an attached PDF document
C) A text message waiting in an SMS inbox
D) A message left on an internet discussion forum
Feedback: Live video calls require real-time, simultaneous interaction between participants, classifying them as synchronous.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following communication channels is an everyday example of Asynchronous Transmission?
A) Sending an electronic mail (e-mail) (correct)
B) A direct face-to-face classroom conversation
C) A live telephone voice conversation
D) A live two-way radio walkie-talkie conversation
Feedback: Email is an asynchronous store-and-forward system; messages are sent and read at independent times chosen by the participants.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: What official community messenger in traditional African kingdoms announced royal decrees and community news using an iron gong?
A) The Town Crier (correct)
B) The Network Administrator
C) The Systems Analyst
D) The Software Developer
Feedback: The Town Crier was the traditional herald who struck an iron gong to command village silence before proclaiming royal decrees.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: How did traditional African talking drums transmit messages across forest valleys?
A) By mimicking the high, mid, and low tonal registers and proverbs of spoken languages through variable drumhead tension (correct)
B) By projecting bright light reflections off polished brass surfaces
C) By transmitting electromagnetic radio waves to satellite dishes
D) By sending electrical Morse code pulses through copper cables
Feedback: Talking drums mimic human speech because African languages are tonal; varying the drum's tension reproduces the exact pitch of spoken words.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: What was a major operational weakness of ancient smoke signals and hilltop bonfires?
A) They could only transmit simple prearranged alerts and were rendered completely useless by rain, fog, and wind (correct)
B) They consumed excessive electrical power from the national power grid
C) They required high-speed wireless internet routers to produce smoke
D) They could only transmit data in the French language
Feedback: Smoke signals had very low message capacity and were easily disrupted or obscured by adverse weather conditions.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: What traditional West African physical symbol is placed on farm boundaries or broken-down vehicles to indicate dispute, warning, or taboo?
A) Fresh yellow palm fronds (Omu) (correct)
B) Plastic computer keyboards
C) Silicon microprocessor wafers
D) Optical compact discs
Feedback: Fresh yellow palm fronds (*Omu*) are culturally recognized traditional markers signaling legal dispute, prohibition, or physical hazard.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: Who invented the movable type printing press in the 1440s, launching the modern era of mass-produced books?
A) Johannes Gutenberg (correct)
B) Alexander Graham Bell
C) Samuel Morse
D) Ray Tomlinson
Feedback: Johannes Gutenberg invented the movable type printing press in Germany around 1440, revolutionizing mass communication.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: What small plastic smart card containing a microchip is inserted into a GSM mobile phone to identify the subscriber on the cellular network?
A) SIM card (Subscriber Identity Module) (correct)
B) Primary RAM module
C) Motherboard BIOS chip
D) Magnetic core ring
Feedback: A SIM card (Subscriber Identity Module) stores user identity credentials and network authorization keys for GSM mobile phones.

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: What telecommunication machine acts like a long-distance photocopier, scanning a paper document and transmitting it over telephone lines to print remotely?
A) Facsimile (Fax) machine (correct)
B) Mechanical typewriter
C) Slide rule
D) Cathode ray tube
Feedback: A Fax machine scans a physical document, converts it into audio tones transmitted over telephone lines, and prints an exact copy at the receiver.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: What 19th-century invention used electrical pulses along copper wires and Morse Code to send text messages faster than physical transportation?
A) The Electric Telegraph (correct)
B) The Inkjet Printer
C) The Abacus
D) The Microprocessor
Feedback: Samuel Morse's electric telegraph enabled text messages (telegrams) to travel along wires at the speed of electricity.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: Who invented network electronic mail (email) in 1971 and introduced the "@" symbol to separate the user from the host computer?
A) Ray Tomlinson (correct)
B) Alexander Graham Bell
C) Johannes Gutenberg
D) Charles Babbage
Feedback: Computer engineer Ray Tomlinson developed network email on ARPANET in 1971 and selected the `@` symbol for addressing.

### Q15 [true-false]
Stem: In an email header, an address placed in the "Bcc" (Blind Carbon Copy) field is completely hidden from all other recipients.
A) True (correct)
B) False
Feedback: `Bcc` (Blind Carbon Copy) conceals recipient email addresses from everyone else, preserving privacy.

### Q16 [true-false]
Stem: Deep-sea submarine fiber-optic cables carry over 95% of international internet data traffic across oceans using pulses of light through glass strands.
A) True (correct)
B) False
Feedback: Undersea fiber optic cables transmit vast terabits of global internet traffic between continents at the speed of light.

### Q17 [multi-select]
Stem: Which of the following communication methods are classified as Modern Methods of Information Transmission? (Select all that apply)
A) Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM) (correct)
B) Communications satellites (correct)
C) Facsimile (Fax) transmission (correct)
D) Striking an iron gong through the village square
Feedback: GSM, satellites, and fax machines are modern electronic methods. Striking an iron gong is an ancient method.

### Q18 [multi-select]
Stem: Which two of the following communication channels operate in Synchronous Transmission mode? (Select two)
A) Live two-way mobile telephone call (correct)
B) Live video conference meeting on Zoom or Google Meet (correct)
C) Sending an email message with an attached file
D) Sending an SMS text message to an offline phone
Feedback: Live phone calls and video meetings require real-time simultaneous interaction, classifying them as synchronous.

### Q19 [matching]
Stem: Match each transmission method to its historical classification:
Clue: Town Crying with Iron Gong -> Ancient traditional acoustic transmission method
Clue: Hilltop Smoke Signals -> Ancient traditional optical transmission method
Clue: Satellite Television Broadcast -> Modern electronic wireless transmission method
Clue: Undersea Fiber Optic Cable -> Modern electronic digital wired transmission method
Feedback: Town crying is ancient acoustic; Smoke signals are ancient optical; Satellite is modern wireless; Fiber optics is modern wired.

### Q20 [matching]
Stem: Match each email header component to its operational role:
Clue: To -> Primary recipient expected to read and act upon the message
Clue: Cc -> Secondary recipient receiving a visible informational copy
Clue: Bcc -> Secret recipient receiving a copy while keeping their address hidden
Clue: Subject -> Concise title summarizing the main topic of the message
Feedback: To is primary; Cc is visible copy; Bcc is hidden copy; Subject is message title.
