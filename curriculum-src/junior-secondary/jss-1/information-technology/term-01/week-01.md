## Hook
When you watch a shopkeeper scan a barcode with a handheld reader at a supermarket in Lagos, or see a bank ATM dispense exact cash notes after you enter a PIN, you are witnessing an extraordinary transformation. Have you ever wondered how a series of meaningless electric signals turns into your exact bank balance, or why entering numbers like "35, 78, 92" means nothing until a teacher prints a term report card? That miraculous difference between raw, scattered facts and meaningful, life-guiding knowledge is the divide between **Data** and **Information**. At the heart of this revolution sits the **Computer**—the most versatile tool ever created by human ingenuity.

## Key Terms
- Term: Computer
  Definition: An electronic device that accepts raw data as input, processes it systematically according to stored program instructions, stores the results, and produces meaningful information as output.
- Term: Data
  Definition: Raw, unprocessed facts, figures, symbols, observations, or measurements that lack contextual meaning on their own.
- Term: Information
  Definition: Data that has been processed, organized, structured, and contextualized so that it becomes meaningful, intelligible, and useful for human decision-making.
- Term: GIGO (Garbage In, Garbage Out)
  Definition: A fundamental computing principle stating that the accuracy of a computer's output depends entirely upon the quality and correctness of the input data provided.
- Term: Processing
  Definition: The internal manipulation, calculation, sorting, comparing, or logical transformation of input data performed by a computer's Central Processing Unit.

## Core Concept
### What is a Computer?
A computer is not merely a screen or a keyboard; it is an integrated electronic system designed to perform high-speed computations and logical operations automatically. Unlike a simple mechanical calculator or typewriter, a computer is **programmable**—meaning it can store complex sequences of instructions in its memory and execute them repeatedly without human intervention.

### The Information Processing Cycle (IPOS)
Every computer system, from the smallest smartwatch to the largest supercomputer, operates on a four-stage architectural cycle known as **IPOS**:
1. **Input:** Capturing raw data and user instructions through devices such as keyboards, mice, optical scanners, and microphones.
2. **Processing:** Manipulating, calculating, evaluating, and transforming input data inside the Central Processing Unit (CPU).
3. **Output:** Presenting the processed results to the user in a human-readable format via monitors, printers, plotters, or audio speakers.
4. **Storage:** Saving data, program instructions, and information permanently or temporarily in storage media (such as hard disks, solid-state drives, or flash memory) for future retrieval.

### Differentiating Data from Information
Understanding the difference between data and information is the foundational cornerstone of Information Technology:
- **Characteristics of Data:**
  - Raw and unorganized (e.g., disconnected numbers: `14`, `16`, `12`, `18`).
  - Lacks specific meaning or context until interpreted.
  - Serves as the raw material or input for the computer.
- **Characteristics of Information:**
  - Structured, meaningful, and organized (e.g., *"The average age of JSS 1 students in our classroom is 15 years"*).
  - Possesses context, purpose, and utility.
  - Serves as the output of computer processing and enables effective decisions.

```
RAW DATA                                  PROCESSING                                MEANINGFUL INFORMATION
[ "Amina", 88, "Maths", 94, "English" ]  ───► [ Add, Average, Format Report ] ───►  "Amina scored an average of 91%
                                                                                    and placed 1st in the class."
```

### Core Characteristics of Computers
What makes the computer superior to manual pen-and-paper calculation?
1. **High Speed:** A computer performs millions and billions of instructions per second. Internal operations are calculated in microseconds ($10^{-6}$ s), nanoseconds ($10^{-9}$ s), and picoseconds ($10^{-12}$ s).
2. **Extreme Accuracy:** When provided with correct data and program logic, computers never make calculation errors. Errors produced by computer systems are almost universally traced to faulty human data entry or defective software logic (**GIGO**).
3. **Diligence & Tirelessness:** Unlike human beings who experience physical exhaustion, boredom, or lapses in concentration after hours of repetitive tasks, a computer can work 24 hours a day, 365 days a year with uniform precision.
4. **Massive Storage Capacity:** Modern computers can store vast libraries of encyclopedias, medical records, and sessional examination scripts in compact digital memory chips measuring less than a thumbnail.
5. **Versatility:** The exact same computer that formats a letter can simulate an aircraft flight, analyze satellite weather telemetry, control hospital life-support monitors, and compose orchestral music.
6. **Automation:** Once programmed, a computer executes multi-step computational procedures automatically from start to finish without requiring continuous human guidance.

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Believing that computers possess independent human intelligence and can think on their own.
  Fact: Computers are deterministic electronic machines; they cannot think, feel, or invent solutions independently. They execute strictly what human programmers instruct them to do.
- Mistake: Using the terms "Data" and "Information" as identical synonyms in computer science examinations.
  Fact: Data is raw and unorganized input; information is processed and meaningful output. Treating them as identical leads to lost marks.
- Mistake: Blaming a computer for an arithmetic mistake when wrong figures were entered by the operator.
  Fact: The GIGO principle (Garbage In, Garbage Out) proves that errors in output are caused by incorrect input or faulty programming, not machine error.

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Differentiating Data from Information
Context: A secondary school bursar collects the following entry slip during morning registration:
`"Tunde, 12, Male, 15000, 10/10/2026"`
Problem: Explain why this record is classified as raw data, and show how the school's computerized billing system transforms it into meaningful information.
Fix:
- *Why it is Data:* In its current state, the numbers and words are disconnected facts. It does not state whether 15000 is an outstanding debt, a tuition fee payment, or a book deposit.
- *Transformation into Information:* When processed by the accounting software, it outputs an official receipt: *"Master Tunde, age 12, has paid ₦15,000 for JSS 1 First Term Tuition Fees on 10th October 2026, leaving an outstanding balance of ₦0.00."* This output is contextual, structured, and actionable.

### Example B: Applying the GIGO Principle
Context: An invigilator enters student examination marks into a spreadsheet. Instead of typing `75`, the operator accidentally keys in `750` for a test scored over 100.
Problem: What will happen to the student's cumulative average, and how does this illustrate GIGO?
Fix:
- *Outcome:* The computerized spreadsheet will calculate an impossible average exceeding 100%, skewing the entire class ranking.
- *GIGO Principle:* The computer executed the mathematical formula with flawless accuracy ($Sum \div Count$). The erroneous result occurred solely because false data (`750`) was fed into the machine.
