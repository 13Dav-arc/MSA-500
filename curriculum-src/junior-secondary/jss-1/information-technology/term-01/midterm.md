## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the primary distinction between data and information in computing?
A) Data consists of raw, unorganized facts, while information is processed, structured data that conveys clear meaning (correct)
B) Data is produced exclusively by computer screens, while information is entered only by keyboards
C) Data cannot be saved in storage media, whereas information can never be erased
D) Data is only utilized by software engineers, while information is reserved for bankers
Feedback: Data refers to raw, unprocessed facts, numbers, and observations that lack context. Information is data that has undergone processing, organizing, and structuring into a meaningful format useful for human decision-making.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the correct chronological sequence of the fundamental Information Processing Cycle?
A) Input -> Processing -> Output -> Storage (correct)
B) Output -> Storage -> Input -> Processing
C) Processing -> Input -> Storage -> Output
D) Storage -> Output -> Processing -> Input
Feedback: The standard computer processing cycle (IPOS) follows the strict sequence: Input (accepting raw data), Processing (manipulating data inside the CPU), Output (presenting results), and Storage (saving data and instructions for future retrieval).

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Which computing acronym expresses the principle that incorrect or inaccurate input data will inevitably produce erroneous output results?
A) GIGO (Garbage In, Garbage Out) (correct)
B) BIOS (Basic Input Output System)
C) FIFO (First In, First Out)
D) CMOS (Complementary Metal Oxide Semiconductor)
Feedback: GIGO (Garbage In, Garbage Out) emphasizes that a computer's calculations and reports are only as accurate and trustworthy as the quality of data supplied by human users.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: A computer performs continuous calculations for 48 hours without experiencing physical fatigue, loss of concentration, or degradation in accuracy. Which core characteristic of computers does this illustrate?
A) Diligence (correct)
B) Versatility
C) Artificial consciousness
D) Volatility
Feedback: Diligence refers to a computer's capacity to perform monotonous, repetitive, and complex tasks continuously over extended periods without fatigue, boredom, or computational error.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: What ancient manual calculating device consists of a wooden frame divided into upper and lower decks with movable counting beads strung on vertical rods?
A) Abacus (correct)
B) Napier's Bones
C) Stepped Reckoner
D) Slide Rule
Feedback: The Abacus, utilized by ancient Babylonian, Chinese, and Mediterranean civilizations, consists of beads sliding on rods divided by a beam into an upper deck (heaven) and lower deck (earth) for positional arithmetic.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: Which Scottish mathematician developed a set of marked bone or ivory calculating rods in 1617 based on lattice multiplication and invented logarithms?
A) John Napier (correct)
B) Blaise Pascal
C) William Oughtred
D) Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz
Feedback: John Napier invented logarithms and developed Napier's Bones—a set of numbered rectangular rods carved from bone or wood used for rapid multiplication and division.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: What mechanical calculating instrument, invented by William Oughtred in the 1620s, uses sliding logarithmic scales to perform multiplication and division without gear wheels?
A) Slide Rule (correct)
B) Pascaline
C) Difference Engine
D) Tabulating Machine
Feedback: The Slide Rule, invented by William Oughtred, uses adjacent sliding logarithmic scales to multiply, divide, and calculate roots rapidly, remaining the primary calculation tool for engineers until modern electronic calculators.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: Why did French mathematician Blaise Pascal invent the Pascaline in 1642?
A) To assist his father, a government tax superintendent, in tedious addition and subtraction calculations (correct)
B) To calculate the census returns for the French naval military fleet
C) To weave complex silk patterns on industrial fabric looms
D) To decode encrypted military messages during the Napoleonic wars
Feedback: Blaise Pascal constructed the Pascaline (Arithmetic Machine) in 1642 at age 19 to relieve his father, a royal tax collector in Rouen, from exhausting manual arithmetic.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: Which mechanical component did Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz invent to enable the Stepped Reckoner to perform direct multiplication and division in addition to addition and subtraction?
A) The stepped drum (fluted cylinder) (correct)
B) The mercury vacuum tube
C) The magnetic ferrite core
D) The punched paper tape reader
Feedback: Leibniz invented the stepped drum (stepped gear cylinder), a cylinder with nine toothed ridges of incrementally increasing length that allowed direct multiplication by repeated addition.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: How did Joseph Marie Jacquard's 1801 textile loom contribute a foundational concept to modern computer architecture?
A) It used interchangeable punched cards containing punched holes to automate and control complex weaving patterns (correct)
B) It replaced mechanical wooden rods with electrical vacuum tubes
C) It performed automatic floating-point decimal division
D) It introduced the binary magnetic storage disk
Feedback: Jacquard's automated weaving loom read sequences of punched cards with coded holes, pioneering the concept of programmable machine instructions and external data input.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: In Charles Babbage's design for the Analytical Engine, what was the function of the unit designated as the "Mill"?
A) The processing unit that performed arithmetic computations (equivalent to a modern CPU) (correct)
B) The internal storage area that held numbers permanently (equivalent to modern RAM)
C) The printing mechanism that engraved copper display plates
D) The input hopper that sorted paper sheets
Feedback: In Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine, the "Mill" was the processing unit that performed arithmetic calculations upon numbers retrieved from the "Store" (memory).

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: Why is Charles Babbage universally honored in computer history as the "Father of Computing"?
A) He formulated the fundamental architectural concept of a programmable general-purpose computer comprising input, storage, processing, and output (correct)
B) He built the first working silicon microprocessor in Silicon Valley
C) He established the International Business Machines (IBM) corporation
D) He invented the binary machine language used in personal laptops
Feedback: Charles Babbage earned the title "Father of Computing" because his design of the Analytical Engine incorporated the core architectural elements of all modern computers: an input mechanism, memory store, processing mill, and printed output.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: Why is Lady Ada Augusta Lovelace recognized as the world's first computer programmer?
A) She authored the first published algorithm intended for implementation on Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine (correct)
B) She designed the mechanical gears and brass shafts of the Difference Engine
C) She soldered the first electronic transistor logic circuits
D) She established the first commercial software company in London
Feedback: Lady Ada Lovelace documented Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine and created a detailed step-by-step algorithm to calculate Bernoulli numbers, earning her recognition as the world's first computer programmer.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: What national crisis in the United States led Herman Hollerith to invent the electric Tabulating Machine using punched cards?
A) The manual tabulation of the 1880 US Census had taken almost eight years, threatening paralysis for the upcoming 1890 Census (correct)
B) The failure of the national railway telegraph signaling system during winter storms
C) The bankruptcy of the United States Postal Service due to lost mail bags
D) The lack of accurate navigation tables for trans-Atlantic steamships
Feedback: The 1880 US Census required nearly eight years to count manually. Herman Hollerith developed an electro-mechanical tabulator using punched cards that counted the 1890 Census population of 62.9 million people in just weeks.

### Q15 [true-false]
Stem: Raw data by itself always provides actionable insight and can be directly used for executive policy decisions without any sorting, calculating, or processing.
A) True
B) False (correct)
Feedback: Raw data consists of isolated numbers, symbols, and unverified facts that lack context. It must first be processed, structured, and organized into information before it can guide sound decisions.

### Q16 [true-false]
Stem: Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine was designed with a "Store" for memory, a "Mill" for processing, and punched cards for input and program instructions, mirroring modern computer architecture.
A) True (correct)
B) False
Feedback: Charles Babbage's 1837 design of the Analytical Engine accurately anticipated the modern von Neumann computer architecture by separating data storage (the Store) from computational processing (the Mill).

### Q17 [multi-select]
Stem: Which of the following are the core functional operations comprising the Information Processing Cycle (IPOS) of computer systems? (Select all that apply)
A) Input (correct)
B) Processing (correct)
C) Output (correct)
D) Storage (correct)
Feedback: The Information Processing Cycle consists of four interdependent operations: Input, Processing, Output, and Storage.

### Q18 [multi-select]
Stem: Which two historical computing devices directly utilized punched cards as a medium for encoding instructions or recording data? (Select two)
A) Jacquard's automated weaving loom (correct)
B) Herman Hollerith's electric tabulating machine (correct)
C) John Napier's bone calculating rods
D) William Oughtred's logarithmic slide rule
Feedback: Joseph Marie Jacquard utilized punched cards to direct decorative weaving patterns in 1801, and Herman Hollerith adapted punched cards in 1890 to encode demographic census data.

### Q19 [matching]
Stem: Match each historical computing pioneer to their groundbreaking contribution:
Clue: Charles Babbage -> Conceived the Analytical Engine and formulated modern computer architecture
Clue: Ada Lovelace -> Created the first published algorithmic program to calculate Bernoulli numbers
Clue: Herman Hollerith -> Built the electro-mechanical punched card tabulator and founded the precursor to IBM
Clue: Blaise Pascal -> Invented the first commercial mechanical gear-driven adding machine
Feedback: Charles Babbage formulated modern computing architecture; Ada Lovelace authored the first algorithm; Herman Hollerith developed the punched card tabulator; Blaise Pascal built the Pascaline.

### Q20 [matching]
Stem: Match each early calculating device to its defining physical mechanism or operational principle:
Clue: Abacus -> Horizontal wire rods holding movable counting beads separated by a beam
Clue: Napier's Bones -> Carved rectangular rods marked with multiplication tables based on logarithms
Clue: Slide Rule -> Sliding ruler strips with continuous logarithmic scales for rapid multiplication
Clue: Leibniz Stepped Reckoner -> Fluted cylindrical drum with nine graduated gear teeth
Feedback: The Abacus uses beads on rods; Napier's Bones uses numbered rods; the Slide Rule utilizes sliding logarithmic scales; Leibniz's Stepped Reckoner utilizes a fluted cylindrical stepped drum.
