## Hook
Imagine your school investing millions of naira to set up a brand-new, ultra-modern computer laboratory equipped with fifty high-speed computers. The monitors are crystal clear, the keyboards are spotless, and the room smells clean. But within just three months, five keyboards have sticky, jammed keys because students spilled sugary soda on them; two computers suffered burnt motherboards during an unexpected electrical power surge; someone tripped over a loose power cable on the floor and broke their wrist; and thick dust has clogged the cooling fans so badly that systems overheat and crash every fifteen minutes! Why did a magnificent academic facility deteriorate into a chaotic graveyard of broken machines? The answer is simple: the users ignored **Computer Room Management Ethics** and laboratory safety rules! Because computers are sensitive, delicate electronic instruments, keeping them running smoothly requires strict environmental controls, specialized protection equipment, and disciplined human behavior.

## Key Terms
- Term: Computer Room (Computer Laboratory)
  Definition: A specially designed, secure, and climate-controlled room configured specifically for housing computer systems, peripheral devices, networking hardware, and students during practical instructional sessions.
- Term: Computer Room Management Ethics
  Definition: The established set of rules, behavioral standards, maintenance protocols, and moral guidelines that govern how users conduct themselves and interact with equipment inside a computer laboratory.
- Term: Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)
  Definition: An electrical backup device containing internal rechargeable batteries that provides instantaneous, continuous emergency electrical power to computers when the main public electricity supply suddenly fails.
- Term: Voltage Stabilizer
  Definition: An electrical protection appliance that automatically regulates and stabilizes fluctuating electrical voltage, delivering a constant, safe voltage level to shield delicate electronics from brownouts and surges.
- Term: Surge Protector
  Definition: An electrical device with built-in metal-oxide varistors and circuit breakers designed to divert sudden, dangerous electrical voltage spikes (such as those caused by lightning strikes) safely away from computer components.
- Term: Dust Cover
  Definition: A protective cloth or antistatic vinyl cover draped over computer monitors, keyboards, and system units when not in use to prevent the accumulation of airborne dust particles.
- Term: Computer Logbook
  Definition: An official administrative record book wherein students and users record their names, assigned computer numbers, time of entry, time of departure, and any hardware faults noticed during the session.

## Core Concept
### Essential Components of a Modern Computer Laboratory
A computer room is far more than just a cluster of desks and screens; it is an engineered technical environment. According to standard curriculum guidelines, a fully equipped computer laboratory must feature:

1. **Computing Systems & Peripherals:** Desktop workstations, laptops, laser printers, optical flatbed scanners, multimedia data projectors, interactive whiteboards, and external multimedia speakers.
2. **Power Protection & Distribution Hardware:**
   - **Automatic Voltage Stabilizers (AVS):** Smooths out unstable voltage fluctuations from the national grid.
   - **Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS):** Keeps computers running for 10 to 30 minutes during a blackout, giving users time to save documents and shut down properly.
   - **Surge Protectors & Extension Boxes:** Safely distribute power to multiple devices while shielding against sudden voltage spikes.
3. **Environmental Controls:**
   - **Air Conditioners & Ceiling Fans:** Keeps internal room temperature cool, preventing CPU thermal throttling and component burnout.
   - **Window Curtains & Blinds:** Blocks harsh direct sunlight and eliminates blinding glare on monitor screens.
   - **Dust-Free Flooring:** Tiled or linoleum floors that do not trap or shed dust fibers like thick carpets.
4. **Safety & Security Infrastructure:**
   - **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$) Fire Extinguishers:** Specialized non-conductive electrical fire extinguishers (water must NEVER be used on electrical fires!).
   - **Smoke Detectors & Alarms.**
   - **Cable Trunking Channels:** Protective plastic ducting along walls and under floors to house all electrical cords neatly and eliminate tripping hazards.
   - **Burglar-Proof Window Grilles & Reinforced Security Doors.**

### Protocols for Computer Care and Maintenance
Because electronic microchips are vulnerable to heat, moisture, dust, and electrical shock, users must follow strict maintenance routines:
1. **Dust Prevention & Cleaning:** Dust acts like a thick blanket inside a system unit, trapping heat on processor heat sinks and causing fan motors to seize. Always keep computer rooms dust-free, clean desks regularly with a soft dry microfiber cloth, and cover equipment with dust covers after each day's session.
2. **Proper Ventilation:** Ensure system units are positioned on desks or raised stands in well-ventilated areas with at least 15 cm of open air around vents. Never enclose a system unit inside a tight, sealed wooden cabinet where heat cannot escape.
3. **Orderly Shutdown Procedure:** Never turn off a computer by flipping the wall switch or yanking out the plug! Such abrupt power cuts corrupt the operating system files and can damage hard drive magnetic platters. Always shut down systematically:
   $$\text{Click Start Menu} \longrightarrow \text{Select Power} \longrightarrow \text{Click Shut Down} \longrightarrow \text{Wait for fans to stop} \longrightarrow \text{Switch off monitor & wall socket}$$
4. **Proper Lighting & Anti-Glare:** Position computer monitors away from direct sunlight and adjust screen brightness to prevent eye fatigue.

### Golden Rules and Regulations of the Computer Laboratory
To safeguard expensive school infrastructure and protect students, the following rules must be strictly enforced:
- **Rule 1: Absolute Prohibition of Food and Drinks:** Eating or drinking inside the computer room is strictly forbidden! Accidental spills of water, juice, or tea cause instant electrical short circuits that destroy motherboards and keyboards. Crumbs attract ants and rodents that chew through internal electrical wiring.
- **Rule 2: No Unauthorized Hardware or Software Modifications:** Users must never load unauthorized software, video games, or pirated media. Never change system settings, screen resolutions, or system passwords.
- **Rule 3: Anti-Virus Flash Drive Scanning:** Every external USB flash drive or memory card must be scanned with antivirus software before opening files to prevent malware infections.
- **Rule 4: Professional Conduct & Quiet Atmosphere:** Loud music, shouting, and cell phone calls are strictly prohibited. The computer room is a quiet study environment.
- **Rule 5: Cable Safety:** Never pull an electrical cable from the socket by tugging on the flexible wire; always grasp the solid molded plug head firmly. Keep walkways completely free of loose cords.
- **Rule 6: Mandatory Logbook Registration:** Every user must sign into the computer logbook upon arrival and report any pre-existing faults immediately to the teacher or lab attendant.
- **Rule 7: Never Attempt Unauthorized Repairs:** If a computer sparks, smokes, or fails to start, immediately alert the instructor. Students must never open system units or attempt electrical repairs themselves.
- **Rule 8: Educational Internet Use Only:** Laboratory Internet access is provided exclusively for curriculum learning, academic research, and official CBT assessments.

## Comparative Matrix

| Protection Appliance / Tool | Primary Hazard Prevented | How It Operates | Protection Target | Consequence of Its Absence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Voltage Stabilizer** | Unstable high/low voltage (sags & surges) | Automatically adjusts internal transformer coils to output constant voltage | Motherboard, power supply unit, and internal chips | Burnt power supplies, melted capacitors from sudden high voltage |
| **Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)** | Abrupt blackouts / sudden power loss | Delivers immediate battery backup power for 10–30 minutes | Open software files, file systems, operating system stability | Corrupted OS files, loss of unsaved documents, damaged hard drives |
| **Surge Protector** | High-energy transient voltage spikes / lightning | Shunts excess voltage to ground using metal-oxide varistors | All connected electrical components | Instant catastrophic frying of delicate silicon chips |
| **Dust Cover** | Airborne dust accumulation & debris | Physical barrier draped over equipment when powered off | Keyboards, monitor screens, ventilation grilles | Sticky key mechanisms, clogged fans, overheating crashes |
| **$CO_2$ Fire Extinguisher** | Electrical fires | Smothers flames with cold carbon dioxide gas without conducting electricity | Human lives, building structure, electronic equipment | Fatal electric shocks from using water on electrical fires |

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1: Believing a Voltage Stabilizer and a UPS are the exact same appliance.**
  *Correction:* A **Voltage Stabilizer** smooths fluctuating voltage, but it has no internal battery; when power cuts, the computer turns off instantly! A **UPS** contains a rechargeable battery that keeps the computer running during blackouts so you can save your work.
- **Mistake 2: Thinking you can turn off a computer by simply pulling the power plug.**
  *Correction:* Pulling the power cord cuts electricity while the hard drive is writing and the operating system is updating system registries, causing corrupted files and operating system boot failure.
- **Mistake 3: Believing that eating a dry biscuit or chewing gum in the lab is harmless.**
  *Correction:* Even dry crumbs fall between keyboard keys, where they decay and attract insects (such as ants and roaches) that nest inside system units and short-circuit electronic contacts.
- **Mistake 4: Attempting to put out an electrical fire with water.**
  *Correction:* Water is a conductor of electricity. Pouring water on a burning computer will cause massive electrocution! Electrical fires must be extinguished strictly with Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$) or dry chemical powder extinguishers.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Handling an Emergency Power Outage in the Laboratory
A student is typing an assessment in the school computer lab when the national grid power suddenly cuts out. Describe the correct emergency response procedure:
1. **UPS Activation:** The connected **Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)** immediately begins beeping, indicating it is running on battery power.
2. **Immediate Save:** The student does not panic. They immediately press `Ctrl + S` or click the "Save" icon to preserve their active document.
3. **Close Applications:** The student safely closes all open software applications.
4. **Orderly Shutdown:** The student clicks the Start Menu and executes a normal system shutdown.
5. **Switch Off UPS:** Once the system unit powers down, the student switches off the UPS power switch to preserve its battery for the next class.

---

### Example 2: Cable Management & Hazard Elimination
An IT instructor inspects a computer lab and notices power extension cords stretching across the walkway between desk rows.
- **Hazard Identified:** Students walking across the aisle can trip over the loose cables, causing severe physical injury and violently yanking desktop computers off the tables onto the concrete floor.
- **Correct Solution:** Install **plastic floor cable trunking ducting** or re-route cables along perimeter walls using cable ties and under-desk cable baskets, ensuring walkways are 100% clear.

## Guardian Sync
Together with your parent or guardian, conduct an electrical safety and appliance protection audit in your home:
1. **Identify Power Protection Devices:** Check if your home television, refrigerator, or desktop computer is plugged into a voltage stabilizer or surge protector. Discuss why the unstable electrical voltage supply in Nigeria makes these devices essential.
2. **Discuss Electrical Fire Safety:** Ask your guardian: *"What should we do if an electrical appliance catches fire in our house?"* Emphasize to them that water must never be thrown on an electrical fire until the main building circuit breaker is completely switched off, and explain how a $CO_2$ fire extinguisher works.
