- Term: Force
  Definition: Any push or pull action that moves an object, stops it, or changes its uniform motion in a given direction.
- Term: Friction
  Definition: The contact force that opposes motion and tries to stop one body from sliding over another.
- Term: Gravitation
  Definition: The universal non-contact force by which the Earth (or any massive body) pulls all objects downward toward its centre.
- Term: Satellite
  Definition: Any natural body (like the Moon) or man-made machine that revolves in an orbit around a planet.

## Core Concept
### 1. Forces, Types, and Calculations (Weeks 01 & 02)
- **Definition and Unit:** Force is any push or pull measured in **Newtons (N)**, calculated as mass multiplied by acceleration ($F = ma$).
- **Classification:** 
  - *Contact Forces:* Require physical touch (e.g., push, pull, friction, tension).
  - *Non-Contact Forces (Force Fields):* Act across space without touching (e.g., gravity, magnetism, and electrostatic charges where unlike charges attract and like charges repel).
- **Gravitational Force & Weight:** Weight is the downward pull of gravity ($F = mg$), measured with a spring balance using $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ or $10\text{ m/s}^2$.
- **Work Done Against Gravity:** Calculated in Joules ($\text{J}$) using $W = mgh$ (when mass, gravity, and height are given) or $W = Fh$ (when force in Newtons is already given).

### 2. Friction and Its Control (Week 03)
- **Role of Friction:** Friction opposes motion. It is essential for walking, running, gripping roads with tyres, sharpening tools, and striking matches.
- **Disadvantages:** Causes wear and tear, noise, excessive heat, and wasted energy in machines.
- **Control Methods:** Reduced using lubricants (oil and grease), ball/roller bearings, polishing, and streamlining.

### 3. Gravitation and Weightlessness (Week 04)
- **Universal Pull:** Gravity keeps objects grounded, holds the Moon in orbit around the Earth, and keeps planets revolving around the Sun.
- **Weightlessness:** The sensation of not feeling gravity's pull in space orbit.
- **Moon Gravity:** An object's weight on the Moon is exactly one-sixth ($\frac{1}{6}$) of its weight on Earth.

### 4. The Earth in Space & Eclipses (Week 06)
- **Solar System:** Consists of the Sun, eight planets (separated by the asteroid belt into inner planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars; and outer planets: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune), moons, comets, and asteroids.
- **Movements:** 
  - *Rotation:* Earth spinning on its axis every 24 hours causes day and night.
  - *Revolution:* Earth orbiting the Sun every 365 days causes the seasons.
- **Eclipses:** Solar eclipse (`Sun -> Moon -> Earth`) occurs when the Moon blocks the Sun; Lunar eclipse (`Sun -> Earth -> Moon`) occurs when the Earth blocks the Moon.

### 5. Space Travel and Satellites (Weeks 07 & 08)
- **Space Exploration:** Highlighted by milestones such as Yuri Gagarin (first in space, 1961) and Neil Armstrong (first on the Moon, 1969) using massive Saturn V rockets. Benefits include medical research, weather tracking, and GPS, while hazards include bone calcium loss and radiation.
- **Satellites:** Natural (the Moon) vs. Artificial (Sputnik 1 in 1957; Nigerian milestones NigComSat-1 and Sat-2). Artificial types include communications, weather, navigation (GPS), and surveillance satellites, alongside the International Space Station (ISS).

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Believing that weight and mass are the exact same physical quantity.
  Fact: Mass is the amount of matter measured in kilograms ($\text{kg}$), whereas weight is the downward gravitational pull measured in Newtons ($\text{N}$).
- Mistake: Thinking that friction has no useful purpose and should always be eliminated.
  Fact: Without friction, humans could not walk, cars could not move or stop, and screws would slip out of walls.
- Mistake: Confusing the order of bodies during eclipses.
  Fact: In a solar eclipse, the **M**oon is in the middle (`Sun–Moon–Earth`); in a lunar eclipse, the **E**arth is in the middle (`Sun–Earth–Moon`).

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Comprehensive Force and Work Calculation
Context: A factory worker lifts a heavy iron crate of mass $20\text{ kg}$ straight up through a vertical height of $4\text{ metres}$. Take gravity $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$.
Problem: Calculate (i) the weight of the crate in Newtons and (ii) the total work done against gravity.
Fix: 
- (i) Weight ($F = mg$): $20\text{ kg} \times 10\text{ m/s}^2 = 200\text{ Newtons (N)}$.
- (ii) Work Done ($W = mgh$): $20\text{ kg} \times 10\text{ m/s}^2 \times 4\text{ m} = 800\text{ Joules (J)}$.

### Example B: Determining Weight on the Moon
Context: A space instrument package weighs $300\text{ N}$ when tested in a laboratory on Earth.
Problem: What will be the weight of this exact instrument package when it lands on the Moon?
Fix: The weight of any object on the Moon is one-sixth ($\frac{1}{6}$) of its weight on Earth. Divide the Earth weight by $6$:
$$\text{Weight on Moon} = \frac{300\text{ N}}{6} = 50\text{ N}$$

### Example C: Identifying Eclipse Alignments
Context: During a school quiz, a student is asked which heavenly body blocks sunlight during a total lunar eclipse.
Problem: State the correct alignment of bodies during a lunar eclipse.
Fix: A lunar eclipse occurs when the **Earth** comes directly between the Sun and the Moon (`Sun -> Earth -> Moon`), casting Earth's shadow across the lunar surface.

A) Gravitational force (correct)
B) Pushing a wheelbarrow
C) Friction between shoe soles and ground
D) Tension in a stretched rope
Feedback: Gravity is a force field that acts across space without physical contact.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the work done when an object of mass $5\text{ kg}$ is lifted through a height of $2\text{ metres}$? (Take $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$)

B) $10\text{ Joules}$
C) $50\text{ Joules}$
D) $25\text{ Joules}$
