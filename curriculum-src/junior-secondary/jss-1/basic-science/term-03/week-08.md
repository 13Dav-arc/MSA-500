## Hook
Have you ever wondered how live television broadcasts of international football matches reach your home screen from across the globe in a fraction of a second, or how your phone's GPS instantly finds your exact location on a digital map? These technological wonders are made possible by satellites spinning silently thousands of miles above our heads. From the natural Moon that lights up our night sky to sophisticated man-made machines, satellites help us communicate, navigate, and study our planet.

## Key Terms
- Term: Satellite
  Definition: Any natural or artificial body that revolves around a planet or larger body in space.
- Term: Natural Satellite
  Definition: A celestial body created by nature, such as the Moon, that orbits a planet.
- Term: Artificial Satellite
  Definition: A man-made machine launched by rockets into space to orbit the Earth and perform specific tasks like communication and research.
- Term: Communications Satellite
  Definition: A satellite placed in orbit to receive signals from Earth and transmit them back to other locations for phone calls, television, and internet.
- Term: Weather Satellite
  Definition: A satellite used to monitor atmospheric conditions, track storms, and help meteorologists forecast weather.

## Core Concept
### Meaning of a Satellite
A **satellite** is any body that revolves around the Earth or another planet. Satellites are grouped into two major types:
1. **Natural Satellites:** Bodies created by nature that orbit planets. For example, the Earth has one natural satellite, which is the **Moon**.
2. **Artificial Satellites:** Man-made devices launched into space by high-powered rockets to carry out scientific experiments, collect information, and monitor the Earth.
   - The first artificial satellite ever launched into space was **Sputnik 1**, sent into orbit by the Soviet Union in **1957**, which orbited Earth once every 90 minutes.
   - Nigeria launched its first satellite, **NigComSat-1**, on September 27, 2003, followed by its communication satellite **Sat-2** on May 13, 2007.

### Types and Uses of Satellites
Artificial satellites are categorized based on their primary functions and tasks:
1. **Communications Satellites:** They receive signals from Earth (such as telephone calls, faxes, television broadcasts, and e-mail messages) and beam them back down to different locations across long distances quickly and cheaply.
2. **Weather Satellites:** They take photographs of cloud patterns, monitor atmospheric moisture, and track severe storms like hurricanes (such as Hurricane Mitch in 1998) to help meteorologists forecast weather.
3. **Navigation Satellites (GPS):** Systems like the **Global Positioning System (GPS)** rely on a network of orbiting satellites that allow any receiver device to pinpoint its exact geographical location anywhere on the planet.
4. **Surveillance (Reconnaissance) Satellites:** Used to monitor military activities and detect crimes or security threats within society.
5. **Scientific Satellites & Space Stations:** Space-based laboratories used to observe the Earth and distant planets and conduct long-term research. A prime example is the **International Space Station (ISS)**, a joint project started in 1998 involving 16 nations that allows humans to live and work in space for long periods.

### Summary of Satellite Uses
* For gathering information and photography of outer space.
* For Earth mapping and finding the location of mineral resources underground.
* For global communication, navigation, and weather monitoring.

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Believing that the Earth has many natural moons orbiting it just like Jupiter or Saturn.
  Fact: Planet Earth has only one natural satellite, which is the Moon.
- Mistake: Thinking that artificial satellites stay completely motionless in one fixed spot in the sky.
  Fact: Artificial satellites travel at very high speeds in continuous orbits around the Earth (for example, Sputnik 1 orbited Earth once every 90 minutes).

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Making an International Phone Call
Context: A student in Nigeria makes a clear video phone call to a relative living across the ocean.
Problem: How does the voice and video signal travel across thousands of miles of ocean so quickly?
Fix: The signal is beamed upward from a transmission tower on Earth to an orbiting **communications satellite**, which instantly receives the signal and bounces it down to the receiving phone on the other side of the world.

### Example B: Finding a Location with GPS
Context: A delivery driver uses a smartphone map app to navigate to a new address in an unfamiliar city.
Problem: How does the app know the exact street and building where the car is parked?
Fix: The phone connects to a network of orbiting **navigation satellites (GPS)** that triangulate the device's exact latitude, longitude, and position on Earth.

### Example C: Tracking a Approaching Hurricane
Context: Weather forecasters notice swirling storm clouds over the ocean that could threaten coastal towns.
Problem: How do meteorologists monitor and track the path of a dangerous storm days before it arrives?
Fix: **Weather satellites** orbiting in space capture continuous high-resolution photographs of cloud formations and storm tracks, feeding live data to meteorological stations on Earth.
