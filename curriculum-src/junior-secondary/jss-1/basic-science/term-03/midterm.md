## Quiz
### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: In basic science, what is the simplest and most accurate definition of a force?
A) A push or pull exerted on an object (correct)
B) The speed at which an object travels in a circle
C) The amount of heat energy stored inside matter
D) The chemical breakdown of living cells
Feedback: A force is defined scientifically as a push or a pull exerted upon an object resulting from its interaction with another object.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the standard International System of Units (SI unit) used to measure force?
A) Newton (N) (correct)
B) Joule (J)
C) Kilogram (kg)
D) Metre per second (m/s)
Feedback: Force is measured in Newtons (symbol: N), named after Sir Isaac Newton, whereas energy is measured in Joules and mass in kilograms.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Which scientific instrument is specifically designed to measure the magnitude of a pulling force or the weight of an object?
A) Spring balance (correct)
B) Beam balance
C) Thermometer
D) Rain gauge
Feedback: A spring balance measures force and weight in Newtons by utilizing the extension of an internal coiled spring under load.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following is an example of a contact force?
A) Frictional force between car tyres and a tarred road (correct)
B) Gravitational attraction between Earth and the Moon
C) Magnetic attraction between a bar magnet and iron filings
D) Electrostatic force between a charged plastic comb and paper pieces
Feedback: Frictional force requires physical surface contact between the two interacting bodies, making it a contact force.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: What type of force is responsible for causing a ripe mango to detach from a tree and fall downward to the ground?
A) Gravitational force (correct)
B) Magnetic force
C) Electrostatic force
D) Tensional force
Feedback: Gravitational force is the non-contact attractive pull that Earth exerts on all objects near its surface, pulling them toward its center.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: Why is magnetic force classified as a non-contact or field force?
A) It can attract or repel magnetic materials across space without physical contact (correct)
B) It only acts when two metal objects are firmly welded together
C) It requires electrical wires to touch the metal surface directly
D) It only operates under water and inside heavy vacuum tanks
Feedback: Magnetic forces act through magnetic fields across space without needing direct physical touch between the magnet and magnetic substances.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the fundamental scientific distinction between the mass and the weight of a body?
A) Mass is the quantity of matter in an object, while weight is the gravitational pull acting on that mass (correct)
B) Mass is measured in Newtons, while weight is measured in kilograms
C) Mass changes when moving to the Moon, while weight stays completely constant
D) Mass requires a spring balance, while weight requires a clinical thermometer
Feedback: Mass is the invariant amount of matter in a body (measured in kg), whereas weight is the downward gravitational force acting on that mass (W = mg, measured in N).

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: If an astronaut has a mass of 60 kg on Earth, what will their mass be on the surface of the Moon where gravity is only one-sixth of Earth's gravity?
A) Exactly 60 kg (correct)
B) 10 kg
C) 360 kg
D) 0 kg
Feedback: Mass is an intrinsic property representing the quantity of matter in an object and remains exactly constant (60 kg) regardless of location or gravity.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: Frictional force always acts in which direction relative to the movement of an object?
A) In the direction opposite to the motion (correct)
B) In the exact same direction as the motion
C) Vertically upwards towards the clouds
D) Perpendicularly at right angles towards the right
Feedback: Friction is an opposing resistive force that acts parallel to the contacting surfaces in the direction opposite to the relative motion.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following situations demonstrates a practical advantage of friction in daily life?
A) Allowing vehicle tyres to grip the road and stop safely when brakes are applied (correct)
B) Causing the soles of school shoes to gradually wear out and tear
C) Producing unwanted heat that causes machine engines to overheat
D) Slowing down a bicycle when riding on a flat smooth road
Feedback: Friction provides the necessary grip that enables walking without slipping and allows vehicle brake pads to grip drums/discs to stop safely.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: Which technique is commonly used by mechanical engineers to reduce unwanted friction between moving parts of an engine?
A) Applying lubricating oil or grease between metal surfaces (correct)
B) Pouring dry sand and gravel inside the gearbox
C) Roughening the smooth metal surfaces with a steel file
D) Increasing the surface weight and pressing parts tighter
Feedback: Lubricants like oil and grease create a smooth fluid film between contacting surfaces, preventing direct metal-on-metal rubbing and reducing friction.

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: Why are fast-moving vehicles such as sports cars, high-speed trains, and aeroplanes built with streamlined shapes?
A) To reduce air resistance and drag friction as they move through air (correct)
B) To make them heavier so they stay firmly pressed to the ground
C) To allow water to enter the engine compartments easily
D) To increase surface friction and stop them from moving too fast
Feedback: Streamlining provides a curved, tapered contour that allows air to flow smoothly around the vehicle, reducing air resistance (drag friction).

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: When a plastic ruler is rubbed vigorously against dry hair, it can attract tiny bits of dry paper without touching them initially. This force is called:
A) Electrostatic force (correct)
B) Gravitational force
C) Nuclear force
D) Tensional force
Feedback: Rubbing transfers electric charges to the plastic ruler, creating an electrostatic field that attracts neutral bits of paper by electrostatic induction.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: What type of force is present in a stretched catapult rubber or a tightened guitar string?
A) Tension force (correct)
B) Gravitational force
C) Magnetic force
D) Chemical force
Feedback: Tension is a contact pulling force transmitted along a string, rope, cable, or elastic band when it is pulled tight by forces acting from opposite ends.

### Q15 [true-false]
Statement: A force acting on a stationary object can cause it to start moving, accelerate, or change its physical shape.
Answer: true
Feedback: Applying an external unbalanced force can set a stationary body into motion, alter its velocity, change its direction, or deform its shape.

### Q16 [true-false]
Statement: Friction between the moving gears of a machine is always useful and never causes energy loss or mechanical wear.
Answer: false
Feedback: Friction in machines causes mechanical wear and tear, produces excess waste heat, and reduces mechanical efficiency, which is why lubrication is required.

### Q17 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO examples of non-contact (field) forces:
Correct: Gravitational force, Magnetic force
Incorrect: Frictional force, Tension in a rope
Feedback: Gravitational and magnetic forces act over a distance through field interactions without physical contact; friction and tension require direct physical contact.

### Q18 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO effective methods used to reduce friction between moving mechanical surfaces:
Correct: Applying engine oil or grease (lubrication), Installing ball bearings between rotating shafts
Incorrect: Roughening surfaces with sandpaper, Increasing the downward load and surface roughness
Feedback: Lubrication and ball bearings significantly minimize friction by replacing sliding contact with rolling or fluid layers; roughening surfaces increases friction.

### Q19 [matching]
Stem: Match each type of force with its correct scientific description:
Pairs:
- Gravitational Force -> Downward attraction exerted by Earth on masses
- Frictional Force -> Contact force that opposes motion between surfaces
- Electrostatic Force -> Force between stationary electric charges
Feedback: Gravity pulls masses downward, friction resists sliding motion between contacting bodies, and electrostatic forces act between electric charges.

### Q20 [matching]
Stem: Match each physical quantity with its standard SI unit of measurement:
Pairs:
- Force -> Newton (N)
- Mass -> Kilogram (kg)
- Work Done -> Joule (J)
Feedback: Force is measured in Newtons (N), mass in kilograms (kg), and work done or energy in Joules (J).
