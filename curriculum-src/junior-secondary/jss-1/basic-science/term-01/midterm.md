## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: Sanitation is best defined in environmental science as:
A) The practice of maintaining clean surroundings and disposing of waste safely to prevent disease (correct)
B) The study of heavenly bodies and planetary movement in space
C) The process of consuming a diet composed entirely of fresh fruits
D) The chemical extraction of crude petroleum from offshore reserves
Feedback: Sanitation refers specifically to maintaining hygienic living environments and safe waste containment to break disease transmission chains.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following is considered a microscopic pathogen capable of spreading infectious waterborne disease?
A) Bacteria such as Vibrio cholerae (correct)
B) Adult earthworms living in garden topsoil
C) Dried cowpea beans stored in kitchen sacks
D) Fluted pumpkin leaves growing on farm trellises
Feedback: Pathogens are microscopic agents—such as bacteria, viruses, and protozoa—that cause infectious disease when introduced into food or water supplies.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Which serious tropical illness is transmitted to humans by mosquitoes that breed in stagnant water pools?
A) Malaria (correct)
B) Scurvy
C) Kwashiorkor
D) Rickets
Feedback: Female Anopheles mosquitoes lay eggs in standing, unmoving water; adult mosquitoes then transmit the Plasmodium parasite causing malaria.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: Why should domestic refuse (solid waste) always be stored in covered containers rather than open heaps?
A) To prevent houseflies, rodents, and pests from carrying germs to food (correct)
B) To accelerate the evaporation of rainfall into the atmosphere
C) To allow direct sunlight to chemically convert waste into cooking oil
D) To make the waste heavier before community trucks arrive
Feedback: Covered bins physically block disease vectors like flies and rats from accessing decomposing waste and transferring pathogens onto human food.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: To protect a household well or borehole from fecal contamination, where should a pit latrine be sited?
A) At a safe distance away and downhill from the water source (correct)
B) Immediately adjacent to the wellhead casing
C) Inside the main kitchen food preparation area
D) Directly upstream over the subterranean groundwater entry point
Feedback: Siting latrines at a safe distance away and downhill prevents fecal pathogens and leachate from percolating through porous soil into drinking aquifers.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: Why is it dangerous to conclude that water is completely safe to drink merely because it looks clear and odourless?
A) Microscopic pathogens and dissolved chemical toxins are invisible to the naked human eye (correct)
B) Clear water is always chemically deficient in dietary carbohydrates
C) Pure water always has a dark brown or reddish colour
D) Odourless water cannot dissolve table salt or sugar
Feedback: Disease-causing microbes like bacteria and protozoan cysts cannot be seen without a microscope; visual clarity is never a guarantee of biological purity.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the most hygienic method for drawing drinking water from a wide-mouthed household storage container?
A) Using a clean, dedicated long-handled cup or ladle without dipping fingers (correct)
B) Dipping bare hands and unwashed tumblers directly into the water
C) Siphoning the water through an unwashed rubber engine hose
D) Submerging an open plastic bowl that sits on the kitchen floor
Feedback: A dedicated long-handled ladle allows water to be drawn without introducing bacteria from bare hands or dirty cup exteriors into the clean reservoir.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: How do houseflies mechanically transmit pathogenic bacteria from open waste dumps to human food?
A) Pathogens stick to their hairy legs and mouthparts and detach onto uncovered food (correct)
B) Flies inject bacteria into food using a venomous abdominal stinger
C) Flies convert kitchen food into starchy carbohydrates through photosynthesis
D) Flies create high temperatures that stimulate rapid bacterial synthesis
Feedback: Houseflies land on fecal matter and rotting garbage, picking up pathogens on their sticky feet and proboscis, and subsequently contaminate exposed meals.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: In human biology, the term "nutrition" refers to:
A) The intake and utilization of food nutrients by the body for growth, energy, and repair (correct)
B) The mechanical sweeping of school grounds and drainage channels
C) The treatment of communal borehole water with chemical coagulants
D) The physical exercises performed during morning sports parades
Feedback: Nutrition is the physiological process of taking in, digesting, and assimilating dietary nutrients needed for metabolic function and cellular maintenance.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: A balanced diet is scientifically defined as a daily dietary intake that:
A) Contains all six classes of food in their correct metabolic proportions (correct)
B) Consists exclusively of starchy tubers to provide maximum physical fullness
C) Eliminates all dietary fats and oils to prevent body weight gain
D) Provides equal weights of all food substances regardless of caloric density
Feedback: Nutritional balance requires all six nutrient classes (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water) in proper proportion to support physiological needs.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the primary biological role of dietary carbohydrates in the human body?
A) Providing glucose fuel for cellular respiration and immediate physical energy (correct)
B) Hardening skeletal bones and preventing joint dislocations
C) Producing antibodies to destroy bloodstream viruses
D) Lubricating the surface of internal digestive organs
Feedback: Carbohydrates are catabolized into glucose, which cells oxidize during respiration to yield ATP energy for daily muscular and mental activity.

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: Which group of foods consists entirely of rich Nigerian carbohydrate sources?
A) Cassava garri, white yam, maize, and millet (correct)
B) Dried crayfish, cowpea beans, and boiled eggs
C) Pure red palm oil, groundnut oil, and shea butter
D) Fluted pumpkin leaves (ugwu), oranges, and mangoes
Feedback: Garri, yam, maize, and millet are starchy staples loaded with complex carbohydrates that serve as primary dietary energy sources.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: Why are dietary proteins considered non-negotiable for growing secondary school students?
A) They provide amino acid building blocks for muscle growth, tissue repair, and enzyme synthesis (correct)
B) They are the body's fastest burning source of immediate cellular energy
C) They dissolve calcium carbonate within the teeth to prevent dental plaque
D) They act as the primary biological solvent for urinary excretion
Feedback: Proteins break down into amino acids, which developing bodies require to construct new muscle cells, synthesize enzymes, and repair damaged tissues.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following is an affordable, accessible Nigerian food staple that provides concentrated dietary protein?
A) Cowpeas (beans) (correct)
B) Polished white cassava flour (fufu)
C) Refined granulated white cane sugar
D) Pure palm oil shortening
Feedback: Cowpeas (black-eyed beans or brown beans) are an indigenous legume delivering abundant vegetable protein, dietary fiber, and essential minerals.

### Q15 [single-choice]
Stem: What major nutritional benefit is provided by dietary fats and oils in moderation?
A) They supply dense caloric energy reserves and facilitate the absorption of vitamins A, D, E, and K (correct)
B) They replace water as the primary solvent in the human bloodstream
C) They eliminate the body's requirement for protein synthesis
D) They directly lower metabolic heart rate during vigorous athletic training
Feedback: Lipids deliver twice the caloric density of carbohydrates per gram, cushion vital organs, and are required to dissolve and absorb fat-soluble vitamins.

### Q16 [single-choice]
Stem: In traditional Nigerian cooking, pure unadulterated red palm oil is an outstanding natural source of:
A) Beta-carotene (Provitamin A) (correct)
B) Complete animal protein
C) Insoluble mineral iron shavings
D) Pure crystalline vitamin C
Feedback: The vibrant orange-red hue of unbleached palm oil comes from beta-carotene, an antioxidant precursor that human liver tissue converts into active Vitamin A.

### Q17 [single-choice]
Stem: A young child presenting with a swollen fluid-filled abdomen (edema), skin lesions, and reddish-brown thinning hair is suffering from:
A) Kwashiorkor (severe protein deficiency) (correct)
B) Scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency)
C) Rickets (Vitamin D deficiency)
D) Goitre (Iodine deficiency)
Feedback: Kwashiorkor is a clinical syndrome caused by severe dietary protein deficiency, typically occurring when infants are weaned onto carbohydrate-only diets.

### Q18 [single-choice]
Stem: Swollen, spontaneously bleeding gums and poor wound healing are classic diagnostic symptoms of:
A) Scurvy, caused by a prolonged deficiency of Vitamin C (correct)
B) Kwashiorkor, caused by an acute absence of dietary fats
C) Anemia, caused by drinking unboiled borehole water
D) Night blindness, caused by consuming excessive dietary iron
Feedback: Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C) is essential for collagen synthesis and blood vessel integrity; deficiency leads to weakened capillaries and bleeding gums (scurvy).

### Q19 [single-choice]
Stem: A student who can see normally in bright sunshine but experiences extreme difficulty navigating in dim dusk light is likely deficient in:
A) Vitamin A (correct)
B) Vitamin C
C) Vitamin K
D) Vitamin B12
Feedback: Vitamin A is a core component of rhodopsin, the retinal photo-pigment that enables human vision in low-light and nighttime conditions.

### Q20 [single-choice]
Stem: Nutritional anemia in school-age students is most commonly caused by an inadequate dietary intake of:
A) Iron (correct)
B) Sodium
C) Iodine
D) Starch
Feedback: Iron is the central mineral in hemoglobin; insufficient iron impairs hemoglobin synthesis, decreasing blood oxygen delivery and causing chronic exhaustion.

### Q21 [true-false]
Statement: Water drawn from a borehole that looks completely transparent to the eye is always free of harmful disease-causing pathogens.
Answer: false
Feedback: Many pathogenic bacteria, viruses, and protozoan parasites are microscopic and completely colourless; clarity does not establish biological safety.

### Q22 [true-false]
Statement: Clearing silt and domestic rubbish from roadside gutters every week disrupts the mosquito life cycle by eliminating stagnant breeding pools.
Answer: true
Feedback: Mosquito eggs and larvae require motionless, stagnant water to develop into adult biting vectors; draining blockages interrupts this reproductive cycle.

### Q23 [true-false]
Statement: Eating large portions of starchy cassava products until the stomach feels completely full guarantees that all nutritional needs have been satisfied.
Answer: false
Feedback: Physical fullness (satiety) is easily achieved through bulky carbohydrates alone, whereas balanced nutrition requires adequate proteins, lipids, vitamins, and minerals.

### Q24 [true-false]
Statement: Fat-soluble vitamins, including Vitamins A, D, E, and K, require the presence of dietary lipids in the digestive tract to be properly absorbed.
Answer: true
Feedback: Fat-soluble vitamins dissolve only in lipids and micellar structures; without dietary fats in meals, the intestinal lining cannot absorb them efficiently.

### Q25 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO of the four recognized core methods of environmental sanitation:
Correct: Safe sewage disposal, Proper waste (refuse) disposal
Incorrect: Dumping domestic rubbish into flowing street drains during rain, Siting pit latrines directly uphill from household wells
Feedback: The four approved sanitation methods are proper refuse disposal, safe sewage disposal, regular cleaning of shared spaces, and safe water handling.

### Q26 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO biological classes of food that serve primarily as caloric energy sources in the human body:
Correct: Carbohydrates, Fats and oils
Incorrect: Mineral salts, Dietary water
Feedback: Carbohydrates provide immediate glucose fuel, while fats and oils provide dense stored energy; minerals and water perform structural and metabolic regulation.

### Q27 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO medical conditions that are directly classified as dietary deficiency disorders:
Correct: Kwashiorkor, Scurvy
Incorrect: Malaria, Cholera
Feedback: Kwashiorkor (protein deficiency) and Scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency) are nutritional disorders, whereas Malaria and Cholera are caused by infectious parasites and bacteria.

### Q28 [matching]
Stem: Match each environmental sanitation hazard with its correct scientific intervention:
Pairs:
- Silt-blocked compound gutter -> Clear sediment blockage to restore continuous water flow
- Refuse heap 5 metres from a well -> Relocate waste disposal site far away from the water table
- Open drinking water drum in kitchen -> Seal inside a covered container and draw with a ladle
Feedback: Clearing ditches prevents mosquito breeding, relocating waste shields groundwater from leachate, and covered drums prevent fecal and dust contamination.

### Q29 [matching]
Stem: Match each nutrient class with its primary physiological function in the human body:
Pairs:
- Carbohydrates -> Supplying glucose fuel for immediate cellular respiration
- Proteins -> Building muscle cells, repairing tissues, and synthesizing enzymes
- Mineral Salts -> Supporting bone mineralization and synthesizing blood hemoglobin
Feedback: Carbohydrates fuel metabolism, proteins provide structural tissue building blocks, and minerals perform essential catalytic and structural roles.

### Q30 [matching]
Stem: Match each clinical deficiency condition with the missing dietary nutrient responsible for its onset:
Pairs:
- Kwashiorkor -> Dietary Protein
- Night Blindness -> Vitamin A
- Nutritional Anemia -> Iron
Feedback: Kwashiorkor stems from missing protein, night blindness from lacking Vitamin A, and anemia from insufficient iron required for hemoglobin.
