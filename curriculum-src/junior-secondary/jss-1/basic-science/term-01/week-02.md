## Hook
Have you ever wondered why eating only a large mound of plain garri leaves you feeling exhausted and unable to concentrate in class just two hours later? Your body does not simply need belly-filling bulk—it requires specific biological building blocks to function. Understanding the six food classes will allow you to build an energizing, balanced diet using affordable local foods.

## Key Terms
- Term: Nutrition
  Definition: The biological process by which living organisms take in and utilize food nutrients for growth, energy, and cellular repair.
- Term: Balanced Diet
  Definition: A meal containing all six classes of food in the correct proportions required for daily metabolic needs.
- Term: Malnutrition
  Definition: A pathological physical condition resulting from an insufficient, excessive, or improperly balanced nutrient intake.
- Term: Deficiency Disease
  Definition: A specific illness caused by the prolonged lack of an essential dietary nutrient, such as Kwashiorkor or Scurvy.

## Core Concept
### The Six Classes of Food & Nigerian Sources
Every food item grown or consumed across Nigeria belongs to one or more of six vital chemical classes:
1. **Carbohydrates:** The primary fuel source for cellular respiration, physical labor, and brain function. *Local Sources:* Cassava (garri, fufu), yam, maize, millet, rice, sweet potatoes.
2. **Proteins:** Nitrogenous compounds essential for building new muscle cells, synthesizing metabolic enzymes, and repairing damaged tissues. *Local Sources:* Cowpeas (beans), soybean, fish, crayfish, eggs, groundnuts.
3. **Fats and Oils:** Dense caloric reserves providing twice the energy of carbohydrates, cushioning internal organs, and dissolving fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). *Local Sources:* Red palm oil, groundnut oil, melon seeds (egusi).
4. **Vitamins:** Organic protective compounds required in minute quantities to regulate biological enzymes and resist infectious disease. *Local Sources:* Oranges, mangoes, carrots, pawpaw, green leafy vegetables (ugwu, waterleaf).
5. **Minerals:** Inorganic elements necessary for structural bone density, blood hemoglobin production, and electrolyte balance. *Local Sources:* Ground crayfish, bone meal, iodized salt, dark green leaves.
6. **Water:** The universal solvent making up 60% to 70% of human body weight, responsible for transporting nutrients, removing waste, and regulating temperature.

### Malnutrition: Under-Nutrition vs. Over-Nutrition
Malnutrition includes both under-nutrition (deficiency diseases) and over-nutrition (excessive caloric intake leading to childhood obesity and vascular strain). When children consume almost purely carbohydrates without adequate protein, Kwashiorkor develops. When they face total caloric and protein starvation, Marasmus occurs.

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Deficiency Disorders & Clinical Signs
- Label: Protein Deficiency
  Title: Kwashiorkor
  Body: Swollen fluid-filled abdomen (edema), cracked skin, thinning reddish-brown hair, and severe physical growth retardation.
- Label: Vitamin C Deficiency
  Title: Scurvy
  Body: Swollen and bleeding gums, loose teeth, poor skin wound healing, and fragile blood capillaries.
- Label: Vitamin A Deficiency
  Title: Night Blindness
  Body: Failure of light-sensitive pigments in the retina resulting in an inability to see clearly in low-light environments.
- Label: Iron Deficiency
  Title: Anemia
  Body: Reduced blood hemoglobin leading to pale inner eyelids, pale nail beds, chronic exhaustion, and shortness of breath.

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Believing that eating until your stomach is completely full guarantees that your body has received a balanced diet.
  Fact: Physical fullness is usually achieved by carbohydrate volume alone; nutritional balance requires specific distributions of proteins, lipids, vitamins, and minerals.
- Mistake: Assuming red palm oil is purely fattening and should be eliminated from a healthy diet.
  Fact: Unadulterated Nigerian palm oil is rich in beta-carotene (provitamin A), which protects eyesight and supports the immune system.

## Worked Examples
### Example A: The Carbohydrate Imbalance Trap
Context: A student eats white bread for breakfast, white rice for lunch, and a large mound of garri for dinner.
Problem: The student's diet is over 85% carbohydrate with virtually zero protein, leading to constant afternoon fatigue and impaired mental concentration.
Fix: Replace half the rice portion with steamed cowpea beans or fish, and add crayfish powder and ugwu leaves to the evening soup.

### Example B: Bleeding Gums in Boarding School
Context: A student living in a hostel consumes only dry biscuits and instant noodles for several weeks, refusing fresh vegetables.
Problem: Complete lack of ascorbic acid (Vitamin C), leading to impaired collagen production and early scurvy characterized by bleeding gums.
Fix: Introduce fresh local citrus fruits like oranges, grapefruits, or raw garden eggs into the student's daily food supply.

### Example C: Low-Cost Correction of Iron Deficiency
Context: A student frequently experiences dizziness and pale nail beds during physical education classes.
Problem: Inadequate dietary iron intake, reducing hemoglobin levels and the blood's capacity to transport oxygen.
Fix: Add ground dried crayfish, cowpeas, and dark green leafy vegetables to weekly family stews.

Feedback: A balanced diet supplies all six nutrient classes in correct proportion for healthy metabolism.

### Q2 [single-choice]

A) Supplying immediate cellular fuel and energy for daily physical activities (correct)
B) Hardening skeletal bones and preventing joint fractures
C) Synthesizing antibodies to neutralize blood pathogens
D) Insulating deep visceral organs against cold weather
Feedback: Carbohydrates are broken down into glucose to supply metabolic energy for daily physical and mental exertion.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following groups consists exclusively of rich, accessible Nigerian protein sources?
A) Cowpeas (beans), dried crayfish, and boiled eggs (correct)
B) Cassava fufu, sweet potatoes, and polished white rice
C) Pure red palm oil, shea butter, and groundnut oil
D) Fresh oranges, mango slices, and ripe bananas
Feedback: Beans, crayfish, and eggs provide essential amino acids needed to build and repair body tissues.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: Which dietary deficiency directly causes Kwashiorkor in growing children?
A) A prolonged, severe lack of biological protein (correct)
B) Insufficient consumption of starchy carbohydrates
C) Complete absence of potable drinking water
D) Excessive consumption of fresh citrus juice
Feedback: Kwashiorkor is triggered by severe protein deficiency, typically when young children are weaned on high-carbohydrate pap alone.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: A child who struggles to navigate or see clearly in dim evening light is likely suffering from a deficiency of:
A) Vitamin A (correct)
B) Vitamin C
C) Vitamin K
D) Vitamin B12
Feedback: Vitamin A is needed to synthesize the retinal pigments that allow vision in low-light environments.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: Which clinical symptom is a primary diagnostic indicator of scurvy?
A) Swollen, bleeding gums and delayed wound closure (correct)
B) Bowed leg bones and malformed joints
C) Enlargement of the thyroid gland in the neck
D) Extreme accumulation of adipose fat deposits
Feedback: Scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency) weakens blood capillaries and connective tissues, causing gums to bleed spontaneously.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: In traditional Nigerian cooking, pure red palm oil is a vital natural source of:
A) Beta-carotene (Provitamin A) (correct)
B) Pure structural protein
C) Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C)
D) Soluble calcium carbonate
Feedback: Red palm oil gets its orange-red pigment from beta-carotene, which the human liver converts into active Vitamin A.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: Why is dietary iron an essential nutrient for the human bloodstream?
A) It synthesizes hemoglobin to transport oxygen to working body tissues (correct)
B) It lubricates the surface of digestive intestines
C) It digests complex starch fibers into simple sugars
D) It converts excess water into sweat inside the skin
Feedback: Iron is the core mineral in hemoglobin; lack of iron causes anemia, fatigue, and tissue oxygen starvation.

### Q9 [single-choice]

A) 60% to 70% (correct)
B) 10% to 20%
C) 30% to 40%
D) 90% to 95%
Feedback: Water makes up roughly two-thirds (60%–70%) of human body mass, serving as the biological solvent for all metabolic reactions.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: Which dietary mineral must be included in table salt to prevent goitre (swelling of the neck)?
A) Iodine (correct)
B) Sodium
C) Zinc
D) Magnesium
Feedback: Iodine is required by the thyroid gland to produce hormones that regulate metabolic rate; deficiency causes goitre.

### Q11 [single-choice]

Feedback: While Kwashiorkor is specific to protein deficiency, Marasmus is general wasting caused by severe total calorie and protein deprivation.

A) Vitamin D and Calcium (correct)

### Q13 [single-choice]

A) Dried ground crayfish (correct)

### Q14 [single-choice]

### Q15 [true-false]
Statement: Eating until your stomach is completely filled ensures that you have satisfied your daily requirement for a balanced diet.
Answer: false
Feedback: Fullness is usually just carbohydrate volume, not balance.

### Q16 [true-false]
Statement: Because Vitamin C is water-soluble, excess amounts are excreted and the body cannot store it, meaning it must be consumed regularly.
Answer: true
Feedback: Water-soluble vitamins cannot be accumulated in adipose tissue and must be replenished consistently through diet.
