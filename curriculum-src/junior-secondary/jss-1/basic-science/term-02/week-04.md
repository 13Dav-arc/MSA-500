## Hook
Have you ever felt completely tired and weak after running around during sports day, until you rested and ate a warm plate of rice and beans? Almost immediately, you felt your strength return and were ready to jump back into action. That strength is energy at work! Without energy, nothing moves, grows, lights up, or makes sound. From the beating of your heart to the roaring generators and bright streetlights in our towns, energy powers our entire world.

## Key Terms
- Term: Energy
  Definition: The ability or capacity of a body to do physical work or exert a force.
- Term: Joule (J)
  Definition: The standard scientific unit used to measure energy and work done.
- Term: Kinetic Energy
  Definition: The form of mechanical energy possessed by an object because of its motion.
- Term: Potential Energy
  Definition: The stored energy possessed by an object because of its position, height, or stretched shape.
- Term: Renewable Energy
  Definition: Energy obtained from natural sources that can never run out and are replaced naturally over time.

## Core Concept
### What is Energy and How Do We Measure It?
Energy is defined as the capacity or ability to do work. In science, whenever an object is lifted, pushed, or moved from one point to another, work is done, and energy is used up. 
- The standard scientific unit of energy is the **Joule**, abbreviated with a capital letter **J**. 
- A larger unit called the **Kilojoule (kJ)** is equal to 1,000 Joules and is often used to measure the energy stored in the food we eat.

### Forms of Energy
Energy exists in many different forms all around us:
1. **Mechanical Energy:** The total energy of an object related to its motion and position. It is divided into two types:
   - **Kinetic Energy (KE):** Energy of movement. Anything that is moving has kinetic energy, such as a flying football, a galloping horse, or a moving bus.
   - **Potential Energy (PE):** Stored energy waiting to be released. This can be due to an object's height above the ground (like a ripe mango hanging high on a tree) or its stretched shape (like a pulled catapult rubber).
2. **Chemical Energy:** Energy quietly locked inside the chemical bonds of substances. It is found inside food, dry-cell torch batteries, kerosene, cooking gas, and petrol.
3. **Thermal (Heat) Energy:** Energy that flows between objects because of a difference in temperature. Heat warms our homes, cooks our food, and comes mainly from the Sun and burning fuels.
4. **Light Energy:** The only form of energy that human eyes can see directly. Light travels in straight lines at an incredible speed of about 300,000 kilometres per second, allowing us to see during the day and night.
5. **Sound Energy:** Energy produced whenever an object vibrates back and forth. You hear sound energy when beating a school bell, talking, or playing a drum.
6. **Electrical Energy:** Energy produced by the flow of tiny electric charges along wires. It powers television sets, fans, blenders, and computer monitors.
7. **Nuclear (Atomic) Energy:** Enormous energy released when the central core (nucleus) of an atom is split or joined together.
8. **Solar Energy:** Radiant light and heat energy coming directly from the Sun. The Sun is the ultimate, primary source of energy for all life on Earth.

### Sources of Energy: Renewable vs. Non-Renewable
A source of energy is where we obtain energy to do useful work. Scientists group energy sources into two main categories:

#### 1. Renewable Energy Sources
Renewable energy comes from natural sources that will never run out because nature constantly replenishes them. They are clean and do not release harmful smoke or soot into the air:
- **Solar Energy:** Sunlight captured by solar panels to generate electricity or dry agricultural crops.
- **Hydroelectric Energy:** Electricity generated by falling water spinning giant turbine wheels inside dams, such as the **Kainji Dam** and **Shiroro Dam** in Nigeria.
- **Wind Energy:** Moving wind turning giant windmills to pump water or make electricity.
- **Biomass / Biofuels:** Energy obtained from plant and animal organic waste, such as firewood, cow dung, and sugarcane alcohol.
- **Wave / Tidal Energy:** Energy captured from the natural rise and fall of ocean waves along coastal waters.

#### 2. Non-Renewable Energy Sources
Non-renewable energy comes from sources that exist in limited amounts deep underground. Once we use them up, they cannot be replaced in our lifetime. When burned, they release soot and greenhouse gases that pollute the air:
- **Petroleum / Crude Oil:** Refined into petrol, diesel, and kerosene to drive motor cars and power generators.
- **Coal:** A black rock made of carbon that is mined from the ground and burned in power stations to generate heat and electricity.
- **Natural Gas:** Gas trapped underground, commonly piped or bottled into cylinders for cooking and heating.

  Body: Light energy can travel through empty outer space at 300,000 km/s and stimulates vision, while sound energy requires physical particles (like air or water) to vibrate so our ears can detect it.

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Believing that energy and force mean the exact same thing.
  Fact: A force is simply a push or a pull exerted on an object (measured in Newtons), whereas energy is the ability or capacity to exert that force and do work (measured in Joules).
- Mistake: Thinking that renewable energy means the equipment is completely free of charge.
  Fact: While sunlight, wind, and river water are free natural gifts, purchasing and installing equipment like solar panels, inverters, and hydroelectric dams requires money.

## Worked Examples
### Example A: A Mango Falling from a Tree
Context: A ripe mango hangs motionlessly on a high branch four metres above the ground. The stem snaps, and the mango drops rapidly into the grass below.
Problem: What forms of mechanical energy are present before and during the fall?
Fix: While resting high on the tree branch, the mango has gravitational potential energy (stored energy of height). As it snaps and speeds downward toward the ground, that potential energy converts directly into kinetic energy (energy of motion).

### Example B: Powering a Rural Community
Context: A remote village clinic frequently runs out of expensive petrol for its smoky generator, leaving refrigerators storing life-saving vaccines without electricity.
Problem: What cleaner and more dependable energy source can keep the clinic's vaccine refrigerators running?
Fix: Install rooftop solar panels paired with storage batteries. Sunlight is a clean, abundant, and renewable energy source in Nigeria that produces steady electricity without fuel shortages or toxic exhaust fumes.

### Example C: Shooting a Stone with a Slingshot (Catapult)
Context: A student pulls back the rubber strip of a catapult holding a smooth pebble, holds it tightly in place, and then lets go.
Problem: Where does the energy come from to throw the pebble across the yard?
Fix: Pulling back the rubber strip does work, storing elastic potential energy in the stretched rubber. When released, this stored potential energy instantly converts into kinetic energy, launching the pebble forward at high speed.
