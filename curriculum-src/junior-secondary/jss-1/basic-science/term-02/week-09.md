## Hook
Over the past eight weeks, you have explored how plants and animals survive and adapt, how microscopic particles form all matter around us, how energy transforms to power our homes, and how mixtures are separated in science laboratories and local kitchens. This Mastery Gate is your opportunity to bring all these ideas together, review key concepts, and ensure you are confident and fully prepared for your terminal examination!

## Key Terms
- Term: Mastery
  Definition: Achieving a clear, complete understanding of scientific concepts so you can apply them to explain observations and solve problems accurately.
- Term: MR NIGERCLAD
  Definition: The memory word summarizing the ten fundamental characteristics of living things: Movement, Respiration, Nutrition, Irritability, Growth, Excretion, Reproduction, Competition, Life span/Death, and Adaptation.
- Term: Conservation of Energy
  Definition: The scientific rule stating that energy cannot be created or destroyed, but can only change from one form to another.
- Term: Particulate Nature of Matter
  Definition: The scientific principle that all matter is made of tiny, moving particles called atoms and molecules.
- Term: Separation Technique
  Definition: A physical method used to divide a mixture into its individual substances based on differences in their physical properties.

## Core Concept
### 1. Living Systems and Biological Diversity (Weeks 01–02)
- **The Ten Signs of Life (MR NIGERCLAD):** Living organisms carry out Movement, Respiration, Nutrition, Irritability, Growth, Excretion, Reproduction, Competition (struggling for food, water, and space), Life span/Death, and Adaptation (features that help an organism survive in its habitat).
- **The Five Kingdoms:** Living things are classified into Monera (bacteria without true nuclei), Protista (*Amoeba*), Fungi (mushrooms and moulds that absorb dead organic matter), Plantae (green photosynthetic plants), and Animalia (multicellular animals).
- **Plant Diversity:** 
  - *Monocotyledons:* One seed-leaf, narrow leaves with parallel veins, and fibrous roots (e.g., maize, rice).
  - *Dicotyledons:* Two seed-leaves, broad leaves with net-like veins, and a deep taproot system (e.g., cowpea beans, groundnuts).
- **Animal Diversity:** 
  - *Invertebrates:* Animals without backbones (insects, earthworms, snails).
  - *Vertebrates:* Animals with backbones, divided into Pisces (fish), Amphibia (frogs, toads), Reptilia (lizards, snakes), Aves (birds), and Mammalia (mammals that have fur and nurse young with milk).

### 2. Matter and Physical Phase Changes (Week 03)
- **The Three States of Matter:**
  - *Solids:* Particles packed in tight, regular rows vibrating in fixed positions; fixed shape and volume; cannot be compressed.
  - *Liquids:* Particles close together but free to slide past one another; fixed volume, takes the shape of its container; cannot be easily compressed.
  - *Gases:* Particles spaced far apart and moving rapidly in all directions; no fixed shape or volume; easily compressed.
- **Phase Changes:** Melting (solid $\rightarrow$ liquid), Freezing (liquid $\rightarrow$ solid), Evaporation/Boiling (liquid $\rightarrow$ gas), Condensation (gas $\rightarrow$ liquid), and Sublimation (solid directly to gas, like camphor).
- **Metals vs. Non-Metals:** Metals are shiny, malleable, ductile, and conduct heat and electricity well (e.g., copper, iron). Non-metals are dull, brittle, and act as electrical and thermal insulators (e.g., wood, rubber, sulphur).

### 3. Energy, Transformations, and Calculations (Weeks 04 & 06)
- **Forms and Sources:** Energy is the capacity to do work (measured in Joules, $\text{J}$). Sources are either **renewable** (solar, wind, hydroelectric from Kainji Dam) or **non-renewable** (crude oil, coal, natural gas).
- **Law of Conservation of Energy:** Energy is never created or destroyed, only transformed.
  - *Electric Iron:* Electrical $\rightarrow$ Heat energy.
  - *Electric Fan:* Electrical $\rightarrow$ Mechanical kinetic energy.
  - *Torchlight:* Chemical (battery) $\rightarrow$ Electrical $\rightarrow$ Light energy.
  - *Microphone:* Sound $\rightarrow$ Electrical energy; *Loudspeaker:* Electrical $\rightarrow$ Sound energy.
- **Mechanical Energy Formulas:**
  - Gravitational Potential Energy: $PE = mgh$ (where $m$ is mass in $\text{kg}$, $g \approx 10\text{ m/s}^2$, and $h$ is height in metres).
  - Kinetic Energy: $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ (where $m$ is mass in $\text{kg}$ and $v$ is speed in $\text{m/s}$).

### 4. Pure and Impure Substances & Separation (Weeks 07–08)
- **Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures:**
  - *Element:* Simplest pure substance made of one kind of atom (e.g., Carbon $\text{C}$, Sodium $\text{Na}$, Potassium $\text{K}$, Oxygen $\text{O}$, Calcium $\text{Ca}$).
  - *Compound:* Pure substance of two or more elements chemically bonded in fixed proportions (e.g., Water $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, Table Salt $\text{NaCl}$).
  - *Mixture:* Two or more substances physically blended in any proportion without chemical bonds (e.g., air, muddy water, soaked garri).
- **Separation Techniques:** 
  - *Decantation:* Pouring off liquid after heavy solids settle.
  - *Filtration:* Separating an insoluble solid from a liquid using filter paper (retains the **residue**, passes the clear **filtrate**).
  - *Evaporation to Dryness:* Boiling off the liquid to recover a dissolved solid.
  - *Simple Distillation:* Boiling a solution and condensing the vapour in a Liebig condenser to collect the pure liquid (**distillate**) while leaving the solid behind.
  - *Magnetic Separation:* Using a magnet to extract magnetic metals (iron) from non-magnetic substances.

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Second Term Core Scientific Distinctions
- Label: Plant Architecture
  Title: Monocots vs Dicots
  Body: Monocots feature single cotyledon seeds, parallel leaf veins, and fibrous roots (e.g., maize), whereas dicots have two cotyledons, net-veined leaves, and taproots (e.g., cowpea).
- Label: States of Matter
  Title: Solids vs Gases
  Body: Solids possess tightly packed particles vibrating in place with fixed shape and volume, while gases have widely spaced particles moving rapidly with variable shape and volume.
- Label: Chemical Classification
  Title: Compounds vs Mixtures
  Body: Compounds consist of elements chemically joined in fixed ratios that require chemical reactions to separate, while mixtures are physically combined in any ratio and separated by physical techniques.
- Label: Mechanical Energy
  Title: Potential vs Kinetic Energy
  Body: Gravitational potential energy is stored energy determined by mass and height ($PE = mgh$), while kinetic energy is energy of motion determined by mass and speed ($KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$).

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Believing that dissolving sugar in water forms a brand new chemical compound.
  Fact: Dissolving forms a physical mixture (solution). No chemical bonds are created, and the water can be evaporated away to recover the dry sugar crystals.
- Mistake: Confusing excretion with egestion (defecation).
  Fact: Excretion removes chemical waste made inside body cells (like urine, sweat, and carbon dioxide), while egestion expels undigested food matter through the anus.
- Mistake: Assuming that a moving object creates energy out of nothing.
  Fact: Energy can never be created; a moving car simply transforms stored chemical energy from petrol into mechanical kinetic energy, heat, and sound.

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Separating a Multi-Component Mixture
Context: A laboratory mixture contains dry sand, iron filings, and table salt.
Problem: Devise a step-by-step physical separation plan to recover all three components separately.
Fix: 
1. Pass a strong bar magnet over the dry mixture to pull out the magnetic **iron filings**.
2. Add clean water to the remaining sand and salt mixture and stir thoroughly so the salt dissolves while the sand remains insoluble.
3. Pour the mixture through a funnel lined with filter paper; the **sand** remains on the paper as residue.
4. Heat the clear salt solution (filtrate) in an evaporating dish until all water boils away, leaving pure dry **table salt** crystals behind.

### Example B: Energy Calculation Diagnostic
Context: A coconut with a mass of $2\text{ kg}$ hangs from a palm tree branch $5\text{ metres}$ above the ground. It detaches and falls, reaching a speed of $6\text{ m/s}$ halfway down. Take gravity $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$.
Problem: Calculate (i) the initial potential energy on the tree and (ii) the kinetic energy at $6\text{ m/s}$.
Fix: 
- (i) Potential Energy: $PE = m \times g \times h = 2\text{ kg} \times 10\text{ m/s}^2 \times 5\text{ m} = 100\text{ Joules (J)}$.
- (ii) Kinetic Energy: $KE = \frac{1}{2} \times m \times v^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 2\text{ kg} \times (6\text{ m/s} \times 6\text{ m/s}) = 1 \times 36 = 36\text{ Joules (J)}$.

### Example C: Specimen Classification Diagnostic
Context: A student finds a specimen with moist scaleless skin that lays soft jelly-covered eggs in a freshwater pond.
Problem: Correctly classify this animal into its proper vertebrate class.
Fix: The animal is an amphibian (class Amphibia). Unlike reptiles, amphibians lack waterproof scales and must reproduce in water because their eggs lack protective shells.
