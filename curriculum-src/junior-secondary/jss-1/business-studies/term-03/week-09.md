## Hook
Imagine reading a legal agreement or a doctor’s prescription that says: *"The patient must take 1,000 tablets.If not,he may die!"* Notice how messy and difficult that is to read without proper spacing after the full stop! Or consider a merchant who types *"₦50 . 00"* instead of *"₦50.00"*, accidentally turning fifty Naira into two confusing numbers. In the professional business world, a document is judged not only by the words it contains, but by the mathematical elegance of its spacing and punctuation! If an executive assistant leaves random, irregular gaps between words or ignores standard spacing rules after periods, colons, and commas, the document looks sloppy, unprofessional, and unreliable. Punctuation marks are the road signs of language, and mastering their precise spacing rules is the final hallmark of an elite typist! In this lesson, you will master the spacing rules after all punctuation marks, learn line spacing standards, and unlock your **Sessional 80% Mastery Gate**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Punctuation Marks
- Definition: Standardized typographical symbols (such as full stops, commas, colons, and question marks) used in written text to separate sentences, indicate pauses, and make grammatical meaning crystal clear.
- Term: Spacing Rules
- Definition: Universal typographical conventions defining the exact number of blank spaces that must be inserted before and after punctuation marks to maintain legibility and professional aesthetics.
- Term: Full Stop (Period)
- Definition: A punctuation mark (`.`) placed at the end of a complete sentence, requiring two blank character spaces before beginning the next sentence.
- Term: Comma
- Definition: A punctuation mark (`,`) used to indicate a slight pause or separate items in a list, requiring exactly one blank character space after it.
- Term: Colon
- Definition: A punctuation mark (`:`) used to direct special attention to an explanation, list, or quotation that follows, requiring two blank character spaces after it.
- Term: Semicolon
- Definition: A punctuation mark (`;`) used to connect two closely related independent clauses, requiring exactly one blank character space after it.
- Term: Line Spacing
- Definition: The vertical distance between lines of typed text, typically set to single spacing (1.0), 1.5 line spacing, or double spacing (2.0).
- Term: Parentheses (Brackets)
- Definition: Punctuation symbols `( )` used to enclose explanatory remarks, typed with zero spaces inside against the enclosed words.
- Term: Decimal Point
- Definition: The use of a period within numbers (e.g., ₦25.50) to separate whole currency from kobo, requiring zero spaces before and after.
- Term: Sessional Mastery Gate (80% Benchmark)
- Definition: The comprehensive summative standard requiring learners to achieve an 80% mastery score across all academic competencies of JSS 1 Business Studies (Terms 1, 2, and 3).

### 3. Line Spacing Standards in Corporate Formatting
Line spacing controls the vertical distance between lines of text:
- **Single Line Spacing (1.0):** Standard for routine business letters, memos, and invoices where compactness is required.
- **One-and-a-Half Line Spacing (1.5):** Preferred in academic essays and formal business reports for enhanced reading comfort.
- **Double Line Spacing (2.0):** Mandatory for draft manuscripts, legal documents, and school examination submissions to allow space for handwritten annotations and corrections.
- **Triple Line Spacing (3.0):** Used primarily between major section headings or title display banners.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Leaving Zero Spaces After Commas:** Writing *"apples,oranges,bananas"* is a severe error. Always leave exactly **one space** after every comma.
2. **Leaving Spaces Around Apostrophes:** Typing *"Ada ' s pen"* destroys word unity. The apostrophe connects letters directly with **zero spaces**.
3. **Leaving Spaces Around Decimal Points:** Inserting spaces in *"₦40 . 50"* makes numbers unreadable to financial software. Decimal periods have **zero spaces**.
4. **Failing to Leave 2 Spaces After Sentence-Ending Marks:** In traditional typing, full stops, question marks, and colons require **two spaces** to clearly demarcate sentence boundaries.

## Worked Examples

### Scenario: Formatting Punctuation and Spacing in Business Correspondence
*Problem:* Identify and correct the punctuation and spacing errors in the following draft excerpt from a Nigerian trading firm:
> *"We received your letter dated 14th March,2026.Please supply the following items:twenty reams of paper,ten staplers and five boxes of pens.Did you receive our cheque of # 50,000 . 00?We hope to hear from you soon."*

*Solution:*
1. **After `March,`:** Add **one space** after the comma ──► `March, 2026.`
2. **After `2026.`:** Add **two spaces** after the full stop ending the sentence ──► `2026.  Please`
3. **After `items:`:** Add **two spaces** after the colon introducing the list ──► `items:  twenty`
4. **After `paper,`:** Add **one space** after the comma ──► `paper, ten staplers,`
5. **After `# 50,000 . 00`:** Remove spaces around symbol and decimal point ──► `₦50,000.00`
6. **After `00?`:** Add **two spaces** after the question mark ending the sentence ──► `₦50,000.00?  We`

*Corrected Master Version:*
> *"We received your letter dated 14th March, 2026.  Please supply the following items:  twenty reams of paper, ten staplers, and five boxes of pens.  Did you receive our cheque of ₦50,000.00?  We hope to hear from you soon."*
