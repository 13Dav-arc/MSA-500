## Hook
Imagine you are writing an urgent email to submit your school’s annual scholarship application. Suddenly, as you press the letters on your keyboard, crumbs of chin-chin and drops of spilled sugary soft drink trapped between the keys cause the keys to jam! The letter *"E"* refuses to press, the space bar is stuck, and your computer screen begins typing infinite lines of gibberish! Your computer keyboard is a precision electronic instrument containing millions of micro-circuits beneath every keycap. If you treat it like a dinner tray or let dust accumulate inside, it will fail when you need it most. Furthermore, behind the familiar letters lies a century of brilliant engineering: four distinct horizontal rows, specialized functional clusters, and toggle keys designed to turn your fingertips into an efficient command center! In this lesson, you will explore the anatomy of the computer keyboard, master the four keyboard rows, understand the left/right division of labor, and discover how to protect your computer hardware for years of flawless service!

## Key Terms
- Term: Computer Keyboard
- Definition: A primary hardware input device featuring an arrangement of buttons or keys used to input alphanumeric characters, symbols, and operational commands into a computer system.
- Term: QWERTY Layout
- Definition: The universal standard keyboard layout named after the first six letters on the upper alphabetic row, originally designed for mechanical typewriters and retained in modern computing.
- Term: Function Keys (F1–F12)
- Definition: A row of special keys positioned along the top of a computer keyboard programmed to perform software-specific shortcut commands (e.g., `F1` for Help).
- Term: Toggle Key
- Definition: A keyboard key (such as `Caps Lock`, `Num Lock`, or `Scroll Lock`) that alternates between two operational states (on or off) each time it is pressed.
- Term: Control Keys
- Definition: Keys used in combination with other alphanumeric keys to execute software operations and commands (e.g., `Ctrl`, `Alt`, `Shift`).
- Term: Top Row
- Definition: The upper numerical row consisting of Arabic numerals (`1 to 0`), mathematical symbols, hyphens, and punctuation symbols accessed with the Shift key.
- Term: Upper Row (Second Row)
- Definition: The row immediately above the home row consisting of the letters `Q W E R T Y U I O P`.
- Term: Home Keys Row (Third Row)
- Definition: The central baseline row consisting of `A S D F G H J K L ; '`, where the typist's fingers rest naturally.
- Term: Nottem Row (Bottom Row)
- Definition: The bottom row of letters and punctuation keys consisting of `Z X C V B N M , . /`.
- Term: Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)
- Definition: An electrical hardware device containing a backup battery that provides continuous electrical power to a computer during power outages, preventing hardware damage and data loss.

### 3. The Two Divisions of the Keyboard (Left vs. Right)
The keyboard is divided down the middle into two zones of responsibility:

```
                    DIVISION OF THE KEYBOARD
                    
         LEFT HAND TERRITORY             RIGHT HAND TERRITORY
    ┌───────────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────────┐
    │ Top:    1  2  3  4  5     │   │ Top:    6  7  8  9  0     │
    │ Upper:  Q  W  E  R  T     │   │ Upper:  Y  U  I  O  P     │
    │ Home:   A  S  D  F  G     │   │ Home:   H  J  K  L  ;     │
    │ Nottem: Z  X  C  V  B     │   │ Nottem: N  M  ,  .  /     │
    └───────────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────────┘
```
- **Left Hand:** Operates all keys on the left side up to `5`, `T`, `G`, and `B`.
- **Right Hand:** Operates all keys on the right side from `6`, `Y`, `H`, and `N` across to the right margin.

### 4. Similarities & Differences: Typewriter vs. Computer Keyboard

| Feature | Manual / Electronic Typewriter | Modern Computer Keyboard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Key Layout** | Universal QWERTY layout | Universal QWERTY layout |
| **Output Medium** | Inks directly onto physical paper | Displays digitally on computer monitor |
| **Error Correction**| White correction fluid or correction tape| Instant Backspace and Delete keys |
| **Data Storage** | No digital memory; paper copy only | Saves documents to hard drive, SSD, or cloud|
| **Functional Keys** | Mechanical carriage return lever | Function keys (F1–F12), Ctrl, Alt, Windows |
| **Fatigue Level** | Heavy mechanical key strike required | Light electronic soft touch requires minimal effort |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Backspace with Delete:** Backspace deletes the character to the *left* of the cursor; Delete removes the character to the *right* of the cursor!
2. **Using Wet Cloths to Clean Keyboards:** Water seeps beneath keycaps, short-circuiting electrical traces. Use only dry or lightly damp lint-free micro-fiber cloths.
3. **Reaching Across with the Wrong Hand:** Hitting the letter `Y` or `H` with the left hand breaks keyboard division. The center keys are strictly assigned (`T` and `G` to left index; `Y` and `H` to right index).
4. **Yanking Out Power Plugs to Turn Off Computer:** Causes disk corruption and system failure. Always execute a clean operating system shutdown.

## Worked Examples

### Scenario: Diagnosing Keyboard Rows and Divisions
*Problem:* Identify the row and the hand assigned to type each of the following characters during touch keyboarding:
1. The letter `R`
2. The numeral `8`
3. The letter `M`
4. The letter `S`
5. The symbol `?` (question mark)

*Solution:*
1. **Letter `R`:** Located on the **Upper Row**; operated by the **Left Hand** (left index finger reaching up from `F`).
2. **Numeral `8`:** Located on the **Top Row**; operated by the **Right Hand** (right middle finger reaching up from `K`).
3. **Letter `M`:** Located on the **Nottem (Bottom) Row**; operated by the **Right Hand** (right index finger reaching down from `J`).
4. **Letter `S`:** Located on the **Home Keys Row**; operated by the **Left Hand** (left ring finger resting on `S`).
5. **Symbol `?`:** Located on the **Nottem (Bottom) Row** (shift + slash); operated by the **Right Hand** (right little finger, with left little finger holding Shift).
