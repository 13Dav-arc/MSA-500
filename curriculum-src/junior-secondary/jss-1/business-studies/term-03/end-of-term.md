### 2. Systematic Synthesis of Term 03 Core Disciplines

#### A. Principles and Mechanics of Double Entry Bookkeeping
- **Universal Rule:** Every monetary transaction has a dual effect: one account receives value (**Debit**), and another account surrenders value (**Credit**). Total Debits = Total Credits.
- **T-Account Anatomy:** Left side is Debit (DR); right side is Credit (CR). Title at the top; columns for Date, Particulars (naming counterpart account), Folio, and Amount (₦).
- **Assets & Capital:**
  - *Assets:* Economic resources owned. Increases are **Debited**; decreases are **Credited**.
  - *Capital:* Owner's equity contribution. Initial investment is **Credited** to Capital Account and **Debited** to Cash or Bank Account.
- **Liabilities & Credit Transactions:**
  - *Liabilities:* Debts owed to third parties. Increases are **Credited**; debt settlements are **Debited**.
  - *Credit Purchases:* Debit Purchases Account, Credit Supplier’s Personal Account (Creditor).
  - *Credit Sales:* Debit Customer’s Personal Account (Debtor), Credit Sales Account.
- **Expenses & Revenue:**
  - *Expenses:* Operational outlays consumed. Always **Debited** to the specific expense account (Rent, Salaries, Electricity, Stationery) and **Credited** to Cash or Bank.
  - *Revenue:* Earnings generated. Always **Credited** to the revenue account (Sales, Commission Received) and **Debited** to Cash or Bank.
- **Balancing Ledger Accounts:**
  - Balance c/d (carried down): Inserted on the smaller side to equalize totals on the last day of the month.
  - Balance b/d (brought down): Brought forward to the opposite (normal) side beneath the double underline on the first day of the new month.

#### B. Taxonomy of Accounts and Specialized Ledgers
- **The Ledger:** The "Principal Book of Account" or "Book of Second Entry."
- **Four Classes of Ledgers:**
  1. *Sales Ledger (Debtors' Ledger):* Personal accounts of credit customers.
  2. *Purchases Ledger (Creditors' Ledger):* Personal accounts of credit suppliers.
  3. *General Ledger:* Real accounts (tangible assets) and Nominal accounts (expenses and revenues).
  4. *Private Ledger:* Confidential accounts (Capital, Drawings, Loans, Annual Profit and Loss) kept securely by the owner.
- **Three Families of Accounts:**
  - *Personal Accounts:* Real people, businesses, debtors, creditors. Rule: *Debit the receiver, Credit the giver.*
  - *Real Accounts:* Tangible physical property (Vehicles, Machinery, Buildings, Cash). Rule: *Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out.*
  - *Nominal Accounts:* Intangible expenses, revenues, profits, losses. Rule: *Debit expenses and losses, Credit gains and revenues.*

#### C. Touch Keyboarding and Ergonomics
- **Sitting Posture:** Head upright, spine straight with slight hip tilt forward, elbows close to sides, wrists hovering low and straight (never resting flat on desk), feet planted flat on floor with one foot slightly in front.
- **Manuscript Position:** Placed to the **right-hand side** of the machine for natural line of sight.
- **Touch Typing Discipline:** **Never look down at the keyboard.** Rely entirely on finger muscle memory.

#### D. Keyboard Anatomy, Rows, and Divisions
- **Four Keyboard Rows:**
  1. *Top Row:* Numbers `1 to 0` and symbols.
  2. *Upper Row:* Letters `Q W E R T Y U I O P`.
  3. *Home Row:* Baseline keys `A S D F G H J K L ; '`.
  4. *Nottem Row:* Bottom keys `Z X C V B N M , . /`.
- **Keyboard Divisions:** Left hand controls up to `5`, `T`, `G`, `B`; Right hand controls from `6`, `Y`, `H`, `N` rightward.
- **Tactile Ridges:** Raised bumps on `F` and `J` allow index fingers to locate base camp by touch.

#### E. Service Keys, Shortcuts, and Punctuation Spacing
- **Basic Service Keys:**
  - *Space Bar:* Struck by the right thumb.
  - *Tab Key:* Indents 0.5 inches (5 spaces); struck by left little finger.
  - *Caps Lock:* Toggles permanent uppercase; struck by left little finger.
  - *Shift Keys:* Used with opposite little finger for individual capital letters and top-tier symbols.
  - *Enter / Return:* Starts new paragraph or submits; struck by right little finger.
  - *Backspace:* Deletes character to the left; struck by right little finger.
  - *Delete:* Removes character to the right.
  - *Escape (Esc):* Cancels active command/dialog; struck by left little finger.
- **Keyboard Shortcuts:** `Ctrl+S` (Save), `Ctrl+C` (Copy), `Ctrl+X` (Cut), `Ctrl+V` (Paste), `Ctrl+A` (Select All), `Ctrl+Z` (Undo).
- **Punctuation Spacing Master Rules:**
  - Full stop at sentence end: **2 spaces**. Decimal point: **0 spaces**.
  - Comma: **1 space**. Semicolon: **1 space**.
  - Colon: **2 spaces**. Question mark: **2 spaces**. Exclamation mark: **2 spaces**.
  - Apostrophe & Hyphen: **0 spaces**.
  - Parentheses: **0 spaces inside**, **1 space outside**.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Debit with Subtraction:** In accounting, debiting an asset or expense increases it.
2. **Placing Balance b/d on the Wrong Side:** Balance b/d must always be brought down to the opposite side of Balance c/d beneath the double underline.
3. **Classifying Cash as a Nominal Account:** Cash is physical currency that you can see and touch; it is a **Real Account**.
4. **Looking Down While Keyboarding:** Looking at the keys destroys muscle memory and permanently reduces typing speed.
5. **Leaving Zero Spaces After a Comma:** Commas require exactly **one space** after them.
6. **Leaving Spaces Around Decimal Points:** Decimal points (e.g., ₦50.00) require **zero spaces** before and after.

### Scenario: Comprehensive Sessional Synthesis
*Problem:* Answer each of the following practical business studies problems:
1. Alhaji Aminu starts business with ₦800,000 cash. He buys a delivery truck for ₦500,000 by cheque, buys ₦100,000 of goods on credit from Kano Mills, and pays ₦30,000 in cash for office stationery. Identify the accounts debited and credited in each transaction.
2. An accounts clerk needs to look up the balance owed by a credit customer, the cost of shop rent, and the owner’s capital. In which ledgers are these accounts kept?
3. A typist sits down to type a two-page letter. Where is the draft placed, which finger strikes the Tab key, which thumb taps the Space Bar, and how many spaces follow a period ending a sentence?

*Solution:*
1. **Transaction Double Entries:**
   - *Capital Introduction:* Debit **Cash Account** ₦800,000; Credit **Capital Account** ₦800,000.
   - *Truck Purchase:* Debit **Motor Vehicle Account** ₦500,000; Credit **Bank Account** ₦500,000.
   - *Credit Purchases:* Debit **Purchases Account** ₦100,000; Credit **Kano Mills Account** (Creditor) ₦100,000.
   - *Stationery Payment:* Debit **Stationery Expense Account** ₦30,000; Credit **Cash Account** ₦30,000.
2. **Ledger Locations:**
   - Credit customer (Debtor): **Sales Ledger**.
   - Shop rent (Nominal Expense): **General Ledger**.
   - Owner’s capital (Confidential Equity): **Private Ledger**.
3. **Keyboarding Setup:**
   - Draft placement: On the **right-hand side** of the machine.
   - Tab key: Struck by the **left little finger**.
   - Space Bar: Tapped smoothly by the **right thumb**.
   - Spacing after period: Exactly **two (2) spaces**.

- Little Finger: Key A
- Ring Finger: Key S
- Middle Finger: Key D
- Index Finger: Key F
Feedback: Left fingers: Little=A, Ring=S, Middle=D, Index=F.

### Q49 [matching]
Stem: Match each Basic Service Key with its primary operating digit:
- Space Bar: Operated by the Right Thumb
- Tab Key: Struck by the Left Little Finger
- Enter (Return) Key: Struck by the Right Little Finger
- Backspace Key: Struck by the Right Little Finger
Feedback: Space Bar=Right Thumb; Tab=Left Little; Enter=Right Little; Backspace=Right Little.

### Q50 [matching]
Stem: Match each punctuation mark with the required number of blank spaces after it:
- Sentence-ending Full Stop: Two (2) spaces
- Comma: Exactly one (1) space
- Decimal Point: Zero (0) spaces
- Question Mark at sentence end: Two (2) spaces
Feedback: Sentence full stop=2 spaces; Comma=1 space; Decimal=0 spaces; Question mark=2 spaces.
