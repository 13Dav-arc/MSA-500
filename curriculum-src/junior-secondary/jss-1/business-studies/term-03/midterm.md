## Key Terms
- Term: Double Entry Principle
- Definition: The fundamental rule that every financial transaction has a dual effect, requiring an equal debit and credit entry across two or more accounts.
- Term: First Golden Rule
- Definition: **"Debit the account that receives value, and Credit the account that gives value."**
- Term: DEAD CLIC
- Definition: The standard accounting mnemonic indicating that Debits increase Expenses, Assets, and Drawings, while Credits increase Liabilities, Income, and Capital.
- Term: Ledger
- Definition: The principal book of accounts where transactions from books of prime entry are permanently classified and posted.
- Term: Personal Account
- Definition: A ledger account bearing the name of an individual, debtor, creditor, or corporate entity.
- Term: Real Account
- Definition: An impersonal account recording tangible, material property or physical assets that can be seen and touched.
- Term: Nominal Account
- Definition: An impersonal account recording intangible operational events such as expenses, losses, revenues, and profits.
- Term: Balance Carried Down (c/d)
- Definition: The closing balancing figure inserted on the smaller side of an account to equalize debit and credit totals.
- Term: Touch Typing
- Definition: The skill of typing without looking at the keyboard by relying on finger muscle memory and keeping eyes on the manuscript.
- Term: Home Row Keys
- Definition: The foundational resting keys on the middle row: `a s d f` for the left hand and `; l k j` for the right hand.

### 2. Foundational Accounting & Keyboarding Principles

#### A. Double Entry Mechanics
- **The Golden Rule:** Every debit must have an equal corresponding credit.
- **Assets:** Tangible or intangible resources owned. Increases are **Debited**; decreases are **Credited**.
- **Capital:** Owner's equity. Money introduced into business is **Credited** to Capital Account and **Debited** to Cash/Bank Account.
- **Liabilities:** Debts owed to third parties. Increases are **Credited**; decreases are **Debited**.
- **Expenses:** Costs consumed to operate the business. Always **Debited** to the specific expense account.
- **Revenue:** Earnings from sales or fees. Always **Credited** to the revenue account.
- **Balancing:** Balance c/d closes the smaller side at month-end; Balance b/d opens the opposite side at the start of the next period beneath the double underline.

#### B. The Taxonomy of Accounts and Ledgers
- **Personal Accounts:** People, firms, debtors, creditors. Located in **Sales Ledger** (Debtors) or **Purchases Ledger** (Creditors).
- **Real Accounts:** Tangible physical property (Vehicles, Furniture, Machinery, Buildings, Cash). Located in **General Ledger**.
- **Nominal Accounts:** Intangible expenses, revenues, profits, losses (Rent, Salaries, Power, Discounts, Sales). Located in **General Ledger**.
- **Private Ledger:** Confidential records (Capital, Drawings, Loans, Net Profit) locked by the proprietor.

#### C. Keyboarding Posture and Home Keys
- **Sitting Posture:** Head erect, back straight with slight forward hip lean, elbows tucked close to body, wrists low and straight without resting on table, feet flat on the floor with one foot slightly in front.
- **Manuscript Position:** Placed to the **right-hand side** of the machine for an unobstructed line of sight.
- **Home Keys:** Left hand fingers on `a s d f` and right hand fingers on `; l k j`. Index fingers guided by tactile ridges on `F` and `J`.
- **Right Thumb:** Designated to tap the Space Bar.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Debit with Subtraction:** In accounting, debiting an asset or expense increases its balance.
2. **Placing Balance b/d on the Same Side as Balance c/d:** Balance b/d must always be brought down to the opposite side of Balance c/d.
3. **Classifying Cash as a Nominal Account:** Cash is physical currency that you can touch and see; it is a **Real Account**.
4. **Looking at the Keyboard While Typing:** Breaking eye contact with the manuscript destroys touch-typing muscle memory.
5. **Resting Wrists Heavily on Desk:** Creates pressure on nerves, leading to Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI).

## Worked Examples

### Scenario: Diagnostic Synthesis Review
*Problem:* Analyze each of the following statements and provide the correct accounting classification or keyboarding action:
1. Alhaji Garba buys an office computer for ₦300,000 cash. What accounts are debited and credited, and what class of account is the computer?
2. Emeka sells goods on credit to Chidi for ₦45,000. In which ledger does Chidi's account live, and what class of account is it?
3. A typist sits at a keyboard. Where should the manuscript be placed, which fingers rest on keys `F` and `J`, and which digit operates the space bar?

*Solution:*
1. **Computer Purchase:**
   - Debit: **Computer Hardware Account** ₦300,000 (Asset increases).
   - Credit: **Cash Account** ₦300,000 (Asset decreases).
   - Classification: Computer is a **Real Account** (tangible physical property).
2. **Credit Sale:**
   - Chidi is a trade debtor. His personal account is maintained in the **Sales Ledger**.
   - Classification: Chidi's account is a **Personal Account**.
3. **Keyboarding Setup:**
   - Manuscript: Placed on the **right-hand side** of the machine.
   - Keys `F` and `J`: Operated by the **left index finger** (`F`) and **right index finger** (`J`).
   - Space Bar: Struck smoothly by the **right thumb**.

## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the fundamental principle of Double Entry Bookkeeping?
A) Transactions are recorded every two weeks
B) Every debit entry must have an equal and corresponding credit entry (correct)
C) Debits must always exceed credits by 10%
D) Records are kept only in foreign currencies
Feedback: Double entry means every financial transaction results in equal debit and credit entries.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: According to the First Golden Rule of Bookkeeping, how should the receiver and giver of value be treated?
A) Credit the receiver, Debit the giver
B) Debit the receiver, Credit the giver (correct)
C) Ignore the giver, Debit the receiver
D) Credit both accounts
Feedback: The First Golden Rule dictates: Debit the receiver, Credit the giver.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: When a business owner starts operations with ₦500,000 in cash as capital, what is the correct double entry?
A) Debit Capital Account, Credit Cash Account
B) Debit Cash Account, Credit Capital Account (correct)
C) Debit Purchases Account, Credit Sales Account
D) Debit Drawings Account, Credit Bank Account
Feedback: Cash receives value (Debit Cash); Capital provides the stake (Credit Capital).

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: When a business purchases office furniture for ₦80,000 paying in cash, which account is debited?
A) Cash Account
B) Furniture Account (correct)
C) Capital Account
D) Sales Account
Feedback: Furniture Account receives the new asset and is debited.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: When a business pays ₦40,000 for shop electricity by cheque, what is the double entry?
A) Debit Bank Account, Credit Electricity Account
B) Debit Electricity Expense Account, Credit Bank Account (correct)
C) Debit Cash Account, Credit Capital Account
D) Debit Rent Account, Credit Wages Account
Feedback: Electricity consumes value (Debit Expense); Bank account pays funds (Credit Bank).

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: In the accounting rule "DEAD CLIC", which accounts increase when they are DEBITED?
A) Capital, Liabilities, Income
B) Expenses, Assets, Drawings (correct)
C) Creditors, Loans, Commission Received
D) Sales, Rent Received, Interest
Feedback: Under DEAD: Debits increase Expenses, Assets, and Drawings.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: What is "Balance c/d" when balancing a ledger account at month-end?
A) The discount granted to a customer
B) The closing balancing figure placed on the smaller side to bring both totals into balance (correct)
C) The tax paid to the government
D) The money stolen from the till
Feedback: Balance c/d (carried down) equalizes the two sides at period-end.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: Which specialized ledger contains the personal accounts of all credit customers (Trade Debtors)?
A) Purchases Ledger
B) Sales Ledger (Debtors' Ledger) (correct)
C) General Ledger
D) Private Ledger
Feedback: The Sales Ledger holds the personal accounts of trade debtors.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: Into which account classification does a "Motor Delivery Van Account" fall?
A) Nominal Account
B) Real Account (correct)
C) Personal Account
D) Private Account
Feedback: A delivery van is a tangible physical asset, making it a Real Account.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: Into which account classification does a "Salaries and Wages Expense Account" fall?
A) Real Account
B) Nominal Account (correct)
C) Personal Account
D) Fixed Asset Account
Feedback: Salaries and wages represent an operational expense, making it a Nominal Account.
