## Hook
Imagine a multi-million Naira legal contract arriving at your company's head office in a sealed envelope marked *"URGENT: Submit within 24 hours to secure ₦50,000,000 project."* The mail clerk carelessly tosses the envelope into an overflowing cardboard box behind the door, where it sits buried under old newspapers for three weeks. By the time the Managing Director discovers the letter, the deadline has passed, the lucrative project has been awarded to a fierce competitor, and the company has suffered a catastrophic financial blow! In the corporate world, an organization's lifeblood flows through its **mail and correspondence**. Every day, hundreds of critical business documents arrive and depart: bank payment drafts, customer purchase orders, government tax queries, supplier bills, and legal notices. If incoming mail is misdirected or outgoing letters are delayed, businesses collapse into legal disputes and financial ruin. In this lesson, you will master the seven sequential steps of processing incoming mail, explore outgoing mail dispatch systems, and learn how to maintain professional mail registers!

## Key Terms
- Term: Mail (Correspondence)
- Definition: All written or printed communications entering or leaving an organization, including letters, memoranda, invoices, parcels, courier deliveries, and electronic transmissions.
- Term: Inward (Incoming) Mail
- Definition: All letters, documents, circulars, and packages received by an organization from external sources (clients, suppliers, government agencies, banks).
- Term: Outward (Outgoing) Mail
- Definition: All letters, quotations, payment cheques, and documents drafted, signed, and dispatched from an organization to external individuals or business partners.
- Term: Inward Mail Register (Inward Mail Book)
- Definition: An official administrative record book where key details of all incoming correspondence are systematically logged before distribution to action officers.
- Term: Outward Mail Register (Despatch Book)
- Definition: An official administrative logbook used to record all outgoing mail sent out of the organization, providing legal proof of dispatch and delivery.
- Term: Postage Book
- Definition: A financial control register maintained in the mailroom to account for the purchase, usage, and remaining balance of postal stamps and franking credits.
- Term: Enclosure
- Definition: Any additional document, cheque, price list, contract draft, or photograph inserted inside an envelope alongside the primary covering business letter.
- Term: Date-Stamping
- Definition: The clerical procedure of imprinting an official ink stamp displaying the exact date and time of arrival onto the surface of an incoming document.
- Term: Letter Opener (Paper Knife)
- Definition: A smooth, flat, blunt-bladed tool used by mail clerks to slice open the top flap of paper envelopes cleanly without tearing internal documents.
- Term: Confidential Mail
- Definition: Letters or packages explicitly marked *"Private," "Confidential," "Strictly Personal,"* or *"Secret,"* which must never be opened by a mail clerk.

### 2. The Seven Steps of Handling Inward (Incoming) Mail

1. **Receiving the Mail:** Mail arrives via postal delivery, collection from the post office box/private mail bag (PMB), or through physical delivery by courier dispatch riders (DHL, FedEx, NIPOST).
2. **Sorting the Mail:**
   - The mail clerk sorts envelopes into three distinct categories:
     - *Category A:* Official general mail addressed to the organization.
     - *Category B:* Urgent / Express correspondence.
     - *Category C:* Mail clearly marked **"Confidential," "Private," "Personal,"** or **"Secret."**
   > [!IMPORTANT]
   > The Cardinal Rule of Mail Handling: Letters marked **"Confidential"** or **"Private"** must **NEVER be opened by the mail clerk**! They must be delivered intact, sealed, and unopened directly to the named officer.
3. **Opening the Mail:** Non-confidential official letters are slit open along the upper flap using a smooth **letter opener**. Slicing roughly with fingers or ripping envelopes tears important enclosed documents.
4. **Checking for Enclosures:** The clerk removes all contents and checks the letter for the notation *"Enc."* or *"Encl."* (e.g., *"We enclose our cheque for ₦50,000"*). If an enclosure is missing, the clerk immediately writes *"Enclosure Missing"* in red ink on the letter margin and attaches the envelope.
5. **Date-Stamping:** Every incoming letter is stamped with the official **Inward Date Stamp** in the top right-hand corner, permanently recording the exact date and time of arrival.
6. **Recording in the Inward Mail Register:** The clerk logs the details in the Inward Mail Book:
   - Date received, Date on the letter, Name of sender and company, Subject matter, Action department/officer assigned, and File reference number.
7. **Distribution to Action Officers:** Letters are placed in dedicated departmental dispatch folders or circulation trays and delivered to the relevant officers for immediate action.

### 3. Steps in Handling Outward (Outgoing) Mail
1. **Collection:** Collecting signed letters and duplicate office file copies from executive desks.
2. **Verification of Enclosures:** Checking that all attachments mentioned in the letter (cheques, catalogues, vouchers) are physically pinned behind the covering letter.
3. **Folding & Inserting into Envelopes:** Folding the letter neatly in thirds and inserting it into a matching envelope. If a **window envelope** is used, the letter must be folded so that the recipient's typed address aligns perfectly with the clear plastic window.
4. **Weighing & Stamping:** Weighing the envelope on a postal scale to determine the correct postage rate. The envelope is then stamped with adhesive stamps or processed through a **franking machine**.
5. **Recording in the Outward Mail Register (Despatch Book):**
   - Date, Name and address of recipient, Department originating the letter, Postage cost, and Method of dispatch (hand delivery, registered post, courier).
6. **Dispatch:** Depositing mail at the post office, handing it to a courier agent, or dispatching an office messenger with an internal delivery book.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Opening a letter clearly stamped *"STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL - FOR MANAGING DIRECTOR'S EYES ONLY."*
  *Correction:* Opening confidential mail is a severe breach of trust and gross misconduct. It must be delivered completely sealed to the named recipient.
- **Mistake 2:** Tossing an incoming envelope into the wastebasket before checking if an important enclosure is missing.
  *Correction:* Always retain the envelope if an enclosure is missing, or if the postmark date differs significantly from the date written on the letter; the envelope serves as legal evidence.
- **Mistake 3:** Guessing postage stamp amounts instead of using a postal scale.
  *Correction:* Under-stamping an envelope results in the recipient being penalized with heavy surcharge fees or the letter being returned to sender.
- **Mistake 4:** Dispatching an outgoing letter that mentions an enclosed payment cheque without actually inserting the cheque.
  *Correction:* Always cross-check the bottom of the letter for the *"Encl."* notation and physically verify that all attachments are inside before sealing the flap.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Handling a Missing Enclosure
*Scenario:* A mail clerk in an engineering company in Port Harcourt opens an incoming official letter from a client. The letter states: *"Please find enclosed our payment draft of ₦1,200,000 as initial deposit for the warehouse project."* However, when the clerk empties the envelope, no cheque or bank draft is inside.
- What step-by-step procedure should the mail clerk follow immediately?

*Procedure & Action:*
1. **Inspect the Envelope Carefully:** Shake the envelope and shine light inside to confirm the cheque is not stuck in a corner.
2. **Note the Defect on the Document:** Write clearly in red ink on the top margin of the letter: *"Enclosure (Bank Draft of ₦1,200,000) MISSING upon opening."* Sign and date the note.
3. **Attach the Envelope:** Staple the empty envelope to the back of the letter (the postmark and packaging condition serve as vital evidence).
4. **Log in the Inward Mail Register:** In the Remarks column of the Inward Mail Register, record: *"Bank draft missing."*
5. **Notify the Action Officer:** Deliver the letter immediately to the Accounts Manager so they can contact the client without delay.

### Example 2: Balancing the Mailroom Postage Book
*Scenario:* At the start of the week (Monday), the mailroom clerk has ₦8,000 worth of postage stamps in hand. During the week:
- On Tuesday, stamps worth ₦1,500 were used on letters to Kaduna.
- On Wednesday, stamps worth ₦2,200 were used on legal notices.
- On Thursday, the clerk purchased ₦10,000 in new stamps from NIPOST.
- On Friday, stamps worth ₦3,800 were used on annual reports.

*Task:* Calculate the remaining balance of postage stamps in hand at the close of business on Friday.

*Solution:*
1. $\text{Initial Balance} = ₦8,000$.
2. $\text{Total Stamps Used} = ₦1,500 + ₦2,200 + ₦3,800 = ₦7,500$.
3. $\text{Total Stamps Purchased} = ₦10,000$.
4. $\text{Final Balance} = (\text{Initial Balance} + \text{Purchases}) - \text{Total Used}$
   $$\text{Final Balance} = (₦8,000 + ₦10,000) - ₦7,500 = ₦18,000 - ₦7,500 = ₦10,500.$$
*Conclusion:* The clerk must have exactly ₦10,500 worth of physical stamps in the postage box on Friday evening.
