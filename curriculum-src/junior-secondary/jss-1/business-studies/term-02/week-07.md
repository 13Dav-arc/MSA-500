## Hook
Imagine that an unscrupulous electronics supplier in Alaba International Market, Lagos, walks into your office claiming that your school purchased twenty brand-new computers from him last month for ₦5,000,000 and has refused to pay. He threatens to sue your school in court and seize the school bus! How can the school principal prove beyond any shadow of a doubt that the school never placed such an order, or that if computers were indeed delivered, the school had already paid every single kobo? The principal cannot simply tell the judge, *"Believe me, I am an honest man."* In the eyes of the law and modern accounting, **if there is no paper trail, it never happened!** The bedrock of all accounting entries is **Source Documents**—the physical or digital vouchers, receipts, invoices, and bank slips created at the exact moment a transaction takes place. In this lesson, you will master the vital source documents that protect businesses from fraud, track commercial dealings, and provide undisputed evidence in bookkeeping!

## Key Terms
- Term: Source Document
- Definition: The original written or digital business paper that provides authentic, verifiable physical evidence that a financial transaction took place.
- Term: Cash Receipt
- Definition: A source document issued by a seller to a buyer acknowledging that cash, cheque, or electronic payment has been received for goods or services.
- Term: Invoice
- Definition: A commercial bill sent by a seller to a credit customer stating the description, quantity, unit price, total cost of goods delivered, and terms of payment.
- Term: Pro-forma Invoice
- Definition: A preliminary or quotation invoice sent to a prospective buyer before goods are dispatched, showing estimated costs without creating a debt obligation.
- Term: Debit Note
- Definition: A document sent by a buyer to a seller informing them that their account has been debited (requesting a credit deduction) for damaged, defective, or overcharged goods.
- Term: Credit Note
- Definition: A document sent by a seller to a buyer informing them that their account has been credited (reducing their debt) due to returned goods, allowances, or past overcharges.
- Term: Cheque
- Definition: A written, unconditional order addressed by an account holder (drawer) to a licensed commercial bank (drawee) to pay a specified sum of money to a named party (payee).
- Term: Pay-in Slip (Bank Teller)
- Definition: A standardized bank voucher used by a depositor to lodge cash or cheques into a designated commercial bank account.
- Term: Petty Cash Voucher
- Definition: An internal office voucher filled out and signed to authorize and document small, incidental cash disbursements from the petty cash fund.
- Term: Retention Period
- Definition: The statutory duration (mandated at six years under Nigerian tax laws) during which businesses must safely store original accounting documents for audit inspection.

## Core Concept
### 1. The Meaning and Indispensable Role of Source Documents
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Source Documents" caption="Source Documents: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

In accounting, source documents are the **primary evidence** of commercial life. Every single debit or credit entry recorded in a journal or cash book must originate from an authentic source document. 

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE ACCOUNTING EVIDENCE PIPELINE                     │
│                                                                        │
│   [Commercial Transaction]                                             │
│             │                                                          │
│             ▼                                                          │
│   [Source Document Generated] (Receipt, Invoice, Cheque, Bank Teller)  │
│             │                                                          │
│             ▼                                                          │
│   [Book of Original Entry] (Sales Day Book, Purchases Day Book, Cash)  │
│             │                                                          │
│             ▼                                                          │
│   [Ledger Account Posting] (Debits and Credits)                        │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Without source documents:
1. Bookkeepers could fabricate imaginary transactions or record fraudulent expenditures.
2. External auditors and tax inspectors from the **FIRS** would reject financial accounts.
3. Commercial disputes between buyers and sellers would result in chaotic, unwinnable court battles.

### 2. Major Types of Source Documents

#### A. The Cash Receipt
- **Issued By:** The seller / payee.
- **Received By:** The buyer / payer.
- **Purpose:** Proves immediate cash or electronic settlement. It shows the date, receipt number, payer's name, amount in words and figures, description of payment, and the authorized receiver's signature.

#### B. The Commercial Invoice
- **Issued By:** The seller when goods are sold **on credit**.
- **Contains:** Date of delivery, invoice serial number, buyer's name and address, itemized goods (quantity, unit rate, gross amount), trade discounts granted, Value Added Tax (VAT at 7.5%), and credit payment terms (e.g., *"2% discount if paid within 10 days, net 30 days"*).
- **Pro-forma Invoice Distinction:** A pro-forma invoice is an advance quotation sent before purchase confirmation or delivery to enable the buyer to see costs or apply for foreign exchange/import licenses. It is **not** an entry document in the accounting books.

#### C. Debit Note vs. Credit Note
When goods are damaged in transit, inferior in quality, or incorrectly invoiced, adjustments are made:
- **Debit Note ("We Debit Your Account"):**
  - Usually issued by the **buyer** to the seller when returning defective goods, stating: *"We have debited your account in our books by ₦50,000 for 5 broken crates of juice returned."*
  - Alternatively, issued by a seller if the buyer was accidentally **undercharged** on an initial invoice.
- **Credit Note ("We Credit Your Account"):**
  - Issued by the **seller** to the buyer acknowledging the return of defective merchandise or correcting an invoice overcharge.
  - Traditionally printed in **red ink** to immediately distinguish it from regular sales invoices.
  - Reduces the amount of money the customer owes the seller.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 ADJUSTMENT CYCLE FOR RETURNED GOODS                     │
│                                                                         │
│  BUYER discovers damaged goods ──► Sends DEBIT NOTE with goods          │
│                                           │                             │
│                                           ▼                             │
│  SELLER inspects goods & agrees ──► Issues CREDIT NOTE (in Red Ink)     │
│                                     (Reduces customer's debt in ledger) │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### D. The Cheque
A cheque is a negotiable banking instrument that transfers funds safely without carrying bulky physical cash.
- **Three Essential Parties to a Cheque:**
  1. **The Drawer:** The account holder who writes, signs, and issues the cheque ordering payment.
  2. **The Drawee:** The commercial bank holding the drawer's account that is ordered to pay the money (e.g., Zenith Bank, First Bank).
  3. **The Payee:** The person or organization named on the cheque to receive the payment.
- **Types of Cheques:**
  - **Open (Bearer / Order) Cheque:** A cheque that can be cashed directly over the bank counter by the payee or bearer. Highly risky if lost or stolen.
  - **Crossed Cheque:** A cheque bearing two parallel transverse lines across its face (often with words like *"& Co."* or *"Account Payee Only"*). A crossed cheque **cannot be cashed over the counter**; it must be deposited into the payee's commercial bank account. This creates an unalterable paper trail and prevents unauthorized cashing.

#### E. Bank Pay-in Slip (Deposit Teller)
A pre-printed voucher provided by commercial banks. The depositor records the date, account name, account number, depositor's name, phone number, and a detailed breakdown of currency notes (e.g., number of ₦1,000, ₦500 notes) or cheques being deposited. The bank cashier stamps and signs the duplicate counterfoil, returning it to the depositor as proof of deposit.

#### F. Petty Cash Voucher (PCV)
Used for small, minor office disbursements (such as postage stamps, cleaning detergent, tea supplies, or local bus fare). An employee requesting petty cash writes the purpose and amount, secures approval from the office manager, and signs upon receiving the physical cash from the petty cashier.

### 3. Essential Features Common to All Source Documents
Every valid source document must contain:
1. **Name and Address** of both the issuing and receiving organizations.
2. **Date** on which the transaction occurred.
3. **Serial Number** (pre-printed for chronological control and audit tracking).
4. **Description** of goods or services provided.
5. **Quantity and Unit Price**.
6. **Total Monetary Value** written in both **figures** and **words** (to prevent fraudulent alteration).
7. **Official Signature** of the authorized officer or cashier.

---

### 4. Preservation, Filing, and Statutory Retention
Source documents must be stored systematically using chronological or numerical filing systems:
- **Statutory Mandate:** Under the Nigerian Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) and the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) tax regulations, businesses are legally required to preserve all original accounting source documents for a minimum of **six (6) years**.
- **Digital Archiving:** Modern offices scan paper documents into secure cloud-based document management systems (DMS) while retaining original physical documents in climate-controlled archives to prevent insect damage, humidity decay, or ink fading.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Invoices with Receipts:** An invoice is a bill demanding payment for goods sold on credit; a receipt is issued *only after* payment has been made.
2. **Confusing Debit Notes with Credit Notes:** The buyer sends a debit note to request a deduction; the seller issues the credit note to authorize and execute that deduction.
3. **Thinking Pro-forma Invoices are Recorded in Books:** A pro-forma invoice is merely a quotation; no accounting entry is ever made from a pro-forma invoice.
4. **Believing Crossed Cheques Can Be Cashed Over the Counter:** A crossed cheque can **never** be cashed for physical currency over the counter; it must be cleared through a bank account.

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Identifying the Proper Source Document
*Problem:* Identify the exact source document required for each of the following commercial events in a Kaduna trading company:
1. Emeka buys 10 cartons of biscuits with physical cash of ₦50,000.
2. The company sells 100 bags of cement to Danladi on 30 days credit for ₦550,000.
3. Danladi discovers 10 bags of cement hardened by water leaks and returns them to the warehouse with a request for price reduction.
4. The sales manager accepts Danladi's returned bags and reduces his debt balance.
5. The office messenger buys ₦2,500 worth of toilet soap for the staff restroom using office petty cash.

*Solution:*
1. **Cash Receipt:** Issued to Emeka proving physical payment of ₦50,000.
2. **Sales (Commercial) Invoice:** Sent to Danladi demanding settlement within 30 days.
3. **Debit Note:** Sent by Danladi to inform the company that he has debited their account for the 10 spoiled bags.
4. **Credit Note:** Issued by the company (in red ink) crediting Danladi's account by ₦55,000 to officially reduce his debt.
5. **Petty Cash Voucher:** Signed by the messenger and cashier to account for the ₦2,500 minor expenditure.

---

### Scenario 2: Analyzing the Parties to a Cheque
*Problem:* Mrs. Ngozi Okon, who maintains an account at Zenith Bank, issues a crossed cheque for ₦180,000 to Kazeem Motors to buy vehicle spare parts. Identify the three parties to this cheque and explain how Kazeem Motors will access the money.

*Solution:*
- **The Drawer:** Mrs. Ngozi Okon (the account holder issuing the order).
- **The Drawee:** Zenith Bank (the bank holding Mrs. Okon's funds and ordered to pay).
- **The Payee:** Kazeem Motors (the enterprise entitled to receive the funds).
- *Method of Collection:* Because the cheque is **crossed** (two parallel lines on face), Kazeem Motors cannot withdraw physical cash over the counter. Kazeem Motors must deposit the cheque into its commercial bank account (e.g., at Access Bank or GTBank) through the banking clearing system.
