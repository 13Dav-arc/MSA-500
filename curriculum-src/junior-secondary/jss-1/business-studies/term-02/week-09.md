## Hook
Imagine that you run an active bookshop in Ibadan or Port Harcourt. Every day, customers pay you with paper Naira notes, while schools buying textbooks pay with bank cheques and direct mobile transfers. At the same time, you pay your shop assistant weekly cash, buy petrol for the generator from the cash till, and write a ₦200,000 cheque to a publisher in Lagos. On Friday afternoon, you take ₦150,000 in cash from the shop drawer and deposit it into your bank account; on Monday morning, you withdraw ₦50,000 from the bank ATM to refill the shop cash drawer. If your accounts clerk had to open separate books for every single movement between cash and bank, tracking your money would become an exhausting nightmare! To solve this seamlessly, accountants created the most versatile financial tool in business: **The Cash Book**. Uniquely, the Cash Book functions as **both** a Book of Original Entry and a Ledger Account! In this lesson, you will master single and two-column cash books, conquer the mystery of contra entries, learn how to balance accounts, and unlock your 80% Term 2 Formative Mastery Gate!

## Key Terms
- Term: Cash Book
- Definition: A financial accounting book used to record all receipts and payments of money (both physical cash and bank transactions) in chronological order.
- Term: Dual Nature of the Cash Book
- Definition: The unique accounting property whereby the Cash Book serves simultaneously as a Book of Original Entry (prime entry) and as a Ledger Account (Cash Account and Bank Account).
- Term: Single-Column Cash Book
- Definition: A basic cash book with only one monetary column on both the debit and credit sides, used exclusively for recording physical cash transactions.
- Term: Two-Column Cash Book
- Definition: A cash book containing two separate monetary columns—**Cash** and **Bank**—on both the debit and credit sides to track currency in hand and bank transactions simultaneously.
- Term: Contra Entry
- Definition: A transaction that affects both the Cash Account and the Bank Account within the same business simultaneously, marked with a **"C"** in the folio column and requiring no posting to external ledgers.
- Term: Balance Carried Down (c/d)
- Definition: The balancing figure inserted on the smaller side of an account at the close of an accounting period to make both debit and credit totals equal.
- Term: Balance Brought Down (b/d)
- Definition: The opening balance brought forward to the beginning of the new accounting period on the opposite side of the balance c/d.
- Term: Bank Overdraft
- Definition: A negative bank balance occurring when a commercial bank allows a current account holder to withdraw more money than is currently held in the account, resulting in a credit balance in the bank column.
- Term: Dishonoured Cheque
- Definition: A cheque that a paying commercial bank (drawee) refuses to honor or pay upon presentation due to insufficient funds, signature discrepancies, or irregular dates.
- Term: Term 2 Mastery Gate (80% Benchmark)
- Definition: The summative academic standard requiring learners to demonstrate 80% mastery across all Term 2 clerical and bookkeeping competencies before advancing to official terminal examination ranking.

### 2. Layout and Structure: Debit vs. Credit Sides
The Cash Book is divided into two symmetrical halves:
- **Debit Side (Left / DR):** The **Receipts side**. Every time physical cash or bank money comes *into* the business (cash sales, money received from debtors, bank loans, capital introduced), it is entered on the **Debit side**.
- **Credit Side (Right / CR):** The **Payments side**. Every time physical cash or bank money leaves the business (buying goods for cash, paying creditors by cheque, paying salaries, paying rent), it is entered on the **Credit side**.

```
  DEBIT SIDE (RECEIPTS - MONEY IN)       │     CREDIT SIDE (PAYMENTS - MONEY OUT)
┌──────┬───────────┬─────┬──────┬───────┼──────┬───────────┬─────┬──────┬───────┐
│ Date │Particulars│V.No.│Folio │Cash(₦)│ Date │Particulars│V.No.│Folio │Cash(₦)│
└──────┴───────────┴─────┴──────┴───────┴──────┴───────────┴─────┴──────┴───────┴───────┘
```

### 3. The Two-Column Cash Book (Cash & Bank)
Modern businesses operate both a cash register (till) for small currency notes and a commercial bank checking account. The Two-Column Cash Book provides two monetary columns on each side:
- **Cash Column:** Logs all physical currency notes and coins.
- **Bank Column:** Logs all cheques received/issued and electronic bank transfers (NIBSS, POS, mobile banking).

```
                                  TWO-COLUMN CASH BOOK
  DR (RECEIPTS)                                                    CR (PAYMENTS)
┌──────┬───────────┬───┬────────┬────────┬──────┬───────────┬───┬────────┬────────┐
│ Date │Particulars│Fol│Cash (₦)│Bank (₦)│ Date │Particulars│Fol│Cash (₦)│Bank (₦)│
├──────┼───────────┼───┼────────┼────────┼──────┼───────────┼───┼────────┼────────┤
│ 2026 │           │   │        │        │ 2026 │           │   │        │        │

### 4. Contra Entries Explained
A **Contra Entry** (derived from the Latin word *contra*, meaning "against" or "opposite") occurs when a transaction is an internal transfer of funds between Cash and Bank within the same business.

There are only **two standard scenarios** for contra entries:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE TWO CONTRA ENTRY SCENARIOS                       │
├────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┤
│ SCENARIO A: Cash Withdrawn from    │ SCENARIO B: Cash Deposited into   │
│ Bank for Office Use                │ Bank Account                      │
├────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Debit: Cash Column (Cash enters) │ • Debit: Bank Column (Bank enters)│
│ • Credit: Bank Column (Bank pays)  │ • Credit: Cash Column (Cash pays) │
│ • Particulars: "Bank" on DR side   │ • Particulars: "Cash" on DR side  │
│   and "Cash" on CR side            │   and "Bank" on CR side           │
│ • Folio: Insert "C" on both sides  │ • Folio: Insert "C" on both sides │
└────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Crucial Rule for Contra Entries:**
Because the debit entry is recorded in the Cash column and the credit entry is recorded in the Bank column within the *same* Cash Book, the double-entry is **100% complete**. No further posting to any external ledger account is permitted! A bold capital **"C"** is written in the Folio column on both sides to notify auditors that the entry is a self-contained internal contra.

*Warning:* If cash is withdrawn from the bank for the owner's **personal/private use**, this is **NOT** a contra entry! It is classified as **Drawings** (Debit Drawings Account, Credit Bank Column).

### 5. Dishonoured Cheques
A dishonoured (or "bounced") cheque is a cheque returned unpaid by the bank.
- **Common Causes:**
  1. Insufficient funds in the drawer's account (*"Refer to Drawer"*).
  2. Mismatch between the signature on the cheque and the bank's mandate card.
  3. Stale cheque (dated more than 6 months prior to presentation).
  4. Post-dated cheque (dated in the future).
  5. Difference between amounts written in words and figures.
  6. Cheque is mutilated, torn, or has unendorsed alterations.
- **Accounting Treatment:** When a customer's cheque was originally received, it was debited to the Bank column. When dishonoured, the entry must be **reversed**: enter the amount on the **Credit side of the Bank column** and re-debit the customer's account in the Sales Ledger.

### 6. Step-by-Step Balancing of the Cash Book
At the end of every week or month, the Cash Book must be balanced:
1. **Total Cash and Bank columns** on scrap paper.
2. **Determine the greater side:** For Cash, the Debit side is *always* equal to or greater than the Credit side (you can never spend physical cash you do not have!). For Bank, the Credit side may occasionally be greater if the bank allowed an **overdraft**.
3. **Calculate the difference** between total receipts and total payments.
4. **Enter the difference** on the smaller side as **"Balance c/d"** (carried down).
5. **Draw single lines above and double lines below** the grand totals, ensuring totals on both sides sit on the exact same horizontal line.
6. **Bring down the balance** to the opposite side below the double lines as **"Balance b/d"** (brought down) with the date of the next accounting period.

```
DR (Receipts)                                                    CR (Payments)
┌──────┬───────────┬───┬────────┬────────┬──────┬───────────┬───┬────────┬────────┐
│ Date │Particulars│Fol│Cash (₦)│Bank (₦)│ Date │Particulars│Fol│Cash (₦)│Bank (₦)│
├──────┼───────────┼───┼────────┼────────┼──────┼───────────┼───┼────────┼────────┤
│May 01│Balance b/d│   │  80,000│ 400,000│May 12│Wages      │   │  30,000│        │
│May 10│Sales      │   │  50,000│ 150,000│May 20│Equipment  │   │        │ 200,000│
│      │           │   │        │        │May 31│Balance c/d│   │ 100,000│ 350,000│

## Comparative Matrix
| Feature | Single-Column Cash Book | Two-Column Cash Book |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Monetary Columns** | One column (Cash) on each side | Two columns (Cash & Bank) on each side |
| **Types of Money Tracked** | Physical currency notes & coins only | Currency notes, cheques, & digital bank transfers |
| **Contra Entries** | Cannot handle contra entries | Handles internal cash-to-bank transfers with **"C"** |
| **Bank Overdrafts** | Cannot show bank overdrafts | Bank column can reflect credit overdraft balances |
| **Suitability** | Small roadside kiosks & market stalls | Registered commercial enterprises & modern firms |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Writing Particulars Wrongly for Contra Entries:** When cash is deposited into bank, learners often write "Bank" in the bank column! The golden rule is: on the Debit side (Bank column), write **"Cash"** in Particulars (meaning cash came into bank); on the Credit side (Cash column), write **"Bank"** in Particulars (meaning cash went into bank).
2. **Posting Contra Entries to External Ledgers:** Contra entries are self-contained. Never create an extra ledger account for a contra entry!
3. **Assuming Cash Can Have a Credit Balance:** Cash in hand can NEVER be negative. The Credit side of the Cash column can never exceed the Debit side.
4. **Treating Drawings as a Contra Entry:** Withdrawing business cash or bank money for personal or household use is a Debit to Drawings, NOT a contra entry.

## Worked Examples

### Scenario: Recording and Balancing a Two-Column Cash Book
*Problem:* Record the following transactions in the Two-Column Cash Book of Folake Ventures for January 2026, and balance the book on January 31:
- Jan 01: Balances brought forward: Cash ₦40,000; Bank ₦250,000.
- Jan 05: Cash sales of provisions ₦60,000.
- Jan 10: Paid office electricity bill by cheque ₦25,000.
- Jan 15: Withdrew ₦50,000 cash from the commercial bank for office till use.
- Jan 22: Paid cleaner wages in cash ₦20,000.
- Jan 28: Deposited ₦30,000 cash from office till into commercial bank account.

*Solution:*
- *Jan 01:* Enter on Debit side: Balance b/d: Cash ₦40,000, Bank ₦250,000.
- *Jan 05:* Enter on Debit side: Particulars: Sales, Cash column ₦60,000.
- *Jan 10:* Enter on Credit side: Particulars: Electricity, Bank column ₦25,000.
- *Jan 15 (Contra Entry):* 
  - Debit side: Particulars: "Bank", Folio: "C", Cash column ₦50,000.
  - Credit side: Particulars: "Cash", Folio: "C", Bank column ₦50,000.
- *Jan 22:* Enter on Credit side: Particulars: Wages, Cash column ₦20,000.
- *Jan 28 (Contra Entry):*
  - Debit side: Particulars: "Cash", Folio: "C", Bank column ₦30,000.
  - Credit side: Particulars: "Bank", Folio: "C", Cash column ₦30,000.

*Balancing Calculations on Jan 31:*
- **Cash Column:**
  - Total Debit = ₦40,000 + ₦60,000 + ₦50,000 = ₦150,000.
  - Credit outlays = ₦20,000 + ₦30,000 = ₦50,000.
  - Difference (Balance c/d) = ₦150,000 - ₦50,000 = **₦100,000** (entered on Credit side).
- **Bank Column:**
  - Total Debit = ₦250,000 + ₦30,000 = ₦280,000.
  - Credit outlays = ₦25,000 + ₦50,000 = ₦75,000.
  - Difference (Balance c/d) = ₦280,000 - ₦75,000 = **₦205,000** (entered on Credit side).
- *Feb 01:* Bring balances down to Debit side: Balance b/d: Cash ₦100,000; Bank ₦205,000.
