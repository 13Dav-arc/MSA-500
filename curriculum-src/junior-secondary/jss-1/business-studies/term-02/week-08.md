## Hook
Imagine that an active distributor of consumer provisions in Aba or Onitsha handles over 800 individual credit sales and purchases every single day! If the accounts clerk immediately opened the main General Ledger and scribbled every single transaction directly into ledger accounts, the ledger would instantly become a massive, cluttered, illegible disaster of thousands of scattered numbers. If an auditor later asked, *"Which customer bought 50 cartons of milk on the morning of October 12th?"* the accountant would have to search blindly through hundreds of bulky ledger pages! To avoid this chaos, accountants invented a brilliant, orderly staging area: **Books of Original Entry** (also called **Subsidiary Books**, **Day Books**, or **Books of Prime Entry**). Before any credit transaction ever touches a ledger, it is first logged in chronological order in a designated Day Book. In this lesson, you will master the seven subsidiary books, understand their rulings, and discover how trade discounts work!

## Key Terms
- Term: Books of Original Entry
- Definition: The initial accounting books (also called Subsidiary Books or Day Books) in which financial transactions are first recorded in chronological order from source documents before being posted to the ledgers.
- Term: Sales Day Book (Sales Journal)
- Definition: A subsidiary book used exclusively to record goods (merchandise / inventory) sold to customers on credit.
- Term: Purchases Day Book (Purchases Journal)
- Definition: A subsidiary book used exclusively to record goods (merchandise / trading inventory) bought from suppliers on credit.
- Term: Returns Inwards Book (Sales Returns Journal)
- Definition: A subsidiary book used to record goods previously sold on credit that are returned by customers due to defects, damages, or incorrect specifications.
- Term: Returns Outwards Book (Purchases Returns Journal)
- Definition: A subsidiary book used to record goods previously bought on credit that are returned to suppliers.
- Term: General Journal (Journal Proper)
- Definition: A subsidiary book used to record unusual or non-routine transactions that do not fit into any other specialized day book (e.g., credit purchase or sale of fixed assets, opening entries, error corrections).
- Term: Folio (Ledger Folio - LF)
- Definition: A reference column in an accounting book that indicates the specific page number in the ledger where the corresponding entry has been posted.
- Term: Trade Discount
- Definition: A percentage deduction granted by a seller off the catalog or list price of goods to encourage bulk buying or trade customers, deducted directly on the invoice.
- Term: Cash Discount
- Definition: A financial incentive granted to credit buyers to encourage prompt payment within a specified period (e.g., 2% discount if paid within 10 days).
- Term: Narration
- Definition: A brief, concise explanatory note written below an entry in the General Journal explaining the nature and authorization of the transaction.

## Core Concept
### 1. The Meaning and Necessity of Books of Original Entry
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Books of Original Entry" caption="Books of Original Entry: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

A Book of Original Entry is the **first port of call** in the accounting process. When transactions occur, source documents are gathered, verified, and logged chronologically in specialized day books before any entries are posted to the ledger.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               THE FLOW OF FINANCIAL TRANSACTIONS                       │
│                                                                        │
│   [Source Document] (Invoice, Credit Note, Cheque, Receipt)            │
│             │                                                          │
│             ▼                                                          │
│   [BOOK OF ORIGINAL ENTRY] (Prime Entry / Subsidiary Book)             │
│   - Daily chronological record                                         │
│   - Provides transaction narrative and breakdown                       │
│             │                                                          │
│             ▼                                                          │
│   [THE LEDGER] (Principal Book of Double Entry)                        │
│   - Permanent classified accounts (Debit & Credit)                     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Why Do We Need Subsidiary Books Instead of Direct Ledger Posting?**
1. **Prevents Ledger Overcrowding:** Summarizes hundreds of daily transactions into periodic totals, keeping the main ledger neat and manageable.
2. **Division of Labor:** Multiple accounts clerks can work simultaneously—one manages the Sales Day Book, another manages the Purchases Day Book, and another handles the Cash Book.
3. **Chronological History:** Provides a clear, day-by-day record of transactions with invoice numbers and explanations.
4. **Error Detection:** Facilitates easy auditing and verification against original physical source documents.

### 2. The Seven Subsidiary Books of Prime Entry

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE 7 BOOKS OF ORIGINAL ENTRY                        │
├───────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ SUBSIDIARY BOOK               │ TRANSACTIONS RECORDED                  │
├───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Sales Day Book             │ Credit sales of trading goods ONLY     │
│ 2. Purchases Day Book         │ Credit purchases of trading goods ONLY │
│ 3. Returns Inwards Book       │ Credit sales returned by customers     │
│ 4. Returns Outwards Book      │ Credit purchases returned to suppliers │
│ 5. Cash Book                  │ All cash and bank receipts & payments  │
│ 6. Petty Cash Book            │ Minor, incidental office cash expenses │
│ 7. General Journal            │ All other non-routine transactions     │
└───────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### A. Sales Day Book (Sales Journal)
- **Source Document Used:** Duplicate copies of **Sales Invoices** sent to credit buyers.
- **Rule of Inclusion:** Only records goods bought specifically for resale (merchandise/stock) sold **on credit**. 
- *Crucial Exception:* Cash sales are NEVER recorded in the Sales Day Book (they go to the Cash Book). Credit sales of fixed assets (e.g., selling an old office delivery van on credit) are NEVER recorded here (they go to the General Journal).

#### B. Purchases Day Book (Purchases Journal)
- **Source Document Used:** Original **Purchases Invoices** received from suppliers.
- **Rule of Inclusion:** Only records goods intended for resale bought **on credit**.
- *Crucial Exception:* Cash purchases go to the Cash Book. Buying office furniture, computers, or machinery on credit goes to the General Journal.

#### C. Returns Inwards Book (Sales Returns Journal)
- **Source Document Used:** **Credit Notes** issued to customers (or Debit Notes received from them).
- **Rule of Inclusion:** Records goods returned by customers due to spoilage, breakage, or wrong specification.

#### D. Returns Outwards Book (Purchases Returns Journal)
- **Source Document Used:** **Debit Notes** sent to suppliers (or Credit Notes received from them).
- **Rule of Inclusion:** Records defective or incorrect goods returned by our business to suppliers.

#### E. Cash Book & Petty Cash Book
- **Cash Book:** Holds a unique dual identity—it is both a book of prime entry and a ledger account. Records all receipts and payments made via cash or bank accounts.
- **Petty Cash Book:** Uses the Imprest System to log minor outlays (stamps, envelopes, local transport).

#### F. The General Journal (Journal Proper)
The General Journal is the "master diary" or general dustbin for transactions that cannot fit into any of the six specialized subsidiary books. Its functions include:
1. **Purchase or Sale of Non-Current (Fixed) Assets on Credit:** Buying an office generator on credit from Mikano Motors.
2. **Opening Entries:** Recording assets, liabilities, and capital when a new business starts operations or begins a new financial year.
3. **Correction of Accounting Errors:** Reversing wrongly posted entries.
4. **Closing & Transfer Entries:** Moving revenue and expense balances to the Trading, Profit & Loss Account at year-end.

### 4. Trade Discount vs. Cash Discount

| Feature | Trade Discount | Cash Discount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Purpose** | Encourages bulk purchases and rewards loyal traders | Encourages early/prompt payment of credit invoices |
| **When Granted** | At the point of sale/invoicing | At the point of debt payment |
| **Calculation** | Deducted immediately from the list price on the invoice | Deducted from the invoice total if paid within terms |
| **Ledger Appearance** | **Never appears in the ledger!** Only net amount is entered | **Recorded in the ledger** (Discount Allowed or Received) |
| **Accounting Book** | Subtracted in the Sales or Purchases Day Book | Recorded in the Cash Book (discount columns) |

## Comparative Matrix
| Subsidiary Book | Source Document Used | Nature of Transactions Recorded | Periodic Total Transferred To |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sales Day Book** | Outgoing Sales Invoice | Credit sales of merchandise inventory | Credit side of **Sales Account** in General Ledger |
| **Purchases Day Book**| Incoming Purchases Invoice | Credit purchases of merchandise inventory | Debit side of **Purchases Account** in General Ledger |
| **Returns Inwards Book**| Credit Note issued | Merchandise returned by credit customers | Debit side of **Returns Inwards Account** in General Ledger|
| **Returns Outwards Book**| Debit Note / Credit Note received| Merchandise returned to credit suppliers | Credit side of **Returns Outwards Account** in General Ledger|
| **General Journal** | Journal Vouchers, Contracts | Non-routine transactions, fixed assets on credit | Respective Debit and Credit accounts in Ledgers |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Recording Cash Sales in the Sales Day Book:** The Sales Day Book is strictly for *credit* sales of stock. Cash sales go directly to the Cash Book.
2. **Recording Purchase of Fixed Assets in Purchases Day Book:** Buying a delivery truck or computer on credit is NOT a purchase of merchandise. It must be logged in the General Journal.
3. **Posting Trade Discount into a Ledger Account:** Trade discount is deducted immediately on the invoice and day book; there is no such account as "Trade Discount Account" in the ledger.
4. **Omitting the Narration in the General Journal:** Every entry in the General Journal must have a concise narration beneath it explaining the transaction.

## Worked Examples

### Scenario: Logging Transactions in Books of Prime Entry
*Problem:* Classify the appropriate Book of Original Entry for each of the following business events undertaken by Kalu Superstores in Aba:
1. Sold 20 cartons of detergent to Mrs. Ada on 30 days credit for ₦80,000.
2. Purchased 50 bags of flour on credit from Golden Flour Mills for ₦1,500,000.
3. Sold 5 bags of rice for ₦250,000 cash.
4. Mrs. Ada returned 2 damaged cartons of detergent valued at ₦8,000.
5. Returned 3 defective bags of flour to Golden Flour Mills valued at ₦90,000.
6. Bought a new office photocopy machine on credit from Sharp Tech Ltd for ₦450,000.

*Solution:*
1. **Sales Day Book:** Mrs. Ada bought goods on credit.
2. **Purchases Day Book:** Flour is merchandise bought on credit.
3. **Cash Book:** This is a cash transaction; it bypasses day books and goes straight to the Cash Book.
4. **Returns Inwards Book:** Goods returned by a customer.
5. **Returns Outwards Book:** Defective goods returned to a supplier.
6. **General Journal:** A photocopier is a non-current (fixed) office asset, not trading stock. Its credit purchase must be logged in the General Journal with a narration.

## Guardian Sync
- **Family Business Observation:** If you or a family friend run a retail shop, pharmacy, or wholesale store, ask them to show your learner the physical notebook or software where they list daily credit sales ("those owing us").
- **Invoice & Receipt Comparison:** Review household purchase receipts and show your learner why buying groceries for the home is a cash transaction, whereas a company ordering 50 reams of paper on credit generates an invoice and goes into a day book.
- **Math Connection:** Practice calculating trade discounts at home: e.g., *"If 10 bags of rice cost ₦450,000 and the seller gives a 10% trade discount, what is the net invoice amount?"* (Answer: ₦450,000 - ₦45,000 = ₦405,000).

### Q14 [single-choice]
