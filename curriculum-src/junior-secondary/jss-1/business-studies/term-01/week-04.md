## Hook
Have you ever visited a government office or a local business where the receptionist arrives an hour late, chews gum while scrolling through social media, ignores waiting clients, and snaps angrily when asked a simple question? How did you feel about that business? You probably felt frustrated, insulted, and vowed never to return! Now imagine another office where the staff member greets you with a warm smile at 8:00 AM sharp, swiftly finds your file, addresses your request with total dedication, and thanks you for visiting. Which of these two businesses do you think will thrive, attract wealthy investors, and expand across Nigeria? The dramatic difference between commercial failure and glorious success does not lie in giant buildings or expensive computers; it lies in the human heart and mind—a concept known as the **Right Attitude to Work**! In this lesson, you will discover the core attributes of professional work ethics, explore why punctuality and diligence build legendary careers, and uncover the disastrous consequences of negative workplace attitudes!

## Key Terms
- Term: Attitude
- Definition: A settled way of thinking, feeling, or behavioral disposition that influences how an individual perceives and responds to people, tasks, and situations.
- Term: Right Attitude to Work
- Definition: A positive, committed, ethical, and diligent psychological disposition characterized by hard work, honesty, punctuality, and loyalty towards assigned professional duties.
- Term: Punctuality
- Definition: The strict habit of arriving at work, official meetings, and appointments on or before the designated scheduled time.
- Term: Regularity
- Definition: Consistent, dependable attendance at one's duty post every working day, avoiding frivolous absenteeism or abandonment of work.
- Term: Diligence
- Definition: Careful, thorough, energetic, and persistent effort applied to assigned tasks to ensure high-quality execution without carelessness.
- Term: Devotion to Duty
- Definition: Deep personal commitment, dedication, and loyalty to one's responsibilities, often willingly going beyond minimum job expectations to ensure the firm's success.
- Term: Eye-Service
- Definition: The hypocritical, deceitful habit of pretending to work hard only when a supervisor or boss is watching, but becoming lazy and idle the moment the supervisor leaves.
- Term: Absenteeism
- Definition: The chronic, unjustified practice of staying away from work or duty post without valid medical or official permission.
- Term: Productivity
- Definition: The measure of economic output generated per unit of input (such as labor hours, materials, or capital) over a specified period.
- Term: Corporate Goodwill
- Definition: The established reputation, positive public perception, and customer trust earned by a business through consistent excellence, integrity, and courteous service.

### 2. Key Attributes of the Right Attitude to Work

#### A. Punctuality
- Arriving at the office at least 15 minutes before official resumption time.
- Allows the worker to settle down, plan daily tasks, and organize files before clients arrive.
- Demonstrates respect for employers, colleagues, and customers.

#### B. Regularity
- Consistent presence at the workplace throughout the working year.
- Frivolous excuses ("it was raining," "I woke up late") disrupt team workflows and overburden colleagues who must cover extra shifts.

#### C. Diligence and Meticulousness
- Paying strict attention to operational accuracy and detail.
- Checking figures twice, proofreading letters before printing, and following company procedures without taking sloppy shortcuts.

#### D. Honesty and Integrity
- Handling cash, inventories, and company assets with total transparency.
- Refusing bribes, kickbacks, or gifts designed to compromise professional judgment.
- Giving an honest day's labor for an honest day's pay (avoiding "time theft," such as sleeping on duty or running personal errands during office hours).

#### E. Devotion and Commitment
- Viewing the company's success as one's personal success.
- Remaining willing to work extra hours during emergencies or peak periods without complaining.

#### F. Politeness and Team Spirit
- Cooperating harmoniously with colleagues across all departments.
- Treating visitors, clients, and superiors with warm courtesy, active listening, and respect.

### 4. Negative Attitudes to Work and Their Consequences
When employees adopt toxic workplace habits, the results are catastrophic for both the individual and the organization:

#### Manifestations of Negative Work Attitudes
- **Tardiness & Habitual Late-Coming:** Sneaking into the office late and rushing out early.
- **Chronic Absenteeism:** Taking unauthorized sick leaves, feigning family emergencies, and abandoning one's desk.
- **Eye-Service:** Feverishly typing on a computer only when the manager walks past, but browsing social media or chatting the moment they turn their back.
- **Carelessness & Sloppiness:** Producing documents full of spelling errors, losing customer files, and mishandling equipment.
- **Insubordination & Rudeness:** Disobeying lawful instructions from superiors and insulting customers.
- **Corruption & Pilfering:** Demanding bribes from customers before rendering legitimate service, stealing office stationery, or forging payment receipts.

#### Severe Consequences of Negative Work Attitudes
- **To the Individual:** Disciplinary queries, loss of annual salary increments, demotion, summary dismissal (sacking), ruined professional reputation, and potential criminal prosecution.
- **To the Organization:** Drastic plunge in production, missed deadlines, customer exodus to competitors, financial losses, and ultimate business bankruptcy.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing that coming late to work is acceptable as long as you stay late in the evening.
  *Correction:* Customers and workflow schedules operate during official business hours. Arriving late delays teammates, misses morning client calls, and demonstrates unreliability.
- **Mistake 2:** Thinking that eye-service is a clever way to impress the boss without working hard.
  *Correction:* Supervisors evaluate tangible, measurable business results (sales figures, processed files, customer satisfaction), not mere physical posturing. Eye-service always results in poor performance records.
- **Mistake 3:** Assuming that using company printers and paper for personal business is a harmless perk.
  *Correction:* Using employer resources for private use without permission is theft of company property and a serious violation of workplace integrity.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking that attitude matters less than academic certificates.
  *Correction:* Employers universally agree that while certificates get you an interview, your **attitude to work** determines whether you keep the job, get promoted, or get fired.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Evaluating Workplace Performance and Attitude
*Scenario:* Apex Microfinance Bank in Ilorin reviews two junior loan clerks for an upcoming promotion to Senior Credit Officer:
- **Clerk A (Ade):** Has a university degree with Second Class Upper. However, he is frequently late by 20–30 minutes, spends hours watching videos on his phone when the branch manager is out, and recently shouted at an elderly market woman who did not understand loan terms.
- **Clerk B (Bola):** Has an Ordinary National Diploma (OND). She arrives at 7:45 AM daily, meticulously double-checks loan calculations, treats all customers with patience and empathy, and willingly stays late during month-end account reconciliations.

*Task:* As the Human Resources Manager, evaluate both candidates and decide who should receive the promotion based on workplace attitude.

*Evaluation & Decision:*
- **Evaluation of Clerk A:** Despite his strong academic credentials, Ade displays serious negative work attitudes: **tardiness**, **eye-service**, **carelessness**, and **gross lack of tact/customer courtesy**. Promoting him would destroy customer goodwill and demoralize staff.
- **Evaluation of Clerk B:** Bola exhibits the **Right Attitude to Work**: **punctuality**, **diligence**, **devotion to duty**, and **exemplary customer care**. She creates value and reflects the bank's corporate ideals.
- **Decision:** **Clerk B (Bola) receives the promotion!** In the business world, character, diligence, and positive attitude outweigh mere paper qualifications.

### Example 2: Analyzing the Financial Impact of Absenteeism
*Scenario:* A garment manufacturing enterprise in Aba employs 20 tailors. Each tailor produces an average of 10 school uniforms per day, generating a net profit of ₦1,500 per uniform for the company.
- Tailor Kalu was absent without permission for 8 working days during the month of August.
- Tailor Ngozi had 100% attendance (22 working days).

*Tasks:*
1. How many uniforms did Ngozi produce in August, and how much profit did she generate?
2. How many potential uniforms and how much profit did the firm lose due to Kalu's 8 days of absenteeism?

*Solution:*
1. **Ngozi's Production & Profit:**
   $$\text{Uniforms Produced} = 22 \text{ days} \times 10 \text{ uniforms/day} = 220 \text{ uniforms}.$$
   $$\text{Profit Generated} = 220 \times ₦1,500 = ₦330,000.$$
2. **Kalu's Lost Production & Profit:**
   $$\text{Uniforms Lost} = 8 \text{ days absent} \times 10 \text{ uniforms/day} = 80 \text{ uniforms}.$$
   $$\text{Lost Enterprise Profit} = 80 \times ₦1,500 = ₦120,000.$$
*Conclusion:* Kalu's absenteeism directly cost the enterprise ₦120,000 in lost profits and delayed orders for school pupils, illustrating why absenteeism is severely penalized by employers.
