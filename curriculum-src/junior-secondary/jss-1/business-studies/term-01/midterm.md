## Core Concept
### 1. The Commercial Framework (Weeks 01–02)
- **The Business Ecosystem:** Business involves creating, distributing, and selling goods and services to satisfy human wants and earn a profit ($\text{Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses}$).
- **The Five Pillars:** 
  1. *Commerce:* Trade and aids to trade (banking, insurance, transport, warehousing, advertising).
  2. *Bookkeeping:* Systematic logging of daily financial transactions.
  3. *Office Practice:* Administrative routines, clerical filing, and communication.
  4. *Keyboarding:* Rapid, accurate touch-typing and document layout.
  5. *Marketing:* Customer research, pricing, promotion, and distribution.
- **The Office Engine:** The office is the organizational brain fulfilling six vital functions: Receiving, Recording, Processing, Storing, Giving Out Information, and Safeguarding Assets.
- **Layout Choices:**
  - *Open-Plan:* Cost-effective, easy supervision, flexible, but noisy and lacks privacy.
  - *Closed Office:* High confidentiality, quiet, prestigious, but expensive and isolates communication.

---

### 2. Administrative People and Ethics (Weeks 03–04)
- **Departmental Roles:**
  - *Management:* Overall corporate policy and direction.
  - *Human Resources (HR):* Workforce recruitment, training, promotions, welfare.
  - *Accounts:* Cash management, payroll calculation, invoicing, auditing.
  - *Purchasing:* Sourcing raw materials, vendor negotiations, purchase orders.
  - *Marketing & Sales:* Revenue generation, promotions, customer fulfillment.
- **Clerical Excellence:** Clerks (general, accounts, filing, sales, mail) form the operational foundation. Outstanding clerks embody **absolute confidentiality**, **honesty/integrity**, **punctuality**, **diligence**, and **tact**.
- **The Right Attitude to Work:** Punctuality, regularity, meticulous effort, and loyalty drive organizational productivity and personal career advancement. In contrast, negative work habits like **eye-service**, **tardiness**, and **absenteeism** cause operational errors, customer flight, and business collapse.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing that an open-plan office is always superior because modern tech companies use it.
  *Correction:* While open offices foster creative collaboration, they are unsuitable for roles requiring strict privacy (auditing, HR disciplining, legal negotiations).
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming that bookkeeping and accounting mean the exact same thing.
  *Correction:* Bookkeeping is the clerical recording of daily monetary transactions; Accounting is the higher-level analytical auditing and interpretation of financial data.
- **Mistake 3:** Thinking that an employee who practices eye-service is harmless as long as they appear polite.
  *Correction:* Eye-service is deceitful and toxic; it creates backlogs, causes calculation errors, and destroys organizational productivity.
- **Mistake 4:** Calculating profit without deducting overhead costs like rent, utilities, and transport.
  *Correction:* True net profit requires subtracting all operating expenses and cost of goods sold from gross sales turnover.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Calculating Business Net Profit
*Scenario:* Hajia Amina operates a commercial stationery and typing center in Zaria. For the month of October:
- Total cash received from document typing, printing, and sales of stationery: ₦250,000.
- Cost of paper, ink, and stationery stock bought: ₦120,000.
- Shop electricity and generator fuel bill: ₦25,000.
- Shop rent: ₦20,000.
- Salary paid to the junior typist: ₦35,000.

*Tasks:*
1. Calculate the total operating expenses.
2. Determine Hajia Amina's net profit for October.

*Solution:*
1. $\text{Total Expenses} = ₦120,000 + ₦25,000 + ₦20,000 + ₦35,000 = ₦200,000$.
2. $\text{Net Profit} = \text{Sales Revenue} - \text{Total Expenses}$
   $$\text{Net Profit} = ₦250,000 - ₦200,000 = ₦50,000.$$
*Conclusion:* Hajia Amina achieved a net profit of ₦50,000 for October.

### Example 2: Resolving an Interdepartmental Conflict
*Scenario:* In a flour mill in Kano, the Purchasing Manager bought 500 bags of wheat without consulting the Accounts Department or obtaining an approved purchase budget. When the wheat delivery truck arrived, the Accounts Manager refused to sign the cheque to pay the driver, causing the driver to block the factory gate.
- What administrative mistake was made?
- What document should have been approved before the wheat was ordered?

*Analysis & Resolution:*
- **Administrative Mistake:** The Purchasing Manager bypassed proper administrative channels and budgetary controls. Purchases must be coordinated with Accounts to ensure funds are allocated and available.
- **Required Document:** The Purchasing Manager should have issued an **Internal Purchase Requisition** and received an authorized **Purchase Order (PO)** counter-signed by Accounts before committing the company to the supplier.
