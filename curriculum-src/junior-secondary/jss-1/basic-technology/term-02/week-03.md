## Hook
A precision drafting tool is only as reliable as the care given to it by the draftsman[cite: 2]. A single nick on the straightedge of a tee-square can distort every horizontal line across an engineering plan, while a bent divider point or a scarred drawing board surface can ruin delicate paper and destroy millimeter accuracy[cite: 2]. Drawing instruments are precision investments designed to last for decades if handled with disciplined craftsmanship[cite: 2]. Learning how to clean, handle, protect, and store your drawing boards, acrylic curves, compasses, and blades ensures that every line you draw remains clean, precise, and professional[cite: 2].

## Key Terms
- Term: Instrument Maintenance
  Definition: The systematic care, cleaning, and proper storage of drafting equipment to preserve accuracy and prolong service life[cite: 2].
- Term: Working Edge
  Definition: The perfectly straight, true edge along the left side of a drawing board or along the blade of a tee-square that guides straight lines[cite: 2].
- Term: Acrylic Instruments
  Definition: Drafting tools manufactured from transparent plastic or acrylic resin (such as set-squares, protractors, and French curves) that are sensitive to heat, scratching, and impact[cite: 2].
- Term: Alignment True
  Definition: The state of an instrument remaining free from warping, bending, or angular distortion so that lines drawn with it remain mathematically accurate[cite: 2].
- Term: Fitted Case
  Definition: A dedicated protective box or pouch lined with compartments designed to secure compasses, dividers, and spare parts away from dust and mechanical damage[cite: 2].

## Core Concept
### Aspect 1: Importance of Caring for Drawing Instruments and Materials
Technical drawing instruments are precision-engineered devices manufactured to strict dimensional tolerances[cite: 2]. 
- **Prolonging Service Life:** Quality drawing equipment is costly; diligent care prevents premature damage and extends the useful life of the tools over many school years[cite: 2].
- **Preserving Accuracy:** Even microscopic dents on a tee-square blade or a bent compass pivot will introduce angular and linear errors into technical drawings[cite: 2].
- **Cleanliness and Presentation:** Dusty, greasy, or graphite-coated instruments smudge drawing paper, resulting in messy, unreadable drawings[cite: 2].

### Aspect 2: Handling and Care of the Drawing Board
The drawing board provides the flat foundation for all manual drafting[cite: 2]:
1. **Never Use Thumb Pins:** Do not use drawing pins or thumbtacks to fasten paper to the wooden surface[cite: 2]. Pins puncture permanent holes into the wood, creating an uneven surface that snags pencil leads during future work[cite: 2]. Always use adhesive masking tape or metal drawing clips[cite: 2].
2. **Never Cut on the Board Surface:** Do not use razor blades, penknives, or craft cutters to slice paper directly on the face of the board[cite: 2]. Blade cuts create deep grooves that ruin the smooth drawing surface[cite: 2].
3. **Surface Protection:** Keep the board surface covered with clean cardboard or thick wrapping paper when not in use to protect it from dust, ink spills, and scratches[cite: 2].
4. **Safe Storage:** Store drawing boards flat on shelves or vertically in dedicated slotted storage racks inside an equipment cupboard when practical sessions conclude[cite: 2].

### Aspect 3: Handling and Care of the Tee-Square
The tee-square maintains the primary horizontal axis across the drawing sheet[cite: 2]:
1. **Never Misuse as a Walking Stick or Cane:** The tee-square is a delicate instrument, not a plaything or discipline cane[cite: 2]. Dropping it, striking desks with it, or leaning on it as a walking stick loosens the joint between the stock and blade, ruining its $90^\circ$ alignment[cite: 2].
2. **Avoid Cutting Along the Blade Edge:** Never draw a utility knife or razor blade along the transparent working edge of the tee-square blade[cite: 2]. Slicing against the edge causes nicks and irregularities, making it impossible to draw true straight lines[cite: 2].
3. **Hanging for Storage:** Always hang the tee-square vertically by the hole punched at the end of its blade when it is not in use[cite: 2]. Leaving it lying flat on crowded desks or leaning against walls causes the blade to warp over time[cite: 2].
4. **Routine Cleaning:** Regularly wipe down the blade with a clean, lint-free cloth to remove graphite residue and dust before placing it across fresh paper[cite: 2].

### Aspect 4: Handling and Care of Set-Squares, Protractors, Scale Rules, and French Curves
These transparent acrylic instruments require gentle handling to preserve their clear edges and graduated scales[cite: 2]:
1. **Protect Edges from Sharp Blades:** Never run a craft knife or razor blade along the plastic edges of set-squares, scale rules, or French curves[cite: 2]. Sharp blades shave away plastic, destroying straightness and bevel angles[cite: 2].
2. **Keep Away from Heat and Open Flames:** Acrylic plastics warp, soften, and blister when exposed to high temperatures, sunlight on hot windowsills, or open flames[cite: 2]. Warped instruments cannot lie flat on the drawing sheet[cite: 2].
3. **Safe Storage:** Return these instruments to their protective plastic envelopes or folders immediately after use to prevent surface scratching and accidental breakage[cite: 2].
4. **Cleaning:** Wash acrylic tools with lukewarm water and mild soap when they become soiled with graphite; avoid using harsh solvents like thinner, petrol, or acetone, which dissolve plastic[cite: 2].

### Aspect 5: Handling and Care of Compasses, Dividers, and Storage Systems
Compasses and dividers possess hardened steel points that demand safety precautions and regular maintenance[cite: 2]:
1. **Dedicated Container Storage:** When not in use, always store compasses and dividers inside their velvet-lined cases or fitted containers[cite: 2]. This shields the needle points from becoming blunted and prevents accidental injury[cite: 2].
2. **Never Misuse as Paper Holders or Punches:** Never use compasses or dividers as clamps, paper holders, or tools to punch holes through cardboard, desks, or walls[cite: 2]. Forcing the legs apart strains the central friction joint, while punching blunts or bends the needle points[cite: 2].
3. **Joint Tension Maintenance:** Periodically check and gently tighten the small pivot screws at the compass joint using a watchmaker's screwdriver so the legs do not slip open while drawing circles[cite: 2].
4. **Classroom Cupboard Storage:** The drawing laboratory should maintain a dedicated, lockable wooden or metal cupboard where drawing boards, tee-squares, and boxed instrument sets are systematically arranged by class and set number[cite: 2].

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Using a razor blade or penknife to trim drawing paper directly on the drawing board[cite: 2].
  Fact: Cutting on the board leaves deep score lines in the wood that derail pencil tips during future drawings; always trim paper on a dedicated cutting mat[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Using the tee-square as a walking stick, play sword, or cane[cite: 2].
  Fact: Leaning on or hitting objects with a tee-square breaks the joint between the stock and blade, destroying its $90^\circ$ perpendicularity[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Using thumb pins to secure paper to preserve tape[cite: 2].
  Fact: Pins leave permanent holes in the wooden board that catch pencil tips; use drafting tape or metal clips instead[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Storing compasses and dividers loose inside bags or pencil cases[cite: 2].
  Fact: Loose storage allows needle points to blunt, pierce bags, or bend; always store them secured in their fitted cases[cite: 2].

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Establishing a Daily Board Maintenance Routine
Context: A student enters the technical drawing studio for a two-hour practical session and prepares their workstation[cite: 2].
Problem: Outline the correct sequence of steps to inspect, prepare, and protect the drawing board before drafting begins[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Step 1 (Inspection):** Inspect the board surface and working edge for dirt, dried glue, or stray pencil graphite[cite: 2].
- **Step 2 (Cleaning):** Wipe the surface with a clean, dry duster or soft lint-free cloth[cite: 2].
- **Step 3 (Paper Mounting):** Fasten the drawing sheet using four small strips of masking tape or two metal clips; avoid thumb pins[cite: 2].
- **Step 4 (Protection After Work):** When the session concludes, remove tape residue, cover the board with clean cardboard or paper, and place it in the storage cupboard[cite: 2].

### Example B: Restoring and Caring for an Acrylic Set-Square
Context: An acrylic $30^\circ/60^\circ$ set-square is coated with dark pencil graphite, and a student considers cleaning it with chemical paint thinner[cite: 2].
Problem: Evaluate whether paint thinner is safe and prescribe the correct cleaning procedure[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Evaluation:** Chemical paint thinner contains organic solvents (like acetone or toluene) that dissolve, cloud, and crack acrylic plastic; it must never be used[cite: 2].
- **Prescribed Procedure:**
  - Wash the set-square gently in a basin of lukewarm water mixed with a small amount of mild hand soap[cite: 2].
  - Wipe the edges with a soft sponge or cotton cloth to lift graphite marks without scratching the transparent plastic[cite: 2].
  - Dry thoroughly with a clean towel and store in a protective envelope away from direct sunlight and heat[cite: 2].

### Example C: Safe Handling and Adjustment of Compasses
Context: During a circle-drawing exercise, a student notices that the compass legs spread outward under slight pressure, altering the circle radius[cite: 2].
Problem: Identify the cause of the problem and explain the safe maintenance procedure to correct it[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Cause:** The pivot friction screw at the head of the compass has loosened through repeated use[cite: 2].
- **Maintenance Procedure:**
  - Retrieve the small screwdriver provided in the fitted instrument case[cite: 2].
  - Hold the compass legs steady and tighten the central pivot screw until firm resistance is felt, ensuring the legs hold their radius without slipping[cite: 2].
  - Verify that the needle point extends slightly past the pencil lead tip for stable center-point pivoting[cite: 2].
  - Return the compass to its fitted case immediately after use[cite: 2].
