## Hook
When an architect in Lagos designs a bridge, a builder in Kaduna can construct it to the exact millimeter without speaking a single spoken word to the designer[cite: 2]. This is possible because technical drawing serves as a universal graphical language understood by engineers, builders, and technicians across the globe[cite: 2]. Just as a master surgeon relies on sterilized scalpels and forceps, a draftsman relies on precision tools—such as the tee-square, compasses, set-squares, and graded pencils—to turn abstract technical ideas into exact physical realities[cite: 2].

## Key Terms
- Term: Technical Drawing
  Definition: A universal graphical language of communication used among technical personnel that conveys shapes, dimensions, and specifications using standardized lines, curves, symbols, and conventions[cite: 2].
- Term: Drawing Board
  Definition: A smooth, flat wooden platform upon which drawing paper is fastened and aligned for accurate drafting work[cite: 2].
- Term: Tee-Square
  Definition: A T-shaped drawing instrument consisting of a stock and a blade, used primarily for drawing horizontal parallel lines and guiding set-squares[cite: 2].
- Term: Set-Square
  Definition: A triangular drafting instrument made of transparent plastic, available in $30^\circ/60^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ angles, used to draw vertical and inclined lines[cite: 2].
- Term: Compass
  Definition: An instrument with two legs joined by a pivot (one fitted with a pointed steel needle and the other holding a pencil lead), used for drawing circles and arcs[cite: 2].

## Core Concept
### Aspect 1: Definition and Purpose of Technical Drawing
**Technical drawing** is the universal graphical language used for communication among technical people, such as engineers, architects, technicians, and craftsmen[cite: 2]. 
- Unlike artistic drawing, which relies on personal expression, feelings, and aesthetic perception, technical drawing demands mathematical accuracy, dimensional fidelity, and adherence to standardized engineering conventions[cite: 2].
- It communicates the exact shape, size, surface texture, and assembly details of physical objects using lines, curves, symbols, and standard notations[cite: 2].
- A reliable and accurate drawing can only be achieved through continuous practice with the correct selection and application of drawing instruments and materials[cite: 2].

### Aspect 2: Board Platform and Alignment Tools
1. **The Drawing Board:**
   - Provides a flat, rigid wooden surface upon which drawing paper is placed and secured before drafting begins[cite: 2].
   - Constructed from well-seasoned timber to avoid warping and ensure a perfectly straight working edge on the left-hand side[cite: 2].
   - Standard sizes include[cite: 2]:
     - **Full Imperial Size:** $812\text{ mm} \times 585\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
     - **Half Imperial Size:** $585\text{ mm} \times 452\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
2. **The Tee-Square:**
   - Consists of two main parts joined rigidly at a right angle: the **stock** (which slides along the left edge of the drawing board) and the **blade** (which lies across the paper)[cite: 2].
   - Primarily used for drawing horizontal parallel lines in conjunction with the drawing board working edge[cite: 2].
   - Functions as a moving base upon which set-squares are supported to draw vertical and inclined lines[cite: 2].

### Aspect 3: Line Generation, Angular, and Scaling Tools
1. **Set-Squares:**
   - Triangular drafting instruments made of clear plastic[cite: 2].
   - Come in two standard angular varieties[cite: 2]:
     - The **$45^\circ$ Set-Square:** Contains angles of $45^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $90^\circ$[cite: 2].
     - The **$30^\circ/60^\circ$ Set-Square:** Contains angles of $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$[cite: 2].
   - Used sitting squarely on the blade of the tee-square to draw vertical lines ($90^\circ$) and inclined/diagonal lines at specified angles ($30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$)[cite: 2].
2. **Protractor:**
   - A semi-circular ($180^\circ$) or circular ($360^\circ$) transparent instrument graduated in degrees[cite: 2].
   - Used for measuring, setting, and marking out arbitrary angles on drawing paper[cite: 2].
3. **Metric Rule and Scale Rule:**
   - **Metric Rule:** Used for direct linear measurements in millimeters and centimeters[cite: 2].
   - **Scale Rule:** Contains graduated ratios ($1:1$, $1:2$, $1:5$, $1:100$, etc.) used to produce reduced or enlarged representations of physical objects on paper without manual calculation[cite: 2].

### Aspect 4: Precision Transfer and Curved Drafting Tools
1. **Compasses:**
   - An instrument fitted with one pointed metal pivot leg and one pencil-lead holder[cite: 2].
   - Used for drawing circles, circular arcs, and stepped curve radii[cite: 2].
2. **Dividers:**
   - Resembles a compass but possesses two sharp, pointed steel legs[cite: 2].
   - Used for transferring measurements accurately from the metric rule to the drawing paper, or for stepping off equal distances along a straight or curved line[cite: 2].
3. **French Curves and Flexible Curves:**
   - Specially contoured templates made of smooth plastic or bendable lead-core rubber[cite: 2].
   - Used to draw smooth, irregular curved lines that cannot be formed with a standard circular compass[cite: 2].

### Aspect 5: Media, Fasteners, and Consumables
1. **Drawing Paper:**
   - The paper substrate on which technical representations are drafted[cite: 2]. Standard international ISO sizes include[cite: 2]:
     - **$\text{A0}$:** $841\text{ mm} \times 1189\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
     - **$\text{A1}$:** $594\text{ mm} \times 841\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
     - **$\text{A2}$:** $420\text{ mm} \times 594\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
     - **$\text{A3}$:** $297\text{ mm} \times 420\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
     - **$\text{A4}$:** $210\text{ mm} \times 297\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
2. **Paper Fasteners:**
   - **Adhesive Drafting / Masking Tape:** Applied across the four corners to secure the sheet without damaging board timber[cite: 2].
   - **Metal Drawing Clips:** Clamped along paper borders to hold sheets firmly in position without adhesive residue[cite: 2].
3. **Pencils, Erasers, and Sharpeners:**
   - **Drawing Pencils:** Classified into hard grades (H to 8H) for faint technical construction lines, medium grades (HB, F) for outlines and lettering, and soft grades (B to 8B) for artistic work[cite: 2].
   - **Erasers:** Soft rubber blocks designed to erase pencil lines cleanly without picking, tearing, or smudging the paper surface[cite: 2].
   - **Sharpeners or Razor Blades:** Used to maintain clean conical points or chisel edges on pencil cores[cite: 2].

## Comparative Matrix

  Title: Pair of Compasses vs Pair of Dividers
  Body: Compasses have one steel point and one pencil lead to construct circles and arcs[cite: 2], while dividers have two steel points to transfer exact dimensions from rules to paper[cite: 2].
- Label: Angular Geometry
  Title: $45^\circ$ Set-Square vs $30^\circ/60^\circ$ Set-Square
  Body: The $45^\circ$ set-square features angles of $45^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $90^\circ$[cite: 2], whereas the $30^\circ/60^\circ$ set-square features angles of $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$[cite: 2].
- Label: Measurement Mode
  Title: Metric Rule vs Scale Rule
  Body: A metric rule measures absolute full-size physical distances in millimeters[cite: 2], while a scale rule produces proportionally enlarged or reduced drawings according to set mathematical ratios[cite: 2].
- Label: Curvature Drafting
  Title: Compass vs French Curve
  Body: A compass produces regular circular curves with a constant radius[cite: 2], while a French curve draws irregular, non-circular smooth curves through varied guide points[cite: 2].

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Using thumb pins to fasten drawing paper onto the drawing board[cite: 2].
  Fact: Thumb pins puncture and ruin the smooth surface of the wooden board; always use masking tape or metal drawing clips[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Using a tee-square to draw vertical lines on the board[cite: 2].
  Fact: The tee-square is designed strictly for horizontal lines; vertical lines must be drawn using a set-square sitting on the tee-square blade[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Using soft pencils (such as 2B or 4B) for technical construction lines[cite: 2].
  Fact: Soft pencils leave dark, smudgy, thick graphite lines; use hard pencils like 2H for faint, precise construction lines[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Using a scale rule as a straightedge for drawing heavy pencil lines[cite: 2].
  Fact: Drawing directly against the beveled edges of a scale rule damages its graduated calibration marks; use a standard straightedge or set-square instead[cite: 2].

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Selecting Instruments for a Drafting Task
Context: A student needs to construct a technical drawing of a rectangular machine bracket measuring $120\text{ mm} \times 80\text{ mm}$ with a central circular hole of $30\text{ mm}$ diameter and two $45^\circ$ chamfered corners[cite: 2].
Problem: Identify the specific drawing tools required to execute each geometric feature accurately[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Horizontal Outlines ($120\text{ mm}$):** Drawn with a 2H pencil along the blade of the **Tee-Square** aligned against the board[cite: 2].
- **Vertical Outlines ($80\text{ mm}$):** Drawn using a **Set-Square** resting firmly on the horizontal blade of the tee-square[cite: 2].
- **$45^\circ$ Chamfered Edges:** Drawn using the hypotenuse edge of a **$45^\circ$ Set-Square**[cite: 2].
- **Circular Hole ($\varnothing 30\text{ mm}$):** Drawn using a **Pair of Compasses** set to a radius of $15\text{ mm}$ measured from a metric rule[cite: 2].
- **Dimensioning:** Linear lengths verified using a **Metric Rule** or transferred with **Dividers**[cite: 2].

### Example B: Paper Size Selection and Board Compatibility
Context: A draftsman is preparing to mount an engineering drawing sheet on a Half Imperial drawing board[cite: 2].
Problem: Verify whether an $\text{A3}$ sheet fits on a Half Imperial board, leaving adequate margin space[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Half Imperial Board Dimensions:** $585\text{ mm} \times 452\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
- **$\text{A3}$ Paper Dimensions:** $297\text{ mm} \times 420\text{ mm}$[cite: 2]
- **Calculation of Clearances:**
  - Length Clearance: $585\text{ mm} - 420\text{ mm} = 165\text{ mm}$ (leaving $\approx 82.5\text{ mm}$ margin on left and right)
  - Width Clearance: $452\text{ mm} - 297\text{ mm} = 155\text{ mm}$ (leaving $\approx 77.5\text{ mm}$ margin on top and bottom)
- **Verdict:** An $\text{A3}$ sheet fits the Half Imperial board with margin space for tee-square stock movement[cite: 2].

### Example C: Selecting Fasteners and Erasing Tools
Context: A student needs to secure a fresh sheet of paper and correct an erroneous construction line without damaging the sheet surface[cite: 2].
Problem: Choose the appropriate fastening material and eraser characteristic[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Fastening Material:** Use four small strips of **adhesive masking tape** applied diagonally across the corners, or two **metal drawing clips**[cite: 2]. Avoid pins to preserve the board's smooth face[cite: 2].
- **Eraser Selection:** Use a clean, soft, pliable plastic or rubber **eraser** that removes graphite marks without abrading or tearing the paper fibers[cite: 2].
