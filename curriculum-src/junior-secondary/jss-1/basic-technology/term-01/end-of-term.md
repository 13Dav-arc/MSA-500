## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: What is technology defined as in technical education?
A) The systematic application of scientific knowledge, tools, and skills to solve human problems (correct)
B) The theoretical study of animal biology and cellular functions
C) The memorization of ancient political structures
D) An artistic form of decorative landscape painting
Feedback: Technology is applied science—the systematic application of knowledge, tools, materials, and skills to solve practical problems and satisfy societal needs.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the primary difference between science and technology?
A) Science explores why natural phenomena occur, while technology creates practical solutions to accomplish tasks (correct)
B) Science deals exclusively with computers, while technology deals only with hand tools
C) Science requires physical workshops, while technology requires no tools
D) Science is only studied by children, while technology is practiced only by adults
Feedback: Science investigates natural laws to answer "why", whereas technology applies that knowledge to design devices that answer "how" to achieve practical goals.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: What does the term "technological literacy" mean?
A) The basic understanding and ability to use, manage, and evaluate technical systems safely (correct)
B) The ability to write computer programming languages without errors
C) The memorization of mechanical engineering textbooks
D) The skill of smelting raw iron ore in a blast furnace
Feedback: Technological literacy refers to a citizen's basic understanding, competence, and ability to handle, evaluate, and use technological tools responsibly.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: What Greek root word meaning "art, craft, or skill" does the word "technology" originate from?
A) Technē (correct)
B) Logos
C) Keramos
D) Polis
Feedback: Technology comes from the Greek root *technē*, meaning art, craft, or practical skill.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: Which two metals were alloyed together to produce bronze during the Bronze Age?
A) Copper and tin (correct)
B) Iron and zinc
C) Lead and carbon
D) Gold and aluminum
Feedback: Smelting copper and mixing it with tin produced bronze, which was tougher and more durable than pure copper or stone.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: What revolutionary invention powered factories, locomotives, and mass production during the Industrial Revolution?
A) The improved steam engine (correct)
B) The personal computer microprocessor
C) The silicon solar cell
D) The stone hunting spear
Feedback: The steam engine, perfected by James Watt, provided the mechanical driving force that replaced manual hand labor during the Industrial Revolution.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: What primary technological development characterizes the modern Information Age?
A) Microprocessors, computers, and digital telecommunication networks (correct)
B) The smelting of iron ore for medieval plows
C) The breeding of horses for pulling carriages
D) The hand-chipping of flint stone implements
Feedback: The Information (Digital) Age is defined by silicon microchips, digital computing, satellite uplinks, and high-speed data networks.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: What energy source primarily powered primitive technology?
A) Human and animal muscle power (correct)
B) High-voltage national electrical grids
C) Nuclear fission power plants
D) Refined petroleum diesel fuel
Feedback: Primitive technology relied almost entirely on human physical exertion and domesticated draft livestock.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: What is "e-waste" in modern technological society?
A) Discarded electronic equipment like old computers, monitors, and smartphones (correct)
B) Organic food waste generated by agricultural harvesters
C) Untreated timber shavings from carpentry workshops
D) Natural river silt deposited behind concrete dams
Feedback: E-waste refers to obsolete or broken electrical and electronic devices that contain hazardous chemicals and heavy metals.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: Which professional is primarily responsible for planning, designing, and supervising the construction of bridges, roads, and dams?
A) Civil engineer (correct)
B) Mechanical engineer
C) Architectural draftsman
D) Computer technician
Feedback: Civil engineers specialize in the planning, design, structural calculation, and construction of public infrastructure.

### Q11 [single-choice]

A) Welder and metal fabricator (correct)
B) Carpenter and joiner
C) Surveyor and cartographer

Feedback: Welders and fabricators specialize in cutting, shaping, and fusing metals using heat and electric arcs.

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: What is an accident defined as in workshop practice?
A) An unplanned, unexpected, and uncontrolled event that results in injury or property damage (correct)
B) A scheduled break taken by technicians during work hours
C) A deliberate procedure performed to test the sharpness of a chisel
D) An engineering calculation used to determine the scale of a drawing
Feedback: An accident is defined as an unexpected, unplanned, and uncontrolled event that causes personal injury, property damage, or work disruption.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following is an unsafe personal act that frequently causes workshop accidents?
A) Engaging in horseplay, running, and inattention (correct)
B) Sweeping wood shavings off the workshop floor
C) Wearing safety goggles during drilling operations
D) Storing hand tools neatly in cupboards
Feedback: Horseplay, running, practical jokes, fatigue, and carelessness are leading human causes of workshop accidents.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: Why are open-toed sandals and slippers strictly forbidden in the technology workshop?
A) They provide no protection against dropped heavy tools, hot metal, and sharp nails (correct)
B) They make too much noise on the concrete floor
C) They cause drawing boards to warp
D) They prevent the student from operating a computer mouse
Feedback: Sandals offer no physical barrier against dropped sharp objects, falling timber, iron bars, or stepping on sharp nails.

### Q15 [single-choice]
Stem: What piece of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is essential to shield the eyes from flying chips during cutting and drilling?
A) Safety goggles (correct)
B) Steel-toed boots
C) Leather apron
D) Foam earplugs
Feedback: Safety goggles protect the eyes from high-velocity wood chips, metal filings, sparks, and chemical splashes.

### Q16 [single-choice]
Stem: Why must water fire extinguishers NEVER be used on live electrical equipment fires?
A) Water conducts electricity and will cause severe or fatal electric shock to the operator (correct)
B) Water makes electrical cables burn twice as hot
C) Water freezes inside electrical wiring panels
D) Water causes fire alarms to stop ringing
Feedback: Water is an electrical conductor; directing water onto energized equipment transfers high-voltage current directly into the operator's body.

### Q17 [single-choice]
Stem: Which fire extinguisher is specifically recommended for extinguishing live electrical fires without leaving messy chemical residue?
A) Carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) extinguisher (correct)
B) Water extinguisher
C) Foam extinguisher
D) Wet chemical extinguisher
Feedback: Carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) extinguishers discharge a non-conductive, clean gas that smothers electrical fires without damaging electronic components.

### Q18 [single-choice]
Stem: What type of fire involves flammable liquids such as petrol, kerosene, oil, and paint thinners?
A) Class B fire (correct)
B) Class A fire
C) Class D fire
D) Class E fire
Feedback: Class B fires involve flammable liquids and liquefiable solids like petrol, kerosene, oils, and solvents.

### Q19 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the primary purpose of a workshop fire blanket?
A) Smothering clothing fires on an individual by cutting off oxygen (correct)
B) Covering drawing boards to keep off dust
C) Polishing timber surfaces after planing
D) Providing shade from sunlight in the machine bay
Feedback: Fire blankets are wrapped around a person whose clothes have caught fire to smother flames by cutting off air.

### Q20 [single-choice]
Stem: What are the three primary objectives (the 3 Ps) of First Aid?
A) Preserve life, prevent deterioration, and promote recovery (correct)
B) Plan the project, purchase supplies, and produce joints
C) Protect the tools, paint the bench, and polish the floor
D) Prepare drawings, place timber, and punch drill centers
Feedback: First Aid aims to preserve life, prevent the casualty's condition from worsening, and promote recovery.

### Q21 [single-choice]
Stem: Which item in the First Aid Box is specifically used to support and immobilize a fractured arm or collarbone?
A) Triangular bandage (correct)
B) Calamine lotion
C) Cotton wool
D) Methylated spirit
Feedback: A triangular bandage is folded into an arm sling to support, elevate, and immobilize fractured or dislocated upper limbs.

### Q22 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the correct immediate first aid treatment for a minor first-degree heat burn on the skin?
A) Immerse or flood the burned area under clean, cold running water for $10$ to $15$ minutes (correct)
B) Smear thick motor grease or petroleum jelly over the skin
C) Rub salt and palm oil vigorously across the burn
D) Cover the wound with dry wood sawdust
Feedback: Cold running water absorbs heat, halts cellular destruction, and relieves acute pain; oils and grease trap heat and foster infection.

### Q23 [single-choice]
Stem: If a worker is in contact with a live electrical wire, what must the rescuer do FIRST?
A) Switch off the main electrical circuit breaker immediately (correct)
B) Grab the victim's wrists firmly with bare hands and pull
C) Throw a bucket of clean tap water over the wire
D) Wrap a wet cloth around the victim's head
Feedback: The electric current must be cut at the main breaker before touching the casualty; never touch an energized victim with bare hands.

### Q24 [single-choice]
Stem: How should a casualty who has fainted be positioned on the workshop floor?
A) Laid flat on their back with their legs elevated about $30\text{ cm}$ (correct)
B) Forced to sit upright on a tall wooden stool
C) Held upside down by their ankles
D) Laid face down with heavy toolboxes placed on their back
Feedback: Laying a fainting victim flat with elevated feet directs oxygenated blood back to the brain, restoring consciousness.

### Q25 [single-choice]
Stem: What is wood called after a mature tree has been felled, sawn, and prepared for engineering use?
A) Timber (correct)
B) Latex
C) Bark
D) Plastic
Feedback: Timber (or lumber) is the technical term for wood obtained from felled and seasoned trees prepared for engineering, carpentry, and construction use.

### Q26 [single-choice]
Stem: Which internal layer of a tree trunk is responsible for diameter growth by producing new cells?
A) Cambium layer (correct)
B) Outer bark
C) Pith
D) Medullary ray
Feedback: The cambium layer is the thin, living reproductive zone between the inner bark and sapwood that divides to create new wood and bark cells.

### Q27 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the small, soft, spongy core at the very center of a tree trunk called?
A) Pith (correct)
B) Heartwood
C) Sapwood
D) Cambium
Feedback: The pith (or medulla) is the soft, spongy central core of the tree formed during its initial seedling growth.

### Q28 [single-choice]
Stem: Why is heartwood darker, denser, and preferred over sapwood for construction?
A) Its cells are filled with resins, gums, and tannins, making it durable and resistant to decay (correct)
B) It carries water and living mineral sap directly to the leaves
C) It is made entirely of green photosynthetic chlorophyll cells
D) It can be bent by hand without heating or steaming
Feedback: Heartwood is formed as inner cells mature and become clogged with resins and tannins, producing a dense, rot-resistant core ideal for timber construction.

### Q34 [single-choice]

A) Blast Furnace (correct)

### Q35 [single-choice]
Stem: Which ferrous metal has a high carbon content ($2\%$ to $4\%$), possesses high compressive strength, but is brittle?
A) Cast iron (correct)
B) Wrought iron
C) Aluminum

Feedback: Cast iron contains $2\%–4\%$ carbon, making it rigid and strong under compression, but brittle under impact.

### Q36 [true-false]
Statement: Stainless steel contains chromium and nickel alloyed with steel, giving it high resistance to corrosion and staining.
Answer: true
Feedback: Alloying steel with chromium ($18\%$) and nickel ($8\%$) produces stainless steel, which forms a self-repairing oxide layer that resists rust.

### Q37 [true-false]
Statement: Ceramics possess high compressive strength but very low tensile strength.
Answer: true
Feedback: Ceramics can support massive downward crushing loads, but they fail rapidly under tensile pulling forces and impact shock.

### Q38 [true-false]
Statement: Glass is classified as a crystalline solid because its atoms are arranged in a perfectly repeated geometric grid.
Answer: false
Feedback: Glass is an amorphous (non-crystalline) solid; its atomic structure is disordered and frozen like a supercooled liquid.

### Q39 [true-false]
Statement: Thermoplastics can be repeatedly softened by heating and hardened by cooling without undergoing permanent chemical breakdown.
Answer: true
Feedback: Thermoplastics possess linear chains that soften upon heating and harden on cooling, making them reshapeable and recyclable.

### Q40 [true-false]
Statement: Raw unvulcanized natural rubber has higher tensile strength and abrasion resistance than vulcanized automobile tyre rubber.
Answer: false
Feedback: Vulcanization dramatically increases the tensile strength, elasticity, and abrasion resistance of rubber; raw rubber is weak and tacky.
