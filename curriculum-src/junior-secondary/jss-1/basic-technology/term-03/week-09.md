## Hook
Imagine a motorist setting out on a five-hundred-kilometer highway journey without checking the engine oil, radiator water, or brake fluid[cite: 2]. A breakdown in the middle of nowhere is practically guaranteed, bringing costly repairs, ruined schedules, and severe safety hazards[cite: 2]. In engineering and technology, whether dealing with a simple hand plane, a motorized workshop lathe, or an automobile, physical machines do not stay in working condition on their own[cite: 2]. Maintenance is the vital pulse of engineering—it ensures that tools operate smoothly, catastrophic failures are prevented, and valuable machinery lasts for decades[cite: 2].

## Key Terms
- Term: Maintenance
  Definition: The systematic work done on any engineering equipment, machine, or tool to keep it in good operating condition[cite: 2].
- Term: Preventive Maintenance
  Definition: Scheduled servicing and care carried out on equipment before breakdown occurs to keep it running smoothly and avoid sudden failure[cite: 2].
- Term: Predictive Maintenance
  Definition: Maintenance carried out using computers, sensors, and diagnostic devices to monitor equipment performance and predict impending breakdowns before they happen[cite: 2].
- Term: Corrective Maintenance
  Definition: Unscheduled emergency actions taken to repair, overhaul, or restore broken-down equipment back to a functional state after a failure has occurred[cite: 2].
- Term: Breakdown
  Definition: A complete stoppage or functional failure of a machine or tool that halts operations and requires technical intervention[cite: 2].

## Core Concept
### Aspect 1: The Concept of Maintenance
**Maintenance** is defined as the work done on any engineering equipment to keep it in good operating condition[cite: 2].
- Everything used in our schools, workshops, factories, and homes must be properly cared for so that it can serve us reliably and efficiently[cite: 2].
- Everyday maintenance is practiced all around us; for example, a responsible driver routinely checks the oil level, radiator water, battery terminals, tyres, and brakes before hitting the road to verify that there are no underlying mechanical problems[cite: 2].
- Neglecting maintenance allows minor wear to develop into major, dangerous component failures[cite: 2].

### Aspect 2: The Need for Maintenance
Systematic maintenance is essential across all technical fields for several reasons[cite: 2]:
1. **Ensuring Proper Function:** To make the equipment function properly and achieve its intended design output[cite: 2].
2. **Preventing Failures:** To avoid preventable breakdowns that disrupt operations[cite: 2].
3. **Safety and Accident Prevention:** To reduce the chances of workshop accidents and ensure the safe use of machinery by technicians[cite: 2].
4. **Eliminating Inconvenience:** To avoid the frustration and operational disruptions caused by unexpected equipment failure[cite: 2].
5. **Extending Service Life:** To make tools, machines, and infrastructure last significantly longer[cite: 2].
6. **Cost and Time Savings:** To reduce downtime, minimize productivity loss, and avoid the heavy financial expense of purchasing replacement machinery[cite: 2].

### Aspect 3: Preventive Maintenance
**Preventive maintenance** is the type of maintenance carried out on equipment before breakdown occurs[cite: 2]. It is proactive, planned, and performed at regular calendar or operational intervals[cite: 2]:
- **Regular Servicing:** Routine engine servicing, changing oil filters, and tuning motor vehicles[cite: 2].
- **Lubrication:** Regular oiling or lubricating of bearings, gears, slideways, and moving linkages to minimize friction and wear[cite: 2].
- **Corrosion Control:** Regular painting or protective coating of corrosive metals exposed to moisture and air to prevent rust formation[cite: 2].
- **Cleaning and Hardware Inspection:** Regular cleaning of equipment, routine checks on lubricant levels, safety guards, signal indicators, and tightening loose nuts, bolts, and studs[cite: 2].
- **Wear and Tension Checks:** Routine inspections to measure levels of wear, verify shaft alignment, and check the tension of drive belts and transmission chains[cite: 2].

### Aspect 4: Predictive Maintenance
**Predictive maintenance** is an advanced maintenance strategy carried out using computers, sensors, and diagnostic monitoring devices to predict impending breakdowns before they occur[cite: 2]:
- **Early Warning Analysis:** Specialized sensors continuously track variables like vibration frequencies, heat build-up, and acoustic signatures in operating machinery[cite: 2].
- **Targeted Intervention:** Once predictions of impending failure are detected, necessary remedies and repairs are designed and applied immediately, preventing actual breakdowns[cite: 2].
- **Minimizing Routine Interventions:** Predictive maintenance optimizes workshop resources by reducing the need for arbitrary, excessive preventive teardowns and eliminating unexpected corrective work[cite: 2].
- **System Checks:** To maintain effectiveness, technicians must conduct routine checks on the diagnostic facilities and sensors themselves to ensure they remain functional and properly calibrated[cite: 2].

### Aspect 5: Corrective Maintenance
**Corrective maintenance** involves actions taken to correct or restore broken-down equipment to a functional state after a failure has taken place[cite: 2]:
- **Reactive Nature:** It is inherently reactive because work begins only after a machine has failed, stalled, or suffered mechanical damage during operation[cite: 2].
- **Scope of Work:** The equipment may need a total overhaul, structural welding, or the complete replacement of damaged parts[cite: 2].
- **High Costs and Disruptions:** Corrective maintenance is the least desirable maintenance approach because it requires substantial financial expenditure, causes prolonged downtime, and can damage surrounding components during catastrophic failures[cite: 2].

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Believing that maintenance is only necessary after a machine stops working[cite: 2].
  Fact: Waiting for failure leads to expensive corrective overhauls; maintenance should be performed proactively to prevent breakdowns[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Confusing predictive maintenance with routine preventive maintenance[cite: 2].
  Fact: Preventive maintenance follows fixed routine schedules (like oiling weekly)[cite: 2], while predictive maintenance uses computers and sensors to monitor machine condition and forecast impending faults[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Assuming that painting metal surfaces is purely for decoration[cite: 2].
  Fact: Painting is an important preventive maintenance measure that seals corrosive metals from moisture and air to prevent rust[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Neglecting to verify diagnostic sensors used in predictive maintenance[cite: 2].
  Fact: Technicians must perform routine checks on the diagnostic sensors and monitoring systems to ensure they remain calibrated and operational[cite: 2].

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Designing a Vehicle Pre-Trip Maintenance Check
Context: A transport driver is preparing a commercial passenger bus for a cross-country journey[cite: 2].
Problem: Outline the essential preventive maintenance checks that must be carried out before hitting the road[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Engine Fluids:** Check engine oil level and top off if low to prevent internal friction and overheating[cite: 2].
- **Cooling System:** Inspect radiator water/coolant levels to prevent engine overheating during transit[cite: 2].
- **Electrical & Starting:** Check battery terminals for corrosion and verify that connections are tight[cite: 2].
- **Safety & Braking:** Inspect tyre pressure, check tread wear, and test brake response before moving[cite: 2].

### Example B: Categorizing Factory Maintenance Scenarios
Context: A technical supervisor in a woodworking factory handles three distinct equipment events[cite: 2]:
1. A vibration sensor on a high-speed router detects an anomalous bearing frequency and alerts the computer system to schedule a bearing swap before failure occurs[cite: 2].
2. A maintenance technician lubricates the lead screws of all bench vices and inspects drive belt tensions every Friday afternoon[cite: 2].
3. A bandsaw blade snaps during production, requiring an emergency shutdown to install a replacement blade and realign pulleys[cite: 2].
Problem: Classify each event under its correct maintenance type[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Event 1:** **Predictive Maintenance** (uses computers and sensors to forecast impending breakdown and design remedies)[cite: 2].
- **Event 2:** **Preventive Maintenance** (scheduled routine oiling and tension checks carried out before breakdown occurs)[cite: 2].
- **Event 3:** **Corrective Maintenance** (reactive repair and replacement carried out after equipment failure to restore operation)[cite: 2].

### Example C: Analyzing Why Corrective Maintenance Should Be Minimized
Context: A manufacturing workshop relies exclusively on running machines until they break down before performing repairs[cite: 2].
Problem: Explain the negative consequences of this policy and identify which maintenance strategy should replace it[cite: 2].
Fix:
- **Consequences:** Waiting for failure leads to unpredictable production stoppages, increased risk of accidents, and expensive total overhauls involving part replacements[cite: 2].
- **Recommended Strategy:** Adopt **preventive maintenance** (routine cleaning, regular lubrication, and inspections) combined with **predictive maintenance** where possible, keeping machines in good operating condition and reducing overall maintenance costs[cite: 2].
