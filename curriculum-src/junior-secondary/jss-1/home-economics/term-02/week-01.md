## Hook
Imagine your parents give you ₦5,000 on a Saturday morning in Lagos, Kano, or Enugu. You walk into a shopping complex, and your eyes immediately spot a flashy pair of brand-new sunglasses and a trendy video game cartridge. But wait! You also remember that you have not eaten breakfast, your school notebook has run out of pages with an examination on Monday, and your pair of school sandals has a giant hole in the sole that lets in water when it rains. Which items do you buy first? What will happen if you spend all your money on the sunglasses and video game, leaving yourself hungry and unable to write your Monday exam? Every single day, both individuals and families face this exact challenge: balancing our endless human desires against limited financial resources! To survive, prosper, and build stable homes, we must understand the fundamental difference between **needs** and **wants**. In this lesson, you will master the classification of primary and secondary family needs, and learn how to prioritize spending to achieve lifelong security!

## Key Terms
- Term: Family Needs
- Definition: Essential goods, services, and conditions that are absolutely necessary for the physical survival, health, security, and well-being of family members.
- Term: Wants
- Definition: Desires, luxuries, and comforts that an individual or family would like to have to enhance enjoyment, but which are not strictly essential for physical survival.
- Term: Primary (Basic) Needs
- Definition: Fundamental biological necessities without which human beings would suffer physical illness, severe deprivation, or death (specifically: food, clothing, and shelter).
- Term: Secondary Needs
- Definition: Important human requirements that improve the quality, safety, and social functioning of life after basic physical survival needs are satisfied (e.g., education, healthcare, security, recreation).
- Term: Scarcity of Resources
- Definition: The universal economic reality that human desires and family wants are infinite, while available resources (money, time, energy) are strictly limited.
- Term: Scale of Preference
- Definition: An organized list of unsatisfied family needs and wants arranged in descending order of urgency and importance to guide rational expenditure.
- Term: Opportunity Cost
- Definition: The real economic cost of choosing one alternative, measured by the value of the next best alternative that had to be sacrificed or forgone.
- Term: Resource Allocation
- Definition: The deliberate, strategic distribution of available family income, time, and physical energy among competing family needs.

## Core Concept
### 1. Differentiating Needs from Wants
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Family Needs, Goals, and Standards I" caption="Family Needs, Goals, and Standards I: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

In Home Management and family economics, the distinction between needs and wants is the bedrock of financial wisdom:
- **Needs are Universal Necessities:** Every human being on earth requires nutritious food, clean water, protective clothing, and safe shelter to stay alive. A lack of needs results in physical suffering, disease, malnutrition, or death.
- **Wants are Culturally Conditioned Preferences:** Wants are shaped by personal tastes, social prestige, advertisements, and peer pressure. For instance, while a family *needs* water to survive (need), wanting cold bottled sparkling water from France is a *want*. A student *needs* shoes to protect their feet from hookworms (need), but demanding ₦150,000 imported designer sneakers is a *want*!

### 2. Classification of Family Needs

#### A. Primary (Basic / Biological) Needs
These represent the non-negotiable physical foundations of human existence:
1. **Food and Water:**
   - Provides essential calories, proteins, vitamins, and hydration.
   - Without clean water, humans dehydrate and die within days; prolonged absence of food causes starvation, wasting, and death.
2. **Clothing:**
   - Shields the human body from temperature extremes, wind, rain, and solar radiation.
   - Upholds human dignity, modesty, and cultural decency.
3. **Shelter (Housing):**
   - Provides a secure physical boundary protecting the family from weather, dangerous animals, and criminals.
   - Provides an environment for sleep, hygiene, cooking, and emotional sanctuary.

#### B. Secondary (Social and Developmental) Needs
Once physical survival is secured, secondary needs allow individuals to thrive, develop intellect, and contribute productively to society:
1. **Education and Vocational Training:** Equips children and adults with literacy, numeracy, critical thinking, and technical skills needed to earn a livelihood.
2. **Healthcare and Medical Services:** Access to hospitals, medications, immunizations, and prenatal care to treat disease and maintain longevity.
3. **Security and Peace of Mind:** Safe neighborhoods, locks, and civil law enforcement protecting life and property.
4. **Transportation and Communication:** Bicycles, buses, roads, and mobile phones allowing family members to commute to work/school and maintain family ties.
5. **Recreation and Leisure:** Sports, family outings, and entertainment that relieve psychological stress and promote mental health.

### 3. Managing Scarcity: Scale of Preference and Opportunity Cost
Because no family possesses unlimited wealth, wise homemakers utilize economic decision-making tools:
- **The Scale of Preference:**
  - A homemaker writes down all competing household items in order of priority:
    1. Monthly food supply (Rice, beans, yam, palm oil) — *Most urgent*
    2. School tuition fees and textbooks — *Highly urgent*
    3. Cooking gas refill and electricity bill — *Urgent*
    4. New shoes to replace worn-out pair — *Necessary*
    5. New living room curtains — *Comfort (Can wait)*
    6. Big screen smart television — *Luxury (Lowest priority)*
- **Opportunity Cost in Family Spending:**
  - Whenever a family chooses to spend ₦30,000 on an expensive birthday party rather than buying school textbooks, the **opportunity cost** of that party is the lost academic textbooks!
  - Prioritizing wants over primary needs leads to family bankruptcy, chronic debt, domestic conflict, and deprivation.

## Comparative Matrix
| Parameter | Family Needs | Family Wants |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fundamental Nature** | Essential for biological survival and basic human health | Non-essential desires that enhance personal pleasure |
| **Consequence of Deprivation**| Illness, malnutrition, physical suffering, or death | Minor disappointment, temporary frustration, zero physical harm |
| **Flexibility / Elasticity** | Inflexible; cannot be completely eliminated | Highly flexible; can easily be postponed, modified, or canceled |
| **Universality** | Universal to all human beings regardless of culture or wealth | Highly individualistic; varies by personal taste, social status |
| **Representative Examples** | Clean water, staple foods, basic clothing, safe roof | Designer jewelry, luxury sports cars, expensive perfumes |
| **Budgeting Priority** | Must always be funded first in the family budget | Funded only if surplus disposable income remains |

## Common Mistakes
- **Confusing Brand-Name Wants with Basic Needs:** Claiming that a specific ₦200,000 smartphone is a "need" for a JSS 1 student. Communication and information are needs, but demanding an ultra-expensive luxury gadget is a **want**.
- **Spending Disposable Income on Wants Before Funding Needs:** Spending family salary on a lavish party or new clothes while rent and school fees remain unpaid. This causes evictions, school dropouts, and crushing debt.
- **Believing Secondary Needs are Frivolous Wants:** Treating education and medical care as unnecessary luxuries. Education and healthcare are vital **secondary needs** essential for human development and long-term economic independence.
- **Assuming Wealthy Families Have No Scale of Preference:** Believing rich people buy everything without choosing. Even billionaires have finite resources and must choose between competing investments.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Constructing a Family Scale of Preference
**Scenario:** Mr. and Mrs. Balogun in Abeokuta have a monthly disposable income of ₦120,000. Their household needs and desires for the month of September are:
- A. Buying a 50 kg bag of rice, beans, and cooking oil: ₦55,000
- B. Paying Junior Secondary School tuition and books for their two children: ₦45,000
- C. Buying an expensive gold necklace for an upcoming wedding: ₦40,000
- D. Refilling the cooking gas cylinder and paying the electricity bill: ₦15,000
- E. Buying a new video game console: ₦35,000
- **Task:** Construct a rational Scale of Preference that fits within their ₦120,000 budget and identify the items that must be forgone.
- **Solution:**
  1. *Priority 1 (Primary Need - Food):* Bag of rice, beans, and oil = **₦55,000**
  2. *Priority 2 (Secondary Need - Education):* School tuition and books = **₦45,000**
  3. *Priority 3 (Primary Need - Utility/Energy):* Cooking gas and electricity = **₦15,000**
  - *Total Essential Expenditure:* $₦55,000 + ₦45,000 + ₦15,000 = \mathbf{₦115,000}$.
  - *Surplus Savings Remaining:* $₦120,000 - ₦115,000 = \mathbf{₦5,000}$.
  4. *Items Forgone (Wants postponed):*
     - Gold necklace (₦40,000) — Postponed.
     - Video game console (₦35,000) — Postponed.
  - By following this rational scale of preference, the family is fed, educated, and powered without incurring debt.

### Example 2: Calculating Opportunity Cost
**Question:** A student receives ₦3,000 from an uncle. She must choose between buying an authorized Home Economics textbook costing ₦3,000 or buying a ticket to an amusement waterpark costing ₦3,000. She chooses the amusement park. State the opportunity cost of her decision.
- **Solution:**
  - The opportunity cost is the **authorized Home Economics textbook** that she sacrificed. The real economic cost of her waterpark entertainment is the academic study material she forfeited.

## Guardian Sync
- **Shopping Cart Challenge:** Take your child along on your next grocery shopping trip. As you select items, ask your child: "Is this a need or a want?" Discuss why you are purchasing certain items and passing on others.
- **Budgeting Transparency:** Share a simplified breakdown of your family's monthly budget categories (Food, Housing/Utilities, School Fees, Transportation, Savings). Show your child how prioritizing needs keeps the household peaceful and secure.
- **Allowance Planning:** If you give your child a small allowance, help them draw up a simple 3-item scale of preference so they learn to save for essentials before spending on snacks.
