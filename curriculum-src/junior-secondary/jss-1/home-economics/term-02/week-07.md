## Hook
Have you ever accompanied your parents to the supermarket or open market in Owerri, Kano, or Ibadan and been shocked at how expensive commercial cleaning products have become? A small tin of scouring powder (*Vim*), a bottle of bleach, or a specialized tile cleaner can cost a significant portion of the family's weekly budget! But did you know that inside your family kitchen and compound, you already possess all the raw ingredients needed to produce powerful, sparkling clean scouring powders that perform just as well as expensive store-bought brands—for almost zero Naira? The eggshells you throw into the trash after breakfast, the cold gray ash left behind inside a firewood stove, and the rough green leaves of the sandpaper tree growing beside the road are all valuable chemical and abrasive resources! In this lesson, you will learn the science of traditional and homemade cleaning agents, master step-by-step recipes for creating your own scouring powders, and discover how practical Home Economics turns household waste into sparkling savings!

## Key Terms
- Term: Cleaning Agents
- Definition: Natural or synthetic chemical substances formulated to remove dirt, grease, stains, and biological pathogens from household surfaces.
- Term: Homemade Cleaning Agents
- Definition: Frugal, eco-friendly cleaning preparations manufactured at home using readily available natural and recycled domestic ingredients (such as eggshells, wood ash, sand, and citrus juice).
- Term: Scouring Powder
- Definition: An abrasive powdered cleaning compound that combines fine friction-generating particles with soap or detergent to lift stubborn grime, tarnish, and burnt food.
- Term: Abrasive Action
- Definition: The mechanical wear and rubbing effect of fine, hard particles that scrub away adhered dirt and smooth down rough surface tarnish through physical friction.
- Term: Potash (Potassium Carbonate - $\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3$)
- Definition: The natural alkaline mineral compound present in wood ash that chemically reacts with fats and oils to break down grease into water-soluble soap.
- Term: Saponification
- Definition: The chemical reaction between an alkaline substance (like potash) and fats or oils, producing natural soap that dissolves in water.
- Term: Sifting (Sieving)
- Definition: The physical separation of fine, uniform powder from coarse, jagged lumps using a fine mesh screen to prevent scratching delicate surfaces.
- Term: Surfactant
- Definition: A surface-active chemical agent (found in soaps and detergents) that loosens grease and holds dirt particles suspended in water so they can be rinsed away.

### 2. The Science Behind Local Cleaning Materials

#### A. Eggshells (Calcium Carbonate - $\text{CaCO}_3$)
- Eggshells are composed of approximately 95% **calcium carbonate**.
- When washed, dried, and crushed into a fine powder, the tiny micro-granules act as a **gentle abrasive**.
- They provide sufficient friction to scrub away stubborn grease, tea stains, and dried food residues from stainless steel sinks and ceramic tiles **without deeply scratching or damaging delicate surfaces**.

#### B. Wood Ash (The Ancient Grease Cutter)
- Ash from burned firewood and charcoal contains **potash (potassium carbonate)**.
- Potash is a natural alkali. When rubbed against fatty, oily cooking pans with a wet sponge, the potash chemically bonds with the fatty acids through **saponification**, transforming sticky grease into soluble soap right on the surface of the pan!
- The fine charcoal particles simultaneously act as a mild abrasive to scour away black firewood soot.

#### C. Sifted River Sand
- Clean, washed white or river sand consists of hard quartz crystals.
- Sifted through a fine wire mesh to remove sharp pebbles, it provides heavy-duty scouring power for rough cement floors, unpainted iron pots, and rusted garden hand tools.

#### D. Sandpaper Leaf (*Ficus exasperata* - *Ewe Epin* in Yoruba / *Asese* in Igbo)
- The rough, coarse leaves of the sandpaper tree are coated with microscopic, rigid **silica hairs**.
- When rubbed vigorously against wooden cutting boards, wooden cooking spoons, and wooden mortars, they act like natural industrial sandpaper, lifting embedded food grease and stains while leaving the wood smooth and hygienic.

#### E. Citrus Juices (Limes and Lemons)
- Contain natural **citric acid**, a mild organic acid that dissolves mineral limescale, cuts through grease, removes rust stains, and leaves a fresh, natural citrus fragrance.

### 3. Step-by-Step Recipes for Homemade Cleaning Agents

#### Recipe A: Eggshell-Based Household Scouring Powder

##### Ingredients & Equipment
- 2 cups of clean eggshells (from boiled or fried eggs)
- 1 cup of grated laundry bar soap (or leftover soap scraps)
- 2 tablespoons of baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) or dry detergent powder
- Pestle and mortar (or dry grinding mill)
- Fine wire sieve (flour sifter)
- Clean, dry shaker container (an empty spice jar, salt shaker, or recycled powder tin with perforated lid holes)

##### Step-by-Step Method
1. **Cleaning and Sterilization:** Wash collected eggshells in warm water to remove any lingering egg white or yolk. Spread them out on a tray under hot tropical sun for 1 to 2 days until bone-dry and brittle.
2. **Grinding:** Place dry eggshells into a clean mortar and pound vigorously with a pestle (or blend in a dry electric mill) until reduced to a fine, flour-like powder.
3. **Sifting (Crucial Step!):** Pour the powder through a **fine wire sieve**. Discard any large, jagged shell fragments that do not pass through, as coarse pieces can scratch glossy surfaces.
4. **Soap Preparation:** Finely grate dry laundry bar soap (or traditional black soap) using a kitchen grater.
5. **Blending:** In a clean bowl, mix **2 parts sifted eggshell powder**, **1 part grated soap powder**, and **2 tablespoons of baking soda** until completely uniform.
6. **Packaging and Storage:** Pour the finished scouring powder into your shaker container. **Keep tightly capped in a dry location.**

---

#### Recipe B: Traditional Wood Ash and Sand Scouring Powder (For Pots & Pavements)

##### Ingredients & Method
- 2 cups of cold firewood ash
- 1 cup of fine, clean river sand
- ½ cup of grated laundry soap powder
1. Sift cold wood ash through a fine sieve to eliminate charcoal lumps, stones, and nails.
2. Sift clean river sand to remove gravel.
3. Thoroughly mix the sifted ash, sand, and soap powder together.
4. Store dry in a labeled plastic bucket or container with a tight lid.
5. *Application:* Sprinkle directly onto wet, soot-blackened aluminum or iron pots; scrub with a coconut husk sponge or scouring pad, then rinse with clean water. The pots will gleam like new!

### 4. Proper Use, Safety, and Storage Rules
- **Keep Homemade Cleaners Bone-Dry:** Exposure to moisture causes homemade scouring powders to cake into hard lumps and causes soap shavings to melt prematurely. Store in airtight containers in a dry cupboard.
- **Never Use on Clear Glass or Mirrors:** While eggshell powder is gentle on ceramic and steel, abrasives should never be used on clear glass or polished optical mirrors.
- **Labeling and Child Safety:** Clearly label all containers: *"HOMEMADE SCOURING POWDER — FOR CLEANING ONLY."* Store on high shelves out of reach of toddlers and pets.

## Common Mistakes
- **Pounding Wet Eggshells:** Trying to crush eggshells that are still moist. Wet shells turn into a sticky, gummy paste that cannot be pulverized or sifted. Eggshells must be **100% bone-dry and brittle** before pounding!
- **Skipping the Sifting Stage:** Mixing unsifted crushed eggshells directly with soap. Coarse, jagged pieces of shell act like broken glass, permanently scratching and gouging the enamel glaze of sinks and bathtubs! Always sift through a fine mesh!
- **Using Hot Ash Directly from the Fire:** Scooping ash directly from a burning stove. Hot ash can ignite plastic containers, burn hands, or cause serious domestic fires. Ash must be allowed to cool completely for at least 24 hours!
- **Storing Powders in Open Basins:** Leaving homemade scouring powder uncovered in a humid bathroom. The powder will absorb moisture from the air, clump into rock-hard bricks, and lose its free-flowing shaker utility.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Calculating Production Savings
**Scenario:** The Okon family in Calabar uses two 500-gram tins of commercial scouring powder (*Vim*) every month to clean their sinks, bathtubs, and pots, with each tin costing ₦1,200 (Total: ₦2,400 monthly). The student learns to produce high-grade eggshell scouring powder at home using collected eggshells, free wood ash, and ₦300 worth of laundry soap shavings.
- **Question:** How much money does the family save annually by switching to homemade scouring powder?
- **Solution:**
  1. *Annual Commercial Cost:* $₦2,400\text{ per month} \times 12\text{ months} = \mathbf{₦28,800}$.
  2. *Annual Homemade Production Cost:* $₦300\text{ per month} \times 12\text{ months} = \mathbf{₦3,600}$.
  3. *Annual Family Financial Savings:* $₦28,800 - ₦3,600 = \mathbf{₦25,200}$.
  - The family saves over ₦25,000 every year, which can be redirected toward school textbooks or healthy food!

### Example 2: Selecting the Safest Cleaner for Specific Surfaces
**Question:** A student needs to clean two different household items:
1. An expensive glazed porcelain washbasin with tea stains.
2. An iron cooking pot with heavy black soot from firewood.
Which homemade cleaning formulation should be selected for each item to achieve maximum cleanliness without causing surface damage?
- **Solution:**
  1. *Glazed Porcelain Washbasin:* Use **finely sifted Eggshell-Based Scouring Powder with soap**. The calcium carbonate granules are smooth and gentle enough to lift tea stains without scratching the delicate vitreous porcelain glaze.
  2. *Soot-Blackened Iron Pot:* Use **Wood Ash and Sand Scouring Powder**. The heavy quartz sand and charcoal grit provide the aggressive mechanical friction needed to scrape off hard baked-on soot, while the alkaline potash in the ash cuts through grease.
