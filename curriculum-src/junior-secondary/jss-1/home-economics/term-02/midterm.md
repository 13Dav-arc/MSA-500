# Second Term Midterm Continuous Assessment Review

This diagnostic continuous assessment evaluates student mastery across the foundational Home Management, Family Economics, and Textile Craft units covered during Weeks 01 through 04 of the Second Term:
- **Week 01:** Family Needs, Goals, and Standards I (Needs vs. wants, primary and secondary needs, scale of preference, and opportunity cost).
- **Week 02:** Family Needs, Goals, and Standards II (Hierarchy of goals: short, intermediate, long-term; rigid vs. flexible standards; and the four-step management cycle).
- **Week 03:** Production of Clothing and Household Articles: Sewing Tools and Equipment (Measuring, marking, cutting, stitching, and pressing tools; maintenance and safety).
- **Week 04:** Practical Production of Household Articles and Basic Sewing Stitches (Temporary, permanent, and decorative stitches; pillowcase and apron construction).

---

## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: Goods, services, and conditions that are absolutely necessary for physical survival and health are defined as:
A) Wants
B) Family needs (correct)
C) Fixed assets
D) Luxuries
Feedback: Needs are essential requirements for human survival and physical well-being.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following groups consists entirely of primary (basic) biological needs?
A) Video games, television, and sports cars
B) Food, clothing, and shelter (correct)
C) Expensive jewelry, perfumes, and designer shoes
D) Airplanes, hotels, and cinemas
Feedback: Food, clothing, and shelter are the universal primary necessities of human existence.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: An organized list of unsatisfied family needs and wants arranged in descending order of urgency and priority is called:
A) A family tree
B) A scale of preference (correct)
C) A balance sheet
D) A shopping receipt
Feedback: A scale of preference ranks needs and wants in order of importance to guide rational spending.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: The real economic cost of choosing one alternative, measured by the next best alternative that had to be sacrificed, is termed:
A) Marginal profit
B) Opportunity cost (correct)
C) Fixed interest
D) Gross domestic product
Feedback: Opportunity cost represents the forgone alternative when a financial choice is made.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: Specific, measurable targets and desired future outcomes that an individual or family works systematically to achieve are called:
A) Standards
B) Goals (correct)
C) Resources
D) Abrasives
Feedback: Goals are specific targets and aims that direct the use of family resources.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: Objectives that can be accomplished in a brief period, ranging from a few days to less than three months, are classified as:
A) Long-term goals
B) Short-term goals (correct)
C) Intermediate goals
D) Lifelong ambitions
Feedback: Short-term goals are immediate targets achieved in days, weeks, or under three months.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: The yardsticks, criteria, or measures of quality and excellence used to judge how well a goal has been achieved are called:
A) Goals
B) Standards (correct)
C) Resources
D) Needs
Feedback: Standards are the criteria or measures of quality used to evaluate goal attainment.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the first step in the four-step family management cycle where decisions are made on what needs to be done and how?
A) Evaluating
B) Planning (correct)
C) Implementing
D) Disinfecting
Feedback: Planning is the initial managerial step where strategies, tasks, and resource allocations are formulated.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: Which measuring tool consists of a flexible 150 cm (60 inch) fiberglass ribbon with metal tips, used for body measurements?
A) Yardstick
B) Tape measure (correct)
C) Sewing gauge
D) Metre rule
Feedback: The tape measure is the flexible tool used for taking human body dimensions and measuring fabric yardage.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the cardinal rule regarding the care and preservation of dressmaker's fabric cutting shears?
A) They must be soaked in vinegar every night
B) They must NEVER be used to cut paper, cardboard, wire, or rope (correct)
C) They should be heated over a fire before cutting silk
D) They should only be used by left-handed persons
Feedback: Cutting paper quickly blunts the finely honed blades of fabric shears; they must only be used on cloth.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: What cutting tool has zigzag saw-tooth blades that produce a notched edge on fabric to prevent threads from fraying?
A) Seam ripper
B) Pinking shears (correct)
C) Kitchen knife
D) Embroidery scissors
Feedback: Pinking shears cut a zigzag edge that inhibits the raveling/fraying of woven fabrics.

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: What protective pitted cap is worn on the middle finger to push needles through heavy fabric without puncturing the finger?
A) Thimble (correct)
B) Seam ripper
C) Emery bag
D) Pincushion
Feedback: A thimble shields the middle finger while pushing needles through tough cloth.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the strongest hand sewing stitch, which looks identical to an electric machine stitch on the right side?
A) Running stitch
B) Backstitch (correct)
C) Hemming stitch
D) Uneven basting
Feedback: The backstitch is the strongest hand stitch, forming a continuous unbroken line on the right side.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: What permanent stitch is sewn diagonally over the raw cut edges of a seam allowance to prevent woven threads from fraying?
A) Overcasting stitch (correct)
B) Running stitch
C) Uneven basting
D) Satin stitch
Feedback: Overcasting wraps thread over raw cut edges to prevent fraying and raveling.

---

### Q15 [true-false]
Stem: Human needs and human wants mean the exact same thing in Home Economics and can be used interchangeably.
A) True
B) False (correct)
Feedback: False. Needs are essential for survival; wants are non-essential desires for comfort.

### Q16 [true-false]
Stem: The backstitch is the strongest hand sewing stitch and resembles machine stitching on the right side of the fabric.
A) True (correct)
B) False
Feedback: True. The backstitch provides superior strength and creates an unbroken line like machine stitches.

---

### Q17 [multi-select]
Stem: Which of the following are primary (basic) human needs? (Select all that apply)
A) Nutritious food and potable clean water (correct)
B) Weather-protective clothing (correct)
C) Safe and sanitary residential shelter (correct)
D) An imported sports car with gold rims
Feedback: Food, clothing, and shelter are primary needs. Luxury cars are wants.

### Q18 [multi-select]
Stem: Which of the following stitches are classified as temporary stitches? (Select all that apply)
A) Even basting (tacking) (correct)
B) Uneven basting (correct)
C) Tailor's tacks (thread marking) (correct)
D) Backstitch
Feedback: Even basting, uneven basting, and tailor's tacks are temporary. Backstitch is permanent.

---

### Q19 [matching]
Stem: Match each economic term with its correct definition:
- Need: Goods and conditions essential for physical survival and health
- Want: Non-essential luxury or desire that enhances comfort
- Scale of Preference: List of competing items ranked in order of priority
- Opportunity Cost: The value of the next best alternative forgone
Feedback: Need is survival; Want is comfort; Scale of preference is priority ranking; Opportunity cost is the sacrificed alternative.

### Q20 [matching]
Stem: Match each sewing stitch with its primary operational use:
- Backstitch: Providing maximum strength for seams and repairing tears
- Overcasting Stitch: Finishing raw cut seam edges to prevent fraying
- Hemming Stitch: Securing folded hem edges invisibly on the right side
- French Knot: Creating raised decorative beaded dots in embroidery designs
Feedback: Backstitch provides strength; Overcasting prevents fraying; Hemming secures hems; French knots make beaded dots.
