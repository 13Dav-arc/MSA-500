## Hook
Have you ever watched an expert tailor or fashion designer in Aba, Lagos, or Kano cut and sew a magnificent piece of clothing? Before they even touch the sewing machine, they reach into a neat wooden box and pull out a tape measure, a piece of triangular colored chalk, gleaming sharp shears that glide through silk like a knife through soft butter, and tiny silver pins that hold fabric folds perfectly in place. But imagine trying to cut out a school uniform using a pair of dull kitchen scissors that chew the fabric, trying to measure a skirt with a broken plastic ruler, or pushing a sharp needle through heavy denim with your bare thumb until blood starts dripping! That would turn sewing into a painful nightmare! In clothing and textiles, your finished garment is only as good as the tools you use and how you handle them. In this lesson, you will master the classification, functions, maintenance, and safety rules of essential sewing tools—from measuring and cutting to stitching and pressing!

## Key Terms
- Term: Sewing Tools
- Definition: Handheld instruments and devices utilized by a tailor or dressmaker to measure, mark, cut, stitch, and finish textile fabrics and garments.
- Term: Sewing Equipment
- Definition: Larger, durable machines and furniture utilized in clothing construction, such as sewing machines, cutting tables, dressmaker's dummies, and pressing irons.
- Term: Cutting Shears
- Definition: Heavy-duty scissors (20 to 25 cm long) featuring bent or offset handles that allow fabric to rest completely flat on the cutting table while being cut.
- Term: Pinking Shears
- Definition: Specialized cutting shears with saw-toothed, zigzag blades that produce a notched zigzag edge on fabric to prevent woven threads from fraying or raveling.
- Term: Seam Ripper
- Definition: A small, pen-like handheld tool equipped with a sharp curved blade and a protective safety ball point used to pick out and cut unwanted stitches safely.
- Term: Thimble
- Definition: A small pitted metal or plastic protective cap worn on the middle finger of the sewing hand to push needles through tough fabric without puncturing skin.
- Term: Tailor's Chalk
- Definition: Hard, waxy or clay-based colored chalk used to transfer pattern construction markings, darts, and seamlines temporarily onto fabric.
- Term: Emery Bag
- Definition: A small, firm bag filled with abrasive emery powder or fine mineral sand, often shaped like a strawberry attached to a pincushion, used to sharpen and de-rust needles and pins.

### 2. Systematic Analysis of Sewing Tool Groups

#### A. Measuring Tools
Accurate measurement is the first rule of successful garment construction:
1. **Tape Measure:** A flexible, non-stretch ribbon made of fiberglass or reinforced plastic, typically **150 cm (60 inches)** long, with protective metal tips on both ends. Used for taking human body measurements (bust, waist, hips, length) and measuring fabric yardage.
2. **Sewing Gauge:** A small 15 cm (6 inch) metal or plastic ruler equipped with an adjustable sliding pointer. Used for measuring short, repeated dimensions such as hem depths, buttonholes, pleats, and seam allowances.
3. **Metre Rule / Yardstick:** A rigid wooden or metal 100 cm ruler used for drawing long, straight grainlines and checking hanging hemlines.

#### B. Marking Tools
Used to transfer construction lines from paper patterns onto cloth:
1. **Tailor's Chalk:** Formulated from clay or wax in various colors (white, blue, yellow). Marks seam allowances, darts, and zipper placements, and brushes or washes out of fabric easily.
2. **Tracing Wheel and Dressmaker's Tracing Paper (Carbon Paper):** A serrated or smooth metal wheel mounted on a handle. When rolled over tracing paper, it transfers pattern markings onto the wrong side of the fabric with precision.

#### C. Cutting Tools
Sharp, high-quality cutting tools prevent chewed fabric edges and hand fatigue:
1. **Dressmaker's Cutting Shears:** Long shears (20–25 cm) with asymmetric handles: a small round loop for the thumb and a larger oval loop for three fingers. The **bent handle design** allows the lower blade to slide flat along the cutting table, ensuring the fabric is not lifted or distorted while cutting.
2. **Sewing Scissors:** Smaller shears (10–15 cm) with symmetric handles used for trimming seam allowances, clipping thread ends, and cutting delicate curves.
3. **Pinking Shears:** Feature zigzag saw-tooth blades. Used to finish raw seam edges on woven fabrics, creating a decorative zigzag cut that prevents fabric threads from fraying without needing immediate stitching.
4. **Seam Ripper:** Features a sharp inner sickle blade and a protective red safety ball. Glides under incorrect or puckered stitches, severing the thread without tearing the surrounding fabric.

#### D. Stitching and Sewing Tools
1. **Hand Sewing Needles:** Slender steel shafts with a sharp point at one end and an eye for thread at the other:
   - *Sharps:* Medium-length needles with round eyes, used for general hand sewing.
   - *Betweens:* Shorter needles used for fine tailoring and quilting.
   - *Crewel Needles:* Have long, oval eyes designed to accommodate thick embroidery floss.
   - Needle sizes range from 1 (very thick) to 12 (ultra-fine); **sizes 7, 8, and 9** are standard for school needlework.
2. **Dressmaker's Pins:** Slender, rust-proof stainless steel or brass pins with rounded heads used to hold pattern paper to fabric and anchor fabric seams together before permanent stitching.
3. **Pincushion and Emery Bag:** Pincushions hold pins safely while working. The attached **emery bag** contains abrasive mineral grains; stabbing dull or tarnished needles into it removes surface rust and sharpens the points.
4. **Thimble:** A small dome-shaped metal or plastic cap with tiny indentations (pits) on its surface. Worn on the **middle finger** of the sewing hand, the pits catch the back of the needle, allowing the sewer to push the needle through thick fabric with firm pressure without stabbing their finger.
5. **Sewing Thread:** Spun strands of cotton, mercerized cotton, or polyester selected to match the color, thickness, and fiber content of the fabric.

#### E. Pressing Tools
1. **Pressing Iron (Electric or Charcoal Iron):** Smooths out wrinkles, presses open seam allowances, and creates crisp pleats during garment construction.
2. **Pressing Cloth:** A clean square of unbleached cotton or muslin placed between the hot iron and delicate fabric to prevent scorching, melting, or unsightly iron shine.

### 3. Care, Maintenance, and Safety Rules for Sewing Tools
A responsible sewer maintains tools with care and follows strict safety protocols:

#### Tool Maintenance Guidelines
- **The Golden Rule of Fabric Shears:** **NEVER use fabric cutting shears to cut paper, cardboard, wire, or rope!** Paper contains abrasive wood pulp and mineral fillers that dull the finely honed edge of fabric blades, ruining them for cutting cloth.
- **Preventing Rust:** Keep steel pins, needles, and shears clean and dry; store them with a small piece of chalk or silica gel in a dry sewing box to absorb humidity.
- **Oiling:** Apply a drop of light machine oil to the pivot screw of shears occasionally, wiping away excess oil before cutting fabric.

#### Critical Sewing Safety Protocols
1. **Never Put Needles or Pins in Your Mouth:** A sudden cough, laugh, or accidental bump can cause you to swallow the needle or inhale it into your lungs, requiring emergency surgery!
2. **Handing Tools Safely:** Always pass scissors and shears to another person **handle-first**, holding the closed blades safely in your own hand. Never throw scissors!
3. **Pin Storage:** Never leave loose pins scattered across tables, chairs, or floors where people can step on them; always return pins directly to a pincushion or magnetic container.
4. **Disconnection of Pressing Irons:** Never leave a hot pressing iron resting face-down on an ironing board fabric; always stand it upright on its heel rest, and unplug the cord from the wall outlet immediately after use.

## Common Mistakes
- **Cutting Paper with Fabric Shears:** Using expensive tailor's shears to cut paper patterns or school notebooks. Paper rapidly blunts the razor edge of fabric shears; always keep a separate, cheap pair of paper scissors for patterns!
- **Holding Pins in the Mouth:** Placing sewing pins between your lips while adjusting a garment on a dressmaker or friend. This is an extremely dangerous habit that leads to accidental swallowing or choking.
- **Pressing Synthetics on High Heat Without a Pressing Cloth:** Applying a scorching hot iron directly onto nylon or polyester fabric. Synthetic fibers melt instantly into sticky plastic, destroying the garment and ruining the iron soleplate!
- **Pulling a Tape Measure Tightly Around the Body:** Pulling the tape so tight that it pinches the skin when measuring a waist or bust. Measurements must be taken with the tape resting snugly and comfortably against the body with one finger slipped beneath the tape.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Assembling a Student Needlework Sewing Kit
**Scenario:** A JSS 1 student is preparing for practical clothing construction classes. She has a budget to assemble a starter sewing box.
- **Task:** List the 6 most essential, indispensable hand tools she must include in her sewing kit and state the specific purpose of each.
- **Solution:**
  1. *Tape Measure:* For measuring fabric lengths and personal body dimensions.
  2. *Dressmaker's Cutting Shears:* For cutting fabric cleanly without chewing or distorting the edges.
  3. *Tailor's Chalk:* For marking seamlines and dart locations temporarily on cloth.
  4. *Hand Sewing Needles (Assorted Sharps, Sizes 7–9):* For basting, sewing, and hemming.
  5. *Dressmaker's Pins and Pincushion:* For pinning patterns to fabric and holding seams securely before sewing.
  6. *Matching Sewing Thread and Thimble:* For stitching the fabric and protecting the finger while pushing the needle.

### Example 2: Analyzing Cutting Tool Selection
**Question:** Explain why a dressmaker chooses **cutting shears with bent handles** rather than standard kitchen scissors with straight handles when cutting out a dress from folded fabric on a cutting table.
- **Solution:**
  - *Design Difference:* Cutting shears feature an offset, **bent lower handle** that positions the lower blade parallel and flat against the table surface.
  - *Practical Advantage:* The lower blade slides smoothly beneath the fabric along the tabletop without lifting the cloth. This keeps both fabric layers completely flat and aligned with the paper pattern.
  - In contrast, standard straight-handled scissors force the user to lift the fabric off the table with every cut, distorting the grainline and resulting in uneven, inaccurate pattern pieces.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Sewing Box Tour:** Open your family sewing box with your child. Identify the tape measure, shears, needles, thimble, and tailor's chalk, letting your child handle them and explain what each tool is used for.
- **The Paper Test Warning:** Demonstrate why fabric shears must never be used on paper. Show your child the difference between your sharp fabric shears and an ordinary pair of paper craft scissors, reinforcing the rule: "Fabric shears cut fabric only!"
- **Safety First Practice:** Practice handing scissors back and forth safely with your child—holding the closed blades firmly and offering the handle to the other person.
