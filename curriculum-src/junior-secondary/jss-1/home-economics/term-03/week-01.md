## Hook
Have you ever noticed how the roadside markets in your town or village suddenly transform throughout the year? In May and June, wheelbarrows and market stalls overflow with bright yellow mangoes, golden corn roasted over glowing coals, and fresh African pears (*Ube*). A few months later, mountains of freshly harvested white yams take center stage, while citrus trees groan under heavy bunches of sweet oranges! Yet, many young people choose to bypass these fresh, vitamin-packed natural foods in favor of expensive carbonated soft drinks, artificial biscuits, and oily pastries. Did you know that eating foods according to nature's harvest calendar—known as **eating foods in season**—not only supplies your growing adolescent body with peak vitamins and minerals at their freshest, but also saves your family thousands of Naira? In this lesson, you will discover the science of healthy eating habits, master the six essential nutrient groups, and learn how harnessing local, seasonal Nigerian foods fuels peak mental and physical energy!

## Key Terms
- Term: Feeding (Eating) Habits
- Definition: The habitual patterns, conscious choices, and daily routines that govern what, when, and how an individual or family selects, prepares, and consumes food.
- Term: Adequate (Balanced) Diet
- Definition: A meal plan that supplies all six essential classes of food nutrients in the correct proportions required to sustain life, fuel metabolic energy, promote growth, and repair body tissues.
- Term: Foods in Season
- Definition: Agricultural crops and produce that are harvested during their natural peak period of growth, yielding abundance, maximum nutritional value, fresh flavor, and low market cost.
- Term: Nutrients
- Definition: Chemical substances present in food that the body digests, absorbs, and metabolizes to provide energy, build and repair cells, and regulate biological processes.
- Term: Macronutrients
- Definition: Nutrients required by the human body in relatively large quantities for energy and physical structure (carbohydrates, proteins, and fats).
- Term: Micronutrients
- Definition: Nutrients needed by the body in minute quantities to catalyze chemical reactions and protect against disease (vitamins and mineral salts).
- Term: Dietary Fiber (Roughage)
- Definition: The indigestible plant cellulose that adds bulk to feces, stimulates intestinal peristalsis, and prevents constipation.
- Term: Empty Calories
- Definition: Foods and beverages high in refined sugars and unhealthy fats that provide rapid energy without accompanying vitamins, minerals, or dietary fiber.
- Term: Food Security
- Definition: The state where all family members have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food to meet dietary needs for an active, healthy life.

## Core Concept
### 1. Meaning and Importance of Healthy Feeding Habits
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Healthy Feeding and Eating Practices I" caption="Healthy Feeding and Eating Practices I: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

Feeding habits are not formed overnight; they develop from childhood through domestic traditions, cultural preferences, and personal choices. 
- **Healthy Feeding Habits:** Result from consciously selecting wholesome, nutrient-dense foods, eating at structured intervals, and drinking adequate clean water.
- **Why Healthy Feeding Matters:**
  - **Accelerated Growth and Brain Function:** Powers rapid adolescent growth, bone elongation, and neurological cognitive performance in school.
  - **Robust Immune Defense:** A well-nourished body heals rapidly from physical injury and mounts powerful immune responses against infectious pathogens.
  - **Prevention of Chronic Illness:** Protects against adult degenerative diseases, including cardiovascular disorders, type-2 diabetes, hypertension, and obesity.
  - **Sustained Energy:** Balances blood glucose levels, preventing fatigue, lethargy, and afternoon brain fog in the classroom.

### 2. The Science of Foods in Season
In Nigeria, our tropical climate is divided into distinct wet (rainy) and dry seasons. Agricultural produce ripens in natural cycles governed by seasonal rainfall and sunshine:

#### A. Characteristics of Seasonal Produce
- **Peak Nutritional Value:** Crops harvested at natural maturity contain their highest concentration of ascorbic acid (vitamin C), beta-carotene (provitamin A), and antioxidants.
- **Superior Flavor and Aroma:** Sun-ripened fruits and freshly harvested vegetables develop natural sugars and aromatic esters, tasting far sweeter and richer than artificial shelf-stored foods.
- **Economic Affordability:** The economic law of supply and demand ensures that when crops are in season, supply peaks in local markets, causing prices to plummet. Families can buy large quantities for modest expenditure.
- **Freedom from Preservatives:** Fresh seasonal crops do not require chemical preservatives, refrigeration gases, or prolonged artificial storage.

#### B. Nigerian Seasonal Harvest Calendar Examples
1. **Early Rainy Season (April – June):**
   - Fresh sweet corn (maize), mangoes, avocados, African bush pear (*Ube*), garden eggs, cashew fruits.
2. **Peak Rainy Season (July – September):**
   - New yam (*Yam festival* season), okra, pumpkins, leafy green vegetables (*Ugwu*, waterleaf, jute leaves/*Ewedu*, spinach).
3. **Dry Season / Harmattan (November – February):**
   - Sweet oranges, grapefruits, carrots, tomatoes, onions, watermelons, dry grains (cowpeas/beans, sorghum, millet).

### 3. Principles of Healthy Eating
To build physical stamina and mental agility, every adolescent should observe the following foundational feeding practices:
1. **Never Skip Breakfast:** Breakfast breaks the overnight fasting period, replenishing glycogen stores to power morning school concentration.
2. **Eat a Variety of Local Foods:** No single food item contains all essential nutrients. Combine local grains, legumes, vegetables, and animal or plant proteins daily.
3. **Hydrate with Clean Water:** Drink at least 6 to 8 glasses of safe drinking water daily. Avoid substituting clean water with sugary carbonated soft drinks.
4. **Practice Portion Control:** Balance calorie intake with physical energy expenditure. Do not overeat (causing sluggishness and obesity) or severely undereat (causing nutrient starvation).
5. **Incorporate Abundant Green Vegetables:** Leafy greens supply vital dietary fiber, iron for red blood cells, and folic acid.
6. **Limit Refined Sugars, Salt, and Deep-Fried Fats:** Minimize table sugar, overly salted seasonings, and reheated oxidized frying oils.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Equating fullness with good nutrition.
  *Correction:* Stuffing oneself with mountains of white cassava flour (*Eba*) or plain white rice provides carbohydrates but lacks essential proteins, vitamins, and minerals. True nutrition requires balance, not mere bulk.
- **Mistake 2:** Believing that expensive imported foreign foods are superior to local produce.
  *Correction:* Fresh Nigerian vegetables like *Ugwu*, waterleaf, and bitter leaf, paired with local beans and fish, are far richer in vitamins and minerals than imported canned or processed supermarket goods.
- **Mistake 3:** Drinking sugary carbonated soda instead of clean water when thirsty.
  *Correction:* Soft drinks contain high concentrations of refined sugar and phosphoric acid that dehydrate cells and erode dental enamel. Clean water is the only fluid that properly hydrates the human body.
- **Mistake 4:** Discarding seasonal fruits because they are "cheap and common."
  *Correction:* Abundance does not mean inferiority; mangoes and oranges in season offer nature's most potent defense against infections.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Economic and Nutritional Comparison
*Scenario:* A family in Makurdi has ₦1,500 allocated for afternoon refreshments for three children.
- **Option A (Ultra-Processed):** Buy 3 cans of commercial fizzy soda (₦350 each = ₦1,050) and 3 small packaged meat pies made with refined flour (₦150 each = ₦450). Total = ₦1,500.
- **Option B (Seasonal Fresh Produce):** Buy 6 large freshly harvested sweet oranges (₦300), 3 freshly roasted ears of seasonal corn (₦450), and 6 boiled African bush pears (*Ube*) (₦350). Total = ₦1,100, leaving ₦400 in change.

*Analysis:*
- **Option A:** Delivers approximately 120g of refined sugar, saturated trans-fats, zero dietary fiber, and almost no vitamin C. It spikes blood sugar followed by energy crashes.
- **Option B:** Delivers complex dietary fiber, natural vitamin C, potassium, healthy plant lipids from *Ube*, sustained satiety, and saves ₦400!
*Conclusion:* Option B is economically and nutritionally superior.

### Example 2: Designing a Balanced Nigerian Daily Adolescent Menu
*Task:* Formulate a balanced 3-meal daily menu for a JSS 1 student using accessible Nigerian food items.

*Model Menu:*
- **Breakfast:** Boiled sweet potatoes with scrambled egg and vegetable sauce (tomatoes, onions, and pumpkin leaves), accompanied by a glass of clean warm water or milk.
  *(Nutrients: Carbohydrates, proteins, vitamin A, iron, calcium, lipids).*
- **Lunch:** Brown cowpea beans cooked with red palm oil and sweet corn, served with a piece of smoked mackerel fish.
  *(Nutrients: High plant and animal protein, complex carbohydrates, vitamin A, essential fatty acids).*
- **Dinner:** Moderate portion of yam flour (*Amala*) served with fresh jute leaf soup (*Ewedu*) and melon seed soup (*Egusi*), garnished with diced beef and steamed garden eggs.
  *(Nutrients: Energy, dietary fiber, minerals, vitamins, lipids).*
- **Hydration & Snacks:** Fresh seasonal oranges or pawpaw slices between meals and at least 6 glasses of clean borehole water throughout the day.
