# Third Term Terminal Examination: Home Economics

This comprehensive terminal examination rigorously evaluates student knowledge, scientific analysis, and practical problem-solving across the complete Third Term curriculum and foundational sessional competencies:
- **Week 01:** Healthy Feeding and Eating Practices I (Feeding/eating habits, the six essential nutrient classes, characteristics and benefits of foods in season, and principles of balanced nutrition).
- **Week 02:** Healthy Feeding and Eating Practices II (Table manners, cutlery handling, dining decorum, and unique adolescent nutritional demands: growth spurts, calcium, and iron needs).
- **Week 03:** Effects of Unhealthy Feeding Practices (Spectrum of malnutrition; differential diagnosis of Protein-Energy Malnutrition: Kwashiorkor vs. Marasmus; micronutrient deficiency disorders: scurvy, rickets, goiter, night blindness, and anemia; overnutrition and dental caries).
- **Week 04:** Food Additives: Types, Uses, and Misuse (Preservatives, flavor enhancers/MSG, colorants, emulsifiers, antioxidants, food fortification vs. banned chemical carcinogens: potassium bromate and Sudan dye).
- **Midterm:** Midterm Diagnostic Synthesis of Weeks 01–04.
- **Week 06:** Food Contaminants and Food Adulteration (Biological, chemical, and physical hazards; pathogenic microbes: *Salmonella*, *E. coli*, botulism; aflatoxins; market food fraud; the four pillars of food safety: Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill).
- **Week 07:** Drug Abuse and Society (Pharmacological definitions; drug use vs. misuse vs. abuse; classification into stimulants, depressants, narcotics/opioids, and hallucinogens; adolescent vulnerability factors).
- **Week 08:** Effects of Drug Abuse on Body, Family, and Society (Neurobiology of addiction; tolerance, physical dependence, and withdrawal syndrome; organ pathology: liver cirrhosis, brain damage, respiratory arrest; domestic violence, crime, and economic ruin).
- **Week 09:** Prevention of Drug Abuse, Life Skills, and Regulatory Agencies (The CLEAR refusal formula; statutory mandates of NDLEA and NAFDAC; clinical detoxification, rehabilitation, and social reintegration; Sessional 80% Gate).

## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following best defines an adequate (balanced) diet?
A) A meal that consists purely of fried plantains and sugary soda
B) A diet that provides all six essential nutrient classes in the correct proportions needed to maintain health, growth, and energy (correct)
C) Eating only imported processed foods from foreign supermarkets
D) Eating whatever food is cheapest on the market floor
Feedback: A balanced diet delivers carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, and water in required proportions.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: Which nutrient class serves as the human body's primary and most readily accessible source of metabolic fuel?
A) Carbohydrates (correct)
B) Mineral salts
C) Dietary fiber
D) Vitamin D
Feedback: Carbohydrates are broken down into glucose, the body's primary metabolic energy currency.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the main nutritional advantage of purchasing and consuming agricultural produce that is "in season"?
A) It contains high levels of industrial chemical dyes
B) It supplies peak natural vitamins, fresh flavor, and high nutritional density at an affordable market price (correct)
C) It has zero water content
D) It never rots or spoils
Feedback: Foods in season offer peak nutrient potency and freshness at economical prices due to harvest abundance.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the primary function of insoluble dietary fiber (roughage) in the human gastrointestinal system?
A) Building bicep muscles
B) Adding bulk to food to stimulate healthy intestinal peristalsis and prevent constipation (correct)
C) Dissolving human teeth
D) Supplying immediate glucose to the brain
Feedback: Dietary fiber provides non-digestible bulk that ensures regular, healthy bowel movements.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: Why do adolescent girls require a higher dietary intake of iron compared to boys of the same age?
A) Girls have a faster walking speed
B) To replenish red blood cells and hemoglobin lost during monthly menstruation (correct)

Feedback: Menstrual blood loss increases physiological iron demand; inadequate iron causes anemia.

### Q7 [single-choice]

### Q10 [single-choice]

### Q11 [single-choice]

### Q12 [single-choice]

### Q13 [single-choice]

### Q14 [single-choice]

A) Goiter
B) Rickets (correct)
C) Night blindness

### Q15 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the primary dietary deficiency responsible for nutritional night blindness (nyctalopia)?
A) Vitamin A (correct)
B) Vitamin C
C) Iron
D) Iodine
Feedback: Vitamin A is needed to synthesize visual purple (rhodopsin) in retinal cells for vision in dim light.

### Q16 [single-choice]
Stem: Which mineral deficiency leads to an abnormal enlargement of the thyroid gland in the neck, known as Goiter?
A) Iron
B) Iodine (correct)
C) Calcium
D) Sodium

### Q17 [single-choice]

### Q18 [single-choice]

### Q24 [single-choice]
