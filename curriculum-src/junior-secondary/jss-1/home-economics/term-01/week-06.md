## Hook
Have you ever walked past a busy traffic intersection in Lagos, Kano, or Onitsha at ten o'clock on a Tuesday morning and seen a twelve-year-old child carrying a heavy tray of cold soft drinks or roasted plantains on their head, dodging moving cars in the scorching heat while other children are sitting safely in classrooms learning mathematics and science? Or have you heard of a brilliant young girl forced to drop out of school to become a domestic child maid or forced into early marriage? Is that fair? Does a child simply exist to be exploited, or does every child possess legal protections guaranteed by national and international law? In 2003, the Federal Republic of Nigeria enacted a historic law called the **Child's Rights Act (CRA 2003)**, declaring that every child has inalienable rights to education, health, protection, and dignity! But rights do not exist in a vacuum; every right comes with corresponding **responsibilities**. In this lesson, we will explore your fundamental rights as a child, uncover the legal laws that protect you, and examine the vital duties you owe to your parents, your school, and your nation!

## Key Terms
- Term: Child
- Definition: According to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child and Nigeria's Child's Rights Act (2003), a child is defined as any human being below the age of eighteen (18) years.
- Term: Child Rights
- Definition: Inherent, fundamental legal and moral entitlements possessed by every child to guarantee their survival, healthy development, full protection from abuse, and participation in society.
- Term: Child's Rights Act (CRA 2003)
- Definition: The comprehensive Nigerian federal legislation passed in 2003 that incorporates international conventions into national law to protect the welfare, dignity, and legal rights of all Nigerian children.
- Term: Child Labor
- Definition: Work that deprives children of their childhood, potential, and dignity, which is mentally, physically, socially, or morally harmful, and interferes with their regular schooling.
- Term: Child Abuse
- Definition: Any intentional act or failure to act by a parent, guardian, or caregiver that results in physical harm, emotional injury, sexual exploitation, or severe neglect of a child.
- Term: Child Exploitation
- Definition: The unjust, self-serving use of a child for commercial, sexual, or economic profit, such as child trafficking, street begging, or hazardous labor.
- Term: Responsibility
- Definition: A moral, domestic, or civic duty and obligation that an individual is expected to perform toward themselves, their family, their school, and their community.
- Term: Best Interests of the Child
- Definition: The overarching legal principle of the Child's Rights Act dictating that in all actions and decisions concerning children, the child's ultimate welfare must be the primary consideration.

### 2. Fundamental Rights of the Child (Child's Rights Act 2003)

#### A. Survival Rights
- **Right to Life:** Every child has an inherent right to life; infanticide or denial of medical survival care is a capital crime.
- **Right to Adequate Standard of Living:** Entitlement to balanced nutritious food, potable drinking water, weather-appropriate clothing, and secure, sanitary shelter provided by parents and supported by the state.
- **Right to Health and Healthcare Services:** Free access to life-saving childhood immunizations (against polio, measles, tuberculosis), primary healthcare, and medical treatment when sick.

#### B. Development Rights
- **Right to Free, Compulsory Basic Education:** Every Nigerian child has the legal right to free, compulsory primary and junior secondary education under the Universal Basic Education (UBE) framework. Keeping a child out of school is a punishable offense for parents.
- **Right to a Name and Legal Nationality:** Every child must be registered at birth with the National Population Commission (NPC), receiving a legal birth certificate, a recognized name, and Nigerian citizenship.
- **Right to Leisure, Rest, and Recreation:** Children are entitled to age-appropriate play, physical recreation, sports, and cultural arts to foster healthy emotional and physical growth.
- **Right to Parental Care and Upbringing:** The right to live with and be nurtured by one's parents, except when separation is legally determined to be in the child's best interest.

#### C. Protection Rights
The Child's Rights Act strictly shields children from harmful societal practices:
- **Freedom from Child Labor:** Prohibits hazardous economic exploitation, including street hawking during school hours, child domestic servitude, quarry mining, and industrial factory labor.
- **Protection from Child Abuse:** Bans all physical brutality, harsh corporal injury, emotional cruelty, and sexual abuse.
- **Prohibition of Child Marriage:** Section 21 of the CRA (2003) explicitly declares: *"No person under the age of 18 years is capable of contracting a valid marriage."* Any adult who marries a child or arranges the marriage of a child commits a serious criminal offense.
- **Protection from Harmful Traditional Practices:** Prohibits harmful practices such as **Female Genital Mutilation (FGM)**, forced tribal scarification, and witchcraft accusations against children.
- **Protection of Children with Disabilities:** Children living with physical, sensory, or intellectual disabilities have the right to special dignity, inclusive education, assistive devices, and barrier-free public facilities.

#### D. Participation Rights
- **Right to Freedom of Thought, Conscience, and Expression:** Children are entitled to express their thoughts and opinions respectfully in matters affecting their lives, appropriate to their age and maturity.

### 3. Responsibilities and Duties of the Child
A healthy society requires balance: **Rights cannot exist without corresponding responsibilities!** A child who demands their rights must fulfill their moral, domestic, and civic obligations:

#### A. Responsibilities to Parents and Family
1. **Respect, Honor, and Obedience:** Treating parents, guardians, and older family members with deep respect, politeness, and gratitude.
2. **Assisting with Household Chores:** Willingly and cheerfully performing age-appropriate domestic tasks (washing dishes, sweeping floors, making beds, weeding gardens, caring for younger siblings).
3. **Upholding Family Honor:** Behaving with integrity, honesty, and moral dignity in public so as not to bring shame, embarrassment, or dishonor to the family name.

#### B. Responsibilities to Oneself
1. **Dedication to Education:** Attending classes punctually, completing homework assignments diligently, listening to teachers, and studying hard to achieve academic excellence.
2. **Personal Hygiene and Health Care:** Keeping the body, clothing, and living space clean, eating healthy food, and exercising regularly.
3. **Avoiding Harmful Vices:** Resisting negative peer pressure; saying an absolute **"NO"** to illicit drugs, alcohol, smoking, cultism, sexual misconduct, and examination malpractice.

#### C. Responsibilities to the School and Peers
1. **Obeying School Regulations:** Adhering to school uniform rules, attending assemblies, and respecting teachers and school prefects.
2. **Kindness and Anti-Bullying:** Treating classmates with empathy and fairness; standing up against bullying, harassment, and discrimination.
3. **Protecting School Property:** Caring for school desks, library books, laboratory apparatus, and campus gardens.

#### D. Responsibilities to the Community and Nation
1. **Patriotism and Civic Duty:** Respecting Nigerian national symbols (the National Flag, National Anthem, and National Pledge); obeying civil laws.
2. **Community Service:** Participating in environmental sanitation, keeping drainage gutters clear of plastic waste, and promoting peaceful coexistence across ethnic and religious lines.

## Common Mistakes
- **Demanding Rights While Refusing Responsibilities:** A teenager insisting on their "right to play" while refusing to sweep their room, wash their plate, or do their homework. Rights and responsibilities are inseparable partners; enjoying rights requires fulfilling domestic duties.
- **Thinking a Child is Property:** Believing parents "own" a child and can treat them however they like, including pulling them out of school or marrying them off. Under the Child's Rights Act, a child is a legal human being with protected constitutional rights.
- **Confusing Normal Chores with Child Labor:** Claiming that washing plates or making one's bed at home is "illegal child labor." Age-appropriate domestic chores teach valuable life skills and responsibility; child labor is hazardous, commercial, exploitative work that prevents schooling or harms health.
- **Believing Child Marriage is a Private Family Matter:** Viewing the marriage of an underage girl as a private cultural choice. Under Section 21 of the CRA 2003, child marriage is a **federal criminal offense** carrying severe prison penalties.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Differentiating Domestic Chores from Exploitative Child Labor
**Scenario:** Examine the daily schedules of two 12-year-old boys living in Enugu:
- *Boy A (Emeka):* Wakes up at 6:00 AM, sweeps the compound, washes his breakfast plate, goes to school from 7:30 AM to 2:00 PM, returns home, completes his homework, plays football with friends for an hour, and washes the dinner dishes at 7:30 PM.
- *Boy B (Musa):* Does not attend school. Wakes up at 5:00 AM, is handed a wooden tray with 50 cold meat pies by an adult, and must walk 15 kilometers along busy expressways selling meat pies until 8:00 PM every day to generate cash for a guardian.
- **Task:** Classify each boy's situation and explain whether it constitutes legitimate domestic duty or illegal child labor.
- **Solution:**
  - *Boy A (Emeka):* Represents **legitimate domestic chores and healthy child development**. His household duties are age-appropriate, do not interfere with his basic education, leave time for play and rest, and build valuable life skills.
  - *Boy B (Musa):* Represents **illegal child labor and economic exploitation** under the Child's Rights Act (2003). It denies him his fundamental right to basic education, exposes him to vehicular accidents and physical danger, deprives him of rest, and constitutes commercial exploitation.

### Example 2: Resolving a Conflict of Rights and Responsibilities
**Question:** A 13-year-old student tells her father: "The Child's Rights Act gives me the right to leisure and play, so I will play video games all evening and I will not do my homework or wash my school uniform." How should the father explain the relationship between rights and responsibilities to her?
- **Solution:**
  - The father should explain that under the Child's Rights Act, **every right is accompanied by an inseparable corresponding responsibility**.
  - While she has the right to recreational leisure, she has an equal legal and moral responsibility to:
    1. Prepare for her own future by completing her school studies and homework diligently.
    2. Maintain personal cleanliness and care for her own clothing and uniforms.
    3. Contribute to the smooth running of the family home by performing domestic duties before engaging in entertainment.
  - Mature individuals balance recreation with discipline, earning their leisure time through the responsible fulfillment of duties.

## Guardian Sync
- **Family Responsibilities Rota:** Create a simple weekly chore chart together with your child. Assign age-appropriate tasks (setting the dinner table, making their bed, feeding pets) and celebrate their contribution to the family's welfare!
- **Child's Rights Discussion:** Discuss the Child's Rights Act at the dinner table. Reassure your child that your home is a safe space where they are loved, protected from harm, and supported in their education.
- **Civic Awareness Walk:** When you encounter a child working or begging on the street during school hours, take the opportunity to explain to your child why universal basic education is vital and how society must work to protect every child's future.
