## Hook
Imagine traveling across the length and breadth of Nigeria: from the stilt houses floating peacefully on the riverine waters of the Niger Delta in Bayelsa, to the cool mud-and-thatch round huts in rural villages of Plateau and Bauchi, and the towering glass-and-concrete multi-story duplexes of Lekki in Lagos and Maitama in Abuja! Human beings have devised hundreds of ingenious ways to build shelters. But why do we need houses in the first place? Could human civilization survive if we slept exposed on bare soil in the rain, wind, and darkness? A house is not just an assembly of walls and roofs; it is our primary fortress against weather, wild beasts, and danger, and the sanctuary where our families grow, rest, and bond. But even the most beautiful building will quickly turn into an unlivable, disease-ridden ruin if it is not cared for properly! In this lesson, we will explore the major types of houses in Nigeria, tour the functional rooms of a home, and master the daily, weekly, and seasonal cleaning routines that keep our living spaces sparkling, safe, and welcoming!

## Key Terms
- Term: Housing (Shelter)
- Definition: Any physical structure and its surrounding environment that provides human beings with protection, security, privacy, and facilities for daily living and personal development.
- Term: Traditional House
- Definition: A residential dwelling constructed from locally sourced natural materials (such as mud, clay, timber, bamboo, and thatched grass) based on indigenous cultural architecture.
- Term: Modern House
- Definition: A durable residential building constructed from industrial, engineered materials such as cement blocks, reinforced concrete, steel, glass, and corrugated zinc or aluminum tiles.
- Term: Bungalow
- Definition: A single-story modern residential building with all its rooms located on the same ground level.
- Term: Duplex (Storey Building)
- Definition: A two-story modern residential dwelling where rooms are arranged across two vertical floor levels connected by an internal staircase.
- Term: Functional Areas of the Home
- Definition: Specific designated rooms or zones in a house arranged and furnished to support distinct human activities (e.g., living area, sleeping area, food preparation area, sanitary area).
- Term: Ventilation
- Definition: The continuous, unobstructed circulation and replacement of stale indoor air with fresh, cool outdoor air through windows, vents, and doors.
- Term: Periodic (Seasonal) Cleaning
- Definition: Thorough, deep cleaning carried out occasionally (monthly, quarterly, or during festive seasons) to maintain parts of the house not reached during daily cleaning.

## Core Concept
### 1. Importance and Functions of Housing
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Housing" caption="Housing: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

Housing is universally recognized as one of the three basic biological necessities of human life (alongside food and clothing):
- **Physical Protection:** Shields the family from harsh environmental extremes: torrential downpours, scorching tropical sun, freezing harmattan dust, and howling storms.
- **Security and Safety:** Provides physical security against wild animals, poisonous snakes, pests, burglars, and unauthorized intruders.
- **Privacy and Rest:** Affords individuals and couples personal privacy to rest, sleep, dress, and recuperate away from public scrutiny.
- **Storage of Possessions:** Secures family possessions, food supplies, clothing, household tools, and valuable documents.
- **Venue for Family Cohesion:** Provides a comfortable physical setting where family members gather to eat, play, converse, pray, and nurture relationships.

### 2. Types of Houses in Nigeria

#### A. Traditional Houses
Constructed using indigenous architectural methods and natural raw materials:
- **Mud and Thatched Roof Huts:** Common across rural communities in the savanna and forest belts. Walls are made of sun-dried clay or compacted laterite mud; roofs are thatched with dried Spear grass (*Imperata*) or woven palm fronds.
  - *Merit:* Excellent thermal insulation—thick mud walls keep interiors naturally cool during blistering afternoons and warm during chilly harmattan nights; almost zero cash cost.
  - *Demerit:* Vulnerable to water erosion during heavy rains; thatched roofs can harbor snakes, scorpions, and termites, and pose serious fire hazards during the dry season.
- **Riverine Stilt Houses:** Built by fishing communities in the Niger Delta swamps and coastal lagoons (e.g., Makoko in Lagos). Houses are elevated on sturdy wooden or bamboo stilts above the water surface to protect against tidal flooding.

#### B. Modern Houses
Constructed using industrialized, engineered building technologies:
- **Bungalow:** A single-story building where all rooms (living room, bedrooms, kitchen, bathrooms) are on the ground floor. Ideal for families with small children or elderly relatives who cannot climb stairs easily.
- **Duplex (Two-Story Building):** A residential building arranged on two vertical levels connected by an internal staircase. The ground floor typically houses the public reception areas (living room, dining, kitchen, guest room), while the upper floor contains private family bedrooms.
- **Block of Flats (Apartments):** A multi-story building containing several separate, self-contained apartments occupied by different families sharing common stairways and compound facilities.
- **Terraced Houses (Townhouses):** A continuous row of identical houses joined together by common side walls (*party walls*).

### 3. Functional Areas (Rooms) of the Home
A well-planned house is divided into distinct zones designed for specific domestic activities:
1. **Social / Living Area (The Living Room / Parlor):**
   - The central public room for family relaxation, entertaining guests, and conversation.
   - *Furnishings:* Comfortable sofa set, coffee table, television/media unit, curtains, reading lamps.
2. **Dining Area:**
   - Designed for family meals. Located adjacent to the kitchen for convenient serving.
   - *Furnishings:* Sturdy dining table, comfortable dining chairs, sideboard for crockery and cutlery.
3. **Food Preparation Area (The Kitchen):**
   - The culinary workshop of the home. Requires impervious, washable floor surfaces and abundant cross-ventilation to remove smoke and cooking heat.
   - *Equipment:* Gas cooker/stove, washing sink with running water, food preparation counter, refrigerator/freezer, ventilated storage cabinets for food and cookware.
4. **Rest and Sleeping Area (Bedrooms):**
   - Private sanctuaries designed for uninterrupted sleep, rest, and personal dressing.
   - *Furnishings:* Bed with supportive mattress, pillows, wardrobes/closets, bedside table, dressing mirror.
5. **Sanitary Area (Bathrooms and Toilets):**
   - Dedicated to personal hygiene, bathing, and waste elimination. Requires tiled walls and floors, adequate drainage, and good ventilation.
   - *Fixtures:* Water closet (WC), wash hand basin with soap dish, shower or bathtub, mirror, towel rail, ventilation window.
6. **Compound and Exterior Grounds:**
   - Outdoor grounds for recreation, drying laundry, parking, and gardening.

### 4. Cleaning Routines and Care of the Home
A clean home prevents disease, eliminates pest infestations (cockroaches, rats), and creates a peaceful atmosphere:

1. **Daily Cleaning Routines:**
   - Open windows early to let in fresh morning air and bright sunlight (sunlight naturally kills airborne pathogens).
   - Air bed linens, shake pillows, and make beds neatly.
   - Wash all breakfast and cooking dishes promptly; never leave dirty plates overnight in the sink to attract cockroaches and rodents.
   - Sweep all rooms from the corners toward the center and out to the dustpan; wipe down kitchen counters and dining tables.
   - Wash the toilet bowl with a brush and toilet cleaner; empty domestic waste bins into outdoor covered bins.
2. **Weekly Cleaning Routines:**
   - Use a long-handled broom or vacuum to sweep down ceiling cobwebs and wall dust.
   - Strip bed sheets and pillowcases for thorough laundering.
   - Scrub bathroom wall tiles, floor drains, and toilet bowls with disinfectant soap.
   - Damp-mop all hard tile or terrazzo floors using warm water and pine disinfectant.
   - Wipe down refrigerator door handles, light switches, and doorframes.
3. **Periodic (Seasonal / Special) Cleaning:**
   - Conducted monthly, quarterly, or before major festive holidays (Christmas, Eid):
   - Take down curtains and window blinds for washing.
   - Empty, defrost, and scrub all shelves inside refrigerators and freezers with warm water and baking soda.
   - Move heavy furniture away from walls to sweep and mop behind them.
   - Polish wooden furniture; inspect cupboards for signs of termite or weevil infestation.
   - Clear debris and dead leaves from roof gutters; scrub and disinfect outdoor drainage gutters to prevent mosquito breeding.

## Common Mistakes
- **Sweeping into the Center of the Room and Leaving Dirt There:** Pushing swept dirt into a heap in the hallway or under a rug. Swept dust must always be gathered immediately into a dustpan and deposited into a covered trash bin!
- **Leaving Dirty Dishes Overnight in the Kitchen Sink:** Leaving plates soaked with food scraps until the next morning. Overnight dirty dishes are an open invitation for cockroaches, ants, and rats, which contaminate food preparation surfaces with salmonella bacteria.
- **Keeping Windows Permanently Closed:** Locking all windows all day and night. Closed rooms trap moisture, carbon dioxide, cooking fumes, and body odors, creating a stale atmosphere that fosters mold growth and respiratory illnesses.
- **Using Acid to Clean Glazed Bathroom Tiles:** Pouring harsh, unbuffered industrial acid on bathroom tiles. Strong acid dissolves the grout between tiles, erodes porcelain glaze, and releases toxic, lung-burning chlorine fumes. Always use safe, formulated bathroom detergents!

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Planning a Functional Household Layout
**Scenario:** An architect is designing a four-bedroom modern bungalow for a family in Abeokuta.
- **Question:** How should the functional rooms be positioned relative to each other to ensure hygiene, efficiency, and quiet rest?
- **Solution:**
  1. *Kitchen and Dining Placement:* The **kitchen** must be placed directly adjacent to the **dining room** with a connecting service door. This minimizes walking distance with hot food and heavy dishes. The kitchen must have external windows and a back door for garbage disposal and ventilation.
  2. *Living Room Placement:* The **living room** should be near the main entrance to welcome visitors without them walking through private family quarters.
  3. *Bedrooms and Bathrooms:* The **bedrooms** should be grouped in a private wing away from the noisy living room and kitchen, with direct access to bathrooms for privacy and uninterrupted sleep.

### Example 2: Designing a Weekly Cleaning Schedule for a Working Family
**Question:** The Johnson family in Port Harcourt consists of two working parents and two teenagers in secondary school. Design a practical weekly cleaning schedule that distributes tasks fairly without overwhelming any single person.
- **Solution:**
  - *Daily Routine (15 minutes each morning and evening):*
    - Teenagers: Make beds, sweep bedroom floors, wash dinner dishes, and empty kitchen waste bins.
    - Parents: Wipe down kitchen counters, check stove, and inspect bathrooms.
  - *Saturday Morning Collective Routine (1.5 hours):*
    - *Son:* Clears ceiling cobwebs, sweeps the compound, and washes family footwear.
    - *Daughter:* Strips and washes bed sheets, wipes window sills, and cleans bathroom mirrors.
    - *Mother:* Scrubs kitchen stove, wipes down refrigerator, and mops hard floors with disinfectant.
    - *Father:* Scrubs bathroom tiles, disinfects toilet bowls, and cleans the outdoor drainage gutter.

## Guardian Sync
- **Room-by-Room Tour:** Walk through your home with your child. Point out the functional zones (living, dining, sleeping, cooking, sanitary) and discuss why each room has specific furnishings and rules.
- **Cross-Ventilation Habit:** Make it a morning ritual with your child to open the bedroom and parlor windows, feeling the rush of fresh morning air and discussing why fresh air prevents sickness.
- **Saturday Cleaning Partnership:** Partner with your child during your next Saturday home cleaning. Teach them hands-on how to mop a floor properly (wringing the mop damp, not dripping wet) and celebrate the sparkling clean result together!
