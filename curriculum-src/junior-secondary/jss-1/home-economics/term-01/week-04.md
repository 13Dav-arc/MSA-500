## Hook
Have you ever heard someone in your community whisper fearful rumors about a neighbor, claiming that you could catch HIV just by sitting next to them in a commercial bus, sharing a bench in church or mosque, or being bitten by a mosquito that bit them earlier? Or have you heard teenagers spreading false myths that washing with strong soft drinks or salt water after sexual activity can prevent infections? These dangerous myths and superstitious fears have caused immense suffering, unnecessary panic, and unfair discrimination across our society! In reality, science has unlocked exactly how microscopic pathogens attack our bodies, how infections are transmitted, and how they can be 100% prevented. In this lesson, we will uncover the factual truth about Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) and HIV/AIDS, learn how to protect our health through knowledge and personal values, and discover how empathy and compassion defeat stigma!

## Key Terms
- Term: Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)
- Definition: Infectious diseases passed primarily from one person to another through unprotected sexual intercourse, or secondarily via blood contact and mother-to-child transmission.
- Term: HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)
- Definition: A retrovirus that specifically targets, infects, and progressively destroys the CD4 helper T-lymphocytes (white blood cells) of the human immune system.
- Term: AIDS (Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome)
- Definition: The advanced, life-threatening clinical stage of HIV infection characterized by a severely depleted immune system and the onset of life-threatening opportunistic infections.
- Term: Opportunistic Infections
- Definition: Severe illnesses caused by pathogens that take advantage of a weakened immune system, which would normally be defeated by a healthy body (e.g., tuberculosis, severe pneumonia, fungal candidiasis).
- Term: CD4 T-Lymphocytes
- Definition: White blood cells that serve as the "generals" of the human immune system, orchestrating the body's defense against disease, which are targeted and destroyed by HIV.
- Term: Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)
- Definition: Daily medical drug combinations that suppress HIV replication in the body, lowering viral load to undetectable levels and allowing people living with HIV to live long, healthy lives.
- Term: Abstinence
- Definition: The conscious, deliberate decision to refrain completely from all sexual activity, representing the only 100% foolproof method of preventing sexually acquired STIs and adolescent pregnancy.
- Term: Stigmatization and Discrimination
- Definition: Unfair social prejudice, rejection, shame, and isolation directed against individuals living with or affected by HIV/AIDS or STIs based on ignorance and fear.

### 2. Common Sexually Transmitted Infections in Nigeria

#### A. Gonorrhea
- **Causative Agent:** The bacterium ***Neisseria gonorrhoeae***.
- **Symptoms:**
  - *In Males:* Burning, stinging pain during urination; thick, creamy yellowish-green pus discharge from the penis; painful swelling of the testes.
  - *In Females:* Often mild or asymptomatic initially; burning urination, increased vaginal discharge, pelvic pain, and abnormal bleeding.
- **Consequences of Neglect:** Leads to **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)**, blocked fallopian tubes, permanent ectopic pregnancy, and **infertility** in both men and women. If an infected mother gives birth vaginally, the bacteria can infect the newborn's eyes, causing permanent blindness (**ophthalmia neonatorum**).
- **Treatment:** Curable with doctor-prescribed antibiotics.

#### B. Syphilis
- **Causative Agent:** The spirochete bacterium ***Treponema pallidum***.
- **Progression in Stages:**
  - *Primary Stage:* A single, firm, painless round sore called a **chancre** appears at the infection site (genitals or mouth) and heals naturally after a few weeks, giving a false impression that the infection has gone.
  - *Secondary Stage:* Non-itchy reddish-copper skin rashes appear across the body (especially on palms and soles), accompanied by fever, swollen lymph nodes, and sore throat.
  - *Latent & Tertiary Stages:* If untreated, the bacteria multiply secretly for years, eventually attacking the brain, spinal cord, heart, and bones, causing blindness, mental paralysis (dementia), and death.
  - *Congenital Syphilis:* Transmitted from infected mother to fetus, causing stillbirth, deformities, or infant death.
- **Treatment:** Completely curable with penicillin antibiotics if diagnosed early.

#### C. Chlamydia and Trichomoniasis
- **Chlamydia (*Chlamydia trachomatis*):** Known as the "silent epidemic" because up to 70% of infected individuals show no outward symptoms while the bacteria silently damage reproductive organs, causing scarring and infertility.
- **Trichomoniasis (*Trichomonas vaginalis*):** A microscopic protozoan parasite causing frothy, greenish-yellow, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, intense itching, and discomfort during urination.

### 3. The Biology and Transmission of HIV/AIDS
**HIV** is a virus that specifically attacks the immune system:
- **How It Works:** HIV penetrates **CD4 T-helper cells**, hijacks their genetic machinery to replicate millions of new virus particles, and bursts out, destroying the host immune cell.
- As the CD4 count drops from a healthy 1,000 cells/µL down below **200 cells/µL**, the body loses its ability to fight common germs.
- The person enters the stage of **AIDS**, becoming vulnerable to lethal **opportunistic infections** such as tuberculosis (TB), chronic diarrhea, fungal pneumonia (*Pneumocystis*), and Kaposi's sarcoma (skin cancer).

#### The Four Verified Routes of HIV Transmission
1. **Unprotected Sexual Contact:** Sexual intercourse with an infected partner where infected semen or vaginal fluid enters the bloodstream through mucosal micro-tears.
2. **Mother-to-Child Transmission (MTCT):** From an infected mother to her baby during pregnancy (through the placenta), during labor and vaginal delivery, or after birth through breast milk.
3. **Contaminated Blood Transfusion:** Receiving blood or blood products that have not been screened for HIV antibodies.
4. **Shared Sharp Skin-Piercing Instruments:** Using unsterilized hypodermic needles, syringes, razor blades, traditional circumcision blades, scarification tools, or tattooing equipment contaminated with infected blood.

### 4. Prevention, Treatment, and Destigmatization

#### Prevention Strategies (The ABC Rule)
- **A — Abstinence:** For young adolescents and unmarried students, total abstinence from all sexual activity is the **only 100% effective, zero-risk method** to avoid sexually transmitted infections, HIV, and unwanted teen pregnancy.
- **B — Be Faithful:** For married adults, remaining mutually faithful to one uninfected partner.
- **C — Condom Use:** Correct and consistent use of latex barrier condoms during every sexual encounter reduces STI and HIV transmission risk for non-abstinent adults.
- **Safe Blood & Injections:** Screen 100% of donor blood in certified hospitals; always insist on brand-new, sterile, disposable needles opened from fresh packaging in your presence.
- **PMTCT Programs:** Pregnant mothers living with HIV take daily antiretroviral medication, which suppresses the virus so effectively that babies are born completely **HIV-free**!

#### Modern Management: Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)
- While there is currently no permanent cure or preventive vaccine for HIV, **Antiretroviral Drugs (ARVs)** have transformed HIV from a fatal death sentence into a manageable chronic condition.
- When taken faithfully every day, ARVs suppress the viral load in the blood to undetectable levels.
- **Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U):** A person living with HIV whose viral load is undetectable cannot transmit the virus to their sexual partners and can live a long, active, normal lifespan!

#### Ending Stigmatization and Discrimination
- People living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA) deserve love, respect, dignity, and equal social rights.
- Denying someone school admission, housing, employment, or friendship because of their HIV status is unjust, cruel, and illegal under Nigerian federal anti-discrimination legislation.
- Showing empathy, offering encouragement, and sharing meals with affected persons fosters a compassionate, healthy society.

## Common Mistakes
- **Believing Mosquitoes Can Transmit HIV:** Assuming mosquito bites spread HIV like malaria. HIV cannot survive or multiply inside a mosquito's digestive tract; mosquitoes do not transmit HIV!
- **Stopping Antibiotics Early When STI Symptoms Disappear:** Halting medication after two days because burning stopped. Stopping early leaves resistant bacteria alive to multiply, causing permanent internal reproductive organ scarring. Always complete the full course prescribed by a licensed medical doctor!
- **Thinking HIV Can Be Diagnosed by Looking at Someone:** Assuming someone looks "healthy" and therefore cannot have HIV. A person living with HIV can look completely robust, athletic, and attractive for up to 10 years without showing a single symptom while still carrying the virus. Only a laboratory **blood test** can determine HIV status!
- **Isolating Family Members Living with HIV:** Forcing someone with HIV to eat from separate disposable plates or sleep in an isolated room. HIV is not spread through saliva, sweat, tears, or shared plates; isolation causes immense emotional trauma.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Differentiating Transmission Facts from Myths
**Scenario:** During a youth fellowship meeting in Jos, four teenagers make statements regarding how HIV is spread:
- *Chidi says:* "If you drink water from the same cup as someone who has HIV, you will definitely catch the virus."
- *Zainab says:* "A pregnant woman who has HIV can give birth to a completely healthy, HIV-negative child if she takes prescribed PMTCT antiretroviral drugs."
- *Emeka says:* "A barber who cuts your hair with an unsterilized clipper blade that nicked an HIV-positive client five minutes earlier can transmit the virus if it cuts your scalp."
- *Bisi says:* "Hugging and shaking hands with an infected classmate spreads the disease."
- **Task:** Identify which statements are scientifically correct facts and which are false myths.
- **Solution:**
  - *Chidi's statement:* **FALSE MYTH** (HIV is not transmitted through saliva or shared cups).
  - *Zainab's statement:* **TRUE FACT** (PMTCT programs with antiretroviral drugs prevent vertical mother-to-child transmission).
  - *Emeka's statement:* **TRUE FACT** (Sharing unsterilized, blood-contaminated sharp instruments or barber blades carries a real transmission risk).
  - *Bisi's statement:* **FALSE MYTH** (Casual physical contact like hugging or shaking hands cannot spread HIV).

### Example 2: Analyzing the Danger of Asymptomatic Chlamydia
**Question:** Why is Chlamydia often referred to as a "silent infection," and why is it particularly dangerous to teenage girls?
- **Solution:**
  - Chlamydia is called "silent" because over **70% of infected females show zero visible symptoms**—no painful urination, no foul discharge, and no sores.
  - Because the girl feels completely healthy, she does not seek medical treatment.
  - Meanwhile, the bacteria slowly ascend into the uterus and fallopian tubes, causing **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)** and extensive internal tissue scarring. Years later, when she marries and desires children, she discovers her fallopian tubes are permanently blocked, causing irreversible infertility.

## Guardian Sync
- **Open Moral & Health Discussion:** Discuss with your child the importance of self-control, personal integrity, and abstinence during their teenage years as their strongest shield against STIs.
- **Salon & Barber Safety Rule:** Ensure your son visits a barber shop that uses functional UV sterilizer cabinets or take a personal clipper kit from home; ensure your daughter avoids sharing sharp unsterilized manicure/pedicure tools.
- **Empathy Reflection:** Talk with your child about how they should treat a classmate or community member who is sick or living with HIV, instilling values of kindness, inclusion, and compassion.
