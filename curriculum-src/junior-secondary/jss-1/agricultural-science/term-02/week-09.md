## Hook
Imagine walking into two neighboring poultry farms on the outskirts of Abeokuta or Kaduna. On Farm A, the birds are crammed into a dark, suffocating room with damp, foul-smelling litter; water troughs are coated with green slime, and wild pigeons fly freely in and out of broken windows, spreading germs. Half the flock is coughing, drooping their wings, and dying daily. On Farm B, the air is clean and breezy; the concrete floor is covered with dry, golden wood shavings; automated nipple drinkers provide crystal-clear water; and any visitor must step through a chemical disinfectant foot-bath before entering. The birds on Farm B are robust, energetic, and laying shiny brown eggs every morning! What makes the difference between catastrophic bankruptcy on Farm A and massive commercial profit on Farm B? The answer is **scientific animal husbandry**! In this lesson, you will master the principles of livestock housing, balanced nutrition, biosecurity, and health management that keep farm animals thriving—and unlock the Term 2 80% Mastery Gate!

## Key Terms
- Term: Animal Husbandry
- Definition: The agricultural science and practical art of breeding, housing, feeding, and caring for farm animals to maximize health, welfare, and productivity.
- Term: Intensive Management System (Zero-Grazing)
- Definition: A modern system where animals are permanently confined indoors, with all balanced feed, water, and veterinary care provided directly to them by the farmer.
- Term: Extensive Management System (Free-Range)
- Definition: A traditional system where animals roam freely over large expanses of land to forage for their own food and water with minimal human shelter or supervision.
- Term: Semi-Intensive System
- Definition: A hybrid system where livestock are housed in pens at night or during severe weather and allowed to graze in fenced pastures during the day with supplementary feeding.
- Term: Roughages
- Definition: Bulky livestock feeds containing over 18% crude fiber and relatively low energy, such as fresh pasture grasses, legumes, hay, and silage.
- Term: Concentrates
- Definition: Nutrient-dense, highly digestible livestock feeds with less than 18% crude fiber, packed with high concentrations of carbohydrates or proteins (e.g., maize, soybean meal, fish meal).
- Term: Biosecurity
- Definition: A strict set of preventive hygiene measures and protocols designed to keep infectious diseases, viruses, and biological vectors off a livestock farm.
- Term: Quarantine
- Definition: The strict isolation of newly purchased, returned, or diseased animals in a separate pen for 21 to 30 days to monitor for infectious pathogens before joining the main herd.

### 2. Housing Requirements for Livestock
Proper animal housing provides five fundamental conditions:
- **Environmental Protection:** Shields animals from scorching tropical sun, torrential downpours, strong winds, and dampness.
- **Predator and Theft Security:** Prevents access by snakes, stray dogs, rodents, and livestock rustlers.
- **Cross-Ventilation:** Continuous flow of fresh air that removes noxious **ammonia gas** produced by decaying urine and manure, preventing chronic respiratory infections.
- **Sanitary Flooring and Drainage:** Sloped concrete floors allow quick washing, disinfection, and drainage, preventing mud accumulation and crippling bacterial **foot-rot**.
- **Adequate Floor Space:** Eliminates overcrowding, preventing aggressive vices such as cannibalism and feather pecking in poultry, or ear biting in pigs.

#### Specific Housing Facilities
- **Poultry:** Raised in **Deep-Litter Houses** (floors covered with 5–10 cm of dry wood shavings that absorb droppings) or multi-tier **Battery Cages** (wire cages where eggs roll safely onto collection trays).
- **Pigs:** Housed in concrete pens equipped with separate feeding, sleeping, and dunging areas, plus shallow water **wallows** or mist sprinklers to cool down, since pigs lack sweat glands.
- **Rabbits:** Kept in raised **hutches** with wire-mesh floors that allow droppings and urine to fall through cleanly into collection trays below.
- **Ruminants (Cattle/Goats/Sheep):** Kept in well-ventilated sheds, fenced paddocks, or open-sided barns with shaded feed mangers and clean water troughs.

### 3. Livestock Nutrition: Roughages vs. Concentrates
A balanced animal ration must supply carbohydrates, proteins, fats, minerals, vitamins, and unlimited water:
- **Roughages (For Ruminants & Equines):**
  - High in crude fiber (>18%) and low in digestible energy.
  - *Pasture Grasses:* Elephant grass (*Pennisetum purpureum*), Guinea grass (*Panicum maximum*).
  - *Pasture Legumes:* Centro (*Centrosema pubescens*), Stylo (*Stylosanthes gracilis*), which fix atmospheric nitrogen and provide protein.
  - *Hay:* Sun-dried green pasture grass stored for dry-season feeding.
  - *Silage:* Succulent green forage preserved through anaerobic microbial fermentation in an airtight silo pit.
- **Concentrates (For Monogastrics & High-Yield Dairy/Beef):**
  - Low in fiber (<18%) and high in digestible energy and protein.
  - *Energy Concentrates:* Crushed maize, guinea corn (sorghum), wheat offal, rice bran, cassava meal.
  - *Protein Concentrates:* Soybean meal, groundnut cake (GNC), fish meal, blood meal.
- **Minerals and Water:**
  - Salt licks, dicalcium phosphate, and crushed oyster shells (for eggshell strength).
  - Animals must have access to cool, fresh, pathogen-free water at all times (*ad libitum*).

### 4. Routine Health Management and Biosecurity
- **Sanitation:** Daily sweeping of pens, scrubbing feed and water troughs, and applying lime or virucidal disinfectants.
- **Foot-Baths:** Disinfectant trays positioned at every pen entrance to destroy disease-causing microbes on workers' footwear before entry.
- **Parasite Control:**
  - *Endoparasites (Roundworms, Tapeworms, Liver flukes):* Controlled through scheduled oral administration of **dewormers (anthelmintics)**.
  - *Ectoparasites (Ticks, Mange mites, Lice):* Controlled through periodic dipping in chemical plunge dips, knapsack spraying with acaricides, or pour-on medications.
- **Immunization (Vaccination):** Strict vaccination schedules against deadly contagious epizootics:
  - *Poultry:* Newcastle disease, Gumboro (Infectious Bursal Disease), Fowl Pox.
  - *Goats and Sheep:* Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR).
  - *Cattle:* Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia (CBPP), Anthrax, Foot-and-Mouth Disease.
- **Quarantine:** Isolating any newly brought animal in a dedicated quarantine pen for 21 to 30 days to ensure it is disease-free before mixing with the resident flock or herd.

## Comparative Matrix
| Management Aspect | Extensive System (Free-Range) | Semi-Intensive System | Intensive System (Confinement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Capital & Equipment Investment** | Minimal; basic fencing or no shelter | Moderate; simple night sheds and grazing paddocks | High; automated housing, concrete floors, cages, feeders |
| **Feeding & Nutrition** | Foraging on wild bushes, roadside grasses, wastes | Foraging by day + supplementary concentrate at night | 100% formulated balanced rations provided indoors |
| **Exposure to Parasites & Predators** | Extremely high; frequent snake bites, theft, tick load | Moderate; controlled pasture exposure | Very low; zero tick exposure; screened against predators |
| **Labor Requirement** | Very low; occasional herding | Moderate; regular feeding and daily release/return | High; constant feeding, watering, cleaning, and record-keeping |
| **Growth Rate & Productivity** | Slow growth, low milk yield, irregular egg laying | Moderate growth and medium productivity | Maximum growth rates, peak egg production, high milk output |
| **Disease Biosecurity Control** | Impossible; animals mix with stray and wild stock | Moderate; perimeter fence prevents strange herds | Stringent; foot-baths, quarantine, closed biosecure perimeter |

## Common Mistakes
- **Putting Sick Animals in the Quarantine Pen with New Stock:** Treating quarantine as an isolation hospital for dying animals. Quarantine is strictly for **healthy-looking new arrivals** to verify they are not incubating disease; sick animals should be kept in a separate isolation infirmary.
- **Allowing Wood Shavings in Deep-Litter Poultry Pens to Stay Wet:** Ignoring damp litter around leaking drinkers. Wet litter produces high concentrations of toxic **ammonia gas**, causing eye blindness and predisposing chicks to deadly **coccidiosis**.
- **Feeding Monogastrics with 100% Dry Grass Roughage:** Giving pigs or broiler chickens dried grass hay as their main feed. Pigs and poultry cannot digest crude cellulose fiber; they will starve and fail to gain weight.
- **Skipping Disinfectant Foot-Baths:** Stepping over or around the farm foot-bath. Foot-baths are the primary biosecurity defense preventing viruses and bacteria carried on boots from contaminating clean animal pens.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Calculating Feed Ration Formulation
**Scenario:** A commercial pig farmer in Ibadan needs to formulate 100 kg of pig grower mash. The recipe requires:
- 60% energy source (crushed maize and wheat offal)
- 35% protein concentrate (soybean meal and groundnut cake)
- 3% mineral supplement (bone meal and oyster shell)
- 2% vitamin premix and salt
Calculate the exact weight of each ingredient group required to mix this 100 kg batch.
- **Solution:**
  1. *Energy source:* $100\text{ kg} \times 0.60 = \mathbf{60\text{ kg}}$ of maize and wheat offal.
  2. *Protein concentrate:* $100\text{ kg} \times 0.35 = \mathbf{35\text{ kg}}$ of soybean and groundnut cake.
  3. *Mineral supplement:* $100\text{ kg} \times 0.03 = \mathbf{3\text{ kg}}$ of bone meal and oyster shell.
  4. *Vitamin premix & salt:* $100\text{ kg} \times 0.02 = \mathbf{2\text{ kg}}$.
  - *Total batch weight:* $60 + 35 + 3 + 2 = 100\text{ kg}$.

### Example 2: Implementing Biosecurity Protocols Against Outbreaks
**Question:** African Swine Fever (ASF) has broken out on pig farms five kilometers away. Outline three non-negotiable biosecurity measures the farm manager must enforce immediately to protect their herd.
- **Solution:**
  1. *Strict Perimeter Lockdown:* Ban all outside visitors, middlemen, and unauthorized vehicles from entering the farm premises.
  2. *Mandatory Disinfection at Entrances:* Install deep wheel-baths for feed delivery trucks and fresh disinfectant foot-baths with active virucides for all workers entering the pig pens.
  3. *Total Ban on Kitchen Swill Feeding:* Stop all feeding of unboiled hotel or restaurant food scraps (swill) that could contain contaminated pork products carrying the African Swine Fever virus.
