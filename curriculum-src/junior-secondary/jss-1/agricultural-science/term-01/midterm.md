## Key Terms
- Term: Agriculture
- Definition: The art, science, and business of producing crops, rearing livestock, and processing and marketing agricultural products for human sustenance and economic gain.
- Term: Domestication
- Definition: The historic process of taming wild plants and animals and adapting them for human cultivation, breeding, and management.
- Term: Subsistence Agriculture
- Definition: A small-scale farming system aimed primarily at feeding the farmer and their family, using simple hand tools with little or no commercial surplus.
- Term: Commercial Agriculture
- Definition: A large-scale, highly mechanized farming system focused on producing high crop and livestock yields for sale and maximum profit.
- Term: Monoculture
- Definition: The continuous cultivation of a single crop species exclusively over a vast acreage, typical of commercial plantations.
- Term: Mixed Cropping
- Definition: Cultivating two or more different crop species simultaneously on the same plot to spread risk and provide dietary variety.
- Term: Annual Crop
- Definition: A plant that completes its entire life cycle from seed germination to seed production and death within one growing season or year (e.g., maize, cowpea).
- Term: Biennial Crop
- Definition: A plant requiring two full growing seasons to complete its life cycle, storing food in year one and flowering/seeding in year two (e.g., carrot, onion).
- Term: Perennial Crop
- Definition: A plant that lives, grows, and produces harvests for more than two years without dying after each harvest (e.g., cocoa, oil palm, mango).
- Term: Horticulture
- Definition: The specialized branch of agriculture dealing with the cultivation of fruits (pomology), vegetables (olericulture), and flowers/ornamental plants (floriculture).

1. **Foundations and National Importance (Week 01):**
   - Agriculture provides food security, natural clothing fibers (cotton), shelter materials (timber), and industrial feedstock (rubber, palm oil).
   - Generates non-oil foreign exchange and employs over 35% of the Nigerian labor force.
2. **History and Branches (Week 02):**
   - Transition from nomadic hunting and gathering to settled domestication (Neolithic Revolution).
   - Specialized branches: Agronomy (field crops), Animal Husbandry (livestock), Soil Science (fertility/NPK), Forestry, Aquaculture, Agricultural Economics, and Agricultural Extension (farmer education).
   - Horticulture branches: Pomology (fruits), Olericulture (vegetables), Floriculture (ornamentals).
3. **Farming Systems (Week 03):**
   - **Subsistence:** Small fragmented land (0.5–2ha), unpaid family labor, manual hand hoes, low capital, mixed cropping, low yield per hectare.
   - **Commercial:** Large contiguous land (100–5,000ha), hired skilled professionals, mechanization (tractors/combines), heavy capital investment, monoculture, high yields per hectare.
4. **Crop Classification by Life Cycle (Week 04):**
   - **Annuals ($\le 1$ year):** Maize, rice, cowpea, groundnut, tomato, cotton. Ephemerals (6–12 weeks): amaranthus, waterleaf.
   - **Biennials (2 years):** Carrot, cabbage, onion, ginger. Stored food is typically harvested by farmers at the end of the first year.
   - **Perennials ($> 2$ years):** Cocoa, oil palm, rubber, cashew, mango, citrus. Requires an initial gestation period (3–7 years) but yields for decades.

## Comparative Matrix
| Subject Theme | Subsistence Agriculture | Commercial Agriculture | Biological / Economic Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operational Goal** | Family consumption & basic survival | Maximizing profit & industrial supply | Food security vs economic GDP growth |
| **Cropping Practice** | Mixed cropping (yam, maize, beans) | Monoculture (1,000ha pure soybeans) | Spreads risk vs optimizes machine harvesting|
| **Implements Used** | Hand hoes, cutlasses, axes | Tractors, plows, combine harvesters | High human toil vs high mechanical efficiency|
| **Annual Crops** | Cultivated on 1-acre village ridges | Harvested across 500ha with combines | Rapid food supply within 3 to 5 months |
| **Biennial Crops** | Harvested for family vegetable stew | Processed into commercial flakes/powders| Stores carbohydrates in roots/leaves in Year 1|
| **Perennial Crops** | A few backyard fruit trees | Multi-million Naira tree plantations | Generates continuous cash income for decades |

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Assuming subsistence agriculture is useless. Subsistence farmers feed over 70% of Nigeria's households; they are the backbone of rural survival.
- **Mistake 2:** Believing carrots produce flowers in their first few months. Carrots are biennials; they only flower and produce seeds in their second year of growth.
- **Mistake 3:** Confusing pomology with floriculture. Pomology is the study of fruit trees, while floriculture is the study of flowers and ornamental landscape plants.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking agricultural extension agents are tax collectors. Extension agents are agricultural teachers who train farmers on modern scientific practices.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Differentiating Farm Systems by Input and Output
*Question:* Compare a 1-hectare cassava farm operated by a family in Delta State with a 1,500-hectare cassava plantation operated by an industrial starch processing company.
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Analyze the 1-hectare farm:** Operates on small land, uses manual cutlasses and hoes, family labor, minimal capital, and the cassava roots are processed at home into *garri* and *fufu* for family meals. This is **Subsistence Agriculture**.
2. **Analyze the 1,500-hectare plantation:** Operates on vast contiguous land, uses motorized tractors for ridge making and mechanized harvesters, hires agronomists, requires hundreds of millions of Naira in capital, and sells all roots to a factory for industrial pharmaceutical starch. This is **Commercial Agriculture**.
3. **Conclusion:** Scale, mechanization, capital, and market destination define the farming system.

### Example 2: Calculating Crop Cycle Durations
*Question:* A farmer plants three plots on the same day in May: Plot A with maize, Plot B with onions, and Plot C with oil palm seedlings. Describe when each plot will reach its final biological harvest or maturity.
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Plot A (Maize):** Being an **annual crop**, it matures, produces grain, and dies within 90 to 120 days (3 to 4 months).
2. **Plot B (Onion):** Being a **biennial crop**, its bulbs are harvested for food in 4 to 6 months (vegetative phase), but if left for seed production, it completes its life cycle at the end of 2 full years.
3. **Plot C (Oil Palm):** Being a **perennial tree crop**, it establishes vegetative growth for 3 to 4 years (gestation) and then produces annual bunches of palm fruit for the next 25 to 30 years.
