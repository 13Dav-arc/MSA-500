## Hook
Imagine walking into a school farm or village plot on a Saturday morning. The sun is rising, the grass is wet with morning dew, and you have to clear thick elephant grass, dig planting ridges, transplant delicate tomato seedlings from a nursery bed, gather dry stones, and prune dead branches from an orange orchard. Could you accomplish all that with your bare hands? Absolutely not! Your hands would be blistered, cut, and exhausted within twenty minutes. For thousands of years, the ingenuity of farmers has centered on designing specialized **simple farm tools**. From the curved blade of the West African cutlass to the sharp edge of the West African ridge hoe, every tool has an engineered shape, blade angle, and handle designed for a specific task. But a good tool is only as good as the care you give it: leave a metal hoe lying in the wet grass, and rust will destroy the blade; store a cutlass with a loose wooden handle, and it becomes a dangerous projectile! In this lesson, you will master the classification and correct use of basic hand tools, learn proper cleaning and maintenance techniques, and conquer your Term 1 80% Mastery Gate!

## Key Terms
- Term: Simple Farm Tools
- Definition: Non-motorized, manually operated handheld implements used by farmers to execute basic farm operations like clearing, digging, planting, weeding, and harvesting.
- Term: Cutlass (Machete)
- Definition: A versatile handheld tool with a long, flat steel blade and a wooden or plastic handle, used for clearing bush, felling small trees, weeding, and harvesting.
- Term: West African Hoe
- Definition: A primary tillage implement consisting of a broad, slightly curved steel blade fitted to a short, bent wooden handle, used for making mounds, ridges, and digging.
- Term: Spade
- Definition: A digging and leveling tool with a flat, rectangular steel blade and a long 'D'-shaped handle, designed for cutting clean vertical sods and digging trenches.
- Term: Shovel
- Definition: A scooping and loading tool featuring a broad, curved, concave steel blade with raised sides, used for lifting and moving loose soil, sand, gravel, and manure.
- Term: Hand Trowel
- Definition: A small handheld gardening tool with a miniature curved, scooped metal blade used for digging small planting holes and transplanting seedlings without disturbing root balls.
- Term: Garden Rake
- Definition: A soil-leveling and clearing implement consisting of a horizontal metal bar with sharp vertical tines (teeth) attached to a long wooden handle.
- Term: Term 1 80% Mastery Gate
- Definition: The academic benchmark requiring students to achieve at least 80% competency across all Term 1 Agricultural Science topics before taking terminal examinations.

### Detailed Analysis of Basic Farm Tools

#### 1. The Cutlass (Machete)
- **Description:** A flat, single-edged high-carbon steel blade (ranging from 45 to 60 cm in length) fitted with a riveted wooden or molded plastic handle.
- **Varieties:**
  - *Curved-edge Cutlass:* Broad, curved tip providing extra forward momentum for bush clearing and chopping heavy branches.
  - *Straight-edge Cutlass:* Uniform width used for weeding, planting seed yams, and cutting grass.
- **Primary Uses:** Clearing brush, felling shrubs, weeding between crop rows, harvesting sugarcane and tubers, and chopping firewood.

#### 2. The West African Hoe
- **Description:** A broad, slightly curved steel blade fixed into a short, bent wooden handle (often carved from a natural tree fork).
- **Primary Uses:**
  - Making planting mounds, heaps, and ridges for yams, cassava, and sweet potatoes.
  - Loosening soil (primary tillage) and digging up mature tubers during harvest.
  - *Note:* The Dutch/English hoe has a long handle and is pushed forward for surface weeding.

#### 3. The Spade vs. The Shovel
Understanding the difference between a spade and a shovel is a classic agricultural examination benchmark:
- **The Spade:**
  - *Blade Shape:* Flat, rectangular, sharp-edged blade designed to cut into hard ground.
  - *Handle:* Long handle ending in a 'D' or 'T' grip.
  - *Primary Function:* Digging holes, leveling seedbeds, digging irrigation trenches, and cutting clean rectangular sods.
- **The Shovel:**
  - *Blade Shape:* Broad, curved, concave (dished) blade with raised shoulders.
  - *Primary Function:* Scooping, lifting, and transferring loose materials (topsoil, manure, compost, gravel, fertilizer) into wheelbarrows or trucks. **Not designed for digging hard ground!**

#### 4. The Hand Trowel
- **Description:** A miniature handheld implement with a curved, pointed stainless or carbon steel blade and a short wooden handle.
- **Primary Uses:**
  - Lifting delicate seedlings from nursery seed trays or nursery beds with their root soil balls intact.
  - Digging small transplanting holes in garden beds.
  - Mixing potting soil and fertilizer for nursery seedlings.

#### 5. The Garden Rake
- **Description:** A horizontal steel bar carrying 12 to 16 sharp, downward-pointing teeth (tines) attached to a long wooden pole.
- **Primary Uses:**
  - Leveling tilled soil to create a fine, crumbly tilth for planting small seeds.
  - Breaking up surface soil clods.
  - Gathering up cleared weeds, dead leaves, stones, and farm debris.

#### 6. The Wheelbarrow
- **Description:** A single-wheeled conveyance with two supporting legs, two steering handles, and a sturdy metal tray.
- **Primary Uses:** Transporting bulky farm inputs (manure, compost, seedlings, fertilizer sacks) and harvested produce from fields to farm stores.

#### 7. Pruning Shears and Secateurs
- **Secateurs:** Small, spring-loaded handheld clippers with scissor-action curved blades used for cutting small branches (up to 2cm thick), pruning dead twigs, and harvesting fruit bunches.
- **Pruning Shears (Hedge Shears):** Two long, straight steel blades with long handles used with both hands for trimming hedges, ornamental shrubs, and clearing farm borders.

### Maintenance and Storage of Simple Farm Tools
Neglected farm tools rust, lose their cutting edges, harbor fungal pathogens, and break down quickly. Farmers must observe four cardinal maintenance rules:
1. **Cleaning After Every Use:** Wash off all soil, plant sap, and manure with water and a stiff brush, then dry thoroughly with a rag before storage.
2. **Rust Prevention (Lubrication):** Coat metal surfaces with a thin film of clean engine oil, grease, or kerosene to prevent oxidation (rusting).
3. **Sharpening Cutting Edges:** Sharpen blunt blades regularly using a fine-grit carborundum whetstone or smooth flat bastard file. Never overheat blades on high-speed grinding wheels as it destroys steel temper.
4. **Wooden Handle Care:** Inspect wooden handles for splits or loose rivets; coat wooden handles with boiled linseed oil to prevent cracking, drying, or insect termite attack.
5. **Secure, Dry Storage:** Store tools in a dry, well-ventilated farm store; hang hoes and cutlasses on wall racks or peg boards rather than leaving them resting on damp concrete floors.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Using a shovel to dig hard, unplowed soil. Shovels are concave scooping tools designed for loose materials; using them to dig hard clay will bend the blade and break the handle. Digging requires a spade or mattock.
- **Mistake 2:** Washing tools with water and storing them while still wet. Storing wet tools accelerates rusting and rots wooden handles. Always dry tools completely before oiling.
- **Mistake 3:** Using engine oil on vegetable leaves. Oil is applied strictly to metal tool blades in storage, never onto live plants.
- **Mistake 4:** Leaving tools scattered in the field overnight. Unattended tools pose severe puncture injuries to farm workers, rust in dew, and are easily stolen.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Selecting Tools for a Tomato Production Cycle
*Question:* A JSS 1 student is assigned to manage a tomato project from land preparation to nursery transplanting and weeding. Identify the specific hand tool required for each stage:
1. Clearing dense elephant grass on the plot.
2. Creating raised nursery seedbeds and breaking fine soil clods.
3. Lifting four-week-old tomato seedlings from the nursery bed into field holes.
4. Transporting five buckets of decomposed poultry manure from the poultry house to the vegetable plot.
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Bush Clearing:** **Cutlass (Machete)** — engineered for slashing tall grasses and woody stalks.
2. **Seedbed Leveling and Clod Breaking:** **Garden Rake** — its tines pulverize clods and level soil tilth.
3. **Transplanting Seedlings:** **Hand Trowel** — scooped blade lifts seedling root balls intact without damage.
4. **Manure Haulage:** **Wheelbarrow** — single-wheeled balance transports heavy loads efficiently.
5. **Conclusion:** Every stage of farm production has an engineered tool designed for that specific operation.

### Example 2: Diagnosing Tool Degradation and Applying Preventive Maintenance
*Question:* A school farm storekeeper discovers that several cutlasses and hoes have reddish-brown flakes on their blades and loose, wobbling handles. Explain the chemical process that occurred, the risk it poses, and how to restore and maintain the tools.
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Chemical Process:** The reddish-brown flakes are **iron oxide (rust)**, caused by leaving wet metal exposed to atmospheric oxygen and moisture ($4\text{Fe} + 3\text{O}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{Fe(OH)}_3$).
2. **Hazard of Loose Handles:** A loose hoe or cutlass head can fly off during vigorous swinging, severely injuring nearby students.
3. **Restoration Protocol:**
   - Scrape off the rust using a wire brush or sandpaper.
   - Sharpen the cutting edge with a flat bastard file.
   - Tighten handle rivets or drive a wooden/metal wedge into the eye of the hoe.
   - Coat the restored steel with engine oil or grease.
4. **Preventive Standard:** Always clean, dry, oil, and hang tools on wall racks after every farm practical.

## Guardian Sync
- **Tool Shed Inspection:** Visit your home tool shed or garden corner with your child. Examine your hoes, cutlasses, or shovels together. Have your child demonstrate how to scrape off dirt, wipe the blade dry, and check for loose handles.
- **Safety First:** Emphasize to your child that farm tools are serious cutting and digging implements, not toys; they must always be carried blade-downward and never swung toward classmates.
