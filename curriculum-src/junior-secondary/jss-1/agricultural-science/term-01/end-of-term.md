## Key Terms
- Term: Agriculture
- Definition: The art, science, and business of producing crops, rearing livestock, and processing and marketing agricultural products for human sustenance and economic gain.
- Term: Neolithic Agricultural Revolution
- Definition: The monumental historical turning point when early nomadic humans shifted from hunting and gathering to settled crop cultivation and animal domestication.
- Term: Agronomy
- Definition: The specialized branch of agricultural science devoted to field crop production and soil management.
- Term: Subsistence Agriculture
- Definition: A small-scale, traditional farming system primarily aimed at feeding the farmer and immediate family using manual hand tools.
- Term: Commercial Agriculture
- Definition: A large-scale, highly mechanized farming system aimed at maximizing crop and livestock yields for sale and financial profit.
- Term: Monoculture
- Definition: The agricultural practice of cultivating a single crop species exclusively over a vast land area year after year.
- Term: Annual, Biennial, and Perennial Crops
- Definition: Crops classified by lifespan: annuals complete their life cycle in $\le 1$ year; biennials in 2 years; perennials live and yield for $> 2$ years.
- Term: Agro-Ecological Zones
- Definition: Regional geographical zones defined by rainfall, temperature, and vegetation (Mangrove, Rainforest, Guinea Savanna, Sudan Savanna, Sahel Savanna) that dictate crop adaptation.
- Term: Crop Pest
- Definition: Any living organism (insect, rodent, bird, nematode) that attacks, damages, or transmits diseases to crops and stored farm produce.
- Term: Tool Maintenance
- Definition: The systematic routine of cleaning, drying, sharpening, and lubricating farm tools to prevent rust and ensure operational safety.

1. **Meaning, Scope, and Historical Evolution (Weeks 01–02):**
   - Agriculture is derived from *ager* (field) and *cultura* (cultivation). It is an art (practical skill), science (biology/chemistry), and business (agribusiness/marketing).
   - Importance: Food security, clothing (cotton), shelter (timber), raw materials for agro-allied industries (textiles, breweries, tanneries, rubber), employment (>35% of Nigerian labor force), and non-oil foreign exchange.
   - Historical development: Nomadic hunting and gathering $\to$ Neolithic domestication $\to$ Traditional hand tools $\to$ Modern mechanization and genetics.
   - Branches: Agronomy, Animal Husbandry, Soil Science, Forestry, Aquaculture, Agricultural Economics, Agricultural Extension, and Horticulture (Pomology [fruits], Olericulture [vegetables], Floriculture [flowers]).

2. **Farming Systems (Week 03):**
   - **Subsistence:** Small plots (0.5–2ha), fragmented land, unpaid family labor, manual hand hoes, low capital, mixed cropping, low yield per hectare, family food objective.
   - **Commercial:** Massive contiguous land (100–5,000ha), salaried skilled labor, mechanized machinery (tractors/combines), heavy capital investment, monoculture, high yields per hectare, profit and export objective.

3. **Crop Biology and Economic Classification (Weeks 04 & 06):**
   - **Life Cycles:** Annuals ($\le 1$ year: maize, rice, cowpea), Biennials (2 years: carrot, cabbage, onion), Perennials ($> 2$ years: cocoa, oil palm, rubber, mango). Ephemerals mature in 6–12 weeks (amaranthus, waterleaf).
   - **Economic Classes:** Cereals (starch energy), Legumes (protein and *Rhizobium* nitrogen fixation), Roots & Tubers (stem tubers [yam] vs root tubers [cassava]), Vegetables (vitamins/minerals), Fruits (vitamin C/sugars), Oil crops (oil palm, groundnut, sesame), Spices (ginger, pepper), Beverages & Stimulants (cocoa, coffee, tea, kola), Fiber (cotton, kenaf, sisal), Latex (Para rubber).

4. **Agro-Ecological Distribution in Nigeria (Week 07):**
   - Dictated by the southward-to-northward decrease in annual rainfall (from >3,000mm on coast to <500mm in Sahel) and length of rainy season.
   - **Mangrove Swamp:** Tidal saline wetlands; swamp rice, raffia palm, aquaculture.
   - **Tropical Rainforest:** High rainfall (1,500–2,500mm); cocoa, oil palm, rubber, yam, cassava, plantain.
   - **Guinea Savanna:** "The Food Basket" (1,000–1,500mm); tall grasses; massive commercial yams, maize, rice, soybeans, sesame.
   - **Sudan Savanna:** Short grass, thorny acacia (600–1,000mm); sorghum, pearl millet, groundnut, cowpea, cotton, cattle ranching.
   - **Sahel Savanna:** Arid desert scrub (<500mm); early millet, date palm, gum arabic, nomadic camels/goats.

5. **Weeds, Pests, and Basic Farm Tools (Weeks 08–09):**
   - **Weeds:** Plants out of place competing for water, NPK nutrients, light, and space. Survival: prolific seed output, seed dormancy (5–20 years), underground rhizomes (Spear grass).
   - **Pests:** Organisms damaging crops. Insect mouthparts: Biting/chewing (grasshoppers, armyworms), Piercing/sucking (aphids, whiteflies, vectors of viruses), Boring (stem borers, cowpea bruchids). Non-insects: Rodents (grasscutters), Birds (Quelea birds), Nematodes.
   - **Basic Farm Tools:** Cutlass (clearing/slashing), West African Hoe (ridges/mounds), Spade (flat blade for digging/edging), Shovel (concave blade for scooping/loading), Hand Trowel (transplanting seedlings), Garden Rake (leveling seedbeds/gathering debris), Secateurs (pruning twigs), Wheelbarrow (transporting loads).
   - **Maintenance:** Washing dirt off, complete drying, oiling/greasing against rust, sharpening with files/whetstones, and hanging on wall racks in dry stores.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Calling agriculture an art only. Agriculture is an art (practical skill), a science (biology, chemistry, genetics), and a business (agribusiness, marketing).
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming yam and cassava are identical in plant anatomy. Yam is a modified underground stem tuber (with buds), while cassava is a modified true root tuber.
- **Mistake 3:** Believing a shovel can be used to dig hard, unplowed soil. Shovels are concave tools engineered for scooping loose materials; spades are flat tools engineered for cutting and digging hard ground.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking groundnut is only an oil crop. Groundnut is botanically a grain legume that fixes soil nitrogen, while commercially serving as a prime oil crop.
- **Mistake 5:** Believing cocoa can grow anywhere in Nigeria. Cocoa requires high humidity and rainfall (>1,500mm), which restricts it to the southern Rainforest zone.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Synthesizing the Complete Maize Production Cycle
*Question:* A student cultivates a 1-hectare maize plot from land preparation to harvest and storage.
1. Identify the life cycle category and economic crop class of maize.
2. Name the primary hand tools used for land clearing, ridge making, and weeding.
3. Identify one major insect pest that attacks standing maize stalks and one storage pest that attacks harvested dry maize.
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Biological & Economic Class:** Maize (*Zea mays*) is an **Annual cereal crop** (completes life cycle in 90–120 days; Poaceae grass family; starch carbohydrate energy).
2. **Hand Tools:**
   - Clearing: **Cutlass (Machete)**.
   - Primary tillage / ridges: **West African Hoe** (or tractor plow).
   - Weeding: **Hand hoe** or **straight-edge cutlass**.
3. **Pests:**
   - Field stem pest: **Maize stem borer** (*Busseola fusca*) boring tunnels in stalks.
   - Storage pest: **Maize weevil** (*Sitophilus zeamais*) boring circular holes into dry stored grain.

### Example 2: Correlating Ecological Zones with Agro-Allied Industries
*Question:* A multi-million Naira agribusiness consortium plans to construct three factories in Nigeria: (1) A rubber processing factory, (2) A groundnut oil mill, and (3) A commercial yam flour mill. In which specific Nigerian agro-ecological zones should each factory be established, and why?
*Step-by-step Solution:*
1. **Rubber Factory:** Should be located in the **Tropical Rainforest zone** (Edo, Delta, or Cross River State) because rubber trees (*Hevea brasiliensis*) require high rainfall and humidity to produce latex.
2. **Groundnut Oil Mill:** Should be located in the **Sudan Savanna zone** (Kano, Katsina, or Jigawa State) because groundnuts require sandy-loam soils and dry harvesting seasons.
3. **Yam Flour Mill:** Should be located in the **Guinea Savanna zone** (Benue or Niger State) because the Guinea Savanna is Nigeria's leading commercial producer of giant yam tubers.

A) Production of thousands of viable seeds per plant (correct)
B) Prolonged seed dormancy in the soil across multiple seasons (correct)
C) Underground vegetative spread via rhizomes, tubers, and stolons (correct)
D) Complete inability to grow in sunshine
Feedback: Prolific seed output, seed dormancy, and underground rhizomes make weeds resilient. Weeds thrive in sunlight.

### Q45 [multi-select]
Stem: Which of the following maintenance practices should be regularly carried out on simple farm tools? (Select all that apply)
A) Washing off dirt and drying metal parts thoroughly after each use (correct)
B) Sharpening blunt cutting edges with a file or whetstone (correct)
C) Oiling or greasing metal blades to prevent corrosion and rust (correct)
D) Leaving tools immersed in damp soil to keep the metal soft
Feedback: Cleaning, drying, sharpening, and oiling preserve tools. Leaving tools in damp soil causes rapid rusting and handle rot.
