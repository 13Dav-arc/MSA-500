# Third Term Midterm Continuous Assessment Review

This diagnostic continuous assessment evaluates student mastery across the foundational weed science units covered during Weeks 01 through 04 of the Third Term:
- **Week 01:** Definition and General Characteristics of Weeds (Plant out of place, survival adaptations, seed dormancy, and binomial nomenclature).
- **Week 02:** Classification and Dispersal of Weeds (Habitat, morphology, life cycles, and dispersal agents: wind, water, animals, explosive mechanism).
- **Week 03:** Economic Importance, Harmful Effects, and Beneficial Uses of Weeds (Resource competition, critical period, yield loss, reservoir hosts, allelopathy, and positive utilities).
- **Week 04:** Methods of Weed Control (Cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods; herbicide selectivity, environmental hazards, and Integrated Weed Management).

## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: In agricultural science, what is the most concise definition of a weed?
A) Any plant that produces timber for building
B) A plant out of place (correct)
C) Any green grass eaten by cattle
D) A flower cultivated in a pot
Feedback: Agricultural science defines a weed as "a plant out of place"—growing where it is not desired.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the botanical name of Spear grass, a persistent weed with sharp underground rhizomes?
A) *Panicum maximum*
B) *Imperata cylindrica* (correct)
C) *Ageratum conyzoides*
D) *Talinum triangulare*
Feedback: *Imperata cylindrica* is the botanical name of Spear grass.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following is a physical anatomical feature that distinguishes sedges like Nut grass (*Cyperus rotundus*) from true grasses?
A) Sedges possess round, hollow stems
B) Sedges possess solid, three-sided (triangular) stems (correct)
C) Sedges produce wooden fruits
D) Sedges have giant blue flowers
Feedback: Sedges have solid, triangular (three-sided) stems, whereas grasses have hollow, round stems.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the botanical name of Goat weed, a common annual broad-leaved weed in Nigeria?
A) *Imperata cylindrica*
B) *Ageratum conyzoides* (correct)
C) *Chromolaena odorata*
D) *Amaranthus spinosus*
Feedback: *Ageratum conyzoides* is the scientific name of Goat weed.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: What term describes the biological phenomenon where weeds secrete biochemical toxins into the soil that inhibit the growth of neighboring crops?
A) Allelopathy (correct)
B) Osmosis
C) Parturition
D) Rumination
Feedback: Allelopathy is the release of inhibitory biochemical substances by one plant that harms another.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: The survival adaptation where weed seeds remain viable in the soil for years or decades without germinating until disturbed is:
A) Seed sterility
B) Seed dormancy (correct)
C) High respiration
D) Fallow cycle
Feedback: Seed dormancy allows weed seeds to survive adverse conditions in the soil seedbank.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: Weeds with wide leaf blades, net-like (reticulate) venation, and deep taproot systems belong to which group?
A) Narrow-leaved grass weeds
B) Broad-leaved weeds (Dicots) (correct)
C) Aquatic ferns
D) Monocotyledonous sedges
Feedback: Broad-leaved dicot weeds possess wide leaf blades, net venation, and taproot systems.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: What notorious floating aquatic weed forms dense mats on Nigerian lagoons and rivers, blocking boats and suffocating fish?
A) Waterleaf
B) Water hyacinth (*Eichhornia crassipes*) (correct)
C) Spear grass
D) Goat weed
Feedback: Water hyacinth is an aggressive aquatic weed that blocks waterways and depletes dissolved oxygen.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: Weeds that complete their entire life cycle from germination to flowering and death within one growing season are:
A) Biennial weeds
B) Annual weeds (correct)
C) Perennial weeds
D) Epiphytes
Feedback: Annual weeds complete their entire life cycle in a single growing season or under one year.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: What specialized structure attached to the seeds of *Tridax* and Siam weed enables long-distance flight by wind?
A) Hard cloven hooves
B) Pappus (parachute of fine hairs) (correct)
C) Webbed feet
D) Gizzard stones
Feedback: The pappus is a modified calyx forming a parachute of fine hairs that allows wind flight.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: Weed seeds with stiff hooks, curved spines, or sticky burrs that cling to animal coats and clothing are dispersed by:
A) Wind
B) Animals and Humans (Zoochory) (correct)
C) Explosive mechanism
D) Moonlight
Feedback: Animals and humans disperse hooked or barbed weed seeds externally (epizoochory).

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: What cultural weed control method involves covering bare soil around crops with dry straw, grass, or plastic sheeting to block light?
A) Plowing
B) Mulching (correct)
C) Pruning
D) Roguing
Feedback: Mulching blocks sunlight, preventing dormant weed seeds from germinating.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: What type of herbicide kills specific weed classes (like broadleaf weeds) while leaving cereal crops like maize unharmed?
A) Non-selective herbicide
B) Selective herbicide (correct)
C) Total contact herbicide
D) Defoliant
Feedback: Selective herbicides target specific plant families without injuring tolerant crops.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: What natural biological enemy was successfully released in Nigerian waters to feed exclusively on invasive Water hyacinth?
A) Domestic cats
B) South American weevils (*Neochetina* species) (correct)
C) Soldier ants
D) Tilapia fingerlings
Feedback: *Neochetina* weevils feed exclusively on Water hyacinth, providing effective biological control.

### Q15 [true-false]
Stem: Slicing off the surface foliage of Spear grass with a hand hoe permanently eradicates it because grasses have no underground structures.
A) True
B) False (correct)
Feedback: False. Spear grass regenerates rapidly from food-rich underground rhizomes buried beneath the soil surface.

### Q16 [true-false]
Stem: Integrated Weed Management (IWM) strategically combines cultural, mechanical, biological, and chemical methods to achieve sustainable weed suppression.
A) True (correct)
B) False
Feedback: True. IWM integrates diverse control practices to achieve long-term, eco-friendly weed suppression.

### Q17 [multi-select]
Stem: Which of the following are broad-leaved (dicotyledonous) weeds? (Select all that apply)
A) Goat weed (*Ageratum conyzoides*) (correct)
B) Waterleaf (*Talinum triangulare*) (correct)
C) Spiny amaranth (*Amaranthus spinosus*) (correct)
D) Guinea grass (*Panicum maximum*)
Feedback: Goat weed, Waterleaf, and Spiny amaranth are broad-leaved dicots. Guinea grass is a monocot grass.

### Q18 [multi-select]
Stem: Which of the following are positive, beneficial uses of plants classified as agricultural weeds? (Select all that apply)
A) Provision of nutritious human food (e.g., Waterleaf) (correct)
B) High-protein livestock forage and pasture grazing (e.g., Guinea grass) (correct)
C) Soil conservation and erosion control on sloping land (correct)
D) Spontaneous production of chemical herbicide sprays in the air
Feedback: Weeds provide food, livestock forage, and erosion control. They do not manufacture synthetic chemical sprays.

- Spear grass: *Imperata cylindrica*
- Goat weed: *Ageratum conyzoides*
- Waterleaf: *Talinum triangulare*

### Q20 [matching]
Stem: Match each herbicide category with its correct description:
- Selective Herbicide: Kills specific weed types without harming the growing crop
- Non-Selective Herbicide: Destroys all green plant vegetation indiscriminately
- Pre-Emergence Herbicide: Applied to soil after sowing but before seedlings emerge
- Systemic Herbicide: Translocates through vascular tissues down to kill deep roots
Feedback: Selective spares crop; Non-selective kills all; Pre-emergence is before sprouting; Systemic travels to roots.
