## Hook
Suppose your family buys a circular loaf of freshly baked bread and slices it into eight identical pieces. If you eat three slices, what proportion of the entire loaf have you consumed, and what fraction remains for your siblings? In daily life, quantities rarely come in neat, indivisible whole units. A tailor measures two and three-quarter yards of fabric, a carpenter cuts half an inch from a door frame, and an athlete completes four and a half laps of a running track. Fractions are the mathematical tool that allows us to measure, divide, and represent broken quantities and parts of a whole with absolute precision.

## Key Terms
- Term: Fraction
  Definition: A numerical representation of one or more equal parts of a whole unit or set, written in the form $\frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ is the numerator and $b$ is the non-zero denominator.
- Term: Numerator
  Definition: The upper number in a fraction that indicates how many equal parts of the whole are being taken or considered.
- Term: Denominator
  Definition: The lower number in a fraction that indicates the total number of equal parts into which the whole has been divided ($b \neq 0$).
- Term: Proper Fraction
  Definition: A fraction whose numerator is strictly less than its denominator ($a < b$), representing a value less than one whole unit.
- Term: Equivalent Fractions
  Definition: Two or more fractions that represent the identical mathematical proportion or value, obtained by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.

## Core Concept
### 1. The Anatomy of a Fraction
A fraction represents division: $\frac{a}{b} = a \div b$.
- The **denominator** ($b$) names the fractional unit (e.g., thirds, quarters, eighths). The denominator can **never be zero** because division by zero is mathematically undefined.
- The **numerator** ($a$) counts the number of those fractional units.
- In a unit fraction, the numerator is always $1$ (e.g., $\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{10}$).

### 2. The Three Major Types of Fractions
1. **Proper Fractions:**
   - The numerator is smaller than the denominator: $\frac{\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}} < 1$.
   - Examples: $\frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{4}, \frac{5}{9}, \frac{11}{15}$.
2. **Improper Fractions:**
   - The numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator: $\frac{\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}} \geq 1$.
   - Examples: $\frac{4}{3}, \frac{7}{4}, \frac{13}{5}, \frac{8}{8}$. Note that $\frac{8}{8} = 1$.
3. **Mixed Numbers (Mixed Fractions):**
   - Consists of a whole number part alongside a proper fraction part.
   - Examples: $1\frac{1}{2}, 2\frac{3}{4}, 5\frac{2}{3}$.

### 3. Conversions Between Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions
- **Converting Mixed Numbers to Improper Fractions:**
  Multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and place the result over the original denominator:
  $$W\frac{a}{b} = \frac{(W \times b) + a}{b}$$
  - Example: Convert $3\frac{2}{5}$ to an improper fraction:
    $$\frac{(3 \times 5) + 2}{5} = \frac{15 + 2}{5} = \frac{17}{5}$$
- **Converting Improper Fractions to Mixed Numbers:**
  Divide the numerator by the denominator using integer division:
  $$\frac{\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}} = \text{Quotient}\frac{\text{Remainder}}{\text{Denominator}}$$
  - Example: Convert $\frac{23}{4}$ to a mixed number:
    $23 \div 4 = 5$ with a remainder of $3$.
    $$\frac{23}{4} = 5\frac{3}{4}$$

### 4. Equivalent Fractions and Simplification to Lowest Terms
- **Equivalent Fractions:** Multiplying or dividing both the numerator and denominator by the exact same non-zero number produces an equivalent fraction:
  $$\frac{2}{3} = \frac{2 \times 2}{3 \times 2} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2 \times 3}{3 \times 3} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \frac{8}{12}$$
- **Simplification (Reducing to Lowest Terms):**
  A fraction is in its **lowest terms (simplest form)** when its numerator and denominator share no common factor other than 1 (i.e., their HCF is 1).
  - To reduce a fraction in one step, divide both numerator and denominator by their **Highest Common Factor (HCF)**:
    Reduce $\frac{36}{48}$: $\text{HCF}(36, 48) = 12$.
    $$\frac{36 \div 12}{48 \div 12} = \frac{3}{4}$$

### 5. Comparing and Ordering Fractions
To arrange fractions in ascending (increasing) or descending (decreasing) order:
1. Find the **Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)** of all the denominators.
2. Convert each fraction into an equivalent fraction having this common LCM denominator.
3. Compare the resulting numerators directly.
- Example: Compare $\frac{3}{4}$ and $\frac{5}{6}$:
  - $\text{LCM}(4, 6) = 12$.
  - $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \frac{9}{12}$
  - $\frac{5}{6} = \frac{5 \times 2}{6 \times 2} = \frac{10}{12}$
  - Since $9 < 10$, $\frac{3}{4} < \frac{5}{6}$.

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Comparing Fraction Classes and Structural Conversions
- Label: Proper vs Improper
  Title: Less than 1 vs Greater than or Equal to 1
  Body: Proper fractions have numerators smaller than denominators and represent values less than 1; improper fractions have numerators equal to or greater than denominators and represent values of 1 or more.
- Label: Improper vs Mixed
  Title: Single Rational Quotient vs Integer with Remainder
  Body: Improper fractions express whole plus fractional parts as a single top-heavy fraction (e.g., 7/4); mixed numbers display the whole integer separately alongside the remaining proper fraction (e.g., 1 3/4).
- Label: Expansion vs Reduction
  Title: Generating Multiples vs Dividing by HCF
  Body: Expanding multiplies top and bottom by a common integer to find equal fractions; reducing divides top and bottom by their HCF to reach the simplest canonical form.
- Label: Common Denominator vs Unequal Denominator
  Title: Direct Comparison vs Conversion via LCM
  Body: Fractions with equal denominators are compared directly by their numerators; fractions with unequal denominators require converting to a common denominator using the LCM.

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Adding or subtracting the same number to both numerator and denominator to generate equivalent fractions (e.g., thinking $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1+2}{2+2} = \frac{3}{4}$).
  Fact: Equivalent fractions can ONLY be formed by multiplication or division, never by addition or subtraction. $\frac{1}{2} \neq \frac{3}{4}$.
- Mistake: Believing that a fraction with a larger denominator is always smaller than a fraction with a smaller denominator.
  Fact: This rule applies ONLY to unit fractions where the numerator is 1 (e.g., $\frac{1}{8} < \frac{1}{2}$). When numerators differ, fractions must be converted to a common denominator before comparing.
- Mistake: Leaving an improper fraction like $\frac{25}{4}$ as the final answer in a practical measurement word problem.
  Fact: In real-life measurements, improper fractions should be converted into mixed numbers ($6\frac{1}{4}$) so the physical quantity can be easily understood.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Converting Between Fractional Forms
Context: A recipe requires $\frac{19}{4}$ cups of cassava flour.
Problem: Express $\frac{19}{4}$ as a mixed number, and convert $4\frac{3}{8}$ into an improper fraction.
Fix:
1. Convert $\frac{19}{4}$ to mixed number:
   - Divide $19$ by $4$: $19 \div 4 = 4$ with remainder $3$.
   - $\frac{19}{4} = 4\frac{3}{4}\text{ cups}$.
2. Convert $4\frac{3}{8}$ to improper fraction:
   - Formula: $\frac{(4 \times 8) + 3}{8} = \frac{32 + 3}{8} = \frac{35}{8}$.

### Example 2: Reducing a Complex Fraction to Its Lowest Terms
Context: In a junior secondary mathematics test, a student scores 42 marks out of a total possible score of 70.
Problem: Express the student's score as a fraction in its simplest lowest terms.
Fix:
- The fraction is $\frac{42}{70}$.
- Find the HCF of 42 and 70:
  - $42 = 2 \times 3 \times 7$
  - $70 = 2 \times 5 \times 7$
  - $\text{HCF} = 2 \times 7 = 14$.
- Divide both numerator and denominator by 14:
  $$\frac{42 \div 14}{70 \div 14} = \frac{3}{5}$$
The student scored $\frac{3}{5}$ of the total marks.

### Example 3: Ordering Fractions with Different Denominators
Context: Three students run around a school track. Tunde completes $\frac{2}{3}$ of a lap, Emeka completes $\frac{5}{6}$ of a lap, and Amina completes $\frac{7}{12}$ of a lap.
Problem: Arrange the students in ascending order of distance covered (from shortest to longest).
Fix:
1. Identify denominators: $3, 6, 12$.
2. Find the $\text{LCM}(3, 6, 12) = 12$.
3. Convert each fraction to denominator 12:
   - Tunde: $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{2 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \frac{8}{12}$
   - Emeka: $\frac{5}{6} = \frac{5 \times 2}{6 \times 2} = \frac{10}{12}$
   - Amina: $\frac{7}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$
4. Compare numerators: $7 < 8 < 10$.
Ascending order: $\frac{7}{12} < \frac{2}{3} < \frac{5}{6}$ (Amina, then Tunde, then Emeka).

## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: In the fraction 5/8, what does the denominator 8 represent?
A) The total number of equal parts into which the whole has been divided (correct)
B) The number of parts that have been eaten or taken away
C) The whole number multiplied by five
D) The remainder left after long division
Feedback: In any fraction, the denominator indicates the total number of equal sub-parts comprising the whole unit.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following fractions is classified as a proper fraction?
A) 4/7 (correct)
B) 9/5
C) 7/7
D) 12/11
Feedback: In a proper fraction, the numerator is strictly less than the denominator ($4 < 7$), representing a value less than 1.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Convert the improper fraction 29/6 into a mixed number:
A) 4 5/6 (correct)
B) 4 1/6
C) 5 1/6
D) 3 5/6
Feedback: $29 \div 6 = 4$ with a remainder of 5. Therefore, $\frac{29}{6} = 4\frac{5}{6}$.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: Convert the mixed number 5 3/7 into an improper fraction:
A) 38/7 (correct)
B) 35/7
C) 22/7
D) 15/7
Feedback: Multiply whole number by denominator and add numerator: $(5 \times 7) + 3 = 35 + 3 = 38$. Result is $\frac{38}{7}$.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following fractions is equivalent to 3/5?
A) 12/20 (correct)
B) 9/20
C) 6/15
D) 15/30
Feedback: Multiply numerator and denominator by 4: $\frac{3 \times 4}{5 \times 4} = \frac{12}{20}$.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: Reduce the fraction 24/36 to its lowest terms (simplest form):
A) 2/3 (correct)
B) 4/6
C) 6/9
D) 12/18
Feedback: $\text{HCF}(24, 36) = 12$. Dividing both terms by 12 gives $\frac{24 \div 12}{36 \div 12} = \frac{2}{3}$.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: Which fraction has the greatest value among the following?
A) 5/6 (correct)
B) 2/3
C) 7/12
D) 1/2
Feedback: Convert to common denominator 12: $\frac{5}{6} = \frac{10}{12}$, $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{8}{12}$, $\frac{7}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$, $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{6}{12}$. $\frac{10}{12}$ is the largest.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: What missing number makes the statement 4/9 = ?/45 true?
A) 20 (correct)
B) 25
C) 18
D) 36
Feedback: The denominator was multiplied by 5 ($9 \times 5 = 45$). Multiply the numerator by 5: $4 \times 5 = 20$.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: An improper fraction is a fraction in which:
A) The numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator (correct)
B) The denominator is equal to zero
C) The numerator is strictly less than the denominator
D) The whole number is written without a fraction
Feedback: Improper fractions have numerators greater than or equal to denominators ($\frac{a}{b} \geq 1$).

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: Why can the denominator of a fraction NEVER be zero?
A) Division by zero is mathematically undefined (correct)
B) Zero is an odd number that cannot be divided
C) It makes the fraction equal to one million
D) Denominators must always be prime numbers
Feedback: In mathematics, dividing any quantity by zero has no logical meaning and is completely undefined.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: Express 45 minutes as a fraction of one hour in simplest terms:
A) 3/4 (correct)
B) 45/100
C) 2/3
D) 1/2
Feedback: One hour has 60 minutes. Fraction = $\frac{45}{60}$. Dividing top and bottom by 15 gives $\frac{3}{4}$.

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following pairs of fractions are equivalent?
A) 2/5 and 8/20 (correct)
B) 3/4 and 9/15
C) 1/3 and 3/6
D) 5/8 and 10/18
Feedback: $\frac{2 \times 4}{5 \times 4} = \frac{8}{20}$, so $\frac{2}{5} = \frac{8}{20}$.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: Arrange 1/2, 2/5, and 3/10 in descending order (from largest to smallest):
A) 1/2, 2/5, 3/10 (correct)
B) 3/10, 2/5, 1/2
C) 2/5, 1/2, 3/10
D) 1/2, 3/10, 2/5
Feedback: Denominator 10: $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{10}$, $\frac{2}{5} = \frac{4}{10}$, $\frac{3}{10} = \frac{3}{10}$. Descending: $\frac{5}{10} > \frac{4}{10} > \frac{3}{10}$.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the simplest form of the fraction 18/48?
A) 3/8 (correct)
B) 6/16
C) 9/24
D) 1/4
Feedback: $\text{HCF}(18, 48) = 6$. $\frac{18 \div 6}{48 \div 6} = \frac{3}{8}$.

### Q15 [true-false]
Statement: Multiplying or dividing both the numerator and denominator of a fraction by the same non-zero number alters the total numerical value of the fraction.
Answer: false
Feedback: Multiplying or dividing top and bottom by the same number multiplies by 1, preserving the identical value (equivalent fraction).

### Q16 [true-false]
Statement: A proper fraction always represents a value that is strictly less than 1.
Answer: true
Feedback: Because the numerator is smaller than the denominator, the value of a proper fraction is always strictly less than 1.

### Q17 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO fractions that are improper fractions:
Correct: 9/4, 7/5
Incorrect: 3/8, 5/6
Feedback: 9/4 ($9 > 4$) and 7/5 ($7 > 5$) are improper fractions; 3/8 and 5/6 are proper fractions.

### Q18 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO fractions that are in their lowest terms (simplest form):
Correct: 5/9, 7/12
Incorrect: 6/10, 9/15
Feedback: $\text{HCF}(5, 9) = 1$ and $\text{HCF}(7, 12) = 1$ (simplest form). 6/10 simplifies to 3/5, and 9/15 simplifies to 3/5.

### Q19 [matching]
Stem: Match each improper fraction with its equivalent mixed number:
Pairs:
- 13/4 -> 3 1/4
- 17/5 -> 3 2/5
- 19/3 -> 6 1/3
Feedback: $13 \div 4 = 3\text{ R }1$; $17 \div 5 = 3\text{ R }2$; $19 \div 3 = 6\text{ R }1$.

### Q20 [matching]
Stem: Match each fraction with its simplest reduced form:
Pairs:
- 15/25 -> 3/5
- 16/24 -> 2/3
- 28/35 -> 4/5
Feedback: $\frac{15}{25} = \frac{3}{5}$ (divide by 5); $\frac{16}{24} = \frac{2}{3}$ (divide by 8); $\frac{28}{35} = \frac{4}{5}$ (divide by 7).
