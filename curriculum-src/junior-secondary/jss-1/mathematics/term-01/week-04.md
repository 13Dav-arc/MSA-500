## Hook
Suppose a Nigerian farmer allocates $\frac{1}{3}$ of his farmland to cassava cultivation, $\frac{2}{5}$ to maize, and leaves the remaining portion fallow for cattle grazing. How much of the total farm is under active cultivation, and what fraction remains unplanted? Or imagine sharing three-quarters of a bag of rice equally among six families in a community. Solving these everyday practical dilemmas requires performing the four fundamental arithmetic operations on fractions: addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. By mastering the rules of common denominators, reciprocals, and the universal hierarchy of BODMAS, you gain complete command over fractional mathematics.

## Key Terms
- Term: Common Denominator
  Definition: A shared multiple of the denominators of two or more fractions, typically found using the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM), required to add or subtract fractions with unlike terms.
- Term: Reciprocal
  Definition: The multiplicative inverse of a number or fraction, obtained by swapping the numerator and the denominator ($\frac{a}{b} \rightarrow \frac{b}{a}$), such that their product equals 1.
- Term: Cross-Cancellation
  Definition: The mathematical simplification technique of dividing a numerator and a denominator by their common factor before carrying out fraction multiplication.
- Term: BODMAS
  Definition: The standardized mathematical order of operations determining calculation hierarchy: Brackets first, then Of (orders), Division, Multiplication, Addition, and Subtraction.
- Term: Unitary Method
  Definition: A problem-solving strategy that calculates the value of a single unit first before multiplying to find the required total quantity.

## Core Concept
### 1. Addition and Subtraction of Fractions
- **Like Denominators:** Simply add or subtract the numerators while keeping the common denominator:
  $$\frac{3}{7} + \frac{2}{7} = \frac{3 + 2}{7} = \frac{5}{7}, \quad \frac{9}{11} - \frac{4}{11} = \frac{9 - 4}{11} = \frac{5}{11}$$
- **Unlike Denominators:** 
  1. Find the **Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)** of all denominators.
  2. Convert each fraction into an equivalent fraction with the LCM as its denominator.
  3. Add or subtract the resulting numerators and simplify to lowest terms:
     $$\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{8}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{8 + 3}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$$
- **Mixed Numbers Addition and Subtraction:**
  Method: Add or subtract the whole numbers separately, then combine with the fractional part:
  $$3\frac{1}{2} + 2\frac{1}{3} = (3 + 2) + \left(\frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6}\right) = 5 + \frac{5}{6} = 5\frac{5}{6}$$
  When subtraction requires regrouping (borrowing):
  $$4\frac{1}{4} - 1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{17}{4} - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{51 - 20}{12} = \frac{31}{12} = 2\frac{7}{12}$$

### 2. Multiplication of Fractions and the Meaning of "Of"
- **Multiplication Rule:** Multiply numerators together and denominators together:
  $$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}$$
- **Cross-Cancellation:** Divide common factors before multiplying to keep numbers small:
  $$\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{4 \div 4}{9 \div 3} \times \frac{3 \div 3}{8 \div 4} = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6}$$
- **The Word "Of":** In mathematics, "of" signifies multiplication:
  $$\frac{3}{4} \text{ of } 240 = \frac{3}{4} \times 240 = 3 \times 60 = 180$$

### 3. Division of Fractions and Reciprocals
- **The Reciprocal (Multiplicative Inverse):**
  - The reciprocal of $\frac{3}{5}$ is $\frac{5}{3}$, because $\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{3} = 1$.
  - The reciprocal of a whole number $4$ (which is $\frac{4}{1}$) is $\frac{1}{4}$.
  - The reciprocal of a mixed number $2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$ is $\frac{3}{7}$.
- **Division Rule:** Invert the divisor (the second fraction) to its reciprocal and multiply:
  $$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c} = \frac{a \times d}{b \times c}$$
  - Example: $\frac{5}{8} \div \frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{5 \times 1}{2 \times 3} = \frac{5}{6}$.

### 4. Order of Operations with Fractions (BODMAS)
When multiple operations appear in a single expression, execute them strictly according to **BODMAS**:
1. **B**rackets: Evaluate expressions inside grouping symbols $(), [], \{\}$ first.
2. **O**f: Execute "of" multiplications next.
3. **D**ivision and **M**ultiplication: Work from left to right.
4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction: Work from left to right.
- Example: Evaluate $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{3}$:
  - Multiplication before addition: $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{2}$.
  - Addition: $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1$.

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Comparing Fraction Operations and Execution Rules
- Label: Addition vs Multiplication
  Title: Requires Common Denominator vs Direct Multiplication
  Body: Adding or subtracting fractions requires converting to a shared LCM denominator; multiplying fractions does NOT require a common denominator—simply multiply numerators and denominators.
- Label: Division vs Multiplication
  Title: Invert and Multiply vs Direct Cross-Cancellation
  Body: Division cannot be done directly across denominators; it must be converted into multiplication by inverting the second fraction into its reciprocal.
- Label: Mixed Addition vs Mixed Subtraction
  Title: Simple Summation vs Potential Whole Number Regrouping
  Body: Mixed addition always sums integers and fractional parts cleanly; mixed subtraction often requires borrowing 1 whole (as d/d) from the integer when the first fraction is smaller than the second.
- Label: "Of" vs Multiplication Sign (×)
  Title: Precedence in BODMAS vs Standard Product
  Body: In BODMAS, "Of" takes higher priority and must be calculated immediately after brackets, before regular division or multiplication.

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Adding denominators together when adding fractions (e.g., writing $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1+1}{2+3} = \frac{2}{5}$).
  Fact: Denominators cannot be added. Fractions must be converted to equivalent forms with a common denominator first: $\frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$.
- Mistake: Inverting the first fraction instead of the second fraction during division (e.g., writing $\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{5} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{4}{5}$).
  Fact: Always leave the first fraction unchanged; ONLY invert the divisor (the second fraction): $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{4} = \frac{5}{6}$.
- Mistake: Adding before multiplying in an expression lacking brackets (e.g., evaluating $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2}$ by adding first).
  Fact: BODMAS dictates that multiplication must be performed before addition.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Multi-Step Fraction Evaluation Using BODMAS
Context: A student is asked to solve a multi-step algebraic fraction problem on an examination paper.
Problem: Evaluate $2\frac{1}{2} - \left(\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\right) \times \frac{2}{5}$.
Fix:
1. Brackets first: $\left(\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{4} = \frac{5}{4}$.
2. Next is Multiplication: $\frac{5}{4} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{1}{2}$ (5 cancels 5, 2/4 reduces to 1/2).
3. Finally, Subtraction: $2\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2} = 2$.
The final evaluated answer is $2$.

### Example 2: Division of Mixed Numbers
Context: A dressmaker has $7\frac{1}{2}\text{ metres}$ of lace fabric. Each decorative school collar requires $\frac{3}{4}\text{ metres}$ of lace.
Problem: How many complete collars can the dressmaker cut from the available fabric?
Fix:
1. Set up division: $7\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{3}{4}$.
2. Convert mixed number to improper fraction: $7\frac{1}{2} = \frac{15}{2}$.
3. Invert divisor and multiply: $\frac{15}{2} \times \frac{4}{3}$.
4. Cross-cancel: $15 \div 3 = 5$, and $4 \div 2 = 2$.
5. Multiply: $5 \times 2 = 10\text{ collars}$. The dressmaker can cut 10 complete collars.

### Example 3: Real-World Allocation Word Problem
Context: A trader has a sum of $₦60,000$. He spends $\frac{1}{4}$ on school fees, $\frac{2}{5}$ on food, and saves the remainder in a cooperative bank.
Problem: What fraction of the money is saved, and how much money in Naira is placed in the bank?
Fix:
1. Total fraction spent = $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{2}{5}$.
   - $\text{LCM}(4, 5) = 20$.
   - $\frac{5}{20} + \frac{8}{20} = \frac{13}{20}$.
2. Fraction saved = $1 - \frac{13}{20} = \frac{20}{20} - \frac{13}{20} = \frac{7}{20}$.
3. Amount saved in Naira = $\frac{7}{20} \times ₦60,000 = 7 \times ₦3,000 = ₦21,000$.

## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: Evaluate: 3/8 + 1/4
A) 5/8 (correct)
B) 4/12
C) 1/2
D) 7/8
Feedback: $\text{LCM}(8, 4) = 8$. $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: Evaluate: 5/6 - 1/3
A) 1/2 (correct)
B) 4/3
C) 2/3
D) 1/6
Feedback: $\text{LCM}(6, 3) = 6$. $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{2}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Evaluate: 2 1/3 + 1 1/2
A) 3 5/6 (correct)
B) 3 2/5
C) 4 1/6
D) 3 1/6
Feedback: Whole numbers: $2 + 1 = 3$. Fractions: $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{6} + \frac{3}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$. Sum is $3\frac{5}{6}$.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the product of 4/9 and 3/8 in simplest form?
A) 1/6 (correct)
B) 12/72
C) 7/17
D) 1/4
Feedback: Cross-canceling: $\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6}$.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: What is 2/3 of 180?
A) 120 (correct)
B) 90
C) 60
D) 150
Feedback: $\frac{2}{3} \times 180 = 2 \times 60 = 120$.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the reciprocal of the mixed number 2 3/4?
A) 4/11 (correct)
B) 11/4
C) 2 4/3
D) 4/7
Feedback: Convert to improper fraction first: $2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$. The reciprocal of $\frac{11}{4}$ is $\frac{4}{11}$.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: Evaluate: 3/4 ÷ 9/16
A) 1 1/3 (correct)
B) 3/4
C) 27/64
D) 1/2
Feedback: Invert divisor and multiply: $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{16}{9} = \frac{1 \times 4}{1 \times 3} = \frac{4}{3} = 1\frac{1}{3}$.

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: Evaluate using BODMAS: 1/2 + 1/4 × 2/3
A) 2/3 (correct)
B) 1/2
C) 5/12
D) 3/4
Feedback: Multiply first: $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$. Then add: $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: A tailor has 12 metres of cloth. If each shirt requires 1 1/2 metres, how many shirts can be sewn?
A) 8 shirts (correct)
B) 6 shirts
C) 9 shirts
D) 10 shirts
Feedback: $12 \div 1\frac{1}{2} = 12 \div \frac{3}{2} = 12 \times \frac{2}{3} = 4 \times 2 = 8$ shirts.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: Evaluate: 3 1/4 - 1 2/3
A) 1 7/12 (correct)
B) 2 1/12
C) 1 5/12
D) 2 7/12
Feedback: $\frac{13}{4} - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{39 - 20}{12} = \frac{19}{12} = 1\frac{7}{12}$.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: Evaluate: (1/3 + 1/6) ÷ 1/4
A) 2 (correct)
B) 1/2
C) 1/8
D) 4
Feedback: Brackets first: $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$. Then divide: $\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \times 4 = 2$.

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: If 3/5 of a school class are girls and there are 45 students in the class, how many boys are there?
A) 18 boys (correct)
B) 27 boys
C) 15 boys
D) 20 boys
Feedback: Fraction of boys = $1 - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{5}$. Number of boys = $\frac{2}{5} \times 45 = 2 \times 9 = 18$ boys.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the result when 5/8 is divided by 5?
A) 1/8 (correct)
B) 25/8
C) 1
D) 5/40
Feedback: $\frac{5}{8} \div 5 = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{1}{5} = \frac{1}{8}$.

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: Evaluate: 2/5 × 15/16 × 8/9
A) 1/3 (correct)
B) 2/3
C) 1/4
D) 5/8
Feedback: $\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{15}{16} = \frac{3}{8}$. Then $\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{8}{9} = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}$.

### Q15 [true-false]
Statement: The product of any non-zero fraction and its reciprocal is always equal to 1.
Answer: true
Feedback: By definition, $\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{b}{a} = \frac{ab}{ab} = 1$.

### Q16 [true-false]
Statement: To add two fractions with different denominators, you can simply add their numerators together and add their denominators together.
Answer: false
Feedback: Denominators cannot be added together; fractions must be converted to equivalent fractions with a common denominator first.

### Q17 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO correct statements regarding fraction division:
Correct: Division is performed by inverting the divisor and multiplying, The reciprocal of 3/7 is 7/3
Incorrect: Both fractions must be inverted before dividing, Division requires finding a common denominator first
Feedback: Fraction division is executed by inverting the second fraction (the divisor) and multiplying. Common denominators are not required.

### Q18 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO operations that yield a result equal to 1:
Correct: 3/4 + 1/4, 2/5 ÷ 2/5
Incorrect: 1/2 × 1/2, 5/8 - 1/8
Feedback: $\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{4}{4} = 1$; $\frac{2}{5} \div \frac{2}{5} = 1$. $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{5}{8} - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$.

### Q19 [matching]

Pairs:
- 1/2 + 1/3 -> 5/6
- 1/2 - 1/3 -> 1/6
- 1/2 × 1/3 -> 1/6
Feedback: $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{6}$; $\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$; $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$.

### Q20 [matching]
Stem: Match each number with its mathematical reciprocal:
Pairs:
- 5 -> 1/5
- 3/8 -> 8/3
- 1 1/2 -> 2/3
Feedback: Reciprocal of 5 is $\frac{1}{5}$; reciprocal of $\frac{3}{8}$ is $\frac{8}{3}$; reciprocal of $1\frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}$ is $\frac{2}{3}$.
