## Hook
When you score 42 out of 50 in an end-of-term continuous assessment test, your teacher calculates your result as 84%. When a department store offers a 20% discount on school sandals or a bank credits an annual interest rate to a savings account, they are using the universal financial language of percentages. Percentages and decimals are mathematical twins—both allow us to compare proportions, evaluate performance, and negotiate financial transactions on a standardized scale of one hundred. Mastering decimal arithmetic and percentage calculations gives you the practical mathematical power needed for everyday commerce and science.

## Key Terms
- Term: Percentage
  Definition: A fraction whose denominator is fixed at 100, represented by the percent symbol (%), derived from the Latin phrase "per centum" meaning "out of one hundred."
- Term: Percent Symbol (%)
  Definition: The universal mathematical symbol denoting a hundredth part ($1\% = \frac{1}{100} = 0.01$).
- Term: Decimal Alignment
  Definition: The rule of arranging numbers vertically such that their decimal points form a straight vertical column before carrying out addition or subtraction.
- Term: Percentage Increase
  Definition: The relative measure of how much a quantity has grown compared to its original baseline value, expressed as a percentage: $\frac{\text{Actual Increase}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100\%$.
- Term: Discount
  Definition: A percentage reduction deducted from the original marked retail price of an item during a sales promotion.

## Core Concept
### 1. Operations on Decimals
- **Addition and Subtraction of Decimals:**
  Line up the decimal points vertically, pad trailing blank columns with zeros if necessary, and add or subtract as whole numbers:
  $$24.85 + 7.394 \rightarrow \begin{array}{r@{\quad}l} 24.850 \\ +\; 7.394 \\ \hline 32.244 \end{array}$$
  $$15.2 - 6.84 \rightarrow \begin{array}{r@{\quad}l} 15.20 \\ -\; 6.84 \\ \hline 8.36 \end{array}$$
- **Multiplication of Decimals:**
  1. Multiply the numbers as if they were whole numbers, completely ignoring decimal points.
  2. Count the total number of decimal places in both factors combined.
  3. Place the decimal point in the product so that it has that exact number of decimal places from the right:
     $$3.2 \times 0.45 \rightarrow 32 \times 45 = 1440$$
     Total decimal places = $1 + 2 = 3$. Insert decimal point 3 places from right $\rightarrow 1.440 = 1.44$.
  - *Multiplying by Powers of 10 ($10, 100, 1000$):* Shift the decimal point to the **right** by the number of zeros:
    $4.825 \times 10 = 48.25; \quad 4.825 \times 100 = 482.5; \quad 4.825 \times 1000 = 4825$.
- **Division of Decimals:**
  - *Dividing by Powers of 10:* Shift the decimal point to the **left** by the number of zeros:
    $542.8 \div 10 = 54.28; \quad 542.8 \div 100 = 5.428; \quad 542.8 \div 1000 = 0.5428$.
  - *Dividing by a Decimal Divisor:* Multiply both the dividend and divisor by $10, 100,$ or $1000$ to turn the divisor into a whole number before dividing:
    $$4.8 \div 0.12 = \frac{4.8 \times 100}{0.12 \times 100} = \frac{480}{12} = 40$$

### 2. Meaning and Conversions of Percentages
- **Meaning:** A percentage is simply a fraction with a denominator of 100:
  $$x\% = \frac{x}{100} = x \times 0.01$$
- **Converting Percentages to Fractions:**
  Write the percentage over 100 and simplify to lowest terms:
  $$25\% = \frac{25}{100} = \frac{1}{4}, \quad 60\% = \frac{60}{100} = \frac{3}{5}, \quad 85\% = \frac{85}{100} = \frac{17}{20}$$
- **Converting Percentages to Decimals:**
  Divide by 100 (move the decimal point two places to the left):
  $$45\% = 0.45, \quad 7\% = 0.07, \quad 12.5\% = 0.125, \quad 150\% = 1.50$$
- **Converting Fractions and Decimals to Percentages:**
  Multiply the fraction or decimal by $100\%$:
  $$\frac{3}{8} \times 100\% = \frac{300\%}{8} = 37.5\%$$
  $$0.85 \times 100\% = 85\%$$

### 3. Percentage Calculations in Real Life
- **Finding a Percentage of a Given Quantity:**
  Formula: $\text{Result} = \frac{\text{Percentage}}{100} \times \text{Total Quantity}$
  - Example: Calculate $15\%$ of $₦4,000$:
    $$\frac{15}{100} \times 4000 = 15 \times 40 = ₦600$$
- **Expressing One Quantity as a Percentage of Another:**
  Formula: $\text{Percentage} = \frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}} \times 100\%$
  - Example: A student scores 35 marks out of 50. What is the percentage?
    $$\frac{35}{50} \times 100\% = 35 \times 2\% = 70\%$$
- **Calculating Retail Discounts:**
  $\text{Discount Amount} = \frac{\text{Discount Rate}}{100} \times \text{Marked Price}$
  $\text{Final Sale Price} = \text{Marked Price} - \text{Discount Amount}$
  - Example: A school bag marked at $₦12,000$ has a $10\%$ discount:
    $$\text{Discount} = \frac{10}{100} \times 12000 = ₦1,200 \rightarrow \text{Sale Price} = 12000 - 1200 = ₦10,800$$

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Comparing Percentages, Decimals, and Common Fractions
- Label: Percentage vs Decimal
  Title: Scale of 100 vs Base-10 Positional Form
  Body: Percentages display values explicitly out of 100 using the % symbol (e.g., 75%); decimals express the identical proportion through tenths and hundredths without symbols (0.75).
- Label: Multiplication vs Division by 10
  Title: Decimal Shift Right vs Decimal Shift Left
  Body: Multiplying by powers of ten increases magnitude, shifting the decimal point right; dividing by powers of ten decreases magnitude, shifting the point left.
- Label: Percent Part vs Percent Whole
  Title: Calculating Fraction of Amount vs Expressing Relative Ratio
  Body: Finding a percentage of an amount multiplies the percent by the total; expressing one quantity as a percent divides the part by the whole and multiplies by 100%.
- Label: Discount vs Price Increase
  Title: Deduction from Original vs Addition to Baseline
  Body: A discount subtracts a percentage amount from the marked retail price; an increase or tax adds a percentage amount to the original cost.

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Forgetting to move the decimal point when dividing by a decimal divisor (e.g., evaluating $6.4 \div 0.2$ as $0.32$ instead of $32$).
  Fact: Both numbers must be multiplied by 10 to turn the divisor into a whole number before dividing: $\frac{6.4 \times 10}{0.2 \times 10} = \frac{64}{2} = 32$.
- Mistake: Writing $8\%$ as $0.8$ in decimal form instead of $0.08$.
  Fact: $0.8 = \frac{8}{10} = 80\%$. $8\%$ represents 8 hundredths: $\frac{8}{100} = 0.08$.
- Mistake: Misaligning decimal points during addition or subtraction (e.g., adding $12.3 + 4.56$ by aligning the last digits to the right).
  Fact: Decimal points must be placed in a direct vertical line before adding or subtracting columns.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Evaluating a Complex Decimal Division
Context: A workshop technician cuts a metal rod of length $14.4\text{ metres}$ into small pins measuring $0.18\text{ metres}$ each.
Problem: How many complete pins can be cut from the metal rod?
Fix:
1. Set up the division: $14.4 \div 0.18$.
2. Multiply both numerator and denominator by 100 to make the divisor a whole number:
   $$\frac{14.4 \times 100}{0.18 \times 100} = \frac{1440}{18}$$
3. Divide: $1440 \div 18 = 80\text{ pins}$. The technician can cut exactly 80 pins.

### Example 2: Calculating Percentage Score and Grade Verification
Context: In an annual science examination, an assessment consists of 80 total possible marks. A student earns 68 marks.
Problem: Calculate the student's score as a percentage, and state whether the student achieved a distinction grade ($\geq 75\%$).
Fix:
1. Formula: $\frac{\text{Score}}{\text{Total}} \times 100\%$.
2. Calculate: $\frac{68}{80} \times 100\% = \frac{68}{8} \times 10\% = 8.5 \times 10\% = 85\%$.
The student scored $85\%$, successfully achieving a distinction grade.

### Example 3: Solving a Real-World Discount Problem
Context: A bookshop offers a $15\%$ discount on all Junior Secondary textbooks during school resumption. A mathematics textbook is marked at $₦4,800$.
Problem: Calculate the cash discount granted to the customer and the final purchase price paid.
Fix:
1. Cash discount amount = $\frac{15}{100} \times ₦4,800 = 15 \times 48 = ₦720$.
2. Final purchase price = $\text{Marked Price} - \text{Discount} = ₦4,800 - ₦720 = ₦4,080$.
The customer saves $₦720$ and pays $₦4,080$.

### Q15 [true-false]
Statement: The expression 0.5% is mathematically equivalent to the common fraction 1/200.
Answer: true
Feedback: $0.5\% = \frac{0.5}{100} = \frac{5}{1000} = \frac{1}{200}$.

### Q16 [true-false]
Statement: When multiplying two decimal numbers together, you must align their decimal points in a direct vertical line before calculating.
Answer: false
Feedback: Decimal alignment is mandatory only for addition and subtraction. For multiplication, multiply as whole numbers and place the decimal point by counting total decimal places.
