## Hook
When a clinical thermometer measures your body temperature as $37.4^\circ\text{C}$, an Olympic stopwatch clocks a 100-metre sprinter at $9.93\text{ seconds}$, or your local filling station dispenses $15.65\text{ litres}$ of petrol, whole numbers alone are not precise enough. Decimals provide the fine language of exact measurement by extending our familiar base-ten place value system into fractional parts smaller than one whole. By understanding how the decimal point anchors values into tenths, hundredths, and thousandths, you bridge the gap between everyday fractions and scientific precision.

## Key Terms
- Term: Decimal Fraction
  Definition: A fraction whose denominator is a power of ten ($10, 100, 1000, \dots$), conventionally written in positional notation using a decimal point separating the whole number from the fractional parts.
- Term: Decimal Point
  Definition: The period dot (.) placed between the units digit and the tenths digit in a decimal number to indicate the transition from whole numbers to fractional parts.
- Term: Tenths
  Definition: The first place value position to the right of the decimal point, representing fractional parts of one divided into ten equal units ($\frac{1}{10} = 0.1$).
- Term: Terminating Decimal
  Definition: A decimal number that contains a finite number of digits after the decimal point because division results in a remainder of zero (e.g., $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$).
- Term: Recurring (Repeating) Decimal
  Definition: A decimal fraction in which one or more digits after the decimal point repeat indefinitely without terminating, conventionally marked with a dot or bar over the repeating cycle (e.g., $\frac{1}{3} = 0.333\dots = 0.\dot{3}$).

## Core Concept
### 1. Meaning and Place Value of Decimals
In the decimal number system, every step to the right of the units column divides the value by $10$:
- **Units ($10^0 = 1$)**
- *[Decimal Point]*
- **Tenths ($10^{-1} = \frac{1}{10} = 0.1$)**
- **Hundredths ($10^{-2} = \frac{1}{100} = 0.01$)**
- **Thousandths ($10^{-3} = \frac{1}{1000} = 0.001$)**
- **Ten-Thousandths ($10^{-4} = \frac{1}{10000} = 0.0001$)**

| Tens ($10$) | Units ($1$) | . | Tenths ($\frac{1}{10}$) | Hundredths ($\frac{1}{100}$) | Thousandths ($\frac{1}{1000}$) | Value Breakdown |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **4** | **7** | . | **2** | **8** | **5** | $40 + 7 + 0.2 + 0.08 + 0.005$ |

In the number $47.285$:
- The digit 2 has a place value of **Tenths** and a value of $\frac{2}{10} = 0.2$.
- The digit 8 has a place value of **Hundredths** and a value of $\frac{8}{100} = 0.08$.
- The digit 5 has a place value of **Thousandths** and a value of $\frac{5}{1000} = 0.005$.

### 2. Converting Decimals to Common Fractions
To convert a terminating decimal into a common fraction:
1. Count the number of decimal places (digits to the right of the decimal point).
2. Write the digits as the numerator over a power of 10 having that exact number of zeros.
3. Divide numerator and denominator by their **Highest Common Factor (HCF)** to reduce to lowest terms.
- Examples:
  - $0.6 = \frac{6}{10} = \frac{6 \div 2}{10 \div 2} = \frac{3}{5}$
  - $0.75 = \frac{75}{100} = \frac{75 \div 25}{100 \div 25} = \frac{3}{4}$
  - $0.125 = \frac{125}{1000} = \frac{125 \div 125}{1000 \div 125} = \frac{1}{8}$
  - $3.45 = 3\frac{45}{100} = 3\frac{45 \div 5}{100 \div 5} = 3\frac{9}{20}$

### 3. Converting Common Fractions to Decimals
- **Method 1: Equivalent Fractions with Powers of 10 Denominators**
  If the denominator can be multiplied easily to make $10, 100,$ or $1000$:
  - $\frac{3}{5} = \frac{3 \times 2}{5 \times 2} = \frac{6}{10} = 0.6$
  - $\frac{7}{20} = \frac{7 \times 5}{20 \times 5} = \frac{35}{100} = 0.35$
  - $\frac{13}{250} = \frac{13 \times 4}{250 \times 4} = \frac{52}{1000} = 0.052$
- **Method 2: Long Division (Numerator Divided by Denominator)**
  Divide the numerator by the denominator, placing a decimal point and adding trailing zeros:
  - Convert $\frac{3}{8}$ to decimal:
    $$3 \div 8 = 0.375$$

### 4. Terminating vs. Recurring Decimals
- **Terminating Decimals:** The division terminates with a remainder of zero. A fraction in lowest terms produces a terminating decimal **if and only if** the prime factors of its denominator contain ONLY $2$ and/or $5$ (e.g., $\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{5}, \frac{1}{8}, \frac{1}{10}, \frac{1}{16}, \frac{1}{20}, \frac{1}{25}$).
- **Recurring (Repeating) Decimals:** The division never ends; a digit or pattern of digits repeats infinitely. This occurs whenever the reduced denominator contains prime factors other than 2 or 5 (e.g., $3, 7, 11, 13$):
  - $\frac{1}{3} = 0.3333\dots = 0.\dot{3}$
  - $\frac{2}{3} = 0.6666\dots = 0.\dot{6}$
  - $\frac{1}{6} = 0.1666\dots = 0.1\dot{6}$
  - $\frac{1}{7} = 0.142857142857\dots = 0.\overline{142857}$

### 5. Comparing and Ordering Decimals
To compare decimals:
1. Compare the whole number parts first.
2. If whole numbers are equal, align decimal points and compare tenths, then hundredths, then thousandths.
3. Adding trailing zeros to the right does not change a decimal's value: $0.4 = 0.40 = 0.400$.
- Example: Compare $0.385$ and $0.4$:
  $0.4 = 0.400$. Looking at tenths, $4 > 3$, so $0.4 > 0.385$.

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Comparing Decimal Classes and Numerical Forms
- Label: Terminating vs Recurring
  Title: Finite Decimal vs Infinite Repeating Cycle
  Body: Terminating decimals end cleanly because denominators have only prime factors of 2 and 5; recurring decimals repeat infinitely because denominators contain other primes like 3 or 7.
- Label: Tenths vs Hundredths
  Title: First Decimal Place vs Second Decimal Place
  Body: Tenths represent parts out of 10 (0.1) and hold ten times the value of hundredths, which represent parts out of 100 (0.01).
- Label: Decimal Fraction vs Common Fraction
  Title: Positional Notation vs Top-Over-Bottom Form
  Body: Decimal fractions express tenths, hundredths, and thousandths using a decimal point; common fractions express parts using explicit numerators over denominators (e.g., 0.5 vs 1/2).
- Label: Trailing Zero vs Leading Zero
  Title: Insignificant Suffix vs Significant Placeholder
  Body: Adding trailing zeros after the last decimal digit does not change value ($0.5 = 0.50$); adding leading zeros after the decimal point divides value by 10 ($0.5 \neq 0.05$).

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Believing that a decimal with more digits is always larger (e.g., thinking $0.185$ is larger than $0.4$ because $185 > 4$).
  Fact: Decimals must be compared by place value from left to right. Comparing tenths shows that $0.400$ ($4$ tenths) is much larger than $0.185$ ($1$ tenth).
- Mistake: Misplacing the decimal point when converting fractions like $\frac{7}{100}$ into decimals, writing $0.7$ instead of $0.07$.
  Fact: The denominator 100 has two zeros, requiring two decimal places: $\frac{7}{100} = 0.07$.
- Mistake: Claiming that $\frac{1}{3} = 0.3$ exactly.
  Fact: $0.3 = \frac{3}{10}$. $\frac{1}{3}$ is a recurring decimal equal to $0.3333\dots$ or $0.\dot{3}$, never an exact single-digit decimal.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Converting Common Fractions to Terminating and Recurring Decimals
Context: A physics laboratory student records fractions from a micrometer scale: $\frac{7}{8}$ and $\frac{5}{12}$.
Problem: Convert both fractions into decimal form and classify each as terminating or recurring.
Fix:
1. Convert $\frac{7}{8}$:
   - Long division: $7 \div 8 = 0.875$.
   - Since $8 = 2^3$, the decimal terminates. $\frac{7}{8} = 0.875$ (**Terminating Decimal**).
2. Convert $\frac{5}{12}$:
   - Long division: $5 \div 12 = 0.41666\dots$
   - Since $12 = 2^2 \times 3$, the presence of prime factor 3 causes recurring digits. $\frac{5}{12} = 0.41\dot{6}$ (**Recurring Decimal**).

### Example 2: Converting a Mixed Number with Decimals to a Reduced Fraction
Context: An agricultural officer records rainfall in a community as $14.375\text{ millimetres}$.
Problem: Express $14.375$ as a mixed number in its lowest simplest terms.
Fix:
- Whole number part = $14$.
- Decimal part = $0.375 = \frac{375}{1000}$.
- Find the HCF of 375 and 1000:
  - $375 = 3 \times 5^3 = 375$
  - $1000 = 8 \times 125 = 1000$
  - $\text{HCF} = 125$.
- Reduce: $\frac{375 \div 125}{1000 \div 125} = \frac{3}{8}$.
The rainfall measurement is $14\frac{3}{8}\text{ millimetres}$.

### Example 3: Ordering Decimals in Ascending Sequence
Context: Four athletes record race times: $12.05\text{ s}$, $12.5\text{ s}$, $12.005\text{ s}$, and $12.48\text{ s}$.
Problem: Arrange the athletes in order from fastest (smallest time) to slowest (largest time).
Fix:
1. Pad with trailing zeros to three decimal places:
   - $12.05 = 12.050$
   - $12.5 = 12.500$
   - $12.005 = 12.005$
   - $12.48 = 12.480$
2. Compare decimal portions: $005 < 050 < 480 < 500$.
Ascending order: $12.005\text{ s} < 12.05\text{ s} < 12.48\text{ s} < 12.5\text{ s}$.

## Quiz

### Q1 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the place value of the digit 7 in the decimal number 14.375?
A) Hundredths (correct)
B) Tenths
C) Thousandths
D) Tens
Feedback: The first digit after the decimal point (3) is tenths; the second digit (7) is hundredths; the third digit (5) is thousandths.

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: Convert the decimal 0.65 into a common fraction in its lowest terms:
A) 13/20 (correct)
B) 65/100
C) 7/10
D) 13/25
Feedback: $0.65 = \frac{65}{100}$. Dividing numerator and denominator by 5 gives $\frac{65 \div 5}{100 \div 5} = \frac{13}{20}$.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Convert the fraction 7/8 into a decimal:
A) 0.875 (correct)
B) 0.78
C) 0.85
D) 0.75
Feedback: Dividing 7 by 8 gives $7 \div 8 = 0.875$.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following fractions produces a recurring (repeating) decimal?
A) 2/3 (correct)
B) 3/4
C) 7/10
D) 5/8
Feedback: $\frac{2}{3} = 0.6666\dots = 0.\dot{6}$. $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$, $\frac{7}{10} = 0.7$, and $\frac{5}{8} = 0.625$ are terminating decimals.

### Q5 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the decimal representation of the fraction 9/1000?
A) 0.009 (correct)
B) 0.09
C) 0.9
D) 0.0009
Feedback: 1000 has three zeros, requiring three decimal places: $\frac{9}{1000} = 0.009$.

### Q6 [single-choice]
Stem: Which of the following decimal numbers has the greatest numerical value?
A) 0.7 (correct)
B) 0.695
C) 0.098
D) 0.68
Feedback: Aligning to three decimal places: $0.700, 0.695, 0.098, 0.680$. The tenths place of 0.700 has 7, which is larger than 6 or 0.

### Q7 [single-choice]
Stem: What does a recurring decimal contain?
A) One or more digits after the decimal point that repeat infinitely in a pattern (correct)
B) Exactly two digits after the decimal point that terminate with zero
C) Only prime digits that can never be divided
D) A denominator that is a multiple of ten
Feedback: Recurring decimals have digits that repeat indefinitely without terminating (e.g., $0.\dot{3}$).

### Q8 [single-choice]
Stem: Convert the mixed number 3 1/4 into a decimal number:
A) 3.25 (correct)
B) 3.14
C) 3.5
D) 3.4
Feedback: $\frac{1}{4} = 0.25$. Therefore, $3\frac{1}{4} = 3.25$.

### Q9 [single-choice]
Stem: Convert the decimal 0.375 into a fraction in its lowest terms:
A) 3/8 (correct)
B) 375/100
C) 7/20
D) 3/4
Feedback: $0.375 = \frac{375}{1000}$. Dividing numerator and denominator by 125 gives $\frac{3}{8}$.

### Q10 [single-choice]
Stem: A fraction in lowest terms produces a terminating decimal if and only if its denominator's prime factors contain:
A) Only 2 and/or 5 (correct)
B) Only 3 and/or 7
C) Any odd numbers
D) No prime numbers at all
Feedback: In base 10 ($2 \times 5$), only denominators with prime factors of 2 and/or 5 terminate without recurring remainders.

### Q11 [single-choice]
Stem: Express 0.04 as a common fraction in its lowest terms:
A) 1/25 (correct)
B) 4/10
C) 2/5
D) 1/50
Feedback: $0.04 = \frac{4}{100} = \frac{4 \div 4}{100 \div 4} = \frac{1}{25}$.

### Q12 [single-choice]
Stem: Arrange 0.25, 0.09, and 0.3 in ascending order (from smallest to largest):
A) 0.09, 0.25, 0.3 (correct)
B) 0.3, 0.25, 0.09
C) 0.09, 0.3, 0.25
D) 0.25, 0.09, 0.3
Feedback: In hundredths: $0.09, 0.25, 0.30$. Ascending order: $0.09 < 0.25 < 0.30$.

### Q13 [single-choice]
Stem: What is the recurring symbol representation for 1/3?
A) 0.3̇ (correct)
B) 0.3
C) 0.33
D) 1.3
Feedback: A dot (or bar) above the 3 indicates that 3 repeats infinitely ($0.\dot{3} = 0.3333\dots$).

### Q14 [single-choice]
Stem: In the number 52.816, what digit occupies the thousandths place value?
A) 6 (correct)
B) 8
C) 1
D) 2
Feedback: 8 is tenths, 1 is hundredths, and 6 is thousandths.

### Q15 [true-false]
Statement: Adding trailing zeros to the right of a decimal fraction changes its numerical value (e.g., 0.5 is not equal to 0.50).
Answer: false
Feedback: Trailing zeros after the decimal point do not change the number's quantitative value: $0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500$.

### Q16 [true-false]
Statement: The fraction 1/8 produces a terminating decimal when converted into decimal form.
Answer: true
Feedback: $\frac{1}{8} = 0.125$, terminating cleanly because the denominator 8 has prime factors containing only 2 ($2^3$).

### Q17 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO fractions that produce terminating decimals:
Correct: 1/4, 3/5
Incorrect: 1/3, 5/6
Feedback: $\frac{1}{4} = 0.25$ and $\frac{3}{5} = 0.6$ terminate; $\frac{1}{3} = 0.\dot{3}$ and $\frac{5}{6} = 0.8\dot{3}$ are recurring.

### Q18 [multi-select]
Stem: Select TWO decimal numbers that are greater than 0.45:
Correct: 0.5, 0.485
Incorrect: 0.405, 0.054
Feedback: $0.5 = 0.500$ and $0.485$ are greater than $0.450$; $0.405$ and $0.054$ are smaller.

### Q19 [matching]
Stem: Match each common fraction with its exact decimal equivalent:
Pairs:
- 1/2 -> 0.5
- 1/4 -> 0.25
- 3/4 -> 0.75
Feedback: $\frac{1}{2} = 0.5$; $\frac{1}{4} = 0.25$; $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$.

### Q20 [matching]
Stem: Match each decimal with its reduced fraction equivalent:
Pairs:
- 0.2 -> 1/5
- 0.8 -> 4/5
- 0.125 -> 1/8
Feedback: $0.2 = \frac{1}{5}$; $0.8 = \frac{4}{5}$; $0.125 = \frac{1}{8}$.
