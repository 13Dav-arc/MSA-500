## Hook
When the Nigerian government plans how many hospital beds, schools, or roads to build in the coming fiscal year, or when a shopkeeper decides how many loaves of bread to order each morning, they do not rely on wild guesses[cite: 84, 85]. They gather numbers, arrange them into tables and charts, and calculate typical summary figures[cite: 84, 89, 90, 101]. This science of collecting, organizing, presenting, and analyzing data is called statistics, and its central averages—the mean, median, and mode—help us make sense of the world[cite: 84, 101, 102, 103].

## Key Terms
- Term: Statistics
  Definition: The branch of mathematics concerned with the collection, organization, presentation, analysis, and interpretation of numerical data for decision-making and planning[cite: 84, 98].
- Term: Data
  Definition: Raw, unorganized information (such as facts, numbers, or observations) gathered from counts or measurements[cite: 84, 88].
- Term: Frequency
  Definition: The number of times a particular data value or event occurs within a dataset[cite: 89, 98].
- Term: Mean (Arithmetic Average)
  Definition: The sum of all numerical values in a dataset divided by the total number of values[cite: 101, 107].
- Term: Median
  Definition: The middle value in a set of data after the values have been arranged in ascending or descending order of magnitude[cite: 102, 107].
- Term: Mode
  Definition: The particular value or category that appears most frequently within a given dataset[cite: 103, 107].

## Core Concept
### Purpose of Statistics and Data Collection
Statistics provides essential quantitative evidence used in everyday life, industry, and national governance[cite: 84, 85]:
1. **Record Keeping:** Storing systematic historical information[cite: 84].
2. **Planning:** Aiding economic planning, such as using daily petroleum export data to project national budgets or tracking school enrolment to plan classroom expansions[cite: 85, 87].
3. **Forecasting and Prediction:** Projecting future outcomes and community needs based on past trends[cite: 84, 87].
4. **Decision-Making:** Guiding shopkeepers, doctors, and institutions to select the best actions based on real-world evidence[cite: 85, 98].

### Organizing and Presenting Data
- **Rank-Ordered List:** Arranging raw numerical scores or categories in sequential order from lowest to highest or highest to lowest[cite: 89].
- **Frequency Table with Tally Marks:** A structured grid showing values and the number of times they occur[cite: 89]. Tally marks are bundled in groups of five (four vertical strokes crossed by a diagonal stroke) to avoid miscounting[cite: 89, 90].
- **Pictogram:** A visual presentation where standardized pictures or symbols represent specific quantities of items[cite: 90, 97].
- **Bar Chart:** A graphical representation where the heights or lengths of separate, rectangular bars of equal width correspond to the frequencies of each category[cite: 91, 98].
- **Pie Chart:** A circular statistical graphic where a full circle ($360^\circ$) represents the total dataset, and the angle of each sector is proportional to its fraction of the whole[cite: 91, 92]:
  $$\text{Sector Angle} = \frac{\text{Frequency of Item}}{\text{Total Frequency}} \times 360^\circ$$[cite: 92]

### Measures of Central Tendency (Averages)
Averages summarize an entire group of figures into a single representative benchmark[cite: 101]:
1. **Mean ($\bar{x}$):** The arithmetic average of all entries[cite: 101]:
   - For an ungrouped list of $n$ numbers[cite: 101]:
     $$\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Sum of all values}}{n}$$[cite: 101, 108]
   - For a tabulated frequency distribution[cite: 101]:
     $$\text{Mean} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}$$[cite: 101]
2. **Median:** The value occupying the exact centre position when data is ordered by size[cite: 102]:
   - When the count $n$ is **odd**, the median is the single middle number[cite: 102].
   - When the count $n$ is **even**, two numbers share the middle; their sum is divided by 2 to determine the median[cite: 102]:
     $$\text{Median} = \frac{\text{First middle value} + \text{Second middle value}}{2}$$[cite: 102]
3. **Mode:** The most frequent score[cite: 103]. A distribution with one most frequent value is unimodal; if two values share the highest frequency, the distribution is **bimodal**[cite: 104, 106].

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Comparing Measures of Central Tendency
- Label: Mathematical Sensitivity
  Title: Mean vs Median
  Body: The mean incorporates the exact numerical value of every score and is heavily pulled by extreme outliers[cite: 101, 103], while the median is positional and unaffected by extreme scores[cite: 102, 106, 107].
- Label: Categorical Suitability
  Title: Mode vs Mean
  Body: The mode is the only measure of central tendency applicable to non-numerical, categorical data (such as finding the most popular shoe size or vehicle type)[cite: 90, 92, 104], whereas the mean requires numerical values for calculation[cite: 101].
- Label: Uniqueness of Value
  Title: Median vs Mode
  Body: A dataset always possesses exactly one unique median[cite: 102], whereas a dataset can have one mode, multiple modes (bimodal/multimodal), or no mode at all if all values occur equally[cite: 104, 106, 107].
- Label: Graphic Visualisation
  Title: Bar Chart vs Pie Chart
  Body: Bar charts represent discrete categorical frequencies through bar height separated by spaces[cite: 91, 98], while pie charts display proportional parts of an entire whole using angular sectors of $360^\circ$[cite: 91, 92, 98].

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Finding the median without arranging the numbers in order of size first[cite: 102].
  Fact: You must arrange the raw data into an ascending or descending rank-ordered list before identifying the middle number[cite: 89, 102].
- Mistake: Stating that a dataset has a mode of 0 when no numbers repeat[cite: 107].
  Fact: If every value appears with equal frequency, the dataset has "no mode"; stating "mode = 0" implies that the number zero occurs most often[cite: 107].
- Mistake: Dividing the sum of values by 2 instead of by the total number of items ($n$) when calculating the mean[cite: 101].
  Fact: You divide by 2 only when averaging the two middle numbers for the median of an even set; the mean must be divided by $n$ (the total count)[cite: 101, 102].

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Calculating the Arithmetic Mean
Context: A JSS 1 student scored the following marks in five weekly continuous assessment tests: $13, 17, 18, 8,$ and $10$[cite: 101].
Problem: Calculate the student's average (mean) test score[cite: 101].
Fix:
1. Find the sum of all five marks[cite: 101]:
   $$\text{Sum} = 13 + 17 + 18 + 8 + 10 = 66$$[cite: 101]
2. Divide the sum by the total number of tests ($n = 5$)[cite: 101]:
   $$\text{Mean} = \frac{66}{5} = 13.2\text{ marks}$$[cite: 101]

### Example B: Determining the Median of an Even Dataset
Context: A laboratory technician records the following six temperature readings in degrees Celsius: $8.4, 7.8, 6.2, 13.4, 12.6,$ and $10.5$[cite: 102].
Problem: Find the median temperature of the readings[cite: 102].
Fix:
1. Arrange the data in ascending order of magnitude[cite: 102]:
   $$6.2,\ 7.8,\ 8.4,\ 10.5,\ 12.6,\ 13.4$$[cite: 102]
2. Because there are $6$ values (an even count), the median is the average of the 3rd and 4th middle numbers ($8.4$ and $10.5$)[cite: 102]:
   $$\text{Median} = \frac{8.4 + 10.5}{2} = \frac{18.9}{2} = 9.45^\circ\text{C}$$[cite: 102]

### Example C: Interpreting Data from a Pie Chart
Context: A livestock farmer has $120$ domestic farm animals represented on a pie chart[cite: 92]. The sector angle for cows is $180^\circ$, goats is $120^\circ$, and sheep is $60^\circ$[cite: 92].
Problem: Calculate the exact number of cows and goats on the farm[cite: 92].
Fix:
- For cows[cite: 92]:
  $$\text{Number of cows} = \frac{180^\circ}{360^\circ} \times 120 = \frac{1}{2} \times 120 = 60\text{ cows}$$[cite: 92]
- For goats[cite: 92]:
  $$\text{Number of goats} = \frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ} \times 120 = \frac{1}{3} \times 120 = 40\text{ goats}$$[cite: 92]
