## Hook
Imagine your school principal decides to erect a security wire fence around the entire school compound or build a running track around the sports field[cite: 17]. To know the exact length of wire or boundary marking required, nobody measures the space inside; instead, they measure the total distance around the outside edge[cite: 17]. This total outer boundary of any two-dimensional figure is called its perimeter, and calculating it correctly saves time, materials, and money in construction, sports, and agriculture[cite: 17, 21].

## Key Terms
- Term: Perimeter
  Definition: The total distance around the outside boundary or outer edge of a closed plane shape[cite: 17].
- Term: Circumference
  Definition: The special name given to the perimeter or boundary distance around a circle, calculated using $2\pi r$ or $\pi d$[cite: 17, 24].
- Term: Regular Polygon
  Definition: A plane figure with all sides of equal length and all interior angles of equal size[cite: 19, 26].
- Term: Irregular Shape
  Definition: A geometric figure whose boundary sides or angles are of different lengths and sizes[cite: 17, 26].
- Term: Thread Method
  Definition: A practical measuring technique using a flexible piece of thread along curved edges, which is then straightened against a ruler to determine length[cite: 20].

## Core Concept
### What is Perimeter?
The perimeter is the outside boundary or edge of a plane shape[cite: 17]. It also refers to the total linear length of that boundary[cite: 17]. For example, walking along the entire boundary fence of a school compound for 200 paces gives an estimated perimeter of 200 paces[cite: 17]. For straight-sided figures, the perimeter is simply the sum of all external side lengths[cite: 17].

### Circumference of a Circle
The perimeter of a circle is called its **circumference**[cite: 17]:
$$\text{Circumference } (C) = 2\pi r = \pi d$$[cite: 17, 24]
where $r$ is the radius, $d$ is the diameter ($d = 2r$), and $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$ or $3.142$[cite: 18, 24].
- When given the circumference, the radius is found by dividing: $r = \frac{C}{2\pi}$[cite: 18].
- For example, if $C = 44\text{ cm}$ and $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$, then $r = \frac{44}{2 \times \frac{22}{7}} = 7\text{ cm}$, giving a diameter of $d = 2 \times 7 = 14\text{ cm}$[cite: 18].

### Perimeter of Triangles and Regular Polygons
- **Triangles:** For any triangle with sides $a$, $b$, and $c$, the perimeter is $P = a + b + c$[cite: 18, 19].
  - An equilateral triangle with side $s$ has equal sides: $P = 3s$[cite: 18, 19].
  - A right-angled triangle with legs $5\text{ cm}$ and $12\text{ cm}$ and hypotenuse $13\text{ cm}$ has $P = 5 + 12 + 13 = 30\text{ cm}$[cite: 18, 19].
- **Regular Polygons:** If a polygon has $n$ equal sides of length $s$, its perimeter is $P = n \times s$[cite: 19].
  - A regular hexagon with sides of $1.6\text{ cm}$ has $P = 6 \times 1.6\text{ cm} = 9.6\text{ cm}$[cite: 19].

### Perimeter of Quadrilaterals
- **Square:** A regular four-sided shape with equal sides of length $l$[cite: 20].
  $$P = 4l \quad \implies \quad l = \frac{P}{4}$$[cite: 20]
- **Rectangle:** Has two equal lengths ($l$) and two equal breadths ($b$)[cite: 21]:
  $$P = l + b + l + b = 2(l + b)$$[cite: 21]
- **Parallelogram:** Opposite sides are equal in length ($x$ and $y$)[cite: 22, 23]:
  $$P = 2(x + y)$$[cite: 23]
- **Trapezium:** Found by adding the four exterior boundary sides together[cite: 22].

### Measuring Irregular Shapes
For shapes with straight edges, measure each edge directly with a ruler and add the lengths[cite: 17, 20]. If a shape has a curved boundary, lay a flexible thread along the curve, mark the ends, straighten the thread, and read off the measurement against a ruler[cite: 20].

  Body: A circle's perimeter is curved and governed by the irrational ratio $\pi$ ($C = 2\pi r$)[cite: 17, 27], whereas polygons have straight boundaries found by direct side summation ($P = \sum \text{sides}$)[cite: 17].

  Title: Square vs Rectangle
  Body: A square multiplies one side by 4 ($P = 4l$)[cite: 20], while a rectangle must group length and breadth ($P = 2(l + b)$)[cite: 21].
- Label: Side Congruence
  Title: Equilateral Triangle vs Scalene Triangle
  Body: An equilateral triangle uses multiplication ($P = 3s$)[cite: 19], whereas a scalene triangle requires adding three distinct lengths individually ($P = a + b + c$)[cite: 19].
- Label: Boundary Technique
  Title: Ruler Measurement vs Thread Method
  Body: Straight segments are measured directly with a rigid ruler[cite: 19, 20], while curved irregular boundaries require tracing with a thread before ruler measurement[cite: 20].

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Using the area formula ($\pi r^2$) instead of the circumference formula ($2\pi r$) when calculating distance around a circle[cite: 24, 29].
  Fact: Circumference measures outer length ($2\pi r$ in $\text{cm}$ or $\text{m}$), while area measures enclosed surface space ($\pi r^2$ in $\text{cm}^2$ or $\text{m}^2$)[cite: 17, 24, 29].
- Mistake: Including internal partition lines when calculating the perimeter of composite figures.
  Fact: Perimeter strictly includes the outer enclosing edges of a figure, never internal dividing lines[cite: 17].
- Mistake: Forgetting to multiply by 2 in the rectangle formula (e.g., computing only $l + b$)[cite: 21].
  Fact: A rectangle has four sides; $l + b$ is only semi-perimeter, so it must be doubled: $P = 2(l + b)$[cite: 21].

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Finding the Diameter from Circumference
Context: The circumference of a circular bicycle wheel is $44\text{ cm}$[cite: 18].
Problem: Calculate the radius and diameter of the wheel using $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$[cite: 18].
Fix:
$$\text{Circumference} = 2\pi r = 44\text{ cm}$$[cite: 18]
$$2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times r = 44 \implies \frac{44}{7}r = 44$$[cite: 18]
$$r = 44 \times \frac{7}{44} = 7\text{ cm}$$[cite: 18]
$$\text{Diameter } (d) = 2r = 2 \times 7\text{ cm} = 14\text{ cm}$$[cite: 18]

### Example B: Perimeter of a Sports Field
Context: A school football field measures $80\text{ m}$ in length and $50\text{ m}$ in breadth[cite: 21].
Problem: Calculate the total perimeter of the field[cite: 21].
Fix: Use the rectangle perimeter formula[cite: 21]:
$$P = 2(l + b) = 2(80\text{ m} + 50\text{ m}) = 2(130\text{ m}) = 260\text{ m}$$[cite: 21]

### Example C: Missing Side of a Parallelogram
Context: The perimeter of a parallelogram is $48\text{ cm}$, and one side measures $15\text{ cm}$[cite: 23].
Problem: Determine the length of the adjacent side[cite: 23].
Fix:
$$P = 2(x + y) \implies 48 = 2(15 + y)$$[cite: 23]
Divide both sides by $2$:
$$24 = 15 + y \implies y = 24 - 15 = 9\text{ cm}$$[cite: 23]
The adjacent side measures $9\text{ cm}$[cite: 23].
