## Hook
Imagine going to the market to buy 5 baskets of tomatoes, 3 bunches of bananas, and 7 pineapples, and your friend buys another 2 baskets of tomatoes and 4 bunches of bananas[cite: 2]. You would never mix the pineapples with the tomatoes because they are completely different items[cite: 2]! In mathematics, this principle is called collecting like terms: grouping identical variables together so we can add, subtract, multiply, and expand expressions cleanly and without confusion[cite: 2].

## Key Terms
- Term: Algebraic Expression
  Definition: A mathematical combination of constants, numbers, and variables connected by fundamental operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division[cite: 2].
- Term: Term
  Definition: A single number, variable, or number and variable multiplied together, separated from other parts of an algebraic expression by plus or minus signs[cite: 2].
- Term: Coefficient
  Definition: The numerical factor multiplying a variable in an algebraic term, such as the 5 in $5b$ or the 7 in $7d$[cite: 2].
- Term: Like Terms
  Definition: Terms that have the exact same variable letters raised to the exact same powers, which can be combined by adding or subtracting their coefficients[cite: 2].
- Term: Expansion of Brackets
  Definition: Multiplying every term inside a bracket by the number or variable outside the bracket to clear the grouping[cite: 2].

## Core Concept
### What is an Algebraic Expression?
An algebraic expression connects constants and variables using fundamental arithmetic operations ($+, -, \times, \div$)[cite: 2]. In algebra, letters of the alphabet represent numbers or physical objects[cite: 2]. 
For example, the expression $5b + 3c + 7d$ can represent 5 babies, 3 cats, and 7 dogs[cite: 2]:
- The quantities $5b$, $3c$, and $7d$ are called the **terms** of the expression[cite: 2]. This expression contains 3 terms[cite: 2].
- The numbers **5**, **3**, and **7** are called the **coefficients** of $b$, $c$, and $d$ respectively[cite: 2].
- When a term has no visible number in front (like $a$ or $y$), its coefficient is understood to be **1** (e.g., $a = 1a$)[cite: 2].

### Addition and Subtraction of Similar (Like) Terms
When adding or subtracting like terms, we simply add or subtract their numerical coefficients while leaving the variable letter unchanged[cite: 2]:
- $4a + 5a + a = (4 + 5 + 1)a = 10a$[cite: 2]
- $9n - 5n - 3n = (9 - 5 - 3)n = 1n = n$[cite: 2]

When an expression contains both positive and negative like terms, group positive terms together first before subtracting[cite: 2]:
- $11q + 5q - 2q + q = (11q + 5q + q) - 2q = 17q - 2q = 15q$[cite: 2]
- $20p - 9p - p + 2p = (20p + 2p) - (9p + p) = 22p - 10p = 12p$[cite: 2]

### Multiplication and Division of Similar Terms
- **Multiplication:** When multiplying terms, multiply the numerical coefficients together and write the variable letters together in alphabetical order[cite: 2]:
  - $3a \times b = 3ab$[cite: 2]
  - $5y \times 2ab = (5 \times 2) \times (a \times b \times y) = 10aby$[cite: 2]
  - $6p \times 3ap = (6 \times 3) \times a \times (p \times p) = 18ap^2$[cite: 2]
- **Division:** Express the numerator and denominator in factored form and cancel out identical common factors[cite: 2]:
  - $6y^2 \div 2y = \frac{6 \times y \times y}{2 \times y} = 3y$[cite: 2]
  - $36q \div 9q = \frac{36 \times q}{9 \times q} = 4$[cite: 2]

### Collection and Simplification of Like and Unlike Terms
Unlike terms cannot be added or subtracted together into a single term[cite: 2]. To simplify an expression with mixed variables, collect like terms together with their preceding signs[cite: 2]:
- In $4a + 5b - a - 4b$:
  $$\begin{aligned}
  &= 4a - a + 5b - 4b \\
  &= 3a + b
  \end{aligned}$$[cite: 2]
- In $18a - 3a - 6a + 10b$:
  $$\begin{aligned}
  &= (18 - 3 - 6)a + 10b \\
  &= 9a + 10b
  \end{aligned}$$[cite: 2]

### The Use of Brackets
When brackets are present in an algebraic expression, the operations inside the brackets are expanded first by distributing the outside multiplier to every term inside[cite: 2]:
- $4(a + 3b) - 12b = 4a + 12b - 12b = 4a + 0 = 4a$[cite: 2]
- $x(x + 2) + x^2 + 3x = x^2 + 2x + x^2 + 3x = (x^2 + x^2) + (2x + 3x) = 2x^2 + 5x$[cite: 2]

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Comparing Algebraic Structures and Operations
- Label: Variable Nature
  Title: Like Terms vs Unlike Terms
  Body: Like terms share identical variable letters ($4a, 6a, -3a$) and can be combined into one term[cite: 2], whereas unlike terms have different letters ($4a, 5b$) and cannot be merged by addition[cite: 2].
- Label: Arithmetic Rules
  Title: Addition vs Multiplication of Variables
  Body: Adding like variables increases the coefficient ($a + a = 2a$)[cite: 2], whereas multiplying identical variables raises the power ($a \times a = a^2$)[cite: 2].
- Label: Operational Flow
  Title: Expanding Brackets vs Collecting Like Terms
  Body: Expanding brackets distributes an outside multiplier across grouped terms[cite: 2], while collecting like terms rearranges and combines matching letters across the entire expression[cite: 2].
- Label: Component Roles
  Title: Coefficient vs Constant
  Body: A coefficient is a number attached to and multiplying a variable (such as 6 in $6p$)[cite: 2], whereas a constant is an independent number standing without any variable (such as 7).

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Combining unlike terms during addition (e.g., writing $3a + 2b = 5ab$).
  Fact: Terms with different letters cannot be combined by addition or subtraction; $3a + 2b$ remains $3a + 2b$[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Forgetting that a variable written without a visible number has a coefficient of 1.
  Fact: In an expression like $4a + a$, the term $a$ means $1a$, so the sum is $4a + 1a = 5a$, not $4a$[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Multiplying only the first term inside a bracket (e.g., writing $4(a + 3b) = 4a + 3b$).
  Fact: The multiplier outside must multiply every single term inside the bracket: $4 \times a + 4 \times 3b = 4a + 12b$[cite: 2].

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Simplifying an Expression by Collecting Like Terms
Context: Simplify the expression $18a - 3a - 6a + 10b$[cite: 2].
Problem: Group and combine like terms to simplify the statement to its shortest form[cite: 2].
Fix: Identify terms that contain the variable $a$ and terms that contain $b$[cite: 2]:
Combine the $a$ terms: $(18 - 3 - 6)a = 9a$[cite: 2].
The remaining term $10b$ cannot combine with $a$[cite: 2].
$$\text{Simplified Form} = 9a + 10b$$[cite: 2]

### Example B: Simplifying Division of Algebraic Terms
Context: A student needs to simplify the fraction $36q \div 9q$[cite: 2].
Problem: Divide the two terms and find the resulting single value[cite: 2].
Fix: Write the division as a vertical fraction and expand the terms[cite: 2]:
$$\frac{36q}{9q} = \frac{36 \times q}{9 \times q}$$[cite: 2]
Divide the coefficients: $36 \div 9 = 4$[cite: 2].
Cancel the common variable: $\frac{q}{q} = 1$[cite: 2].
$$\text{Result} = 4 \times 1 = 4$$[cite: 2]

### Example C: Expanding Brackets and Collecting Like Terms
Context: Simplify the expression $4(a + 3b) - 12b$[cite: 2].
Problem: Clear the brackets and combine all like terms[cite: 2].
Fix: 
1. Multiply 4 by each term inside the bracket: $4 \times a + 4 \times 3b = 4a + 12b$[cite: 2].
2. Write out the full expression: $4a + 12b - 12b$[cite: 2].
3. Combine the $b$ terms: $12b - 12b = 0$[cite: 2].
$$\text{Result} = 4a + 0 = 4a$$[cite: 2]
