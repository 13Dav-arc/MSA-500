## Hook
Imagine walking into a supermarket to buy snacks with a ₦2,000 note in your pocket, picking up items priced at ₦495, ₦310, and ₦198. You do not need a calculator to know whether your money is enough; in a split second, your brain rounds the prices to ₦500, ₦300, and ₦200 to get a quick estimate of ₦1,000[cite: 3]! That quick, sensible mathematical shortcut is called approximation, and it prevents calculation errors in both school exams and everyday trade[cite: 3].

## Key Terms
- Term: Approximation
  Definition: Replacing a number with another number of nearly equal value that is simpler, shorter, or easier to calculate with[cite: 3].
- Term: Rounding Off
  Definition: The process of dropping unnecessary digits in a calculation result to express it to a specified number of significant figures or decimal places[cite: 3].
- Term: Rounding Up
  Definition: Increasing the last retained digit by 1 when the first digit to be ignored is 5 or greater[cite: 3].
- Term: Rounding Down
  Definition: Leaving the last retained digit unchanged when the first digit to be ignored is less than 5[cite: 3].
- Term: Significant Figures (s.f.)
  Definition: The meaningful digits in a number that carry precision, counted from the first non-zero digit on the left[cite: 3].

## Core Concept
### Rules for Rounding Off Numbers
When performing mathematical calculations, the result often produces more digits than necessary or practical to use[cite: 3]. To round off a number:
1. Locate the last digit that is to be kept[cite: 3].
2. Look at the immediate next digit to its right (the first digit to be ignored)[cite: 3]:
   - **Rounding Up:** If the digit to be ignored is **5 or more** ($5, 6, 7, 8, 9$), increase the last kept digit by $1$[cite: 3].
   - **Rounding Down:** If the digit to be ignored is **less than 5** ($0, 1, 2, 3, 4$), leave the last kept digit unchanged[cite: 3].
3. For whole numbers, replace all the dropped digit places with zeros so that the place value remains intact[cite: 3]. For decimals, simply truncate the dropped places after rounding[cite: 3].

### Significant Figures (s.f.)
Significant figures are counted starting from the first non-zero digit on the left[cite: 3]:
- In $9,562,774$, rounding to $2\text{ s.f.}$ gives $9,600,000$ (since the 3rd digit is 6, which is $\ge 5$, so 5 rounds up to 6)[cite: 3].
- In $768.45$, rounding to $2\text{ s.f.}$ gives $770$[cite: 3].
- In $29,042$, rounding to $2\text{ s.f.}$ gives $29,000$[cite: 3].

### Approximating Results of Addition and Subtraction
Before performing exact calculations, rounding each term to the nearest ten, hundred, or significant figure gives a quick benchmark to catch arithmetic errors[cite: 3]:
- **Addition:** To estimate $43 + 62 + 59$, round each to the nearest ten: $40 + 60 + 60 = 160$ (exact answer is $164$)[cite: 3].
- **Decimals Addition:** For $3.1 + 4.8 + 4.1$, approximate to the nearest whole number: $3 + 5 + 4 = 12$ (exact answer is $12.0$)[cite: 3].
- **Subtraction:** To estimate $53 - 38$, approximate to the nearest ten: $50 - 40 = 10$ (or $50 - 40 = 20$ depending on whether 38 rounds to 40)[cite: 3]. For $841 - 392$, rounding to the nearest ten gives $840 - 390 = 450$ (exact answer is $449$)[cite: 3].

### Approximating Results of Multiplication and Division
Approximation simplifies complex multiplication and division into easy mental calculations[cite: 3]:
- **Multiplication:** To estimate $63 \times 47$, round each number to $1\text{ s.f.}$: $60 \times 50 = 3,000$ (exact calculation is $2,961$)[cite: 3].
- **Division:** To estimate $703 \div 21$, round each number to convenient values: $700 \div 20 = 35$[cite: 3].

## Comparative Matrix
Heading: Comparing Exact Calculations and Approximations
- Label: Precision Level
  Title: Exact Value vs Approximated Value
  Body: An exact value represents the precise arithmetic outcome (e.g., $63 \times 47 = 2,961$), while an approximation represents a rounded estimate for rapid decision-making ($60 \times 50 = 3,000$)[cite: 3].
- Label: Rounding Direction
  Title: Rounding Up vs Rounding Down
  Body: Rounding up occurs when the test digit is 5 or greater, increasing the target digit by 1, whereas rounding down occurs when the test digit is 0 to 4, keeping the target digit unchanged[cite: 3].
- Label: Decimal Rounding
  Title: Decimal Places vs Significant Figures
  Body: Decimal places count positions strictly after the decimal point, while significant figures count all meaningful digits starting from the first non-zero digit on the left[cite: 3].
- Label: Arithmetic Checks
  Title: Addition Estimation vs Multiplication Estimation
  Body: Addition approximation rounds addends to the same nearest place value, whereas multiplication approximation rounds factors to 1 or 2 significant figures to simplify trailing zeros[cite: 3].

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Changing the place value of a whole number when rounding off (e.g., writing 48,057 rounded to the nearest hundred as 481 instead of 48,100)[cite: 3].
  Fact: The dropped whole number places must be filled with zeros to preserve the magnitude of the number[cite: 3].
- Mistake: Counting leading zeros as significant figures (e.g., assuming $0.0045$ has 4 significant figures).
  Fact: Leading zeros only act as place holders; counting significant figures begins at the first non-zero digit ($4$), so $0.0045$ has only $2\text{ s.f.}$[cite: 3].

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Rounding to the Nearest Thousand and Hundred
Context: A town bank records a daily deposit of ₦253,718[cite: 3].
Problem: Round off 253,718 to (i) the nearest thousand and (ii) the nearest ten[cite: 3].
Fix: 
- (i) Nearest thousand: The thousands digit is 3. The digit immediately to its right is 7. Since $7 \ge 5$, round up by adding 1 to 3 ($3 + 1 = 4$) and replace remaining digits with zeros: $254,000$[cite: 3].
- (ii) Nearest ten: The tens digit is 1. The digit immediately to its right is 8. Since $8 \ge 5$, round up by adding 1 to 1 ($1 + 1 = 2$) and replace the units digit with zero: $253,720$[cite: 3].

### Example B: Estimating Magazine Sales Revenue
Context: A stationery shop in Kaduna sells about 340 magazines each week at a price of ₦180 each[cite: 3].
Problem: Estimate the total revenue the shop generates weekly from magazine sales[cite: 3].
Fix: Round each value to 1 significant figure or a convenient benchmark[cite: 3]:
340 rounds to 300, and 180 rounds to 200[cite: 3].
$$\text{Estimated Revenue} \approx 300 \times 200 = ₦60,000$$
(Exact calculation: $340 \times 180 = ₦61,200$)[cite: 3].

### Example C: Estimating Team Players at a Sports Festival
Context: There are 36 football teams gathered at the Ahmadu Bello Stadium, and each team has 12 players[cite: 3].
Problem: Estimate, and then calculate accurately, the total number of players at the stadium[cite: 3].
Fix: 
- Estimation: Round 36 to 40, and round 12 to 10[cite: 3].
  $$\text{Estimated Players} \approx 40 \times 10 = 400\text{ players}$$[cite: 3]
- Accurate calculation: $36 \times 12 = 432\text{ players}$[cite: 3].
