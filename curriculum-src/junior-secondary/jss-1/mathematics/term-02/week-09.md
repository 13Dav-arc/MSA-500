## Hook
Over the past eight weeks of this second term, you have navigated the practical worlds of metric estimation, powers-of-ten rounding, computer-driven binary arithmetic, and algebraic expressions[cite: 2]. This 80% Mastery Gate Assessment and Remediation Block serves as your comprehensive checkpoint, reinforcing your mathematical foundations across every core topic to ensure you are fully prepared for the terminal examination[cite: 2].

## Key Terms
- Term: Mastery Gate
  Definition: A diagnostic review and remediation checkpoint designed to evaluate and ensure at least 80% competency across all term modules before final summative testing[cite: 3].
- Term: Estimation
  Definition: Making an educated guess of nearly correct distances, capacities, masses, times, or prices without using exact measuring tools[cite: 2].
- Term: Approximation
  Definition: Replacing a precise numerical quantity with a rounded, simpler value to a given place value, decimal place, or significant figure[cite: 2].
- Term: Binary Numerals (Base 2)
  Definition: A positional number system using only the digits 0 and 1 with place values doubling at each step ($1, 2, 4, 8, \dots$)[cite: 2].
- Term: Simple Equation
  Definition: A mathematical statement showing that two algebraic expressions are equal, joined by an equality sign ($=$) and containing an unknown variable[cite: 2].

## Core Concept
### 1. Estimation and Unit Conversions (Weeks 01 & 02)
- **Linear Distance:** Metric units increase by multiples of ten with the metre as the base unit ($10\text{ mm} = 1\text{ cm}$, $100\text{ cm} = 1\text{ m}$, $1000\text{ m} = 1\text{ km}$)[cite: 2].
- **Capacity & Volume:** Measured in litres and cubic units ($1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ dm}^3 = 1\text{ litre}$, $1000\text{ litres} = 1\text{ m}^3$)[cite: 2].
- **Mass:** Base unit is the kilogram ($1000\text{ mg} = 1\text{ g}$, $1000\text{ g} = 1\text{ kg}$, $1000\text{ kg} = 1\text{ tonne}$, $200\text{ mg} = 1\text{ carat}$)[cite: 2].
- **Time:** Follows the sexagesimal system (base 60) inherited from ancient Babylon ($60\text{ s} = 1\text{ min}$, $60\text{ min} = 1\text{ h}$, $24\text{ h} = 1\text{ day}$)[cite: 2].

### 2. Approximation and Rounding Rules (Weeks 02 & 03)
- **Rounding Rule:** Check the first digit to be ignored[cite: 2]. If it is 5 or more, round up by adding 1 to the retained digit; if less than 5, round down (leave unchanged) and replace discarded whole-number places with zeros[cite: 2].
- **Significant Figures:** Counted from the first non-zero digit on the left (e.g., $9,562,774$ to $2\text{ s.f.}$ is $9,600,000$; $768.45$ to $2\text{ s.f.}$ is $770$)[cite: 2].
- **Everyday Applications:** Rounding numbers to the nearest 10, 100, or 1000 simplifies market shopping, library shelf planning, and government budgeting[cite: 2].

### 3. Numbers in Base 2 — Addition, Subtraction, and Multiplication (Weeks 04 & 06)
- **Binary Addition:** $0+0=0$, $1+0=1$, $0+1=1$, and $1+1=10_2$ (write down $0$, carry $1$)[cite: 2].
- **Binary Subtraction:** $0-0=0$, $1-0=1$, $1-1=0$, and $10_2-1=1$ (borrowing $1$ from the left column brings a value of $2_{10} = 10_2$ into the current column)[cite: 2].
- **Binary Multiplication:** $0 \times 0 = 0$, $1 \times 0 = 0$, $0 \times 1 = 0$, and $1 \times 1 = 1$, followed by long multiplication and binary addition[cite: 2].

### 4. Algebraic Processes and Simple Equations (Weeks 07 & 08)
- **Variables & Open Sentences:** Letters represent unknown numbers[cite: 2]. An open sentence (e.g., $x + 7 = 12$) becomes true when the correct value is substituted ($x = 5$)[cite: 2].
- **Simplification:** Combine like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients while keeping unlike terms separate ($4a + 5a + a = 10a$; $18a - 3a - 6a + 10b = 9a + 10b$)[cite: 2].
- **Brackets & Division:** Expand brackets by distributing the factor outside to all terms inside ($4(a + 3b) - 12b = 4a$)[cite: 2]; divide terms by simplifying coefficients and canceling matching variables ($36q \div 9q = 4$)[cite: 2].
- **Simple Linear Equations:** Maintain balance by performing the same operation on both sides of the equality sign ($x + 8 = 12 \implies x = 4$; $72 = 9x \implies x = 8$; $\frac{x}{2} = 5 \implies x = 10$)[cite: 2].

  Body: Whole number approximation replaces dropped places with zeros to preserve place magnitude[cite: 2], while decimal rounding drops trailing fractional places past the target precision[cite: 2].

## Common Mistakes
- Mistake: Combining unlike terms into a single product during addition (e.g., writing $3a + 2b = 5ab$)[cite: 2].
  Fact: Unlike terms cannot be merged by addition or subtraction; $3a + 2b$ remains $3a + 2b$[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Treating borrowed digits in binary subtraction as decimal ten.
  Fact: Borrowing $1$ from a higher binary column brings a value of $2_{10}$ ($10_2$), so $10_2 - 1 = 1$[cite: 2].
- Mistake: Forgetting to maintain balance on both sides when solving simple equations.
  Fact: Whatever operation is performed on the left-hand side (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) must be applied identically to the right-hand side[cite: 2].

## Worked Examples
### Example A: Comprehensive Metric and Time Conversion
Context: A long-distance athlete runs for $2\text{ hours } 4\text{ minutes } 15\text{ seconds}$ along a highway course measuring $12\text{ km}$[cite: 2].
Problem: Convert the running time into seconds and the distance into metres[cite: 2].
Fix:
- Time conversion: $2\text{ h} = 2 \times 3600 = 7200\text{ s}$; $4\text{ min} = 4 \times 60 = 240\text{ s}$[cite: 2]. Total time: $7200 + 240 + 15 = 7455\text{ seconds}$[cite: 2].
- Distance conversion: Since $1\text{ km} = 1000\text{ m}$, $12\text{ km} = 12 \times 1000 = 12,000\text{ metres}$[cite: 2].

### Example B: Long Binary Multiplication
Context: Compute the product of $101_2$ and $11_2$ in Base 2[cite: 2].
Problem: Multiply the two binary numerals and verify using decimal equivalents[cite: 2].
Fix:
$$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
    101_2 \\
  \times 11_2 \\
  \hline
    101 \\
  + 101\phantom{0} \\
  \hline
   1111_2
\end{array}$$[cite: 2]
- Column addition: $1$ in units; $0 + 1 = 1$ in twos; $1 + 0 = 1$ in fours; $1$ in eights[cite: 2].
- Result: $1111_2$[cite: 2].
- Verification: $101_2 = 5_{10}$ and $11_2 = 3_{10}$; $5 \times 3 = 15_{10} = 1111_2$[cite: 2].

### Example C: Solving a Word Problem Using Simple Equations
Context: Olu thinks of a secret number, adds 3 to it, and the final result is 8[cite: 2].
Problem: Formulate an algebraic equation and find the secret number[cite: 2].
Fix:
- Let the secret number be $x$[cite: 2].
- Equation: $x + 3 = 8$[cite: 2].
- Subtract 3 from both sides: $x + 3 - 3 = 8 - 3$[cite: 2].
- $x = 5$[cite: 2].
- Verification: $5 + 3 = 8$ (True)[cite: 2].
