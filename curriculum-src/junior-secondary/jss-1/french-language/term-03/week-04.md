## Hook
Imagine taking an unforgettable safari expedition to Yankari National Park in Bauchi State or visiting the vast W National Park spanning Niger, Benin, and Burkina Faso! As your safari jeep drives quietly through the savannah grasslands, you spot majestic herds of elephants splashing by the river, agile monkeys swinging through acacia branches, and a magnificent pride of lions resting in the afternoon shade! Back at home in your compound or family village, you wake up to the proud crow of the rooster (*le coq*), greet your faithful guard dog (*le chien*), and watch lively goats (*les chèvres*) scampering through the courtyard. Being able to name, classify, and describe both domestic companions and magnificent wild creatures in French opens up a vibrant world of natural science, biology, and environmental stewardship. In this lesson, you will master animal vocabulary from pets to predators and celebrate West Africa's breathtaking biodiversity!

## Key Terms
- Term: L'Animal (Plural: Les Animaux)
- Definition: A living creature; notice the irregular plural in French (*un animal* $\rightarrow$ *des animaux*).
- Term: Les Animaux Domestiques
- Definition: Tame animals kept as companions or helpers at home (*le chien, le chat*).
- Term: Les Animaux de la Ferme
- Definition: Livestock raised on farms for milk, eggs, meat, and labour (*la vache, la chèvre, le mouton, la poule*).
- Term: Les Animaux Sauvages
- Definition: Wild, untamed creatures living freely in natural habitats, forests, and game reserves (*le lion, l'éléphant, le singe*).
- Term: Le Chien / La Chienne
- Definition: The dog (male / female), known as man's loyal companion and guard.
- Term: Le Chat / La Chatte
- Definition: The cat (male / female), a domestic feline companion.
- Term: La Chèvre
- Definition: The goat, an agile domestic animal widely raised across Nigerian households.
- Term: Le Mouton
- Definition: The sheep, raised for wool and meat (*le bélier* = the ram).
- Term: La Vache
- Definition: The cow, raised for dairy milk and beef.
- Term: Le Lion / La Lionne
- Definition: The lion (male / female), known as the king of the savannah.
- Term: L'Éléphant
- Definition: The elephant, the largest land mammal on Earth, possessing large ears and a trunk (*la trompe*).
- Term: Le Singe
- Definition: The monkey, an agile primate common in West African trees and wildlife sanctuaries.

### 2. Wild Animals and Predators (Les Animaux Sauvages)
West Africa is home to magnificent wildlife protected in sanctuaries and game reserves like Yankari (Nigeria), Pendjari (Benin), and Niokolo-Koba (Senegal):
- **Le lion:** The lion (*Le lion est le roi de la savane* - The lion is the king of the savannah).
- **L'éléphant (masc.):** The elephant (*L'éléphant est un animal gigantesque et intelligent*).
- **Le léopard / La panthère:** The leopard / panther, famous for stealth and spotted fur.
- **Le singe:** The monkey (*Le singe adore manger des bananes*).
- **La girafe:** The giraffe, known for its extremely long neck (*un long cou*).
- **L'hippopotame (masc.):** The hippopotamus, dwelling in West African rivers and marshes.
- **Le crocodile:** The crocodile, an ancient aquatic reptile.
- **Le serpent:** The snake (*Le serpent rampe dans l'herbe* - The snake crawls in the grass).

### 3. Expressing Animal Ownership and Descriptions
To describe an animal or state that you have a pet:
- *J'ai un chien noir et deux chats blancs à la maison.* (I have a black dog and two white cats at home.)
- *Mon grand-père a une grande ferme à Kano avec dix vaches et vingt chèvres.* (My grandfather has a large farm in Kano with ten cows and twenty goats.)
- *Les oiseaux chantent dans les arbres chaque matin.* (The birds sing in the trees every morning.)

---

### 4. Environmental Conservation and Biodiversity (La Préservation de la Nature)
Wild animals are essential to Africa's ecological balance. In French, we emphasize:
- *Il faut protéger les animaux sauvages contre le braconnage.* (We must protect wild animals against poaching.)
- *Les parcs nationaux préservent la biodiversité de notre continent.* (National parks preserve our continent's biodiversity.)

## Common Mistakes
- **Writing "les animals" or "les chevals":** In French, masculine nouns ending in *-al* change to **-aux** in the plural: **des animaux**, **des chevaux**. Never write *des animals* or *des chevals*.
- **Misspelling "oiseau":** Students often find the French word for bird challenging because of its 5 vowels: **l'oiseau** (plural: **les oiseaux**, ending in '-x').
- **Confusing "le mouton" and "la chèvre":** While both are small ruminants common in Nigeria, *la chèvre* is the goat (feminine) and *le mouton* is the sheep (masculine).

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Plural Transformation of Animals
- *Singular:* *Un cheval et un bel oiseau.* (A horse and a beautiful bird.)
- *Plural Transformation:*
  - *Un cheval* $\rightarrow$ *Des chevaux*
  - *et* $\rightarrow$ *et*
  - *un bel oiseau* $\rightarrow$ *de beaux oiseaux*
- *Result:* **Des chevaux et de beaux oiseaux.**

### Example 2: Describing a Farm in French
- *Prompt:* Translate into French: "On the farm, there are cows, goats, and roosters."
- *Translation:* **À la ferme, il y a des vaches, des chèvres et des coqs.**

### Example 3: Wild Animal Safari Report
- *Prompt:* Translate into French: "At Yankari Park, we see elephants and monkeys."
- *Translation:* **Au parc de Yankari, nous voyons des éléphants et des singes.**

## Guardian Sync
- **Animal Sound and Name Game:** Challenge your child to make animal sounds and name the creature in French! Say: *Le coq fait: cocorico!* (Cock-a-doodle-doo!), *Le chien aboie: ouah ouah!* (Woof woof!), *Le chat miaule: miaou!*.
- **Village Animal Audit:** If you visit relatives or a farm, ask your child to count and identify the animals in French: *Voici trois chèvres, deux moutons et une poule*.
- **Connecting French to Biology:** Highlight that animal classifications in French (*mammifères, reptiles, oiseaux*) reinforce their Basic Science lessons.
