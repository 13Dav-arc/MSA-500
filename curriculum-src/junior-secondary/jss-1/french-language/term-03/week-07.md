## Hook
Every weekday morning as you prepare for school, you put on your neatly pressed uniform—a crisp shirt, smart trousers or a pleated skirt, polished black shoes, and white socks. On festive occasions like Eid, Christmas, or a traditional wedding in Lagos, Enugu, or Kano, the atmosphere explodes with breathtaking color as gentlemen step out in magnificent embroidered *agbada* or crisp *caftans*, and ladies don radiant *iro and buba* crowned with sculptural head-ties (*le gélé*). Fashion is one of the most vibrant forms of human self-expression, and Francophone West Africa is a worldwide epicenter of style and textile art! When you can describe what someone is wearing in French using the versatile regular verb **porter** and weave in colors and accessories, you can describe anyone in a crowd with effortless panache. In this lesson, you will master the wardrobe of daily life, celebrate traditional African attire, and become a master fashion reporter!

## Key Terms
- Term: Le Vêtement (Plural: Les Vêtements)
- Definition: Any item of clothing or apparel worn to cover, protect, and adorn the body.
- Term: La Chemise
- Definition: A buttoned, collared shirt; a feminine noun in French (*une chemise blanche*).
- Term: Le Pantalon
- Definition: Trousers or pants; notice that unlike English (which is always plural), French often treats *le pantalon* as a singular noun.
- Term: La Robe
- Definition: A dress or gown worn by girls and women (*une belle robe*).
- Term: La Jupe
- Definition: A skirt, a feminine lower-body garment.
- Term: Les Chaussures
- Definition: Shoes; a feminine plural noun (*des chaussures noires*).
- Term: L'Uniforme Scolaire (masc.)
- Definition: The standardized school uniform worn by students.
- Term: Porter
- Definition: Regular First Group verb meaning "to wear" or "to carry" (*Je porte mon uniforme*).
- Term: Le Chapeau
- Definition: A hat (plural: *les chapeaux*, ending in '-x').
- Term: La Cravate
- Definition: A necktie worn with a formal collar and suit.
- Term: La Montre
- Definition: A wristwatch used to tell time (*une belle montre dorée*).
- Term: Le Boubou / L'Agbada
- Definition: The grand, flowing traditional West African robe worn across Nigeria, Senegal, Mali, and Niger.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **English vs French Number Difference: "Pants" vs "Le Pantalon":**
> In English, trousers are always treated as a plural pair ("These pants *are* blue"). In French, it is singular: **Ce pantalon est bleu**! (Unless you are talking about multiple pairs of trousers: *des pantalons*).

### 2. Personal Accessories (Les Accessoires de Mode)
To complete an outfit, people wear accessories:
- **La montre:** The wristwatch (*J'ai une belle montre au poignet* - I have a nice watch on my wrist).
- **Les lunettes (fem. pl.):** Glasses / spectacles (*Il porte des lunettes de soleil* - He wears sunglasses).
- **Le chapeau / La casquette:** The hat / cap (*un chapeau de feutre*).
- **La ceinture:** The belt (*une ceinture en cuir*).
- **La cravate:** The tie (*une cravate bleue*).
- **Le sac à dos:** The backpack for schoolbooks.

### 3. Conjugation of the Verb "Porter" (To Wear)
*Porter* is a regular First Group *-ER* verb:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┐
│ CONJUGATION OF PORTER (PRESENT TENSE)│ MEANING                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Je porte                             │ I wear / I am wearing                │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Tu portes                            │ You wear (informal)                  │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Il / Elle porte                      │ He / She wears                       │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Nous portons                         │ We wear                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Vous portez                          │ You wear (formal / plural)           │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Ils / Elles portent                  │ They wear                            │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4. Traditional Nigerian and African Attire in French
West African textiles—like Yoruba *aso-oke*, Hausa *babban riga*, Igbo *isiagu*, and Ghanaian *kente*—are celebrated internationally:
- *Au Nigéria, pendant les cérémonies traditionnelles, les hommes portent un grand **agbada** (un grand boubou brodé) avec un chapeau traditionnel.*
- *Les femmes portent un magnifique **buba**, un **iro** (pagne enroulé) et un **gélé** (foulard de tête sculpté).*
- *C'est une tenue traditionnelle très élégante et colorée.*

### 5. Combining Clothes with Colors and Adjective Agreement
Remember: adjectives of color must agree in gender and number with the clothing item!
- *Une chemise (fem.)* $\rightarrow$ *une chemise blanch**e***.
- *Un pantalon (masc.)* $\rightarrow$ *un pantalon noir*.
- *Des chaussures (fem. pl.)* $\rightarrow$ *des chaussures noir**es***.
- *Des lunettes (fem. pl.)* $\rightarrow$ *des lunettes bleu**es***.

## Common Mistakes
- **Treating "pantalon" as plural in French:** Saying *Je porte des pantalons* when you are only wearing one pair of trousers is incorrect. Say: **Je porte un pantalon**.
- **Forgetting feminine agreement for shoes and socks:** *Les chaussures* and *les chaussettes* are feminine! You must write *des chaussures noir**es***, not *des chaussures noirs*.
- **Misspelling "chapeau" in the plural:** Words ending in *-eau* take '-x' in the plural, not '-s': **des chapeaux**.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Describing School Uniform
- *Prompt:* Translate into French: "Every morning, I wear a blue shirt and a grey skirt."
- *French Breakdown:*
  - Every morning = *Chaque matin*
  - I wear = *je porte* (verb *porter*)
  - a blue shirt = *une chemise bleue* (feminine agreement)
  - and = *et*
  - a grey skirt = *une jupe grise* (feminine agreement)
- *Complete Sentence:* **Chaque matin, je porte une chemise bleue et une jupe grise.**

### Example 2: Describing a Festive Outfit
- *Prompt:* Translate into French: "My uncle is wearing a large white agbada and a brown hat."
- *Translation:* **Mon oncle porte un grand agbada blanc et un chapeau marron.** (Note: *marron* is invariable!).

### Example 3: Changing Subject Pronoun
- *Sentence:* *Tu portes une belle veste.*
- *Change to "Nous":*
  - *Tu portes* $\rightarrow$ *Nous portons*
  - *une belle veste* remains the same (or *de belles vestes* if plural).
  - *Result:* **Nous portons une belle veste.**

## Guardian Sync
- **Wardrobe French Check:** As your child gets dressed in the morning, ask: *Qu'est-ce que tu portes aujourd'hui?*. Have them describe their clothes: *Je porte une chemise, un pantalon et des chaussures*.
- **Fashion Detective Game:** Look at family photographs from a recent event and challenge your child to describe what guests are wearing: *Tante Ngozi porte une robe jaune et un chapeau violet!*.
- **Textile Appreciation:** Discuss the beauty of African prints like Ankara and Aso-Oke, and how knowing French allows Nigerian fashion designers to sell their creations across the Francophone world.
