## Hook
Look at a political map of the African continent. Right in the energetic, beating heart of West Africa sits Nigeria—the Giant of Africa, home to over 220 million people and Africa's largest economy! But look closely at the international borders that touch our country. To our west lies the Republic of Benin; to our north stretch the golden plains of the Republic of Niger; to our northeast lies Lake Chad and the Republic of Chad; and along our eastern border stands the Republic of Cameroon! Every single nation that shares a physical land border with Nigeria is officially **Francophone** (French-speaking)! What does that mean for you as a student of MindStormer Global Academy? It means that French is not an overseas European novelty; it is our regional neighborhood language! If an oil engineer from Port Harcourt negotiates in Douala, or a grain merchant in Kano trades with Maradi, speaking French is the ultimate superpower! In this lesson, you will explore Nigeria's Francophone neighbors and their capitals, master stating your nationality with perfect gender agreement, and cross the official First Term 80% Mastery Gate!

## Key Terms
- Term: La Nationalité
- Definition: The legal and cultural status of belonging to a specific nation or country.
- Term: Le Pays
- Definition: The French word for a country or nation.
- Term: Le Nigéria
- Definition: The Federal Republic of Nigeria (Capital: Abuja).
- Term: Le Bénin
- Definition: The Republic of Benin, Nigeria's French-speaking neighbor to the west (Official Capital: Porto-Novo; Economic Hub: Cotonou).
- Term: Le Niger
- Definition: The Republic of Niger, Nigeria's French-speaking neighbor to the north (Capital: Niamey).
- Term: Le Tchad
- Definition: The Republic of Chad, Nigeria's French-speaking neighbor to the northeast (Capital: N'Djamena).
- Term: Le Cameroun
- Definition: The Republic of Cameroon, Nigeria's bilingual (predominantly Francophone) neighbor to the east (Capital: Yaoundé; Economic Hub: Douala).
- Term: Nigérian / Nigériane
- Definition: The nationality adjective for someone from Nigeria; *nigérian* for males and *nigériane* for females.
- Term: Nigérien / Nigérienne
- Definition: The nationality adjective for someone from the neighboring Republic of Niger; *nigérien* for males and *nigérienne* for females.
- Term: L'Accord en Genre (Gender Agreement)
- Definition: The grammatical rule in French where adjectives of nationality must change their spelling and pronunciation to match the gender (masculine or feminine) of the person described.
- Term: La CEDEAO (ECOWAS)
- Definition: The Economic Community of West African States (*Communauté Économique des États de l'Afrique de l'Ouest*), wherein French and English are the primary official languages.
- Term: Le Seuil de Maîtrise de 80%
- Definition: The mandatory continuous assessment benchmark (80%) required to qualify for terminal examinations.

#### Vital Linguistic Distinction: *Nigérian* versus *Nigérien*:
- A citizen of the Federal Republic of Nigeria (capital Abuja) is: **un Nigérian** (male) / **une Nigériane** (female).
- A citizen of the Republic of Niger (capital Niamey) is: **un Nigérien** (male) / **une Nigérienne** (female).
- *Never mix these two up!* They are distinct sovereign nations.

### 2. Asking and Stating Nationality in French
To inquire about someone's nationality:
- **The Question:**
  - *Quelle est ta nationalité?* (What is your nationality? - informal)
  - *Quelle est votre nationalité?* (What is your nationality? - formal / polite)
  - *De quel pays viens-tu?* (Which country do you come from?).
- **The Stating Formula (Uses the verb *Être*):**
  $$\mathbf{Je\ suis} + [\text{adjectif de nationalité}]$$
  - Male student: *Je suis nigérian.* (I am Nigerian.)
  - Female student: *Je suis nigériane.* (I am Nigerian.)
- **Capitalization Rule in French:**
  - When used as an **adjective** describing a person after the verb *être*, the nationality is written in **lowercase**:
    - *Il est nigérian.* / *Elle est française.*
  - When used as a **noun** designating the citizens as a group, it is capitalized:
    - *Les Nigérians aiment le football.* (Nigerians love football.)

### 3. Gender Agreement Rules for Nationality Adjectives
In French grammar, adjectives must agree in gender with the subject:
1. **The General Rule (Add '-e'):**
   - Add a silent **-e** to the masculine singular form.
   - *Acoustic Effect:* Adding '-e' causes the previously silent final consonant to become voiced!
     - *béninois* (silent 's') $\rightarrow$ **béninoise** (voiced like 'z': *bay-nee-nwahz*)
     - *français* (silent 's') $\rightarrow$ **française** (voiced like 'z': *fran-sehz*)
     - *camerounais* $\rightarrow$ **camerounaise**
     - *sénégalais* $\rightarrow$ **sénégalaise**
     - *américain* $\rightarrow$ **américaine**.
2. **The Double-N Rule (Ending in '-ien'):**
   - Adjectives ending in **-ien** double the 'n' and add **-e** ($\mathbf{-ienne}$):
     - *nigérian* $\rightarrow$ **nigériane** (or *nigérien* $\rightarrow$ **nigérienne**)
     - *tchadien* $\rightarrow$ **tchadienne**
     - *canadien* $\rightarrow$ **canadienne**
     - *ghanéen* $\rightarrow$ **ghanéenne**.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing "Nigérian" with "Nigérien":** Calling a Nigerian citizen *nigérien* (which means a citizen of Niger Republic!). Always use **nigérian / nigériane** for Nigeria.
2. **Omitting Feminine Agreement:** A female student saying *"Je suis nigérian"* without the feminine ending. She must say and write: **Je suis nigériane**!
3. **Pronouncing the Final 'S' in Masculine Nationalities:** Saying "béninois-sss" or "français-sss". The final 's' in the masculine form is **silent**; it is only pronounced (as a 'z' sound) in the feminine form (*béninoise*, *française*).
4. **Capitalizing Nationality Adjectives:** Writing *"Je suis Nigérian"* with a capital N. In French grammar, nationality words following *être* are adjectives and must be written in lowercase (*je suis nigérian*).

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Applying Gender Agreement to Nationalities
*Problem:* Convert the following masculine statements into the feminine form:
1. *Musa est nigérian.* $\rightarrow$ **Amina est nigériane.** (Notice the feminine form *nigériane*).
2. *Jean est béninois.* $\rightarrow$ **Marie est béninoise.** (Notice the addition of '-e' and voiced 'z' sound).
3. *Paul est camerounais.* $\rightarrow$ **Chantal est camerounaise.**
4. *Ahmed est nigérien.* $\rightarrow$ **Fatou est nigérienne.** (Notice the doubled 'n' in *-ienne*).

### Example 2: Diplomatic Profile Translation
*Scenario:* A student writes a short introduction for an ambassador visiting Lagos from Porto-Novo:
- *"Voici Monsieur Dossou. Il est béninois et il habite à Porto-Novo. Il parle français et il est notre voisin!"*
- *Translation:* Here is Mr. Dossou. He is Beninese and he lives in Porto-Novo. He speaks French and he is our neighbor!
