## Hook
Imagine standing in front of an international youth conference in Abuja, or welcoming a foreign exchange student from Dakar, Senegal or Cotonou, Benin into your school compound. All eyes are on you. They smile warmly and ask: *"Comment tu t'appelles?"* What do you say? In English, you would say: "My name is Amina, I am 12 years old, and this is my friend Emeka." But how do you express that in French with charm, elegance, and perfect grammar? The secret lies in a beautiful reflexive verb: **s'appeler**—which literally means *"to call oneself"*. In French, you do not just state your name as an object; you announce to the world: *"Je m'appelle..."* (literally, *"I call myself..."*)! In this lesson, you will master the complete art of introducing yourself, presenting your friends and family members to others, exchanging polite courtesies like *Enchanté(e)* (Pleased to meet you), and commanding the fundamental French subject pronouns: *Je, Tu, Il, Elle*!

## Key Terms
- Term: Se Présenter
- Definition: To introduce oneself in French.
- Term: Présenter Quelqu'un
- Definition: To introduce another person (a friend, sibling, or parent) to someone else.
- Term: S'appeler
- Definition: The reflexive French verb meaning "to be named" or "to call oneself," used for stating names.
- Term: Comment tu t'appelles? (Comment t'appelles-tu?)
- Definition: The standard informal question meaning "What is your name?" addressed to a friend or classmate.
- Term: Comment vous vous appelez?
- Definition: The polite, formal question meaning "What is your name, Sir/Ma'am?" addressed to an elder or superior.
- Term: Je m'appelle...
- Definition: The foundational French expression meaning "My name is..." (literally "I call myself...").
- Term: Il s'appelle... / Elle s'appelle...
- Definition: The expressions used to introduce others, meaning "His name is..." (*Il s'appelle*) and "Her name is..." (*Elle s'appelle*).
- Term: Voici...
- Definition: A demonstrative presentation word meaning "Here is..." or "This is...", used to introduce someone in front of you.
- Term: Enchanté / Enchantée
- Definition: The traditional French courtesy expressed upon meeting someone for the first time, meaning "Delighted" or "Pleased to meet you."
- Term: Les Pronoms Sujets
- Definition: The grammatical subject pronouns (Je, Tu, Il, Elle, Nous, Vous, Ils, Elles) that identify who is performing the action.
- Term: Ami / Amie
- Definition: The French word for "friend"; spelled *ami* for a male friend and *amie* for a female friend.

## Core Concept
### 1. The Conjugation of the Verb *S'appeler* (To Be Called)
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Se Présenter et Présenter Quelqu'un" caption="Se Présenter et Présenter Quelqu'un: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

To state your name or ask someone else's name, French uses the reflexive verb *s'appeler*:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   CONJUGATION OF THE VERB S'APPELER                    │
├────────────────────┬───────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┤
│ PRONOUN & VERB     │ PRONUNCIATION         │ ENGLISH MEANING           │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Je m'appelle       │ *zhuh mah-pell*       │ I am called / My name is  │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Tu t'appelles      │ *too tah-pell*        │ You are called (informal) │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Il s'appelle       │ *eel sah-pell*        │ He is called / His name is│
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Elle s'appelle     │ *ell sah-pell*        │ She is called / Her name  │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Nous nous appelons │ *noo nooz ah-puh-lon* │ We are called             │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Vous vous appelez  │ *voo vooz ah-puh-lay* │ You are called (formal/pl)│
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Ils s'appellent    │ *eel sah-pell*        │ They are called (masc/mix)│
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Elles s'appellent  │ *ell sah-pell*        │ They are called (feminine)│
└────────────────────┴───────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘
```
*Note the spelling rule:* In the singular forms (*je, tu, il, elle*) and third-person plural (*ils, elles*), the letter 'l' is **doubled** (*appelle*). In the *nous* and *vous* forms, there is only a **single 'l'** (*appelons, appelez*).

### 2. Asking Names: Informal versus Formal
- **Informal (Classmates and peers):**
  - *Comment tu t'appelles?* (What is your name?)
  - *Comment t'appelles-tu?* (Inverted form)
  - *Tu t'appelles comment?* (Colloquial / spoken form).
- **Formal (Elders, teachers, officials):**
  - *Comment vous vous appelez?* (What is your name, Sir/Ma'am?)
  - *Comment vous appelez-vous?* (Polite inverted form).

### 3. The 5-Point Complete Self-Introduction
When a student introduces themselves in French, they weave together five foundational elements:

```
1. SALUTATION:
   "Bonjour, Monsieur / Bonjour, mes amis!" (Good morning, Sir / friends!)

2. NAME:
   "Je m'appelle Chukwudi Okonkwo." (My name is Chukwudi Okonkwo.)

3. AGE:
   "J'ai douze ans." (I am twelve years old.)

4. NATIONALITY:
   "Je suis nigérian." (Masc.) / "Je suis nigériane." (Fem.) (I am Nigerian.)

5. RESIDENCE & STATUS:
   "J'habite à Abuja et je suis élève en classe de JSS 1."
   (I live in Abuja and I am a student in JSS 1.)
```

### 4. Introducing Someone Else (*Présenter Quelqu'un*)
To introduce a companion, classmate, or family member to others:
- **Using *Voici* (Here is / Meet...):**
  - *Voici mon ami. Il s'appelle Tunde.* (Here is my male friend. His name is Tunde.)
  - *Voici mon amie. Elle s'appelle Fatima.* (Here is my female friend. Her name is Fatima.)
  - *Voici ma mère.* (Here is my mother.)
- **Using *C'est* (This is...):**
  - *C'est mon frère, Samuel.* (This is my brother, Samuel.)
  - *C'est ma sœur, Blessing.* (This is my sister, Blessing.)
- **The Polite Response: *Enchanté(e)*:**
  - When introduced, you smile and say: **Enchanté!**
  - *Spelling Agreement:*
    - If a **male** writes it: **Enchanté** (one 'e').
    - If a **female** writes it: **Enchantée** (adds feminine 'e'). Both sound identical: *on-shon-tay*!

### 5. French Subject Pronouns (*Les Pronoms Sujets*)
Pronouns replace nouns and tell us who performs the action:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        FRENCH SUBJECT PRONOUNS                         │
├───────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┤
│ PRONOUN       │ MEANING                       │ EXAMPLE                │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ Je (or J')    │ I (first person singular)     │ Je m'appelle Ada.      │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ Tu            │ You (singular informal)       │ Tu as douze ans.       │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ Il            │ He / It (masculine singular)  │ Il s'appelle Musa.     │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ Elle          │ She / It (feminine singular)  │ Elle s'appelle Ngozi.  │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ Nous          │ We (first person plural)      │ Nous sommes nigérians. │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ Vous          │ You (formal singular / plural)│ Vous êtes le professeur│
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ Ils           │ They (all-male or mixed group)│ Ils sont étudiants.    │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ Elles         │ They (strictly all-female)    │ Elles sont amies.      │
└───────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┘
```

## Comparative Matrix
| Parameter | Informal Introduction | Formal Introduction | Introducing a Third Party |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Question** | *Comment tu t'appelles?* | *Comment vous vous appelez?* | *Comment s'appelle-t-il / elle?* |
| **Response** | *Je m'appelle [Nom]* | *Je m'appelle [Nom et Titre]* | *Il s'appelle... / Elle s'appelle...* |
| **Target** | Classmate, sibling, peer | Principal, elder, stranger | Introducing friend to teacher |
| **Courtesy Word** | *Salut! / Enchanté!* | *Bonjour, Monsieur / Enchanté(e)*| *Je vous présente mon ami(e)* |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Saying "Mon nom est...":** Literal translation of English "My name is." While technically understood, native French speakers consider it unidiomatic and unnatural. Always say: **Je m'appelle...**!
2. **Forgetting the Feminine 'E' in Writing "Enchantée":** When a female student writes "Enchanté", she must add the feminine agreement letter 'e' (**Enchantée**).
3. **Confusing "Il" and "Elle":** Using *Il s'appelle* for a girl or *Elle s'appelle* for a boy. Remember: **Il** = He; **Elle** = She.
4. **Omitting the 'S' in Plural "Ils" or "Elles":** Writing *il* when referring to a group of boys. Remember: Singular is *il/elle*; plural is *ils/elles*.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Full Classroom Introduction Dialogue
*Scenario:* Two new students meet on the assembly ground of MindStormer Global Academy:
- **Ibrahim:** *"Bonjour! Je m'appelle Ibrahim. Et toi, comment tu t'appelles?"* (Hello! My name is Ibrahim. And you, what is your name?)
- **Kehinde:** *"Bonjour Ibrahim! Je m'appelle Kehinde. J'ai douze ans et je suis nigériane."* (Hello Ibrahim! My name is Kehinde. I am 12 years old and I am Nigerian.)
- **Ibrahim:** *"Enchanté, Kehinde! Voici mon ami Emeka. Il a treize ans."* (Delighted, Kehinde! Here is my friend Emeka. He is 13 years old.)
- **Kehinde:** *"Enchantée, Emeka!"* (Delighted to meet you, Emeka!)
- **Analysis:** Notice the perfect gender agreement: Kehinde writes *nigériane* and *Enchantée* because she is female; Ibrahim uses *nigérian* and writes *Enchanté*.

### Example 2: Selecting the Correct Subject Pronoun
*Problem:* Replace the underlined noun phrase with the correct French subject pronoun:
1. <u>Monsieur Adeleke</u> habite à Ibadan. $\rightarrow$ **Il** habite à Ibadan. (*Il* replaces masculine singular).
2. <u>Madame Bello et Amina</u> parlent français. $\rightarrow$ **Elles** parlent français. (*Elles* replaces an all-female plural group).
3. <u>Chidi et Ngozi</u> ont douze ans. $\rightarrow$ **Ils** ont douze ans. (In French, a mixed group containing both males and females takes the masculine plural pronoun **Ils**).

## Guardian Sync
- **Family Introduction Game:** Have your child introduce family members to each other in French! For example: *"Papa, voici ma mère. Elle s'appelle Bola."* and *"Maman, voici mon frère. Il s'appelle Tobi."* Practice responding with a hearty *"Enchanté!"* or *"Enchantée!"*
- **Spoken Practice:** Ask your child: *"Comment tu t'appelles?"* Verify that they respond proudly in a complete sentence: *"Je m'appelle [their name]!"*
