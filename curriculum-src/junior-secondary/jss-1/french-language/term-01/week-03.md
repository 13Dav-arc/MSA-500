## Hook
Imagine walking through the colorful Dantokpa Market in Cotonou, Republic of Benin, or visiting a bustling bakery in Paris. The aroma of warm, crusty baguettes fills the air. You want to buy three fresh loaves of bread, two bottles of juice, and find out the price. What is the very first tool you need? Numbers! And what is one of the most exciting questions new JSS 1 students ask each other on the first day of school? *"How old are you?"* In English, we say: "I am twelve years old" using the verb *to be*. But in French, you do not *be* your age—you **possess** your age! A French speaker says: *"J'ai douze ans"*—literally, *"I have twelve years!"* In this lesson, you will master counting from zero all the way to thirty with authentic French pronunciation, decode number patterns, and learn how to ask and state age with total confidence!

## Key Terms
- Term: Les Nombres Cardinaux
- Definition: Cardinal numbers used for counting and quantifying items (e.g., *un, deux, trois*).
- Term: Zéro
- Definition: The number zero (0) in French.
- Term: Un / Une
- Definition: The number one (1); *un* is used before masculine nouns and *une* before feminine nouns.
- Term: Le Verbe Avoir
- Definition: The essential irregular French verb meaning "to have," conjugated as *j'ai, tu as, il a, elle a* when stating age.
- Term: Quel âge as-tu?
- Definition: The informal French question meaning "How old are you?" addressed to a friend, peer, or child.
- Term: Quel âge avez-vous?
- Definition: The polite or formal question meaning "How old are you?" used when addressing an adult, elder, or group.
- Term: J'ai ... ans
- Definition: The standard French sentence formula for stating one's age (literally "I have ... years").
- Term: Ans
- Definition: The French word for "years" (plural of *an*), mandatory when expressing age in French.
- Term: Vingt
- Definition: The number twenty (20) in French, pronounced *van* with silent 'g' and 't'.
- Term: Vingt et un
- Definition: The number twenty-one (21), featuring the conjunction *et* (and) without hyphens.
- Term: Trente
- Definition: The number thirty (30) in French.

### 1. Counting from 0 to 10: The Foundation Stones

Every number in French builds upon the first ten digits:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        NUMBERS 0 TO 10 IN FRENCH                       │
├────────┬─────────────────┬──────────┬────────┬────────────────┬────────┤
│ DIGIT  │ FRENCH WORD     │ PHONETIC │ DIGIT  │ FRENCH WORD    │PHONETIC│
├────────┼─────────────────┼──────────┼────────┼────────────────┼────────┤
│   0    │ zéro            │ *zay-ro* │   6    │ six            │ *seess*│
│   1    │ un / une        │ *uhn/oon*│   7    │ sept           │ *set*  │
│   2    │ deux            │ *deuh*   │   8    │ huit           │ *weet* │
│   3    │ trois           │ *twa*    │   9    │ neuf           │ *nuff* │
│   4    │ quatre          │ *kat-ruh*│  10    │ dix            │ *deess*│
│   5    │ cinq            │ *sank*   │        │                │        │
└────────┴─────────────────┴──────────┴────────┴────────────────┴────────┘
```

#### Pronunciation Tips for 0–10:
- **Sept (7):** The letter 'p' is **completely silent**! Say *set*, not "sept."
- **Huit (8):** The letter 'h' is silent. Say *weet*.
- **Six (6) and Dix (10):** The final 'x' sounds like an "s" (*seess*, *deess*).

### 2. The Teens: Counting from 11 to 20
The numbers from 11 to 16 follow a musical rhyme ending in **-ze**:
- **11:** *onze*
- **12:** *douze*
- **13:** *treize*
- **14:** *quatorze*
- **15:** *quinze*
- **16:** *seize*

From 17 to 19, French builds composite numbers using ten (*dix*) joined by a hyphen:
- **17:** *dix-sept* ($10 + 7$, pronounced *deess-set*)
- **18:** *dix-huit* ($10 + 8$, pronounced *deez-weet*)
- **19:** *dix-neuf* ($10 + 9$, pronounced *deez-nuff*)
- **20:** *vingt* (pronounced *van*; both the 'g' and 't' are silent!).

---

### 3. Counting from 21 to 30
Notice the special rule for numbers ending in "one":
- **21:** **vingt et un** (notice the conjunction **et** meaning "and"; there are **no hyphens**!).
- From 22 to 29, the numbers are connected with a simple **hyphen** without the word *et*:
  - **22:** *vingt-deux*
  - **23:** *vingt-trois*
  - **24:** *vingt-quatre*
  - **25:** *vingt-cinq*
  - **26:** *vingt-six*
  - **27:** *vingt-sept*
  - **28:** *vingt-huit*
  - **29:** *vingt-neuf*
- **30:** *trente* (pronounced *tront*).

### 4. Asking and Stating Age in French
In French grammar, age is treated as a **possession**, not a state of being:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   AGE IN ENGLISH VERSUS FRENCH                         │
├──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ENGLISH MINDSET (TO BE)  │ FRENCH MINDSET (AVOIR = TO HAVE)            │
├──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ "I AM twelve years old." │ "J'AI douze ans." (Literally: I have 12 yrs)│
│ (Uses the verb *to be*)  │ (Uses the verb *avoir* = to have)           │
├──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ NEVER SAY IN FRENCH:     │ "Je suis douze ans" (INCORRECT!)            │
│ ALWAYS SAY IN FRENCH:    │ "J'ai douze ans" (CORRECT!)                 │
└──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### The Formula for Stating Age:
$$\mathbf{J'ai} + [\text{Number}] + \mathbf{ans}$$
- *J'ai dix ans.* (I am 10 years old.)
- *J'ai onze ans.* (I am 11 years old.)
- *J'ai douze ans.* (I am 12 years old.)
- *J'ai treize ans.* (I am 13 years old.)

#### Asking Age:
- **Informal (to a friend/peer):**
  - *Quel âge as-tu?* (How old are you?)
  - *Tu as quel âge?* (You have what age?)
- **Formal (to an elder or superior):**
  - *Quel âge avez-vous?* (How old are you, Sir/Ma'am?)
- **Talking about Others:**
  - *Quel âge a-t-il?* $\rightarrow$ *Il a onze ans.* (He is 11 years old.)
  - *Quel âge a-t-elle?* $\rightarrow$ *Elle a douze ans.* (She is 12 years old.)

## Common Mistakes
1. **Saying "Je suis douze ans":** Translating directly from English "I am 12." In French, you must use the verb **avoir** (*J'ai douze ans*).
2. **Dropping the Word "Ans":** Saying only *"J'ai douze."* In French, you cannot stop at the number; you must include the word **ans** (years).
3. **Pronouncing the 'P' in Sept:** Saying "sept" with a hard English 'p'. The 'p' in *sept* is completely silent (pronounced *set*).
4. **Writing Hyphens in "Vingt et un":** Writing *vingt-et-un*. The official rule for 21 is **vingt et un** (separated by spaces, not hyphens).

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Solving French Math Equations
*Problem:* Calculate the answer in French for the following arithmetic problems:
1. *Cinq + Sept = ?*
   - $5 + 7 = 12 \rightarrow$ **douze**
2. *Dix + Neuf = ?*
   - $10 + 9 = 19 \rightarrow$ **dix-neuf**
3. *Vingt + Huit = ?*
   - $20 + 8 = 28 \rightarrow$ **vingt-huit**
4. *Quinze + Quinze = ?*
   - $15 + 15 = 30 \rightarrow$ **trente**

### Example 2: Conversational Age Exchange
*Scenario:* Two JSS 1 students, Musa and Ngozi, introduce themselves in French class:
- **Musa:** *"Salut Ngozi! Quel âge as-tu?"* (Hi Ngozi! How old are you?)
- **Ngozi:** *"Salut Musa! J'ai douze ans. Et toi, tu as quel âge?"* (Hi Musa! I am 12 years old. And you, how old are you?)
- **Musa:** *"Moi, j'ai treize ans!"* (Me, I am 13 years old!)
- **Analysis:** Both students correctly use the formula *J'ai + number + ans*, avoiding the erroneous *je suis*.

## Guardian Sync
- **Age Practice at Home:** Ask your child: *"Quel âge as-tu?"* Guide them to answer in a complete French sentence: *"J'ai [number] ans!"* Then ask them to state the ages of their brothers, sisters, or cousins in French using *Il a... ans* or *Elle a... ans*.
- **Market Counting Game:** When buying fruits or groceries (e.g., oranges, eggs), challenge your child to count the items from 1 to 20 aloud in French (*un, deux, trois, quatre... vingt*).
