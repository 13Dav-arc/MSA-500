## Hook
Imagine traveling just two hours west of Lagos across the Seme border into the Republic of Benin, or traveling north of Kano into the Republic of Niger. Suddenly, the street signs change, market traders speak a musical language, and friendly people greet you with a warm smile: *"Bonjour mon ami! Comment ça va?"* Did you know that Nigeria is completely surrounded on all land borders by French-speaking countries? To our west is Benin; to our north is Niger; to our northeast is Chad; and to our east is Cameroon! Learning French is not just learning a foreign school subject—it is unlocking the master key to West African trade, international diplomacy, friendship, and exciting global travel! Just like in Nigerian cultures where greeting elders with respect (*E nle o*, *E ku aaro*, *Ina kwana*, *Ututu oma*) is the foundation of good character, in French society, proper greetings open every door. In this very first lesson, you will master the art of greeting people politely in the morning, afternoon, and evening, inquire about wellbeing, and take your leave with elegance!

## Key Terms
- Term: Les Salutations
- Definition: Greetings in French; the polite formal and informal words exchanged when meeting someone.
- Term: Prendre Congé
- Definition: To take one's leave or say goodbye when departing.
- Term: Bonjour
- Definition: The universal French daytime greeting meaning "Good day" or "Good morning," used from dawn until late afternoon.
- Term: Bonsoir
- Definition: The French evening greeting meaning "Good evening," used from dusk onwards.
- Term: Salut
- Definition: An informal, friendly greeting used among close friends, peers, and children, meaning "Hi" or "Hello" (and sometimes "Bye").
- Term: Comment ça va? (Ça va?)
- Definition: The common French expression meaning "How are you?" or "How is it going?".
- Term: Ça va bien, merci
- Definition: The polite response meaning "I am doing well, thank you."
- Term: Au revoir
- Definition: The standard formal farewell meaning "Goodbye" (literally "until we see each other again").
- Term: À bientôt
- Definition: A warm farewell expression meaning "See you soon."
- Term: Bonne nuit
- Definition: The expression meaning "Good night," reserved strictly for when someone is going to bed to sleep.
- Term: Le Vouvoiement (Vous)
- Definition: The respectful, formal grammatical practice of addressing elders, teachers, strangers, or superiors using the polite pronoun *Vous*.
- Term: Le Tutoiement (Tu)
- Definition: The informal, familiar practice of addressing close friends, siblings, classmates, and young children using the personal pronoun *Tu*.

## Core Concept
### 1. Greetings Based on the Time of Day
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Les Salutations et Prendre Congé" caption="Les Salutations et Prendre Congé: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

In the French language, choosing the right greeting depends on the time of day:
- **Bonjour (Dawn to Sunset):**
  - Formed from two words: *bon* (good) and *jour* (day).
  - Used from sunrise until dusk to mean "Good morning" or "Good afternoon."
  - Polite additions: Always add the title of respect when speaking to adults:
    - *Bonjour, Monsieur* (Good morning, Sir / Mr.)
    - *Bonjour, Madame* (Good morning, Ma'am / Mrs.)
    - *Bonjour, Mademoiselle* (Good morning, Miss)
- **Bonsoir (Evening / Dusk onwards):**
  - Formed from *bon* (good) and *soir* (evening).
  - Used from late afternoon (around 5:00 PM) throughout the night when arriving at a gathering:
    - *Bonsoir, Monsieur* (Good evening, Sir)
    - *Bonsoir, mes amis* (Good evening, my friends)
- **Bonne nuit (Bedtime only):**
  - Used ONLY when parting at night to go to sleep:
    - *Bonne nuit, maman!* (Good night, mother!)
    - *Bonne nuit et faites de beaux rêves* (Good night and sweet dreams).

### 2. Formal versus Informal Registers: *Vous* versus *Tu*
Just as in Nigerian cultures where Yoruba uses the respectful pronoun *E* or Hausa uses *Ku* when addressing parents and teachers, French has two distinct registers:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      FORMAL (VOUS) VERSUS INFORMAL (TU)                │
├───────────────────┬──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┤
│ SITUATION         │ INFORMAL (LE TUTOIEMENT) │ FORMAL (LE VOUVOIEMENT) │
├───────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ Target Person     │ Classmates, siblings,    │ Teachers, principals,   │
│                   │ close friends, children. │ elders, strangers.      │
├───────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ Greeting Word     │ *Salut!* (Hi / Hello)    │ *Bonjour, Monsieur!*    │
├───────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ Asking Wellbeing  │ *Comment vas-tu?*        │ *Comment allez-vous?*   │
│                   │ (or *Comment ça va?*)    │ (How are you, Sir/Ma'am)│
├───────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ Pronoun Used      │ *Tu* (singular familiar) │ *Vous* (polite/respect) │
└───────────────────┴──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

### 3. Asking and Responding to "How Are You?"
Mastering conversational exchanges of wellbeing:
- **Questions:**
  - *Comment ça va?* (How are you doing? - neutral/everyday)
  - *Ça va?* (Doing okay? - short/casual)
  - *Comment vas-tu?* (How are you? - informal, addressing a friend)
  - *Comment allez-vous?* (How are you? - formal, addressing an elder or group)
- **Responses:**
  - *Ça va très bien, merci!* (I am doing very well, thank you!)
  - *Ça va bien.* (I am fine.)
  - *Pas mal.* (Not bad.)
  - *Comme ci, comme ça.* (So-so / manageable.)
  - *Ça ne va pas.* (Things are not going well.)
- **Returning the Courtesy:**
  - *Et toi?* (And you? - informal)
  - *Et vous?* (And you? - formal / respectful)

---

### 4. Taking Leave (*Prendre Congé*)
When departing from a conversation or class, polite French speakers say:
- **Au revoir:** The standard polite "Goodbye" (used with everyone).
- **À bientôt:** "See you soon!" (when expecting to meet again within a few days).
- **À demain:** "See you tomorrow!" (used leaving school in the afternoon).
- **À tout à l'heure:** "See you in a little while!" (later today).
- **Salut:** Can also be used among close friends to mean "Bye!"
- **Bonne journée:** "Have a good day!" (wished when departing in the morning).
- **Bonne soirée:** "Have a good evening!" (wished when departing in the evening).

## Comparative Matrix
| Expression | Literal Translation | Cultural English Equivalent | Appropriate Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bonjour** | Good day | Good morning / Good afternoon | Dawn to dusk greeting |
| **Bonsoir** | Good evening | Good evening | Dusk to night arrival greeting |
| **Bonne nuit** | Good night | Good night (sleep well) | Bedtime departure only |
| **Au revoir** | Until seeing again | Goodbye | Universal daytime/evening departure |
| **À demain** | Until tomorrow | See you tomorrow | Leaving school / workplace |
| **Salut** | Health / Salvation | Hi / Bye | Relaxed peer-to-peer greeting |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Using "Bonne nuit" as an Evening Greeting:** Walking into a party at 7:00 PM and saying *"Bonne nuit!"* In French, *Bonne nuit* means you are going to sleep! When arriving in the evening, always say **Bonsoir**!
2. **Addressing Teachers with "Salut":** Saying *"Salut!"* to your school principal or teacher. *Salut* is an informal street greeting strictly for friends. With teachers, always say: *"Bonjour, Monsieur"* or *"Bonjour, Madame"*.
3. **Saying "Ça va" Without Saying "Merci":** Answering only *"Ça va"* when someone inquires about your health. Politeness requires adding **merci** (thank you): *"Ça va bien, merci!"*
4. **Forgetting French Gender in Titles:** Calling a male teacher *Madame* or a female teacher *Monsieur*. *Monsieur* = Sir/Mr.; *Madame* = Ma'am/Mrs.; *Mademoiselle* = Miss.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Morning Greeting Dialogue in a Nigerian Classroom
*Scenario:* A JSS 1 student, Chinedu, meets his French teacher, Monsieur Adeleke, at 8:00 AM outside the classroom door:
- **Chinedu:** *"Bonjour, Monsieur!"* (Good morning, Sir!)
- **Monsieur Adeleke:** *"Bonjour, Chinedu! Comment vas-tu?"* (Good morning, Chinedu! How are you?)
- **Chinedu:** *"Je vais très bien, merci Monsieur. Et vous?"* (I am doing very well, thank you Sir. And you?)
- **Monsieur Adeleke:** *"Ça va très bien aussi, merci. Entre en classe!"* (I am doing very well too, thank you. Enter the classroom!)
- **Analysis:** Chinedu correctly uses *Bonjour*, attaches the respectful title *Monsieur*, responds with gratitude (*merci*), and returns the polite formal question (*Et vous?*).

### Example 2: Afternoon Departure Between Classmates
*Scenario:* Two students, Amina and Femi, part ways at the school gate at 3:30 PM:
- **Amina:** *"Au revoir, Femi! À demain!"* (Goodbye, Femi! See you tomorrow!)
- **Femi:** *"Salut Amina! Bonne après-midi!"* (Bye Amina! Have a good afternoon!)
- **Analysis:** Since they will see each other in class the following morning, *À demain* (See you tomorrow) is the perfect phrase.

- **Home French Greeting Challenge:** Practice French morning and evening greetings with your child at home. In the morning, encourage them to say: *"Bonjour, Papa!"* or *"Bonjour, Maman!"* In the evening, practice saying: *"Bonsoir!"* and at bedtime: *"Bonne nuit!"*
- **Geographic Exploration:** Look at a map of West Africa together with your child. Point out Nigeria's borders and locate our French-speaking neighbors: Republic of Benin (Porto-Novo/Cotonou), Niger (Niamey), Chad (N'Djamena), and Cameroon (Yaoundé). Discuss why learning French makes Nigerians regional leaders!
