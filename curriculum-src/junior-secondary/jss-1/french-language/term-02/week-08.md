## Hook
Imagine you are representing your school at an international youth football tournament in Lomé, Togo, or visiting cousins in Cotonou, Benin. During an intense sprint, you twist your ankle, or suddenly develop a pounding headache. You rush to the tournament infirmary, and the French-speaking nurse warmly asks: *"Où as-tu mal?"* (Where does it hurt?). If you only know English, you might point helplessly! But if you know French body parts and the magical phrase **"J'ai mal à..."**, you can speak with total clarity: *"J'ai mal au pied!"* (My foot hurts!) or *"J'ai mal à la tête!"* (I have a headache!). In this lesson, you will explore the anatomy of the human body from head to toe in French, master the contracted prepositions (*au, à la, à l', aux*), and learn how to report illnesses with confidence and precision!

## Key Terms
- Term: Le Corps Humain
- Definition: The human body, comprising the head, trunk, and limbs.
- Term: La Tête
- Definition: The head, housing the brain and sensory facial organs.
- Term: Les Yeux (singular: un œil)
- Definition: The eyes (*un œil* = one eye; *les yeux* = the eyes, an irregular plural in French).
- Term: Le Nez
- Definition: The nose, used for olfaction and breathing.
- Term: La Bouche
- Definition: The mouth, containing the tongue and teeth (*les dents*).
- Term: Les Oreilles
- Definition: The ears, the organs of hearing.
- Term: Le Bras
- Definition: The arm (spelled with a permanent silent 's' in both singular and plural).
- Term: La Main
- Definition: The hand, ending in five fingers (*les doigts*).
- Term: Le Ventre
- Definition: The stomach or belly (*J'ai mal au ventre*).
- Term: La Jambe
- Definition: The leg, used for walking and running.
- Term: Le Pied
- Definition: The foot (*à pied* = on foot; *le pied* = the foot).
- Term: Avoir Mal à
- Definition: The idiomatic French expression meaning "to have a pain in" or "to hurt", conjugated using the verb *avoir*.

### 2. The Idiomatic Construction: *Avoir Mal à...*
In English, we say "My head aches" or "I have a sore throat". 
In French, you use the special formula:
$$\text{Sujet} + \text{Verbe \textbf{Avoir}} + \mathbf{mal} + \mathbf{\grave{a}} + \text{Partie du Corps}$$

Because the preposition **à** must combine with the definite article of the body part, it contracts into **four distinct forms**:

1. **à + le = AU** (before a masculine singular body part):
   - *J'ai mal **au** ventre.* (My stomach hurts.)
   - *Il a mal **au** dos.* (His back hurts.)
   - *Tu as mal **au** bras.* (Your arm hurts.)
   - *Elle a mal **au** genou.* (Her knee hurts.)

2. **à + la = À LA** (before a feminine singular body part):
   - *J'ai mal **à la** tête.* (I have a headache.)
   - *Elle a mal **à la** gorge.* (She has a sore throat.)
   - *Il a mal **à la** jambe.* (His leg hurts.)

3. **à + l' = À L'** (before any singular body part starting with a vowel):
   - *J'ai mal **à l'**œil.* (My eye hurts.)
   - *Tu as mal **à l'**oreille.* (Your ear hurts.)
   - *Il a mal **à l'**estomac.* (His stomach/gut hurts.)

4. **à + les = AUX** (before any plural body part):
   - *J'ai mal **aux** yeux.* (My eyes hurt.)
   - *Elle a mal **aux** dents.* (She has a toothache.)
   - *Nous avons mal **aux** pieds.* (Our feet hurt.)
   - *Ils ont mal **aux** oreilles.* (Their ears hurt.)

### 3. Conjugating the Expression Across Persons
Remember your present tense conjugation of **avoir**:
- *J'ai mal...* (I have a pain...)
- *Tu as mal...* (You have a pain...)
- *Il / Elle a mal...* (He / She has a pain...)
- *Nous avons mal...* (We have a pain...)
- *Vous avez mal...* (You have a pain...)
- *Ils / Elles ont mal...* (They have a pain...)

### 4. A Realistic Dialogue at the School Infirmary (*À l'Infirmerie de l'École*)
- **L'Infirmière (The Nurse):** *Bonjour, Emeka! Qu'est-ce qui ne va pas?* (Hello, Emeka! What is wrong?)
- **Emeka:** *Bonjour, Madame. Je ne me sens pas bien. J'ai très chaud et j'ai mal à la tête.* (Hello, Ma'am. I don't feel well. I feel very hot and I have a headache.)
- **L'Infirmière:** *As-tu mal au ventre aussi?* (Does your stomach hurt too?)
- **Emeka:** *Non, mais j'ai mal aux yeux.* (No, but my eyes hurt.)
- **L'Infirmière:** *D'accord. Prends ce médicament, bois beaucoup d'eau et repose-toi.* (Okay. Take this medicine, drink plenty of water, and rest.)

## Common Mistakes
- **Saying "Mon bras a de la douleur":** English speakers often attempt literal translations like "My arm is hurting" (*Mon bras fait mal*). While understandable, standard natural French overwhelmingly prefers the idiomatic construction: **J'ai mal au bras**.
- **Forgetting contractions (*à le* and *à les*):** In French grammar, *à le* and *à les* are strictly forbidden! You must always contract them: *à le* $\rightarrow$ **au**, and *à les* $\rightarrow$ **aux**. Writing *J'ai mal à le ventre* or *J'ai mal à les yeux* is grammatically incorrect.
- **Confusing "œil" and "yeux":** One eye is *un œil* (/œj/), but two eyes are *les yeux* (/je/). It is one of the most famous irregular plurals in the French language.
- **Pronouncing the 's' in "bras" or "dos":** Both *bras* (arm) and *dos* (back) end in 's' in the singular, but the 's' is completely silent (*bra*, *do*).

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Selecting the Correct Contraction
- *Question:* Fill in the blank: "Khadija a fait beaucoup de sport. Elle a mal ______ jambes."
- *Solution:*
  1. Identify the body part: *jambes* (legs).
  2. Notice it is plural (*les jambes*).
  3. Preposition *à* + plural article *les* contracts to **aux**.
  4. Completed sentence: **Khadija a mal aux jambes.**

### Example 2: Expressing a Headache
- *Question:* How do you say "I have a severe headache" in French?
- *Solution:*
  1. "I have pain" = *J'ai mal*
  2. "Head" = *la tête* (feminine singular)
  3. Preposition = *à la*
  4. "Severe / very" = *très* (or *terriblement*)
  5. Sentence: **J'ai très mal à la tête.**

### Example 3: Translating a Multi-Symptom Statement
- *English:* "Tunde has a stomachache and a toothache."
- *Breakdown:*
  - Tunde has pain = *Tunde a mal*
  - in the stomach (masc. *le ventre*) $\rightarrow$ *au ventre*
  - and = *et*
  - in the teeth (plur. *les dents*) $\rightarrow$ *aux dents*
- *Result:* **Tunde a mal au ventre et aux dents.**
