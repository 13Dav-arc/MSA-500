## Hook
When your alarm clock rings on Monday morning, you leap out of bed knowing that a brand-new school week has begun! When Friday afternoon arrives, students cheer because the weekend is finally here! And when December rolls around, families across Nigeria celebrate the holidays with festive feasts, church services, and the cool breezes of the *harmattan* season. Humans have always organized their lives around the rhythmic dance of the calendar. But did you know that in French, the seven days of the week were named after the ancient planets of our solar system (the Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, and Saturn)? And unlike English where Monday and December are always capitalized, in French, the days of the week and months of the year are **never capitalized**! In this lesson, you will master the seven days of the week, the twelve months of the year, learn how to state today's date, and celebrate your birthday in French!

## Key Terms
- Term: Les Jours de la Semaine
- Definition: The seven days of the week (*lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi, samedi, dimanche*).
- Term: Les Mois de l'Année
- Definition: The twelve months of the year (*janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin, juillet, août, septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre*).
- Term: Aujourd'hui
- Definition: The French word meaning "today."
- Term: Demain
- Definition: The French word meaning "tomorrow."
- Term: Hier
- Definition: The French word meaning "yesterday."
- Term: Quelle est la date aujourd'hui?
- Definition: The standard question meaning "What is the date today?".
- Term: Aujourd'hui, c'est le...
- Definition: The standard response formula meaning "Today is [day number + month]."
- Term: Le Premier (1er)
- Definition: The ordinal number meaning "the first," used exclusively for the 1st day of any month in French (e.g., *le premier octobre* = October 1st).
- Term: L'Anniversaire
- Definition: A birthday (or anniversary); celebrated with the wish *Joyeux anniversaire!* (Happy Birthday!).
- Term: Le Week-end
- Definition: The weekend (Saturday and Sunday).

## Core Concept
### 1. The Seven Days of the Week (*Les Jours de la Semaine*)
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Les Jours de la Semaine et les Mois de l'Année" caption="Les Jours de la Semaine et les Mois de l'Année: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

In the French calendar, the week begins on **lundi** (Monday):

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE SEVEN DAYS OF THE WEEK                           │
├──────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────┬───────────────────────┤
│ FRENCH DAY   │ PRONUNCIATION    │ ENGLISH DAY  │ ANCIENT ORIGIN        │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ lundi        │ *luhn-dee*       │ Monday       │ Moon's day (Lune)     │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ mardi        │ *mar-dee*        │ Tuesday      │ Mars' day             │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ mercredi     │ *mair-kruh-dee*  │ Wednesday    │ Mercury's day         │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ jeudi        │ *zhuh-dee*       │ Thursday     │ Jupiter's day         │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ vendredi     │ *von-druh-dee*   │ Friday       │ Venus' day            │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ samedi       │ *sahm-dee*       │ Saturday     │ Saturn's day          │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ dimanche     │ *dee-monsh*      │ Sunday       │ Lord's day (Dominus)  │
└──────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

#### The Capitalization Rule:
In French grammar, the days of the week are **written in LOWERCASE letters** (e.g., *lundi*, *mardi*), unless they appear as the first word of a sentence! Writing *"Je vais à l'école le Lundi"* with a capital 'L' is an English capitalization error.

### 2. The Twelve Months of the Year (*Les Mois de l'Année*)
The twelve months are all grammatically masculine and also written in lowercase:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE TWELVE MONTHS OF THE YEAR                        │
├──────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────┬───────────────────────┤
│ FRENCH MONTH │ PRONUNCIATION    │ ENGLISH      │ SPECIAL NOTES         │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ janvier      │ *zhon-vyay*      │ January      │ First month of year   │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ février      │ *fay-vree-yay*   │ February     │ Has acute accents (é) │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ mars         │ *marss*          │ March        │ Final 's' is sounded! │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ avril        │ *ah-vreel*       │ April        │ Easter season         │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ mai          │ *may*            │ May          │ Workers' Day (1er mai)│
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ juin         │ *zhwahn*         │ June         │ Mid-year month        │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ juillet      │ *zhwee-yay*      │ July         │ French National Day   │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ août         │ *oot*            │ August       │ Pronounced *oot*!     │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ septembre    │ *sep-tomb-ruh*   │ September    │ School resumption     │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ octobre      │ *ok-tobb-ruh*    │ October      │ Nigeria Indep. (1er)  │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ novembre     │ *no-vomb-ruh*    │ November     │ Harmattan begins      │
├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ décembre     │ *day-somb-ruh*   │ December     │ Christmas (Noël)      │
└──────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

### 3. Stating the Date in French
To state the date, French strictly follows the order: **le + [Number] + [Month]**:
- **The Question:**
  - *Quelle est la date aujourd'hui?* (What is the date today?)
- **The Response Formula:**
  $$\mathbf{Aujourd'hui,\ c'est\ le} + [\text{Numéro}] + [\text{Mois}]$$
  - *Aujourd'hui, c'est le 15 mars.* (Today is March 15th.)
  - *C'est le 25 décembre.* (It is December 25th.)
  - *Demain, c'est le 16 mars.* (Tomorrow is March 16th.)

#### The Special Rule for the First Day of the Month (*Le Premier*):
For the **first day** of any month, French does NOT use the cardinal number "un". You must use the ordinal number **le premier** (written as **le 1er**):
- Nigeria's Independence Day: **Le premier octobre** (October 1st) - *NEVER "le un octobre"*.
- New Year's Day: **Le premier janvier** (January 1st).
- For all other days (2 to 31), standard numbers are used: *le deux, le trois, le quatre...*.

### 4. Birthday Celebrations (*L'Anniversaire*)
- **Asking Birthday:**
  - *Quelle est la date de ton anniversaire?* (When is your birthday?)
  - *C'est quand, ton anniversaire?* (When is your birthday? - informal)
- **Answering:**
  - *Mon anniversaire est le 10 juin.* (My birthday is June 10th.)
  - *Je suis né le 14 février.* (I was born on February 14th.)
- **Wishing Someone Well:**
  - **Joyeux anniversaire!** (Happy Birthday!).

## Comparative Matrix
| Parameter | English Calendar System | French Calendar System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Capitalization** | Days & months ALWAYS capitalized (*Monday, July*) | Days & months written in LOWERCASE (*lundi, juillet*) |
| **First Day of Week** | Sunday or Monday | Officially begins on **lundi** (Monday) |
| **Date Word Order** | Month then Day ("March 15th") | Article + Day + Month (**le 15 mars**) |
| **First Day Word** | Uses ordinal "first" (1st) | Uses ordinal **le premier** (le 1er) |
| **Other Days** | Uses ordinals ("2nd, 3rd, 4th") | Uses standard cardinal digits (**le 2, le 3, le 4**) |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Capitalizing Days and Months:** Writing *Vendredi* or *Décembre* in the middle of a French sentence. In French, they are written in lowercase (**vendredi**, **décembre**).
2. **Saying "Le un octobre":** Saying "le un" for the first day of the month. You must always say **le premier octobre**!
3. **Putting the Month Before the Day:** Saying *octobre 15* (English order). In French, it is strictly **le 15 octobre**.
4. **Mispronouncing "Août":** Saying "ah-oot". The month of August is pronounced crisply as **oot**!

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Writing Famous Nigerian Historical Dates in French
*Problem:* Translate these historic Nigerian dates into French:
1. **October 1st (Independence Day):** $\rightarrow$ **Le premier octobre** (written: *le 1er octobre*).
2. **May 27th (Children's Day):** $\rightarrow$ **Le 27 mai**.
3. **June 12th (Democracy Day):** $\rightarrow$ **Le 12 juin**.
4. **December 25th (Christmas Day):** $\rightarrow$ **Le 25 décembre**.

### Example 2: Conversational Calendar Exchange
*Scenario:* Two students check their class schedule:
- **Tunde:** *"Quel jour sommes-nous aujourd'hui?"* (What day are we today?)
- **Amina:** *"Aujourd'hui, c'est jeudi! Nous avons le cours de français."* (Today is Thursday! We have French class.)
- **Tunde:** *"Et demain, c'est vendredi! C'est le week-end!"* (And tomorrow is Friday! It is the weekend!)

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Birthday Calendar:** Gather your family together and record everyone's birthday in French on a sheet of paper (e.g., *Papa: le 14 août; Maman: le 3 novembre; Moi: le 22 mai*). Hang it on the refrigerator!
- **Daily Date Routine:** Every morning before leaving for school, ask your child: *"Quelle est la date aujourd'hui?"* Guide them to reply in French: *"Aujourd'hui, c'est [jour], le [numéro] [mois]!"*.
