## Key Terms
- Term: Les Membres de la Famille
- Definition: Family vocabulary encompassing parents (*le père, la mère*), siblings (*le frère, la sœur*), and extended relations (*les grands-parents, l'oncle, la tante*).
- Term: Les Adjectifs Possessifs
- Definition: Words indicating ownership (*mon, ma, mes; ton, ta, tes; son, sa, ses*) that agree with the gender and number of the noun possessed.
- Term: La Règle d'Euphonie
- Definition: The French rule requiring masculine possessives (*mon, ton, son*) before singular feminine nouns starting with a vowel or silent 'h' (e.g., *mon amie*).
- Term: L'Accord des Adjectifs
- Definition: The fundamental rule that adjectives must match the gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural) of the noun they describe.
- Term: Les Couleurs Invariables
- Definition: Color adjectives derived from nouns (like *marron* and *orange*) that never take feminine '-e' or plural '-s'.
- Term: Le Calendrier
- Definition: The French days of the week (*lundi* to *dimanche*) and months of the year (*janvier* to *décembre*), which are written in lowercase.
- Term: Le Système Vigésimal
- Definition: The base-20 arithmetic logic in French numbers (such as *quatre-vingts* = $4 \times 20 = 80$).
- Term: Les Verbes du Premier Groupe (-ER)
- Definition: Regular verbs ending in *-er* taking the present tense endings *-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent*.
- Term: Avoir Mal à
- Definition: The idiomatic expression used to report physical pain, contracting into *au, à la, à l',* and *aux*.
- Term: L'Heure et les Moments de la Journée
- Definition: Asking and stating time using *Quelle heure est-il?* and *Il est... heure(s)*, including *midi, minuit, et quart, et demie,* and *moins le quart*.

## Core Concept
This terminal examination synthesizes the nine foundational thematic units delivered during the Second Term:
1. **Week 01 (Family & Possessives):** Kinship terms (*père, mère, frère, sœur, oncle, tante, cousins*), possessive adjectives (*mon, ma, mes; ton, ta, tes; son, sa, ses*), and the euphonic vowel rule (*mon école, son amie*).
2. **Week 02 (Physical & Moral Description):** Qualifying adjectives (*grand, petit, gros, mince, jeune, vieux, beau, belle, intelligent, gentil*), gender formation (+e, doubling consonants, irregulars), and plural formation (+s, -x).
3. **Week 03 (Colors in French):** Color vocabulary, position after the noun (*une chemise verte*), variable colors (*vert, bleu, blanc, noir*), and invariable colors (*orange, marron*).
4. **Week 04 (Calendar: Days & Months):** Days of the week (*lundi* to *dimanche*), months (*janvier* to *décembre*), lowercase convention, and date formulations (*Aujourd'hui, c'est le...*).
5. **Week 05 (Midterm Synthesis):** Diagnostic review of foundational descriptive communication.
6. **Week 06 (Numbers 31 to 100):** Regular tens (*trente, quarante, cinquante, soixante*), the "et un" rule (31, 41, 51, 61), the 70 family (*soixante-dix*), the vigesimal 80 family (*quatre-vingts*), the 90 family (*quatre-vingt-dix*), and *cent*.
7. **Week 07 (First Group -ER Verbs):** Radical identification, present tense endings (*-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent*), silent pronunciation of *-e, -es, -ent*, daily activities (*parler, habiter, aimer, regarder, écouter*), and negation (*ne... pas*).
8. **Week 08 (Anatomy & Health):** Body parts (*tête, yeux, nez, bouche, bras, main, ventre, jambe, pied*), contracted prepositions with *avoir mal à...* (*au, à la, à l', aux*), and infirmary dialogues.
9. **Week 09 (Telling Time):** The impersonal formula *Il est...*, exact hours, noon (*midi*) and midnight (*minuit*), fractions (*et quart, et demie, moins le quart*), and official 24-hour notation.

## Common Mistakes
- **Capitalizing French Days and Months:** Unlike English where we capitalize "Monday" and "March", French days and months are common nouns and must be written in lowercase: *lundi, mardi, janvier, février*.
- **Translating "My head hurts" literally:** Saying *Ma tête fait mal* sounds unnatural. Always use the proper French idiomatic expression: **J'ai mal à la tête**.
- **Forgetting the hyphen in French numbers:** Numbers like *trente-deux* (32) and *quatre-vingt-dix* (90) require hyphens, whereas numbers with *et* (like *trente et un*) do not use hyphens.
- **Using "C'est" for telling time:** Stating the time requires the impersonal pronoun **Il est...** (*Il est trois heures*), not *C'est trois heures*.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Full Sentence Translation (Description + Family)
- *English:* "My mother is very tall, kind, and intelligent."
- *French Breakdown:*
  - My mother = *Ma mère* (feminine singular)
  - is = *est* (verb *être*)
  - very = *très*
  - tall = *grande* (feminine agreement)
  - kind = *gentille* (irregular feminine of *gentil*)
  - and = *et*
  - intelligent = *intelligente* (feminine agreement)
- *Translation:* **Ma mère est très grande, gentille et intelligente.**

### Example 2: Describing Pain and Asking for the Time
- *Prompt:* Translate into French: "Excuse me, what time is it? I have a stomachache and I want to go to the clinic."
- *Translation:* **Pardon, quelle heure est-il ? J'ai mal au ventre et je veux aller à la clinique.**
