## Hook
Imagine walking into a bustling market in Cotonou or a student conference in Yaoundé. You want to tell people: *"I speak English and French"*, *"I live in Abuja"*, *"I love jollof rice"*, or *"We are listening to the teacher"*. In English, you use separate verbs like speak, live, love, and listen. But in French, there is an incredible superpower: **almost 90% of all French verbs belong to a single family called the First Group (-ER verbs)**! Once you master the master key—the magical set of endings **-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent**—you instantly unlock the power to conjugate hundreds of action verbs in the present tense! In this lesson, you will master the art of conjugating regular *-er* verbs, discover which letters are pronounced and which are silent, express your favorite hobbies and daily actions, and start speaking fluid, confident French!

## Key Terms
- Term: Le Verbe
- Definition: An action word or state of being in a sentence that changes its ending according to who is doing the action.
- Term: L'Infinitif
- Definition: The base, unconjugated form of a verb before any endings are attached (ending in *-er*, such as *parler*, *habiter*, *aimer*).
- Term: Le Radical (Stem)
- Definition: The core part of the verb left behind after removing the infinitive ending *-er* (e.g., from *parl-er*, the radical is *parl-*).
- Term: La Terminaison (Ending)
- Definition: The specific letters added to the radical to match the subject pronoun (*-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent*).
- Term: Les Verbes du Premier Groupe
- Definition: The largest group of French verbs, encompassing all regular verbs ending in *-er* (except the irregular verb *aller*).
- Term: Parler
- Definition: To speak or talk (*Je parle*, *tu parles*, *il parle*).
- Term: Habiter
- Definition: To live or reside in a place (*J'habite à Lagos*).
- Term: Aimer
- Definition: To love or like (*J'aime la musique*).
- Term: Regarder
- Definition: To watch or look at (*Nous regardons la télévision*).
- Term: Écouter
- Definition: To listen to (*Vous écoutez le professeur*).
- Term: La Négation (Ne... pas)
- Definition: The two-part structure used to make a sentence negative, wrapping around the conjugated verb (*Je ne parle pas*).

## Core Concept
### 1. The Three Verb Groups in French
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Les Verbes du Premier Groupe (-ER au Présent)" caption="Les Verbes du Premier Groupe (-ER au Présent): Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

French verbs are divided into three major categories based on their infinitive endings:
1. **First Group (-ER):** Over 80% of all French verbs (*parler, habiter, aimer, chanter, danser, étudier*). They are completely regular! (Note: The only irregular verb ending in *-er* is *aller* - to go).
2. **Second Group (-IR):** Regular verbs ending in *-ir* that take *-issant* in the plural (*finir, choisir*).
3. **Third Group (-RE, -OIR, irregular -IR):** Irregular verbs (*être, avoir, faire, prendre*).

---

### 2. The Golden 2-Step Rule for Conjugating -ER Verbs
To conjugate any regular *-er* verb in the present tense (*le présent de l'indicatif*), follow this simple two-step rule:
1. **Drop the *-er*** from the infinitive to find the **radical** (stem).
   - *parler* $\rightarrow$ radical = **parl-**
   - *habiter* $\rightarrow$ radical = **habit-**
   - *aimer* $\rightarrow$ radical = **aim-**
2. **Add the magical present tense endings** corresponding to each subject pronoun:

```
┌─────────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────────┐
│ SUBJECT PRONOUN │ ENDING       │ PARLER (TO SPEAK)│ PRONUNCIATION    │
├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Je (I)          │ -e           │ Je parle         │ *zhuh parl*      │
├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Tu (You - sing.)│ -es          │ Tu parles        │ *too parl*       │
├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Il / Elle (He/She)│ -e         │ Il / Elle parle  │ *eel / el parl*  │
├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Nous (We)       │ -ons         │ Nous parlons     │ *noo par-lohn*   │
├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Vous (You - pl.)│ -ez          │ Vous parlez      │ *voo par-lay*    │
├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Ils / Elles (They)│ -ent       │ Ils / Elles parlent│ *eel / el parl*│
└─────────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────────┘
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Silent 'E' Secret of French Verbs (The "Boot" Verbs):**
> Notice that for **Je, Tu, Il/Elle, and Ils/Elles**, the endings (*-e, -es, -e, -ent*) are completely **SILENT**! 
> In all four cases, *parle, parles, parle,* and *parlent* are pronounced identically as **/paʁl/** (*parl*)! 
> Only **Nous (-ons)** and **Vous (-ez)** have endings that are sounded out (*par-lohn* and *par-lay*).

---

### 3. Elision with Verbs Starting with a Vowel or Silent 'H'
When a verb begins with a vowel or silent *h* (like *aimer*, *habiter*, *écouter*):
- The subject pronoun **Je** contracts to **J'** (élision):
  - *J'aime* (NOT *Je aime*) - I like / I love.
  - *J'habite* (NOT *Je habite*) - I live.
  - *J'écoute* (NOT *Je écoute*) - I listen.
- A liaison occurs with plural pronouns:
  - *Nous habitons* $\rightarrow$ pronounced *noo-zah-bee-tohn* (the silent 's' links as a 'z' sound).
  - *Vous aimez* $\rightarrow$ pronounced *voo-zay-may*.
  - *Ils écoutent* $\rightarrow$ pronounced *eel-zay-koot*.

### 4. Common -ER Verbs in Daily School and Home Life
Here is your essential vocabulary of first-group action verbs:
- **Parler:** To speak (*Je parle français et anglais* - I speak French and English).
- **Habiter:** To live (*J'habite à Abuja au Nigéria* - I live in Abuja, Nigeria).
- **Aimer:** To like/love (*J'aime le football et la musique* - I love football and music).
- **Regarder:** To watch (*Nous regardons un match* - We are watching a match).
- **Écouter:** To listen (*Tu écoutes la radio* - You listen to the radio).
- **Chanter:** To sing (*Elle chante une belle chanson* - She sings a beautiful song).
- **Danser:** To dance (*Ils dansent le bata* - They dance the Bata dance).
- **Étudier:** To study (*J'étudie les sciences* - I study science).
- **Manger:** To eat (*Nous mangeons du riz* - We eat rice).
- **Travailler:** To work (*Mon père travaille à Lagos* - My father works in Lagos).

---

### 5. Making Sentences Negative: *Ne ... Pas*
To say that you do NOT do an action, sandwich the conjugated verb between **ne** and **pas**:
$$\text{Sujet} + \mathbf{ne} + \text{Verbe Conjugé} + \mathbf{pas} + \text{Complément}$$

Examples:
- *Je parle yoruba.* $\rightarrow$ *Je **ne** parle **pas** yoruba.* (I do not speak Yoruba).
- *Il regarde la télévision.* $\rightarrow$ *Il **ne** regarde **pas** la télévision.* (He is not watching television).
- **Before a vowel or silent 'H'**, *ne* becomes **n'**:
  - *J'aime le poisson.* $\rightarrow$ *Je **n'**aime **pas** le poisson.* (I do not like fish).
  - *Il habite à Kano.* $\rightarrow$ *Il **n'**habite **pas** à Kano.* (He does not live in Kano).

## Common Mistakes
- **Pronouncing the "-ent" ending:** English speakers often try to pronounce *-ent* in *ils parlent* like the English word "parent" or with a nasal sound. **Never do this!** The third-person plural ending *-ent* on a conjugated verb is completely silent: *ils parlent* is pronounced exactly like *il parle* (/paʁl/)!
- **Forgetting elision with "Je":** Writing *Je aime* or *Je habite* is incorrect. Whenever the verb begins with a vowel or silent *h*, drop the 'e' and use an apostrophe: *J'aime*, *J'habite*.
- **Using an apostrophe with "Tu":** Students sometimes mistakenly write *T'aimes* or *T'habites*. While common in informal spoken street French, standard academic French never elides *Tu*: always write **Tu aimes** and **Tu habites**.
- **Forgetting "ne" in negative sentences:** In English, we simply add "not" (*I do not speak*). In French, negation is a two-part bracket: you must include both **ne** (or **n'**) before the verb and **pas** after the verb (*Je ne parle pas*).

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Conjugating *Aimer* in Full
- *Question:* Conjugate the regular *-er* verb *aimer* (to like/love) for all subject pronouns.
- *Solution:*
  1. Radical: *aim-*
  2. Add endings:
     - *J'aime* (I like/love)
     - *Tu aimes* (You like/love)
     - *Il / Elle aime* (He / She likes/loves)
     - *Nous aimons* (We like/love)
     - *Vous aimez* (You like/love)
     - *Ils / Elles aiment* (They like/love)

### Example 2: Translating Nigerian Daily Contexts
- *English:* "We live in Ibadan and we speak French."
- *French Breakdown:*
  - We live = *Nous habitons* (verb *habiter*)
  - in Ibadan = *à Ibadan*
  - and = *et*
  - we speak = *nous parlons* (verb *parler*)
  - French = *le français*
- *Complete Sentence:* **Nous habitons à Ibadan et nous parlons français.**

### Example 3: Negating an Activity
- *Positive:* *Chinedu écoute la musique.* (Chinedu listens to music.)
- *Negative:* *Chinedu* is singular (*il*), verb starts with a vowel $\rightarrow$ use *n'* and *pas*.
- *Negative Sentence:* **Chinedu n'écoute pas la musique.** (Chinedu does not listen to music.)

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Practice Prompt:** Ask your child to tell you three things they like (*J'aime...*) and three things they do not like (*Je n'aime pas...*) in French. Examples: *J'aime les mathématiques* (I like mathematics), *Je n'aime pas le bruit* (I don't like noise).
- **The "Silent Boot" Pronunciation Check:** Have your child read aloud: *Je parle, tu parles, il parle, ils parlent*. Verify that they pronounce all four words identically (*parl*) and do not pronounce the *-s* or *-ent*!
- **Real-World Relevance:** When travelling across West Africa, being able to say *J'habite au Nigéria* (I live in Nigeria) and *Je parle un peu français* (I speak a little French) immediately creates friendly rapport with border officials, merchants, and hotel staff.
