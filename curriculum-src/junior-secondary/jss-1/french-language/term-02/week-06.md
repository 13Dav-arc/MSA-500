## Hook
If you have ever thought that mathematics and languages live on two separate planets, prepare to be astonished! When you count from 31 to 100 in French, you do not just memorize new vocabulary words—you unlock an ancient, fascinating mathematical puzzle! While numbers 30, 40, 50, and 60 follow predictable Latin patterns, the moment you reach 70, French switches gears and says: *"sixty-ten"* (**soixante-dix**, $60 + 10 = 70$)! When you reach 80, it switches to a Celtic base-20 multiplication system: *"four-twenties"* (**quatre-vingts**, $4 \times 20 = 80$)! And for 90, it combines multiplication and addition into one magnificent formula: *"four-twenties-ten"* (**quatre-vingt-dix**, $4 \times 20 + 10 = 90$)! Once you understand these brilliant mathematical gears, counting money, prices in West African CFA francs, and reading phone numbers in French becomes pure, joyful fun! In this lesson, you will conquer numbers 31 to 100, master French arithmetic counting logic, and celebrate reaching the magic number 100: **Cent**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Trente
- Definition: The number thirty (30).
- Term: Quarante
- Definition: The number forty (40).
- Term: Cinquante
- Definition: The number fifty (50).
- Term: Soixante
- Definition: The number sixty (60), pronounced *swah-sont* (the 'x' sounds like 'ss').
- Term: Soixante-dix
- Definition: The number seventy (70), mathematically formed as $60 + 10$.
- Term: Quatre-vingts
- Definition: The number eighty (80), mathematically formed as $4 \times 20$ (features a trailing '-s' when standing alone).
- Term: Quatre-vingt-dix
- Definition: The number ninety (90), mathematically formed as $4 \times 20 + 10$.
- Term: Cent
- Definition: The number one hundred (100), pronounced *son*.
- Term: Trente et un
- Definition: The number thirty-one (31), featuring the conjunction *et* without hyphens.
- Term: Soixante et onze
- Definition: The number seventy-one (71), formed as $60 + 11$ with the conjunction *et*.
- Term: Quatre-vingt-un
- Definition: The number eighty-one (81), formed as $4 \times 20 + 1$ (note: NO *et*, hyphenated, and the '-s' on *vingt* is dropped!).
- Term: Le Système Vigésimal
- Definition: The ancient base-20 counting system inherited from the Celts that explains why 80 is expressed as "four twenties" (*quatre-vingts*).

## Core Concept
### 1. Counting the Tens: From 30 to 60
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Les Nombres de 31 à 100" caption="Les Nombres de 31 à 100: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

The tens from 30 to 60 follow regular, single-word patterns:
- **30:** *trente*
- **40:** *quarante*
- **50:** *cinquante*
- **60:** *soixante* (the 'x' is pronounced like 'ss': *swah-sont*).

#### The Rule of "Et Un" (31, 41, 51, 61):
For numbers ending in "1", French uses the word **et** (and) with **NO hyphens**:
- **31:** *trente et un*
- **41:** *quarante et un*
- **51:** *cinquante et un*
- **61:** *soixante et un*.

#### Numbers from 2 to 9:
All other numbers from 2 to 9 are joined by a simple **hyphen** without *et*:
- *trente-deux* (32), *quarante-cinq* (45), *cinquante-huit* (58), *soixante-six* (66).

### 2. The 70 Family: *Soixante-dix* ($60 + 10$)
In standard French, 70 is not a new word; it continues counting directly upward from 60:
$$\mathbf{70} = \text{soixante} + \text{dix} \quad (60 + 10)$$

### 3. The 80 Family: *Quatre-vingts* ($4 \times 20$)
The French word for 80 reflects a historic Celtic base-20 counting system:
$$\mathbf{80} = \text{quatre-vingts} \quad (4 \times 20)$$
- **The Rule of the 'S' on Vingt:**
  - When 80 stands alone, *vingts* takes an **-s**: **quatre-vingts**.
  - As soon as another digit follows (81 to 99), the '-s' is **DROPPED**: **quatre-vingt-un**, **quatre-vingt-deux**!
- **No "Et" in 81:** Unlike 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, and 71, the number 81 does NOT use *et*. It is hyphenated directly: **quatre-vingt-un**!
  - 82: *quatre-vingt-deux*
  - 85: *quatre-vingt-cinq*
  - 89: *quatre-vingt-neuf*.

---

### 4. The 90 Family: *Quatre-vingt-dix* ($4 \times 20 + 10$)
Just as 70 counts upward from 60, 90 counts upward from 80 using teen numbers:
$$\mathbf{90} = \text{quatre-vingt-dix} \quad (4 \times 20 + 10)$$
- **91:** *quatre-vingt-onze* ($4 \times 20 + 11$ - notice NO *et*!)
- **92:** *quatre-vingt-douze* ($4 \times 20 + 12$)
- **93:** *quatre-vingt-treize* ($4 \times 20 + 13$)
- **94:** *quatre-vingt-quatorze* ($4 \times 20 + 14$)
- **95:** *quatre-vingt-quinze* ($4 \times 20 + 15$)
- **96:** *quatre-vingt-seize* ($4 \times 20 + 16$)
- **97:** *quatre-vingt-dix-sept* ($4 \times 20 + 17$)
- **98:** *quatre-vingt-dix-huit* ($4 \times 20 + 18$)
- **99:** *quatre-vingt-dix-neuf* ($4 \times 20 + 19$).

---

### 5. The Number 100: *Cent*
The number one hundred is simply:
$$\mathbf{100} = \mathbf{cent} \quad (\text{pronounced } *son*)$$
- *Cent* does not take an article: 100 students is *cent élèves* (not "un cent élèves").

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **70** | *soixante-dix* | $60 + 10$ | Hyphenated compound |
| **71** | *soixante et onze* | $60 + 11$ | Uses conjunction **et**, no hyphen |
| **80** | *quatre-vingts* | $4 \times 20$ | Has trailing **-s** on *vingts* |
| **81** | *quatre-vingt-un* | $4 \times 20 + 1$ | Drops '-s', NO *et*, hyphenated |

| **100** | *cent* | 100 | Single root word |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Keeping the '-S' in 81 or 82:** Writing *quatre-vingts-un*. You must drop the '-s' as soon as another number follows (**quatre-vingt-un**).
2. **Writing "Quatre-vingt et un":** Adding *et* to 81. Only 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, and 71 use *et*; 81 is strictly **quatre-vingt-un**.
3. **Saying "Septante" or "Nonante":** Using regional Belgian/Swiss French terms. The Nigerian NERDC curriculum and standard international French strictly test **soixante-dix** (70) and **quatre-vingt-dix** (90).
4. **Writing "Un cent" for 100:** Writing *un cent*. In French, 100 is simply **cent**.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Solving Mathematical Number Equations
*Problem:* Translate each arithmetic operation into French numerals:
1. $30 + 10 = 40 \rightarrow$ **quarante**
2. $50 + 20 = 70 \rightarrow$ **soixante-dix** ($60 + 10$)
3. $4 \times 20 = 80 \rightarrow$ **quatre-vingts**
4. $80 + 15 = 95 \rightarrow$ **quatre-vingt-quinze** ($4 \times 20 + 15$)
5. $50 + 50 = 100 \rightarrow$ **cent**

### Example 2: Transcribing Prices in West African CFA
*Scenario:* A trader in Cotonou quotes prices for items:
- Price A: 75 francs $\rightarrow$ **soixante-quinze francs** ($60 + 15$)
- Price B: 84 francs $\rightarrow$ **quatre-vingt-quatre francs** ($4 \times 20 + 4$)
- Price C: 99 francs $\rightarrow$ **quatre-vingt-dix-neuf francs** ($4 \times 20 + 19$)
