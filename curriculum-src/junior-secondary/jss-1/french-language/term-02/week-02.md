## Hook
Look around your school assembly ground or classroom. You see people of all shapes, sizes, and personalities! Perhaps your deskmate is tall and athletic, while your friend in the front row is short, energetic, and wears glasses. Your English teacher might be patient and smiling, while your coach is energetic and demanding. How do you describe these wonderful traits in French? In English, adjectives are completely rigid: whether you talk about a tall boy, a tall girl, or ten tall men, the word "tall" never changes. But in French, adjectives are like dynamic dancers—they change their clothing to match the person they describe! A boy is **grand**, but a girl is **grande**! A hardworking boy is **travailleur**, but a hardworking girl is **travailleuse**! This fascinating grammar rule is called **L'Accord des Adjectifs** (Adjective Agreement). In this lesson, you will master physical and moral adjectives, conquer gender and plural agreement rules, and paint vivid word portraits of people in French!

## Key Terms
- Term: La Description Physique
- Definition: Physical description; describing a person's height, build, age, and appearance.
- Term: La Description Morale
- Definition: Moral or psychological description; describing a person's character, personality, work ethic, and intelligence.
- Term: L'Accord des Adjectifs
- Definition: The fundamental grammatical rule requiring adjectives to agree in both gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural) with the noun they qualify.
- Term: Grand / Grande
- Definition: Tall or big; *grand* for masculine and *grande* for feminine.
- Term: Petit / Petite
- Definition: Short or small; *petit* for masculine and *petite* for feminine.
- Term: Gros / Grosse
- Definition: Fat, heavy, or stout; *gros* for masculine and *grosse* for feminine (doubling the 's').
- Term: Mince
- Definition: Slim or thin; invariable in the singular because it already ends in an unaccented 'e'.
- Term: Beau / Belle
- Definition: Handsome / beautiful; *beau* for masculine and *belle* for feminine.
- Term: Intelligent / Intelligente
- Definition: Intelligent or clever; *intelligent* for masculine and *intelligente* for feminine.
- Term: Travailleur / Travailleuse
- Definition: Hardworking; *travailleur* for masculine and *travailleuse* for feminine.
- Term: Gentil / Gentille
- Definition: Kind, sweet, or nice; *gentil* for masculine and *gentille* for feminine.
- Term: Jeune / Vieux (Vieille)
- Definition: Young (*jeune*) and Old (*vieux* for masculine, *vieille* for feminine).

### 3. Rules for Forming the Feminine of Adjectives
To convert a masculine adjective to its feminine form, follow these five golden rules:
1. **The Universal Rule (Add '-e'):**
   - Simply add an unaccented **-e** to the masculine singular form:
     - *grand* $\rightarrow$ **grande**
     - *petit* $\rightarrow$ **petite**
     - *intelligent* $\rightarrow$ **intelligente**
     - *poli* $\rightarrow$ **polie**.
   - *Phonetic Change:* Adding '-e' makes the final consonant (which was previously silent in *grand* or *petit*) voiced! You pronounce the 'd' in *grande* (*gron-duh*) and the 't' in *petite* (*puh-teet*).
2. **Invariable Adjectives (Already ending in '-e'):**
   - If an adjective already ends with an unaccented **-e** in the masculine, **do not add another 'e'**! It remains identical in both genders:
     - *mince* (masc.) $\rightarrow$ **mince** (fem.)
     - *jeune* (masc.) $\rightarrow$ **jeune** (fem.)
     - *timide* (masc.) $\rightarrow$ **timide** (fem.)
     - *sympathique* (masc.) $\rightarrow$ **sympathique** (fem.).
3. **Adjectives ending in '-eur' $\rightarrow$ '-euse':**
   - *travailleur* $\rightarrow$ **travailleuse** (hardworking).
4. **Adjectives ending in '-eux' $\rightarrow$ '-euse':**
   - *paresseux* $\rightarrow$ **paresseuse** (lazy)
   - *heureux* $\rightarrow$ **heureuse** (happy).
5. **Special Irregular Feminines:**
   - *beau* $\rightarrow$ **belle**
   - *vieux* $\rightarrow$ **vieille**
   - *gros* $\rightarrow$ **grosse**
   - *gentil* $\rightarrow$ **gentille**.

### 4. Rules for Forming the Plural of Adjectives
Adjectives must also match the **number** of the noun (singular vs. plural):
- **The Standard Plural Rule:**
  - Add an **-s** to the singular form (masculine or feminine):
    - *Il est grand.* $\rightarrow$ *Ils sont grand**s**.*
    - *Elle est grande.* $\rightarrow$ *Elles sont grande**s**.*
- **The Invariable Plural Rule:**
  - Masculine singular adjectives already ending in **-s** or **-x** do not change in the plural:
    - *un garçon gros* $\rightarrow$ *des garçons gros* (no change!)
    - *un homme vieux* $\rightarrow$ *des hommes vieux* (no change!).

## Common Mistakes
1. **Omitting the Feminine '-E':** Saying *"Elle est intelligent"* or *"Ma mère est petit"*. You must always apply feminine agreement: **Elle est intelligente**, **Ma mère est petite**.
2. **Adding an Extra 'E' to Words Ending in 'E':** Writing *mincee* or *jeunee*. Adjectives already ending in 'e' remain unchanged (*mince, jeune*).
3. **Using "Beau" for a Female:** Saying *"Ma sœur est beau"*. You must use the feminine form **belle** (*Ma sœur est belle*).
4. **Pronouncing Plural '-S':** Pronouncing the trailing 's' in *grands* or *grandes*. Trailing '-s' is completely silent!

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Creating Sentence Descriptions
*Problem:* Combine the subject, the verb *Être*, and the adjective in brackets with proper agreement:
1. Ali (être / grand) $\rightarrow$ Ali **est grand**.
2. Amina (être / petit) $\rightarrow$ Amina **est petite**.
3. Les filles (être / intelligent) $\rightarrow$ Les filles **sont intelligentes**.
4. Madame Adeleke (être / travailleur) $\rightarrow$ Madame Adeleke **est travailleuse**.

### Example 2: Describing Best Friends
*Scenario:* A student describes two friends in French class:
- *"Voici mon ami, Chidi. Il est grand, mince et très travailleur."* (Here is my friend Chidi. He is tall, slim, and very hardworking.)
- *"Voici mon amie, Blessing. Elle est petite, belle et très gentille."* (Here is my friend Blessing. She is short, beautiful, and very kind.)
