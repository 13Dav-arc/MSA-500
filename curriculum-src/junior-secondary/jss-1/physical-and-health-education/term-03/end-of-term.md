## Key Terms
- Term: Balanced Diet
- Definition: A meal containing all six essential classes of food in adequate and proper proportions tailored to meet an individual's specific physiological needs based on age, sex, health, and activity.
- Term: Kwashiorkor
- Definition: A severe form of protein-energy malnutrition in children characterized by fluid retention (edema) causing a swollen, bloated belly, swollen limbs, and discolored reddish hair.
- Term: Marasmus
- Definition: Total caloric and protein starvation resulting in extreme bodily emaciation, visible skeletal ribs, and a wrinkled "old person's" facial appearance.
- Term: Pathogen (Germ)
- Definition: Any disease-causing biological microorganism or agent—including bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and parasitic worms.
- Term: Communicable Disease
- Definition: An infectious illness caused by specific pathogenic microorganisms that can be transmitted directly or indirectly from an infected host, animal, or environment to a susceptible person.
- Term: Non-Communicable Disease (NCD)
- Definition: A chronic non-infectious medical condition (such as sickle cell anemia, asthma, hypertension, or diabetes) that cannot be caught or transmitted from one individual to another.
- Term: Sickle Cell Anemia
- Definition: An inherited genetic blood disorder where abnormal hemoglobin causes red blood cells to become rigid and crescent-shaped, blocking capillaries and causing painful bone crises.
- Term: Myocarditis
- Definition: An acute, potentially fatal inflammation of the heart muscle triggered by viral or bacterial infections that can cause sudden cardiac arrest during athletic exertion.
- Term: Sewage
- Definition: Liquid wastewater carrying human excreta (feces and urine) and domestic sullage transported through pipes and drains.
- Term: Refuse
- Definition: Dry or semi-solid discarded waste materials generated from households, markets, and schools (such as food scraps, plastics, paper, and metal cans).
- Term: Incineration
- Definition: The controlled burning of combustible solid waste materials at high temperatures inside an engineered furnace, mandatory for sterilizing hospital clinical waste.
- Term: Immunization
- Definition: The artificial stimulation of the body's immune system using vaccines to produce protective antibodies that provide lifelong or long-term defense against deadly diseases.

### Key Review Pillars for the Sessional Examination
1. **Food Groups & The Six Classes of Food (Weeks 01–02):**
   - 5 Basic Food Groups: Milk/Dairy (calcium), Meat/Legumes (protein), Fruits/Vegetables (vitamins & fiber), Cereals (energy), Tubers (starch fuel).
   - 6 Classes: Carbohydrates ($17\text{ kJ/g}$), Proteins (amino acids for building/repair), Fats/Oils ($37\text{ kJ/g}$, insulation/cushioning), Minerals ($Ca, Fe, I$), Vitamins (Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K; Water-soluble: B-complex, C), Water (65% body weight, thermoregulation/sweat).
2. **Balanced Diet & Deficiency Diseases (Week 03):**
   - Balanced diet: proportional intake tailored to age, sex, activity, and health status.
   - Kwashiorkor: Protein lack $\rightarrow$ fluid edema, bloated belly, reddish hair.
   - Marasmus: Total starvation $\rightarrow$ extreme emaciation, visible skeleton, wrinkled "old person" face.
   - Rickets: Vitamin D & Calcium lack $\rightarrow$ soft bones, bow legs, knock knees.
   - Scurvy: Vitamin C lack $\rightarrow$ bleeding spongy gums, loose teeth, poor healing.
   - Goitre: Iodine lack $\rightarrow$ enlarged thyroid gland in the neck. Prevented by iodized table salt.
   - Nutritional Anemia: Iron lack $\rightarrow$ pale eyelids, chronic fatigue, breathlessness.
   - Athletic nutrition: High carbohydrate pre-event meals (3–4 hrs before), continuous hydration, post-workout recovery.
3. **Pathogens & Communicable Diseases (Weeks 04 & 06):**
   - Microbes: Bacteria (prokaryotes; antibiotics effective), Viruses (submicroscopic; vaccines effective, antibiotics useless!), Fungi (spores; ringworm/athlete's foot), Protozoa (*Plasmodium* causing malaria via female *Anopheles* mosquito), Worms (helminths).
   - 5 Transmission Modes: Air-borne (TB, flu droplets), Water-borne (cholera, typhoid), Food-borne (houseflies, contamination), Insect-borne (malaria, yellow fever), Contact/Fomites (ringworm, scabies).
   - Specific diseases: Typhoid (*Salmonella typhi*), Cholera (*Vibrio cholerae*), TB (*Mycobacterium tuberculosis*), Measles (Morbillivirus), Malaria (*Plasmodium*).
   - Severe athletic impacts: Muscle wasting (atrophy), blunted coordination, dehydration shock, fatal sudden cardiac death from **myocarditis**!
4. **Non-Communicable Diseases (Week 07):**
   - 100% non-infectious; cannot be caught from others.
   - Sickle Cell Anemia: Inherited genetic mutation (*SS* genotype); rigid crescent cells block microvessels causing excruciating crises. Premarital counseling ($AS \times AS \rightarrow 25\%\text{ SS}$).
   - Asthma: Airway spasms with musical wheezing; managed by bronchodilator inhalers.
   - Hypertension ($\ge 140/90\text{ mmHg}$) and Type 2 Diabetes (insulin failure): Driven by salt, sugar, obesity, and sedentary living. Managed by daily aerobic physical exercise!
5. **Sewage & Refuse Disposal (Week 08):**
   - Sewage (liquid wastewater/excreta) vs Refuse (solid dry waste).
   - Disposal methods: Water carriage system (WC with S-trap water seal & septic tank), VIP latrine (vent pipe with fly screen; sited $\ge 15–30\text{m}$ from wells), Composting (organic manure), Incineration ($>850^\circ\text{C}$ furnace for hospital waste), Sanitary Landfill (compacted trenches with daily soil cover).
   - Hazards: Leachate groundwater poisoning, blocked gutters causing floods, vector breeding (rats transmitting Lassa fever, flies transmitting cholera).
6. **Personal Cleanliness & Community Health (Week 09):**
   - Organ care: Skin (daily bathing, drying folds), Teeth (brushing 2x daily, morning and before bed with fluoride paste to stop caries), Eyes (clean water, no rubbing to stop Apollo), Hair (washing, no shared combs to stop lice), Nails (trimmed short to stop worm eggs).
   - Immunization: Artificial antibody stimulation via vaccines (BCG at birth for TB, OPV for polio, Measles at 9 months, Pentavalent).
   - Community sanitation: Clearing drains, cutting elephant grass, monthly sanitation.

## Common Mistakes

| Category | Common Student Trap | Verified Examination Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Malnutrition** | Believing a child with a swollen pot-belly is well-fed. | Bloated belly in Kwashiorkor is pathological fluid retention (**edema**), not healthy body fat! |
| **Pharmaceuticals** | Prescribing antibiotics to cure the Common Cold or Measles. | Antibiotics strictly destroy **Bacteria**; they have zero chemical action against **Viruses**! |
| **Sickle Cell** | Believing you can "catch" Sickle Cell Disease from a schoolmate. | Sickle cell is strictly an **inherited genetic condition**; it is 100% impossible to catch! |
| **Waste Engineering**| Digging a pit latrine right next to a drinking water borehole. | Latrines must be sited at least **15 to 30 meters** away and downhill from drinking water sources. |
| **Personal Hygiene** | Believing body sprays replace bathing with toilet soap and water. | Perfume only masks odor; soap and water is mandatory to remove bacteria, sebum, and stale sweat. |
| **Sports with Fever** | Letting an athlete with an active fever play in a tournament. | Fever with exercise risks acute **myocarditis** (heart muscle inflammation) and sudden cardiac death! |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Comprehensive Multi-Disciplinary Public Health Emergency
- **Context:** An environmental health team inspects an urban community and discovers:
  1. A municipal drinking water well located 4 meters away from a leaking open pit latrine.
  2. Several toddlers exhibiting bloated pot-bellies and thin reddish hair.
  3. A local clinic throwing used bloody surgical dressings and needles into an open roadside ditch.
- **Problem:** Analyze and deliver official public health directives for all three hazards.
- **Analysis 1:** Drinking well proximity: The latrine is illegally close ($<15–30\text{m}$). The well is heavily contaminated with fecal pathogens, posing an imminent **Cholera and Typhoid** threat. Condemn the well immediately; provide clean chlorinated water.
- **Analysis 2:** Childhood symptoms: Bloated bellies and reddish hair indicate **Kwashiorkor**. Distribute therapeutic high-protein foods (milk, eggs, soya bean flour).
- **Analysis 3:** Medical waste: Dumping biohazardous clinical waste in open ditches risks hepatitis and HIV transmission. Mandate immediate high-temperature **Incineration**.
- **Verdict:** Strict enforcement of public health engineering and nutritional therapy halts disease outbreaks!

### Scenario 2: Formulating a Sessional Health and Physical Mastery Blueprint
- **Context:** JSS 1 student David wants to ensure he maintains peak athletic performance, disease resistance, and academic alertness throughout his secondary school years.
- **Problem:** Formulate a weekly holistic health blueprint based on JSS 1 PHE curriculum principles.
- **Step 1 (Nutrition):** Consume a balanced diet daily featuring all five basic food groups, emphasizing clean drinking water and iodized salt; avoid skipping breakfast.
- **Step 2 (Physical Activity):** Engage in at least 60 minutes of daily physical exercise (athletics, gymnastics, basketball, football, or swimming) to strengthen cardiac muscle and prevent obesity.
- **Step 3 (Hygiene & Immunity):** Bathe twice daily with soap; brush teeth with fluoride paste morning and night before bed; ensure all routine NPI immunizations are up to date.
- **Step 4 (Safety & Sanitation):** Follow the R.I.C.E. protocol for sprains; use pedestrian overpasses and zebra crossings; keep domestic surroundings free of stagnant water.
- **Verdict:** Integrating nutrition, physical conditioning, hygiene, and safety guarantees lifelong vitality!
