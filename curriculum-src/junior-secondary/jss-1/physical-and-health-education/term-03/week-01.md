## Hook
Picture waking up early on a brisk Monday morning to the mouth-watering sizzle of golden bean cakes (*akara*) and fried plantains (*dodo*) drifting from the kitchen, accompanied by a steaming bowl of maize pap (*ogi*) with milk! Or think of the energy rush that powers your legs across the soccer field after a hearty afternoon plate of jollof rice and grilled fish! Have you ever wondered why your stomach growls like a tiny thundercloud when you are hungry? Your body is an incredible biological machine, and just like a motor vehicle cannot run without petrol, your muscles, heart, and brain cannot sprint, leap, or solve mathematics equations without clean biological fuel! Welcome to the life-giving science of **Food and Nutrition**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Food
- Definition: Any nourishing substance—solid or liquid—which when ingested, digested, and absorbed by a living organism provides energy, promotes growth and tissue repair, regulates bodily functions, and resists disease.
- Term: Nutrition
- Definition: The scientific process by which a living organism takes in, digests, absorbs, transports, and utilizes food substances to support metabolic life processes.
- Term: Nutrients
- Definition: Chemical components present within food (such as carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals) that the body requires to function, grow, and survive.
- Term: Basic Food Groups
- Definition: A classification system that organizes diverse dietary foods into five major categories based on their primary biological functions and nutrient profiles.
- Term: Tuber Group
- Definition: Root crops rich in dense starchy carbohydrates that serve as primary dietary staples in Nigeria (such as yam, cassava, sweet potatoes, and cocoyam).
- Term: Bread and Cereal Group
- Definition: Grains and grain-derived flour foods that provide accessible, long-lasting energy for physical activities (such as rice, maize, millet, sorghum, wheat, and semovita).
- Term: Meat and Protein Group
- Definition: Animal flesh, poultry, seafood, eggs, legumes, and oilseeds that supply building blocks for muscle growth and cellular repair (such as beef, fish, beans, and soya).
- Term: Milk and Dairy Group
- Definition: Animal milk and its cultured derivatives (such as yogurt, cheese, and wara) that supply high-quality protein, calcium, and bone-strengthening minerals.
- Term: Fruit and Vegetable Group
- Definition: Plant parts (such as citrus, tomatoes, watermelons, spinach, bitterleaf, and fluted pumpkin / ugwu) that deliver essential vitamins, mineral salts, and dietary fiber.
- Term: Metabolism
- Definition: The sum of all chemical reactions and biochemical transformations occurring within living cells that convert nutrients into cellular energy and tissue mass.

## Core Concept
### What is Food and Why is it Essential?
Food is any substance taken into the body of a living organism capable of sustaining life, maintaining growth, building tissues, and regulating biological mechanisms.

1. **Eliminating Hunger:** Hunger is the brain's alarm bell signaling that blood glucose has dropped and cells require replenishment.
2. **Fueling Physical Activity:** Every muscle contraction during sprinting, jumping, swimming, or studying demands chemical energy derived from digested food.
3. **Promoting Growth and Tissue Repair:** From infancy through secondary school, food protein builds new cells, elongates bones, and knits healing skin over grazed knees.
4. **Resisting and Overcoming Infections:** Vitamins and minerals stimulate immune antibodies, protecting against microbial pathogens.
5. **Facilitating Metabolic Regulation:** Enzymes and hormones that govern heartbeat, digestion, and temperature control are synthesized from ingested nutrients.

#### 1. Milk and Dairy Group:
- *Sources:* Fresh cow milk, condensed/evaporated milk, powdered milk, cultured yogurt, local curd cheese (*Wara*).
- *Primary Nutritional Role:* Supplies complete biological proteins, Vitamin D, and rich **Calcium** required for building dense bones and teeth in growing adolescents.

#### 2. Meat, Poultry, Fish, and Legume Group:
- *Sources:* Beef, goat meat, chicken, turkey, fresh fish, dried catfish, crayfish, eggs, brown beans, cowpeas, groundnuts, soya beans.
- *Primary Nutritional Role:* Major supplier of **Proteins** and dietary Iron. Essential for building skeletal muscle mass and repairing athletic wear and tear.

#### 3. Fruit and Vegetable Group:
- *Sources:* Oranges, grapefruits, mangoes, pineapples, pawpaws, watermelon, tomatoes, garden eggs, carrots, fluted pumpkin leaves (*Ugwu*), waterleaf, spinach, bitterleaf, okra.
- *Primary Nutritional Role:* Major supplier of **Vitamins A, C, and K**, mineral salts, and indigestible plant fiber (**roughage**) that stimulates bowel peristalsis and prevents constipation.

#### 4. Bread and Cereal Group:
- *Sources:* Parboiled rice, local ofada rice, maize, cornmeal, guinea corn (sorghum), millet, wheat flour, bread, semovita, oats, biscuits.
- *Primary Nutritional Role:* Provides steady, digestable **complex carbohydrates** and B-vitamins that fuel endurance sports and daily academic concentration.

#### 5. Tuber and Root Group:
- *Sources:* White yam, water yam, cassava (processed into garri, fufu, and lafun), sweet potatoes, cocoyam, cassava flour (*elubo*).
- *Primary Nutritional Role:* Energy-dense staple carbohydrates that release glucose steadily to sustain strenuous manual work and athletic conditioning.

## Common Mistakes

| Common Student Mistake | Why It Occurs | Technical Nutritional Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Assuming only expensive imported foods are nutritious** | Misleading commercial advertising and prestige bias. | Locally cultivated Nigerian foods (beans, ugwu, garri, groundnuts, crayfish) possess equal or superior nutritional value! |
| **Equating "eating until full" with "proper nutrition"** | Consuming large mountains of plain cassava without vegetables or protein. | Stomach fullness is not nutrition; the body requires diverse nutrients from all basic food groups to thrive. |
| **Skipping breakfast before school sports** | Believing an empty stomach makes an athlete lighter and faster. | Dangerous! Skipping breakfast depletes blood sugar (hypoglycemia), causing dizziness, nausea, and fainting on the field. |
| **Thinking water is not an essential food component** | Water does not yield calories. | Water is the indispensable fluid medium in which all digestion, metabolic reactions, and thermoregulation take place. |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Categorizing a Traditional Nigerian Lunch into Food Groups
- **Context:** At the school dining hall, Amina is served a lunch plate comprising:
  - Pounded Yam
  - Egusi Soup prepared with chopped *Ugwu* (pumpkin) leaves and palm oil
  - Fresh Fish and boiled cow meat
  - Slices of ripe sweet Orange
  - A clean glass of drinking water
- **Problem:** Identify and verify which basic food groups are represented in Amina's meal.
- **Step 1:** Pounded Yam represents the **Tuber Group** (energy-dense starch).
- **Step 2:** Fresh Fish, boiled meat, and Egusi (melon seed protein) represent the **Meat and Legume Group** (protein for tissue repair).
- **Step 3:** *Ugwu* vegetable leaves and the fresh Orange slices represent the **Fruit and Vegetable Group** (protective vitamins, iron, and roughage).
- **Verdict:** Amina’s lunch combines three major local food groups, creating a wholesome, nutrient-rich meal for an active student!

### Scenario 2: Correcting an Athlete's Monotonous Diet
- **Context:** Biodun, an inter-house 400-meter runner, eats only white bread and sweetened black tea for breakfast, white rice with pepper sauce for lunch, and plain garri with sugar for dinner. He complains of frequent muscle fatigue and poor skin health.
- **Problem:** What critical basic food groups are missing from Biodun's daily intake?
- **Step 1:** Analyze his current intake: Bread, white rice, and garri belong strictly to the **Cereal** and **Tuber** groups (almost 100% carbohydrates).
- **Step 2:** Identify missing groups:
  - Missing the **Meat, Fish & Legume Group** (no eggs, beans, or fish for muscle repair).
  - Missing the **Milk & Dairy Group** (no calcium for bone density).
  - Missing the **Fruit & Vegetable Group** (no greens or citrus for immune vitamins).
- **Verdict:** Biodun suffers from nutrient deficiency; adding beans/fish, green leafy vegetables, and milk will eliminate his muscle fatigue!
