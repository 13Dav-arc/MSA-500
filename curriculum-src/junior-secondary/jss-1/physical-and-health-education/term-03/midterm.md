## Hook

You learned how local Nigerian staples—from golden yam and sweet potatoes to brown cowpeas, fresh fish, and leafy *ugwu*—provide the chemical fuel and building blocks that construct your body. ## Key Terms
- Term: Food
- Definition: Any nourishing solid or liquid substance which, when ingested and absorbed, maintains life, promotes growth, repairs cellular tissues, and provides energy.
- Term: Basic Food Groups
- Definition: The five practical dietary categories organizing local foods based on origin and nutritional contributions (Milk, Meat/Legumes, Fruits/Vegetables, Cereals, Tubers).
- Term: Six Classes of Food
- Definition: The fundamental biochemical nutrient classes required for life: carbohydrates, proteins, fats/oils, mineral salts, vitamins, and water.
- Term: Balanced Diet
- Definition: A meal containing all six classes of food in adequate and proper proportions tailored to an individual’s age, sex, activity level, and health status.
- Term: Malnutrition
- Definition: A state of impaired physical health resulting from a deficiency, excess, or imbalance of essential dietary nutrients.
- Term: Kwashiorkor
- Definition: Protein malnutrition in children characterized by fluid swelling (edema) causing a bloated abdomen, swollen limbs, and discolored reddish-brown hair.
- Term: Marasmus
- Definition: Total caloric and protein starvation resulting in severe emaciation, visible skeletal ribs, and a wrinkled "old person's" facial appearance.
- Term: Rickets
- Definition: Bone-softening childhood disease caused by deficiency of Vitamin D and Calcium, leading to curved bow legs and knock knees.
- Term: Pathogen
- Definition: A disease-causing biological microorganism or parasite—such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, or parasitic worms.
- Term: Communicable Disease
- Definition: An infectious illness capable of being transmitted directly or indirectly from an infected host, animal, or environmental reservoir to a healthy person.

### Core Academic Checkpoints
1. **Food Groups vs. Classes of Food:**
   - **Food Groups (5):** Practical kitchen groupings (Milk, Meat/Fish/Beans, Fruits/Vegetables, Cereals/Grains, Tubers/Roots).
   - **Classes of Food (6):** Biochemical nutrient categories:
     - *Carbohydrates ($17\text{ kJ/g}$):* Primary muscular fuel (yam, rice, garri).
     - *Proteins:* Nitrogenous amino acids for growth and tissue repair (beans, fish, meat, eggs).
     - *Fats and Oils ($37\text{ kJ/g}$):* Concentrated energy, organ cushioning, insulation (palm oil, groundnut oil).
     - *Mineral Salts:* Inorganic elements (Calcium for bones; Iron for hemoglobin; Iodine for thyroid/goitre).
     - *Vitamins:* Organic regulators (Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K; Water-soluble: B-complex, C).
     - *Water:* 65% of body weight; universal solvent and thermoregulatory coolant (sweating).
2. **Balanced Diet & Deficiency Pathologies:**
   - Kwashiorkor: Lack of protein with excess starch $\rightarrow$ bloated belly, edema, reddish hair.
   - Marasmus: Total starvation $\rightarrow$ skin and bones, extreme wasting, wrinkled face.
   - Rickets: Lack of Vitamin D and Calcium $\rightarrow$ soft bones, bow legs, knock knees.
   - Scurvy: Lack of Vitamin C $\rightarrow$ bleeding spongy gums, loose teeth, poor healing.
   - Goitre: Lack of Iodine $\rightarrow$ enlarged thyroid gland in the neck. Prevented by iodized salt.
   - Nutritional Anemia: Lack of Iron $\rightarrow$ pale eyelids, fatigue, shortness of breath.
3. **Pathogens and Modes of Transmission:**
   - **Bacteria:** Single-celled prokaryotes (Cholera, Typhoid, Tuberculosis); killed by antibiotics.
   - **Viruses:** Ultramicroscopic genetic capsules (Measles, Influenza, Polio); antibiotics have zero effect!
   - **Fungi:** Spore-producers (Ringworm, Athlete's foot); treated with antifungals.
   - **Protozoa:** Single-celled eukaryotes (*Plasmodium* causing Malaria via female *Anopheles* mosquito).
   - **Parasitic Worms:** Multicellular helminths (Roundworms, Tapeworms, Hookworms).
   - Modes: Air-borne (TB, flu droplets), Water-borne (cholera, typhoid), Food-borne (houseflies, contamination), Insect-borne (malaria mosquito), Contact/Fomites (ringworm, scabies).

## Common Mistakes

| Common Student Mistake | Why It Occurs | Technical Verified Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Confusing Food Groups with Classes of Food** | Both categorize nutrition. | **Food Groups** are practical food categories (e.g., Tubers, Meat); **Classes of Food** are the 6 chemical nutrient molecules (e.g., Carbohydrates, Proteins). |
| **Treating Viral Infections with Antibiotics** | Misunderstanding pharmaceutical action. | Antibiotics strictly destroy **Bacteria**; they have zero chemical action against **Viruses**! |
| **Believing Kwashiorkor children are fat** | The belly looks rounded and large. | The swollen abdomen is not fat; it is accumulated fluid (**edema**) caused by severe protein deficiency in the blood. |
| **Assuming clear water is safe to drink** | Visual transparency looks pure. | Water-borne pathogens like *Vibrio cholerae* are microscopic; all water should be boiled or chlorinated. |
| **Believing Ringworm is an animal parasite** | Name contains "worm". | Ringworm is a superficial **fungal** infection growing in circular skin patches. |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Identifying Deficiencies from Clinical Symptoms
- **Context:** A school medical inspection identifies two students exhibiting distinct symptoms:
  - Student X has loose teeth, painful swollen gums that bleed when chewing, and cuts that take weeks to heal.
  - Student Y has enlarged, curved leg bones that bow outward at the knees, and complains of bone pain during PE class.
- **Problem:** Diagnose the deficiency disease and missing nutrient for both students.
- **Step 1:** Analyze Student X: Bleeding spongy gums and poor wound healing are classic signs of **Scurvy**, caused by a deficiency of **Vitamin C**.
- **Step 2:** Analyze Student Y: Soft bones bending outward into bow legs are classic signs of **Rickets**, caused by a deficiency of **Vitamin D and Calcium**.
- **Verdict:** Student X has Scurvy (needs fresh citrus/tomatoes); Student Y has Rickets (needs milk, fish, and morning sunlight).

### Scenario 2: Controlling a School Water-Borne Disease Outbreak
- **Context:** Following the flooding of an open pit latrine near the school compound, multiple students fall ill with high fever, abdominal cramps, diarrhea, and vomiting.
- **Problem:** What immediate public health steps must the school administration take?
- **Step 1:** Recognize the transmission mode: Contamination of the shallow drinking water well by sewage runoff indicates a **Water-Borne** bacterial outbreak (likely Typhoid or Dysentery).
- **Step 2:** Immediate containment:
  1. Condemn and lock the contaminated water well immediately.
  2. Provide clean boiled and chlorinated drinking water for all students.
  3. Administer Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) to prevent dehydration.
  4. Isolate affected students in the school clinic for medical antibiotic treatment.
- **Verdict:** Safe water provision and proper sewage containment halt water-borne pathogen spread.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Water Purification Review:** Verify that household drinking water is safely boiled or treated with certified water purification tablets before consumption.
- **Salt Container Check:** Re-check that family cooking salt is clearly labeled **"Iodized"** to prevent iodine deficiency goitre.
- **Breakfast Protein Check:** Ensure the learner consumes an active source of protein (boiled egg, beans, milk, or fish) alongside morning energy staples before school sports.
