## Hook
Have you ever wondered what happens to that steaming plate of yam and scrambled eggs after you swallow it? Your digestive system does not see "yam" or "eggs"—it acts like a master chemical laboratory, breaking that food down into microscopic molecules that enter your bloodstream! Some molecules are burned immediately as clean high-octane fuel to power your sprinting legs; others are sent like biological bricks and mortar to repair microscopic muscle tears; while tiny mineral atoms and vitamin sparks keep your heart beating rhythmically and your immune soldiers standing guard against deadly germs. What are these six indispensable chemical building blocks of life? Welcome to the **Six Classes of Food**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Classes of Food
- Definition: The six distinct biochemical categories of nutrients that the human body requires in proper quantities to sustain metabolic life, growth, tissue repair, and athletic energy.
- Term: Carbohydrates
- Definition: Organic energy-yielding compounds composed of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen that serve as the body's primary and most accessible source of muscular fuel.
- Term: Proteins
- Definition: Complex nitrogenous organic compounds composed of amino acid chains that form the structural building blocks for body growth, tissue repair, enzymes, and hormones.
- Term: Fats and Oils (Lipids)
- Definition: Concentrated, energy-dense organic compounds that provide insulation against cold, protect delicate internal organs, and facilitate the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins.
- Term: Mineral Salts (Minerals)
- Definition: Inorganic chemical elements (such as calcium, iron, phosphorus, and iodine) required in small quantities to regulate physiological processes, form bones, and build blood.
- Term: Vitamins
- Definition: Vital organic micronutrients required in tiny amounts to regulate metabolic biochemical pathways, stimulate enzyme actions, and protect the body against infectious diseases.
- Term: Fat-Soluble Vitamins
- Definition: Vitamins (specifically A, D, E, and K) that dissolve in fats and lipids, stored in the body's liver and adipose fatty tissues for future use.
- Term: Water-Soluble Vitamins
- Definition: Vitamins (specifically the Vitamin B-Complex family and Vitamin C) that dissolve in water, cannot be stored in large quantities, and must be replenished daily in the diet.
- Term: Water
- Definition: The universal inorganic solvent ($H_2O$) composing roughly 65% of human body weight, essential for fluid balance, nutrient transport, joint lubrication, and body temperature regulation.
- Term: Amino Acids
- Definition: The individual chemical molecular subunits that link together in diverse sequences to construct protein molecules in living tissues.

### Detailed Analysis of the Six Classes

#### 1. Carbohydrates:
- *Chemical Nature:* Organic compounds of Carbon, Hydrogen, and Oxygen in a $1:2:1$ ratio ($C_xH_{2y}O_y$). Yields approximately $17\text{ kJ}$ ($4\text{ kcal}$) of energy per gram.
- *Local Nigerian Sources:* White yam, yellow garri, cassava, sweet potato, maize, millet, guinea corn (sorghum), rice, wheat bread, honey, and ripe plantain.
- *Primary Function:* Supplies glucose to power cellular respiration, brain function, and muscular contraction during sports.

#### 2. Proteins:
- *Chemical Nature:* Large molecules composed of **amino acids** containing Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen, and **Nitrogen** (often Sulfur and Phosphorus).
- *Local Nigerian Sources:* Beef, chicken, goat meat, fresh mackerel, dried catfish, crayfish, chicken eggs, brown beans, cowpeas, soya beans, groundnuts, and cheese (*wara*).
- *Primary Function:* Essential for constructing new protoplasm, linear skeletal growth, repairing damaged muscle fibers after workouts, and synthesizing digestive enzymes, hemoglobin, and antibodies.

#### 3. Fats and Oils (Lipids):
- *Chemical Nature:* Glycerol esters bound to fatty acid chains. The most concentrated energy source in human nutrition, providing **$37\text{ kJ}$ ($9\text{ kcal}$)** per gram (more than double carbohydrates!).
- *Local Nigerian Sources:* Red palm oil, groundnut oil, coconut oil, melon seed (*egusi*) oil, butter, margarine, oily fish (sardines), and pork.
- *Primary Function:* Long-term energy storage, thermal insulation under the skin (preventing heat loss in cold Harmattan mornings), shock-absorbing protective padding around kidneys and heart, and dissolving fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K).

#### 4. Mineral Salts (Inorganic Elements):
- **Calcium & Phosphorus:**
  - *Role:* Forms rigid calcium phosphate crystals that harden bones and teeth; essential for blood clotting and muscular contraction.
  - *Sources:* Milk, cheese, bone meal, dried fish with edible bones, and green leafy vegetables.
- **Iron:**
  - *Role:* Essential central atom of **hemoglobin**, the pigment inside red blood cells that transports oxygen from the lungs to working muscles during athletic running.
  - *Sources:* Beef liver, kidney, egg yolk, fluted pumpkin (*ugwu*), and spinach.
- **Iodine:**
  - *Role:* Required by the thyroid gland to produce thyroxine hormone, regulating body metabolism and mental growth; prevents swelling of the neck (**Goitre**).
  - *Sources:* Iodized table salt, seafood (crabs, sea fish).
- **Sodium, Potassium & Chloride:** Maintain osmotic fluid balance and conduct electrical nerve impulses across muscle membranes.

#### 5. Vitamins (Protective Micronutrients):
- **Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K):**
  - **Vitamin A (Retinol):** Promotes sharp night vision, healthy retina function, and clear skin. Sources: Red palm oil, liver, carrots, mangoes.
  - **Vitamin D (Calciferol):** Enables intestinal absorption of calcium for strong bones. Sources: Early morning sunlight (synthesized in skin), fish liver oil, egg yolk.
  - **Vitamin E (Tocopherol):** Acts as an antioxidant protecting cell membranes. Sources: Vegetable oils, nuts.
  - **Vitamin K (Phylloquinone):** Essential for normal blood clotting to stop bleeding from cuts. Sources: Green leafy vegetables, cabbage.
- **Water-Soluble Vitamins (B-Complex & C):**
  - **Vitamin B-Complex ($B_1, B_2, B_3, B_{12}$):** Co-enzymes facilitating cellular release of energy from carbohydrates and nerve function. Sources: Whole grains, beans, liver, yeast.
  - **Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid):** Promotes collagen formation, rapid wound healing, healthy gums, and strong capillary walls. Destroyed by prolonged cooking heat! Sources: Fresh citrus (oranges, grapefruits, limes), guavas, tomatoes, peppers.

#### 6. Water:
- *Physical Role:* Makes up 60% to 70% of total body mass. Acts as the solvent medium for all metabolic biochemistry.
- *Athletic Function:* Essential for **thermoregulation** through perspiration (sweating); when sweat evaporates from the skin during a soccer match, it dissipates excess heat and prevents fatal heatstroke. Transports nutrients, flushes metabolic wastes via the kidneys, and lubricates joint cartilage.

## Common Mistakes

| Common Student Mistake | Why It Occurs | Technical Biochemical Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Believing Vitamins and Minerals provide muscular energy** | Misleading energy tonic drinks advertised on TV. | Vitamins and Minerals contain **zero calories**; they act as biological catalysts and tools, not energy fuels! |
| **Thinking Fats are totally harmful and should be eliminated** | Confusing excess dietary fats with essential lipid needs. | Fats are essential for brain function, cell membranes, nerve myelin sheaths, and absorbing Vitamins A, D, E, and K! |
| **Overboiling vegetables in cooking pots** | Cultural cooking habits of boiling greens for hours. | Vitamin C and B-complex are heat-sensitive and water-soluble; overcooking destroys them completely! |
| **Assuming thirst is the only indicator to drink water** | Waiting until mouth is parched during football. | By the time an athlete feels dry thirst, they are already 2% dehydrated, decreasing physical performance significantly. |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Identifying the Biochemical Cause of Muscle Cramps & Fatigue
- **Context:** During an intense inter-house football match under the hot afternoon sun ($34^\circ\text{C}$), defender Chuka sweats profusely. In the 75th minute, he collapses onto the pitch clutching his calves with excruciating, locked muscle spasms (cramps).
- **Problem:** Which class of food nutrient deficiency caused Chuka’s sudden muscle cramps?
- **Step 1:** Analyze the physiological loss: Excessive sweating depletes large quantities of body **Water** and essential **Mineral Salts** (electrolytes: Sodium, Potassium, Chloride, and Magnesium).
- **Step 2:** Loss of water and electrolytes disrupts the electrical transmission across muscle membranes, causing involuntary locked contractions.
- **Verdict:** Chuka suffered from acute dehydration and mineral electrolyte depletion. Administering oral rehydration solution (water with a pinch of salt and sugar) and stretching relieves the spasm immediately.

### Scenario 2: Selecting Proper Dietary Recovery for a Healing Wound
- **Context:** A track runner scrapes his knee on the cinder track, leaving a broad open abrasion. The school nurse advises him to adjust his diet to accelerate skin re-growth and healing.
- **Problem:** Which two classes of food must be prioritized to rapidly heal the skin wound?
- **Step 1:** Skin tissue regeneration requires biological building blocks to construct new cellular protoplasm $\rightarrow$ **Proteins** (eggs, fish, beans).
- **Step 2:** Collagen connective tissue synthesis and capillary blood vessel rebuilding require ascorbic acid $\rightarrow$ **Vitamin C** (fresh oranges, tomatoes, guavas).
- **Verdict:** Increasing intake of Proteins and Vitamin C accelerates cellular wound closure and prevents scar tissue breakdown.

## Guardian Sync
- **Meal Nutrient Inspection:** During dinner, have the student identify which ingredients on the table supply:
  1. Carbohydrate energy (e.g., rice, yam)
  2. Protein building blocks (e.g., fish, beans)
  3. Protective vitamins and minerals (e.g., vegetables, pepper sauce)
  4. Hydration (clean drinking water)
- **Vitamin C Preservation Drill:** Discuss with the cook or family members why green vegetables should be added near the end of cooking and steamed briefly rather than boiled until brown to protect heat-sensitive Vitamin C.
- **Daily Hydration Goal:** Provide the learner with a dedicated 1-liter reusable water bottle and encourage drinking at least 2 liters of clean water daily.

Stem: How many recognized classes of food are required in human nutrition for complete health and vitality?
A) Six classes of food (correct)
B) Three classes of food
C) Twelve classes of food
D) Two classes of food
Feedback: Human nutrition recognizes exactly six classes of food (Carbohydrates, Proteins, Fats, Minerals, Vitamins, Water).

### Q2 [single-choice]
Stem: Which class of food serves as the body's primary, most easily accessible chemical source of muscular energy?
A) Carbohydrates (correct)
B) Proteins
C) Minerals
D) Water
Feedback: Carbohydrates provide glucose, the body's primary energy currency for physical activities.

### Q3 [single-choice]
Stem: Which class of food contains nitrogen and is built from amino acid chains to construct new body tissues and repair worn-out cells?
A) Proteins (correct)
B) Carbohydrates
C) Fats and oils
D) Mineral salts
Feedback: Proteins are nitrogenous organic compounds built of amino acids that build and repair tissues.

### Q4 [single-choice]
Stem: Which class of food provides the highest concentration of energy per gram (approximately $37\text{ kJ}$ or $9\text{ kcal}$ per gram)?
A) Fats and Oils (Lipids) (correct)
B) Carbohydrates
C) Proteins
D) Vitamins
Feedback: Fats and oils supply more than double the energy density of carbohydrates and proteins per gram.

Stem: Which mineral salt is the central component of hemoglobin in red blood cells required for transporting oxygen to working muscles?
A) Iron (correct)
B) Calcium
C) Iodine
