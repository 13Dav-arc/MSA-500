## Hook
Over the past five weeks of your second term in Junior Secondary School, you have pushed the boundaries of what your body can achieve across earth, air, and water! On the gym mats, you mastered the rolling acrobatics and inverted balances of floor gymnastics, and conquered fear to soar over the vaulting box and balance high on the narrow beam. In the martial arts dojo, you bowed with respect, learned to yield like water in Judo to throw opponents twice your size, and discovered the life-saving science of *Ukemi* breakfalls. In the swimming pool, you unlocked the physics of buoyancy, turned your body into a living boat with the back float, and carved through the water with the front crawl and breaststroke. Now, it is time to prove your integrated knowledge in this **Second Term Midterm Continuous Assessment Review**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Gymnastics
- Definition: A system of standardized physical exercises developing strength, flexibility, balance, and spatial body control, executed on floor mats or elevated apparatus.
- Term: Forward Roll
- Definition: A fundamental floor stunt where the gymnast tucks the chin tightly to the chest and rolls smoothly along the curved spine onto the feet.
- Term: Tripod Base
- Definition: The three-point geometric foundation formed by placing the hairline and two palms in an equilateral triangle on the mat to support a Headstand.
- Term: Vaulting Box
- Definition: A tiered wooden gymnastic structure with a padded leather top over which gymnasts leap using the explosive rebound of a springboard.
- Term: Balance Beam
- Definition: A narrow wooden beam measuring 10 cm (4 inches) wide elevated on upright posts used for balancing, turns, and dismounts.
- Term: Judo
- Definition: A Japanese Olympic grappling martial art founded in 1882 by Dr. Jigoro Kano, translating as "The Gentle Way", emphasizing throws, pins, and yielding force.
- Term: Karate
- Definition: A striking martial art developed in Okinawa, Japan, translating as "The Empty Hand", emphasizing punches, kicks, blocks, and forms (*Kata*).
- Term: Ukemi
- Definition: Japanese breakfall techniques designed to dissipate landing impact force across a broad surface area, protecting the skull, neck, and joints.
- Term: Buoyancy
- Definition: The upward hydrostatic force exerted by water that enables human bodies with air-filled lungs to float on the surface.
- Term: Back Float
- Definition: A primary aquatic survival float where the swimmer lies flat on their back with submerged ears and face clear of water, breathing normally with minimal energy expenditure.

### Key Review Pillars
1. **Floor Gymnastics & Safety:**
   - Always tuck the chin to the chest during rolling stunts (never land on the top/crown of the head).
   - Form an equilateral triangle (Tripod base: hairline + two hands) for stable headstands.
   - Spotters provide physical guidance and protect performers from falling.
2. **Apparatus Gymnastics:**
   - Approach run $\rightarrow$ double-foot springboard punch $\rightarrow$ straight-arm hand placement on box $\rightarrow$ clearance (squat/straddle) $\rightarrow$ shock-absorbing landing on thick mats with bent knees ("sticking the landing").
   - Balance beam: 10 cm wide; look forward at the end of the beam to preserve balance.
   - Parallel bars: Swings, support walks, and dips.
3. **Martial Arts (Judo & Karate):**
   - Judo (Jigoro Kano, 1882): "The Gentle Way", uses leverage, *Ukemi* breakfalls (slapping mat at $35^\circ$, tucking chin), *Ippon* (full point victory).
   - Karate (Gichin Funakoshi): "Empty Hand", striking (punches, kicks, blocks), *Kata* (forms), *Kumite* (sparring).
   - Universal ethics: Respect, courtesy (*Rei* / bowing), belt ranking (White to Black), zero street bullying.
4. **Aquatics & Swimming:**
   - Safety rules: Buddy system, never dive in shallow water, wait 45–60 min after meals, obey lifeguards.
   - Buoyancy: Lungs full of air create upward lift.
   - Four floats: Back float (best survival float; ears submerged, chin up), Mushroom float, Jellyfish float, Prone float.
   - Freestyle (Front crawl): Fastest stroke, alternating arms, flutter kick from hips, side breathing.
   - Breaststroke: Energy-conserving, symmetrical arm pull, whip/frog kick.

## Common Mistakes

| Category | Common Student Error | Verified Correction Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headstands** | Placing head and hands in a straight line. | Must form a 3-point equilateral triangle (tripod base) with forehead ahead of palms. |
| **Vaulting** | Punching the springboard with only one foot. | Springboard take-off strictly requires punching with **both feet simultaneously**. |
| **Judo Falling** | Reaching back with a stiff arm or letting the head bounce. | Tuck chin tightly to chest and slap the tatami with palm and forearm at $35^\circ$. |
| **Swimming Breathing** | Lifting the entire head straight forward in front crawl. | Turn head smoothly to the side into the shoulder wave trough; keeping eyes down maintains hip elevation. |
| **Back Float Posture**| Tilting the head forward to look at the toes. | Submerge ears and tilt chin upward toward the sky; looking forward sinks the hips. |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Diagnosing an Aquatic Emergency
- **Context:** While swimming across a wide pool, 11-year-old Tolu experiences sudden exhaustion and begins to struggle in deep water ($2.5\text{ meters}$ deep).
- **Problem:** What is the correct, life-saving course of action for Tolu?
- **Step 1:** Recognize the danger: Flailing arms and shouting with a vertical head position consumes massive oxygen and causes rapid sinking.
- **Step 2:** Transition to the survival float: Tolu rolls backward onto her back, immerses her ears in the water, pushes her hips up, and spreads her arms like wings (**Back Float**).
- **Step 3:** With her airways completely exposed to air, she breathes steadily, conserves energy, and waves one hand calmly to alert the lifeguard.
- **Verdict:** Tolu stays afloat effortlessly until retrieved safely!

### Scenario 2: Selecting Proper Spotting for a Squat Vault
- **Context:** During gym class, Emeka is about to execute a squat vault over a four-tier vaulting box.
- **Problem:** How many spotters are needed, and where should they stand?
- **Step 1:** Review equipment safety standards: Vaulting involves high horizontal speed and elevated apparatus.
- **Step 2:** Two spotters must be stationed on opposite sides of the box, level with the landing side of the apparatus.
- **Step 3:** The spotters watch the performer's chest and arms, ready to catch the upper arms if his toes or knees catch the box edge.
- **Verdict:** Dual-side spotting ensures total protection from forward somersaulting crashes.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Balance & Posture Drill:** Practice balancing on one foot on a firm floor (the "stork stand") with eyes open for 30 seconds, then eyes closed for 15 seconds to evaluate neuromuscular balance.
- **Water Safety Family Discussion:** Revisit family safety rules for visits to beaches, hotel pools, or rivers. Emphasize why the buddy system and lifejackets are non-negotiable.
- **Gentle Stretching:** Guide the learner through gentle shoulder and hamstring stretches to maintain joint mobility.
