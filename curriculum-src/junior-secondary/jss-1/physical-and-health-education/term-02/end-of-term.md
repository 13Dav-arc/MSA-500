## Key Terms
- Term: Gymnastics
- Definition: A standardized system of physical exercises performed on floor mats or apparatus that develops strength, flexibility, balance, agility, and spatial body control.
- Term: Tripod Base
- Definition: The three-point triangular base formed by the forehead and two palms flat on the mat that provides stability during a Headstand.
- Term: Vaulting Box
- Definition: A tiered wooden apparatus with a padded leather top over which gymnasts leap using the spring-loaded rebound of a beat board.
- Term: Judo
- Definition: A modern Olympic grappling martial art founded in 1882 by Dr. Jigoro Kano, translating as "The Gentle Way", emphasizing throws, pins, and yielding leverage.
- Term: Karate
- Definition: A Japanese striking martial art developed in Okinawa, translating as "The Empty Hand", focusing on punches, kicks, blocks, and pre-arranged forms (*Kata*).
- Term: Ukemi
- Definition: The Japanese art of falling safely onto the mat to dissipate landing impact across large surface areas, protecting the head, spine, and joints.
- Term: Buoyancy
- Definition: The upward hydrostatic force exerted by water that counteracts gravity, enabling bodies with air-filled lungs to float on the surface.
- Term: Axial Skeleton
- Definition: The central longitudinal core of the human skeleton (80 bones) comprising the skull, vertebral column, ribs, and sternum.
- Term: Appendicular Skeleton
- Definition: The bones of the upper and lower limbs, shoulder girdles, and pelvic girdle (126 bones) that facilitate physical movement and manipulation.
- Term: Synovial Joint
- Definition: A freely movable joint enclosed within a fibrous capsule containing friction-reducing synovial fluid (e.g., knee, elbow, shoulder, hip).
- Term: Kyphosis
- Definition: An abnormal outward curvature of the upper thoracic spine resulting in a rounded hunchback and stooped shoulders.
- Term: Lordosis
- Definition: An exaggerated inward curvature of the lower lumbar spine, causing the abdomen to sag forward and pelvis to tilt backward.
- Term: Scoliosis
- Definition: An abnormal sideways (lateral) S-shaped or C-shaped curvature of the vertebral column often caused by carrying heavy loads asymmetrically.
- Term: FRSC
- Definition: The *Federal Road Safety Corps*, Nigeria's statutory agency dedicated to enforcing traffic laws, educating road users, and reducing vehicular casualties.
- Term: First Aid
- Definition: The immediate, temporary care provided to an injured or suddenly ill individual before professional medical services arrive.
- Term: R.I.C.E. Protocol
- Definition: The four-step clinical emergency treatment for acute soft-tissue sprains and strains: **Rest**, **Ice**, **Compression**, and **Elevation**.

### Core Sessional Mastery Review
1. **Gymnastic Fundamentals (Weeks 01–02):**
   - Floor Stunts: Tucking the chin to the chest during forward/backward rolls; forming a 3-point equilateral triangle (tripod base) with forehead and hands for headstands.
   - Apparatus: Vaulting box requires a two-footed punch off the springboard, straight arms on the box, and a shock-absorbing landing on thick crash mats. Balance beam is 10 cm wide. Two spotters flank the vaulting box to prevent dangerous falls.
2. **Combat Disciplines (Week 03):**
   - Judo (Jigoro Kano, 1882): Grappling and throwing based on yielding force (*Seiryoku Zen'yo*). *Ukemi* breakfalls dissipate landing shock; *Ippon* results in an immediate victory.
   - Karate (Gichin Funakoshi): Striking empty-handed (*Kata* forms, *Kumite* sparring).
   - Universal martial arts ethics: Respect, bowing (*Rei*), belt ranking (White to Black), and absolute non-violence outside the dojo.
3. **Aquatics & Water Safety (Week 04):**
   - Buoyancy: Air in the lungs provides upward lift.
   - The Back Float (supine) is the premier survival float: face clear of water, breathing naturally for hours.
   - Basic strokes: Freestyle (front crawl) features alternating over-arm stroke with continuous flutter kick from hips; Breaststroke uses a symmetrical arm pull and circular whip/frog kick.
4. **Anatomy, Physiology & Posture (Weeks 06–07):**
   - 206 bones in adult skeleton: Axial (80 bones: skull, spine, ribs, sternum) and Appendicular (126 bones: limbs and girdles).
   - Synovial joints: Ball-and-socket (shoulder, hip: 360-degree rotation) and Hinge (elbow, knee: uniaxial).
   - Muscles: Skeletal (voluntary), Smooth (involuntary), Cardiac (involuntary heart wall, zero fatigue). Antagonistic pairs (e.g., biceps/triceps).
   - Postural defects: Kyphosis (thoracic outward curve), Lordosis (lumbar inward curve), Scoliosis (lateral sideways curve from carrying bags on one shoulder), Flat Foot (fallen arch), Bow Legs (knees apart), Knock Knees (knees touch).
5. **Safety Education & First Aid (Weeks 08–09):**
   - Safety: Proper warm-up, facility inspection, protective gear (shin guards), home hazard elimination (pot handles inward, locked chemicals), FRSC road rules (pedestrian overpasses, zebra crossings).
   - First Aid: The 3 Ps (Preserve life, Prevent worsening, Promote recovery). Sprain (ligament injury) vs Strain (muscle/tendon injury). Dislocation (displaced bone socket; never force back). Fracture (broken bone; immobilize with splint and sling).
   - The **R.I.C.E. Protocol** for acute soft-tissue injuries: Rest, Ice (15–20 mins, never on bare skin), Compression (firm crepe bandage), Elevation (above heart).

## Common Mistakes

| Category | Common Student Trap | Verified Examination Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Floor Tumbling** | Landing on the top of the head during forward rolls. | Chin must be tucked firmly onto the chest so contact occurs on the shoulders and upper back. |
| **Martial Arts** | Letting the head hit the tatami during a backward fall. | Always tuck the chin onto the chest; slap the mat with forearms and palms at $35^\circ$. |
| **Aquatics Survival**| Trying to tread water frantically when exhausted in deep water. | Roll backward into a relaxed **Back Float** with ears submerged and chin up to conserve oxygen. |
| **Connective Tissues**| Stating that ligaments connect muscles to bones. | **Tendons** connect Muscle to Bone; **Ligaments** connect Bone to Bone at joints. |
| **Postural Habits** | Carrying heavy schoolbags on one shoulder. | Creates severe asymmetrical muscle pulling that permanently warps the spine into **Scoliosis**. |
| **Injury Care** | Applying hot balm or massaging a freshly sprained ankle. | Heat and massage aggravate internal bleeding! Strictly apply **Ice** and the **R.I.C.E.** protocol. |
| **Dislocations** | Attempting to force or pop a dislocated shoulder back in. | Never reduce a dislocation; immobilize the joint in the position found and transport to a hospital. |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Multi-Sport Safety & Emergency Decision
- **Context:** During an active sports afternoon, two emergencies occur simultaneously:
  1. In the gymnastics hall, a student falls awkwardly from the balance beam, spraining her ankle.
  2. Outside on the road, a student dashes across a four-lane highway instead of using the pedestrian bridge 30 meters away.
- **Problem:** Analyze the errors and state the proper safety and first aid procedures.
- **Analysis 1:** Ankle sprain: Damaged ankle ligaments. Immediately apply **R.I.C.E.** (Rest, Ice wrapped in cloth for 20 mins, Compression wrap, Elevation above heart level).
- **Analysis 2:** Road hazard: Dashing across expressways violates FRSC pedestrian rules. Pedestrians must strictly climb the **Pedestrian Overpass Bridge** where zero vehicular conflict exists.
- **Verdict:** Immediate application of R.I.C.E. prevents severe joint swelling; using the pedestrian bridge guarantees life safety!

### Scenario 2: Biomechanical Muscle Pair Analysis During a Sprint Start
- **Context:** As a sprinter launches out of the starting blocks, their rear leg drives backward violently, fully extending the hip and straightening the knee joint.
- **Problem:** Which major muscle groups on the leg act as prime movers (agonists) during this hip and knee extension?
- **Step 1:** Hip extension is driven by the **Gluteus maximus** (buttock muscle) and Hamstrings.
- **Step 2:** Knee extension is driven by the **Quadriceps femoris** contracting on the front of the thigh.
- **Step 3:** The opposing muscles (hip flexors and hamstrings at the knee) relax to permit explosive forward propulsion.
- **Verdict:** Gluteus maximus and Quadriceps act as powerful agonists during block extension.
