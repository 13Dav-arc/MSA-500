## Hook
Have you ever watched an acrobat cartwheel across a grassy village clearing, or held your breath as an Olympic gymnast tumbled through the air with feather-light grace and landed rock-solid on two feet with arms thrust high in triumph? Gymnastics is not just for superheroes—it is the foundational art of controlling every single muscle, joint, and bone in your body! From a simple tuck roll on a cushioned mat to balancing upside down on your hands like a living statue, gymnastics transforms your physical body into an agile, flexible, and powerful instrument. Welcome to **Floor Gymnastics and Tumbling Stunts**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Gymnastics
- Definition: A system of standardized physical exercises designed to develop muscular strength, agility, body coordination, flexibility, and graceful balance, performed with or without apparatus.
- Term: Floor Activities (Floor Exercises)
- Definition: Gymnastic stunts and tumbling sequences performed entirely on cushioned mats or sprung floor areas without the use of elevated apparatus.
- Term: Tumbling
- Definition: Acrobatic gymnastic movements involving rolling, springing, twisting, and flipping of the body across a padded floor surface.
- Term: Stunts
- Definition: Play-like physical activities and self-testing challenges that build balance, agility, and motor coordination (e.g., duck walk, wheelbarrow, crab walk).
- Term: Forward Roll
- Definition: A basic floor tumbling stunt where the gymnast crouches, plants hands on the mat, tucks the chin tightly to the chest, and rolls forward smoothly over the back of the neck and curved spine onto the feet.
- Term: Backward Roll
- Definition: A floor tumbling stunt where the gymnast sits back from a low crouch, rolls backward along the curved spine, pushes forcefully against the mat with hands beside the ears, and lands upright on the feet.
- Term: Headstand
- Definition: An inverted balance where the body is supported upside down in a vertical line by a three-point tripod base formed by the forehead and two hands on the mat.
- Term: Handstand
- Definition: An inverted vertical balance where the entire body weight is supported solely on the palms of the hands with locked elbows and fully extended legs.
- Term: Cartwheel
- Definition: A sideways rotary tumbling movement where the gymnast turns like a spoke of a wheel: hand-hand-foot-foot, maintaining straight arms and legs in a vertical plane.
- Term: Spotter (Spotting)
- Definition: A trained assistant, classmate, or coach who stands alongside a gymnast to physically support, guide, and protect them from falling or neck injury during stunt execution.

## Core Concept
### Origins & Scope of Gymnastics
The word *gymnastics* derives from the ancient Greek word *gymnos* (meaning "unclad" or "naked"), because athletes in ancient Greece trained stripped of heavy clothing in facilities called *gymnasia*. In modern Physical Education, gymnastics is divided into two primary disciplines:
1. **Floor Activities (Floor Stunts & Tumbling):** Executed directly on padded mats or floor areas.
2. **Apparatus Activities:** Executed using elevated gymnastic equipment (such as the vaulting box, balance beam, parallel bars, and horizontal bar).

#### 1. Forward Roll:
- **Phase 1 (Preparation):** Stand at the edge of the mat, lower into a deep squat with knees together, and place hands shoulder-width apart flat on the mat with fingers pointing forward.
- **Phase 2 (Execution):** Tuck the chin tightly onto the chest (look at the belly button!), push off the balls of the feet, raise hips, and bend elbows so the **back of the neck and shoulders** (never the top of the crown!) touch the mat softly.
- **Phase 3 (Follow-through):** Round the spine like a ball, hold knees into the chest (tuck), roll smoothly forward along the spine, and rock onto the feet without touching hands to the floor to stand upright.

#### 2. Backward Roll:
- Start in a deep crouch with palms held up beside the ears (fingers pointing backward over shoulders).
- Sit backward close to the heels, tuck chin tightly to chest, roll smoothly backward along the rounded spine.
- When the hands contact the mat beside the ears, push vigorously against the floor to relieve pressure on the neck, flip the legs over, and land softly on the feet in a squat.

#### 3. Headstand (The Tripod Base):
- Place two hands flat on the mat, shoulder-width apart.
- Place the **hairline / upper forehead** on the mat ahead of the hands to form a stable **equilateral triangle** (Tripod). If your head and hands are in a straight line, you will lose balance and topple sideways!
- Walk feet toward the chest until the hips are over the shoulders.
- Slowly lift one leg, then the other, into a vertical alignment, squeezing abdominal core and glute muscles.

#### 4. Cartwheel:
- Face forward, step into a lunge with the lead foot.
- Reach forward and sideways to place the lead hand on the mat, followed by the second hand, kicking legs overhead sequentially in a wide straddle (*hand-hand-foot-foot* cadence).
- Land on the first foot, followed by the second foot, returning to an upright standing posture.

### Mat Safety Rules and Spotting
- **Always Use Safety Mats:** Never practice floor stunts, rolls, or balances on bare concrete, terrazzo, or gravel!
- **Tuck the Chin:** The single most critical rule in rolling stunts is tucking the chin to the chest so the curved spine absorbs impact rather than the fragile cervical vertebrae (neck).
- **The Spotter’s Duty:**
  - The spotter stands at the side of the performer to stabilize hips, catch the legs during headstands/handstands, and cushion unexpected falls.
  - Spotters must never look away or joke during a stunt execution!

## Common Mistakes

| Common Student Mistake | Danger / Consequence | Technical Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Landing on the top/crown of the head during forward rolls** | Severe neck compression and spinal injury. | Tuck the chin firmly into the chest; look at your navel so the shoulders and upper back touch first. |
| **Placing head and hands in a straight line during headstands** | Athlete loses balance and topples sideways or backward. | Form an equilateral triangle: place the forehead ahead of the two hands on the mat. |
| **Bent elbows during handstands** | The arms collapse, causing the athlete's head to hit the floor. | Lock elbows straight and push the floor away actively through the shoulders. |
| **Failing to push with hands during backward roll** | Heavy body weight jams the neck against the floor. | Place hands beside ears with palms facing up; push hard into the mat as hips roll over. |
| **Performing stunts without safety mats or spotters** | High risk of sprains, dislocations, and head contusions. | Strictly perform stunts on certified gym mats with an alert classmate or coach spotting. |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Correcting a Collapsing Headstand
- **Context:** During a PE class, Dapo attempts a headstand. He places his hands on the mat and puts his head directly between his hands along the exact same horizontal line. When he kicks his legs up, his body immediately topples backward into a messy heap.
- **Problem:** Why did Dapo fall, and how can his technique be corrected?
- **Step 1:** Analyze the base of support: A straight line provides zero forward-backward stability (it is a 1-dimensional base).
- **Step 2:** Apply the geometric tripod principle: A stable 2-dimensional base requires a triangle.
- **Step 3:** The head (hairline) must be placed 20 to 30 cm forward of the hands, creating an equilateral triangle between the head and two palms.
- **Verdict:** Dapo must reset his head forward into a tripod triangle. With this broad base, his inverted balance will be stable!

### Scenario 2: Safely Spotting a Classmate's Handstand
- **Context:** Aisha wants to learn a handstand against a wall. Funke acts as her spotter.
- **Problem:** How should Funke position herself to ensure Aisha’s safety?
- **Step 1:** Funke stands to the side of Aisha (never directly behind, where she could be kicked).
- **Step 2:** As Aisha kicks upward with locked elbows, Funke grasps Aisha’s lower leg (shin or ankle) with both hands to guide her legs into vertical alignment.
- **Step 3:** Funke holds Aisha steadily until Aisha indicates she is ready to step down one foot at a time.
- **Verdict:** Safe and supportive spotting prevents falling, hyperextension of the spine, and wrist injury.

## Guardian Sync
- **Basic Flexibility & Spinal Warm-up:** Guide the learner through gentle cat-cow stretches and seated forward bends on a rug or carpet at home to improve spinal flexibility.
- **Plank Stability Challenge:** Practice holding an upright push-up plank on hands or elbows for 30 to 45 seconds to build the core stability required for gymnastics.
- **Safety Rule Reinforcement:** Reiterate to the learner that gymnastic tumbling should never be attempted on hard cement floors, verandahs, or without proper supervision.
