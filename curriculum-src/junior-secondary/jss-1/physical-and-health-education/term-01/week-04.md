## Hook
Picture the roaring excitement at your school's annual Inter-House Sports competition! The whole school gathers around the long jump sandpit and the high jump landing mats. A student charges down the runway like a cheetah, stamps one foot fiercely onto the wooden take-off board, leaps into the sky like an eagle soaring over the clouds, and lands cleanly deep in the sand! Think of Nigeria’s Olympic medalist **Ese Brume**, who launched herself across a staggering 7.17 meters through sheer explosive power and aerial technique. How do athletes defy gravity to jump higher than a classroom doorway or leap farther than the length of a minibus? It is not pure luck—it is the science of **Field Jumping Events**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Field Events
- Definition: Athletic competitions conducted within the inner field area enclosed by the running track, comprising jumping and throwing events.
- Term: Jumps
- Definition: Field athletic events where an athlete propels their body into the air from a single-foot take-off to achieve maximum vertical height or horizontal distance.
- Term: Long Jump
- Definition: A horizontal jumping event where an athlete sprints down a runway and takes off from a wooden board into a soft sand landing pit.
- Term: High Jump
- Definition: A vertical jumping event where an athlete clears an elevated horizontal crossbar supported by two upright standards without knocking it down.
- Term: Approach Run
- Definition: The fast, rhythmic acceleration sprint down the runway that generates maximum horizontal momentum prior to the take-off.
- Term: Take-off
- Definition: The explosive single-foot stamp and upward plant against the ground or board that converts horizontal momentum into vertical lift.
- Term: Flight Phase
- Definition: The airborne period between leaving the ground and touching the landing surface, during which body styling minimizes bar displacement or maximizes forward landing.
- Term: Western Roll
- Definition: An older high jump technique where the jumper takes off from the inner foot and clears the crossbar on their side or stomach facing the bar.
- Term: Fosbury Flop
- Definition: The modern universal high jump technique invented by Dick Fosbury, where the athlete arches their back over the crossbar with their shoulders facing away from the bar.
- Term: Take-off Board
- Definition: A standardized wooden plank (20 cm wide) embedded level with the runway, marked with a plasticine foul line that jumpers must not overstep.

## Core Concept
### The Universal Four Phases of Jumping
Every jumping event in track and field—whether vertical (High Jump) or horizontal (Long Jump)—consists of four sequential, interconnected phases:

```
┌──────────────────┐     ┌──────────────────┐     ┌──────────────────┐     ┌──────────────────┐
│  APPROACH RUN    │ ──▶ │     TAKE-OFF     │ ──▶ │   FLIGHT PHASE   │ ──▶ │     LANDING      │
│ (Rhythmic Speed) │     │ (Single-Foot Lift)│     │  (Aerial Action) │     │  (Safe Descent)  │
└──────────────────┘     └──────────────────┘     └──────────────────┘     └──────────────────┘
```

### Long Jump: Mechanics & Styles
In the long jump, the goal is to travel as far horizontally as possible into the sandpit from behind the legal take-off line.

#### The Four Phases in Long Jump:
1. **Approach Run:**
   - Usually 16 to 24 strides of smooth, progressive acceleration.
   - Jumpers establish a checkmark to ensure their take-off foot hits the board accurately without looking down at their feet.
2. **Take-off:**
   - The athlete drives the take-off foot firmly flat onto the board.
   - The non-take-off knee (lead knee) punches upward violently while the arms drive forward and up.
   - **Crucial Rule:** The jumper must take off from **one foot only**! Taking off from two feet is an automatic foul. Touching any part of the plasticine line ahead of the board is also ruled a **foul jump** (no distance measured).
3. **Flight Styles:**
   - **Sail Technique:** The jumper brings both legs forward in a sitting posture immediately after lift-off (best for beginners).
   - **Hang Style:** The athlete arches their torso backward, dropping their legs and extending arms overhead to create an arched body posture, snapping forward just before landing.
   - **Hitch-Kick (Running in the Air):** The athlete continues pedaling their legs in a running motion while airborne, preserving balance and preventing early forward rotation.
4. **Landing:**
   - The jumper thrusts both feet as far forward as possible into the soft sand.
   - The knees bend to absorb shock, and the body rolls forward or sideways.
   - **Measurement Rule:** The jump is measured from the front edge of the take-off board to the **nearest mark in the sand** made by any part of the athlete’s body or clothing. If an athlete touches the sand behind their feet with their hand, they lose valuable distance!

### High Jump: Mechanics & Clearance Styles
In the high jump, the athlete must clear a horizontal crossbar set at progressive heights without dislodging it from its upright supports onto the ground.

#### High Jump Techniques:
1. **Scissors Jump:**
   - A basic upright style where the jumper approaches at an angle, kicks the inner leg straight over the bar first, followed by the trailing leg in a scissor-like motion, landing on their feet.
2. **Western Roll:**
   - The athlete approaches from a 45-degree angle, takes off from the foot closer to the bar, and rolls over the bar with their side or chest facing downward, landing on the same take-off foot and hands.
3. **Eastern Cut-off:**
   - An evolution of scissors where the torso turns horizontal along the bar during clearance.
4. **Fosbury Flop:**
   - The standard modern technique used worldwide.
   - The athlete runs a **J-shaped curved approach**, turning their back to the crossbar at take-off.
   - The athlete drives upward, arches their back completely over the bar (allowing the hips to clear while head and shoulders drop), and snaps the legs over before landing flat on the upper back and shoulders on a thick, cushioned foam landing mat (**crash mat**).
   - **Safety Warning:** The Fosbury Flop must **never** be attempted into sandpits; it requires a standardized, deep foam crash bed!

#### Common High Jump Rules:
- The jumper must take off from **one foot only**.
- A trial is deemed a failure (foul) if:
  1. The crossbar falls off the supports due to athlete contact.
  2. The athlete touches the landing mat or ground beyond the vertical plane of the uprights before clearing the bar.
  3. The athlete fails to begin their attempt within the designated time limit (usually 1 minute).
- Three consecutive failures at any given height eliminate the competitor.

## Comparative Matrix

| Characteristic | Long Jump | High Jump |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Direction** | Horizontal distance (length) | Vertical clearance (height) |
| **Runway Surface** | Straight synthetic track (approx. 40m) | J-shaped curved approach run |
| **Take-off Foot** | Strictly one foot on wooden board | Strictly one foot from track apron |
| **Major Airborne Styles** | Sail, Hang, Hitch-kick | Scissors, Western Roll, Fosbury Flop |
| **Landing Surface** | Raked damp sandpit | Deep synthetic foam crash mat |
| **Foul Trigger** | Stepping over take-off board foul line | Dislodging crossbar or two-footed take-off |
| **Measurement Reference**| From board edge to nearest sand mark | Vertical height from ground to lowest point of crossbar |

## Common Mistakes

| Common Student Mistake | Why It Happens / The Consequence | Correction Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Looking down at the board during long jump** | The athlete fears fouling and stares at the board, dropping their chest and killing horizontal speed. | Establish fixed run-up checkmarks; practice approach rhythm so the eyes stay focused high on the pit. |
| **Two-footed take-off** | Students accustomed to playground jumping jump with both feet together. | The official rule mandates single-foot plant; punch the free lead knee upward during plant. |
| **Falling backward into the sand after long jump** | The jumper fails to sweep their hips forward, planting hands behind their heels in the sand. | The distance is measured to the rearmost mark; collapse forward or roll sideways upon heel strike. |
| **Attempting the Fosbury Flop on sand or bare grass** | Extreme safety hazard! Landing on the neck or head on hard ground causes severe spine injury. | Use the scissors jump on sand or grass; reserve Fosbury Flop strictly for certified foam crash mats. |
| **Knocking the high jump bar with trailing heels** | The jumper arches the back over the bar but forgets to whip the lower legs upward to clear. | Practice the rapid lower-leg kick (hip snap) once hips have passed the bar. |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Measuring a Disputed Long Jump
- **Context:** During an inter-house competition, Ade jumps and lands with his heels at the 6.20-meter mark. However, as he loses balance, his left hand slips backward and presses into the sand at the 5.85-meter mark.
- **Problem:** Which distance must the field judge record on the score sheet?
- **Step 1:** Recall the international measurement rule: distance is measured from the take-off line perpendicularly to the *nearest* break in the sand made by *any* part of the competitor's body or limbs.
- **Step 2:** Compare the marks: heels ($6.20\text{ m}$) vs hand mark ($5.85\text{ m}$).
- **Step 3:** The hand mark is closer to the take-off board ($5.85\text{ m}$).
- **Verdict:** The judge must record **5.85 meters**. Ade lost 35 centimeters because he fell backward instead of rolling forward!

### Scenario 2: Determining a High Jump Foul
- **Context:** Ngozi clears the high jump crossbar cleanly without her body touching the bar. However, the wind shakes the uprights, or her knee grazes the bar slightly causing it to wobble vigorously on the pins. She lands safely on the mat.
- **Problem:** When is a jump officially counted as successful?
- **Step 1:** If the crossbar wobbles but remains resting on the support pegs until the athlete leaves the landing mat, the jump is **valid (clearance)**.
- **Step 2:** If the crossbar falls off the supports to the ground before the athlete exits the landing area (even after an initial touch), the jump is recorded as a **failure (foul)**.
- **Verdict:** Since the bar stayed on the pegs, the white flag is raised and the height is officially credited!

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Physical Activity:** Set up two soft markers (e.g., shoes or towels) in a safe, open yard. Practice the **Standing Broad Jump** (landing with soft bent knees) to build explosive leg power.
- **Vertical Reach Test:** Measure standing vertical jump against a smooth wall with chalk on the fingertips to assess lower-body explosive power.
- **Discussion Prompt:** Discuss why jumping safely onto proper landing surfaces (sand or foam mats) prevents sprains and fractures.
