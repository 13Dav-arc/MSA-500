## Hook
Imagine standing inside a marked chalk circle holding a solid iron cannonball that weighs as much as four large yam tubers packed together! You cannot throw it like a cricket ball or baseball—if you do, your elbow will tear under the crushing weight. Instead, you tuck it tightly against your neck beneath your jaw, coil your body down like a compressed steel spring, glide backward across the circle, and violently unleash all the rotational power of your legs, hips, and chest to blast the metal missile high into the sky! Now think of spinning like a whirlwind to release a sleek flying saucer—the discus—sailing fifty meters across the green field! Welcome to the ancient, mighty world of **Throwing Events** in athletics!

## Key Terms
- Term: Throwing Events
- Definition: Field athletic competitions in which competitors propel specialized heavy implements (shot, discus, javelin, or hammer) for maximum distance within a designated landing sector.
- Term: Shot Put
- Definition: A field event where an athlete pushes (puts) a heavy spherical metal ball from the neck area out of a 2.135-meter circle into a marked landing sector.
- Term: Discus Throw
- Definition: A field event where an athlete flings a flat, circular heavy disc made of wood or plastic with a metal rim out of a 2.50-meter circle into a landing sector.
- Term: The "Put" Action
- Definition: A legal pushing action where the shot is released from the shoulder/neck with one hand without dropping behind or below the shoulder plane during the motion.
- Term: Throwing Circle
- Definition: A standardized enclosed concrete or asphalt circle surrounded by a metal rim from which field throwers must execute their throws without overstepping.
- Term: Stop Board (Toe Board)
- Definition: A curved white wooden block measuring 1.21–1.23 m long and 10 cm high situated at the front perimeter of the shot put circle to prevent forward stepping.
- Term: Glide Technique
- Definition: A linear technique in shot put where the athlete hops backward on one foot from the back of the circle to the center to accumulate horizontal acceleration.
- Term: Landing Sector
- Definition: A V-shaped boundary extending outward from the center of the throwing circle at an official angle of 34.92 degrees within which the implement must land.
- Term: Follow-Through & Recovery
- Definition: The rapid switching of the feet and low-crouch balancing action performed immediately after release to prevent stepping over the rim of the circle.
- Term: Foul Throw
- Definition: An illegal attempt caused by touching outside the circle, implement landing outside sector lines, throwing rather than putting, or exiting through the front half.

## Core Concept
### Safety First: The Iron Law of Field Throws
Because implements like the shot and discus are heavy, dense missiles capable of causing fatal injuries, strict safety protocols govern every throwing session:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                       CARDINAL THROWING SAFETY PROTOCOLS                    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. CAGE & SECTOR CLEARANCE: No person is allowed inside the landing sector. │
│ 2. ONE AT A TIME: Only the active thrower steps into the circle on command. │
│ 3. NO THROW-BACK: Implements must be CARRIED back by hand, NEVER thrown.    │
│ 4. REAR EXIT ONLY: The thrower must wait for the implement to land, then    │
│    exit calmly from the REAR half of the circle behind the white line.      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Shot Put: Execution & Technique
At the junior secondary school level, the weight of the shot is calibrated to the physical maturity of the student (typically $3.0\text{ kg}$ or $4.0\text{ kg}$ for boys and $2.72\text{ kg}$ or $3.0\text{ kg}$ for girls; Olympic senior implements are $7.26\text{ kg}$ for men and $4.0\text{ kg}$ for women).

#### The Five Sequential Stages of Shot Put:
1. **The Grip & Hold:**
   - The shot rests on the bases of the three middle fingers (not sitting in the palm!).
   - The thumb and little finger support the sides to maintain stability.
   - The shot is tucked firmly under the chin, resting against the collarbone and pressed against the neck.
   - The throwing elbow is held high and pointed outward at roughly a 45-degree angle.
2. **Stance:**
   - The thrower stands at the rear of the 2.135-meter circle, facing away from the landing sector.
   - Weight is loaded onto the rear dominant leg.
3. **The Glide:**
   - The athlete bends at the knees and hips into a low crouch ("unseating"), kicks the non-dominant leg backward toward the stop board, and hops smoothly on the dominant foot across the circle.
4. **Delivery (The Put):**
   - The rear leg extends violently, rotating the hips forward toward the front of the circle.
   - The chest sweeps open, and the throwing arm punches explosive forward and upward at an optimal angle of approximately **$35^\circ$ to $40^\circ$**.
   - **Crucial Rule:** The shot must be pushed out from the neck; swinging the hand backward away from the neck constitutes an illegal "throwing" motion!
5. **Recovery:**
   - Immediately after release, the thrower jumps and switches feet (reverse), bringing the dominant foot to the toe board to absorb forward momentum without touching the top of the board or stepping out.

### Discus Throw: Execution & Rules
The discus is a lens-shaped disc (circular plate). Secondary school boys typically use a $1.0\text{ kg}$ or $1.25\text{ kg}$ implement; secondary girls throw $1.0\text{ kg}$ (senior men throw $2.0\text{ kg}$).

#### The Four Key Stages in Discus Throw:
1. **Grip:**
   - The flat disc rests against the palm; only the distal joints (outermost knuckles) of the four spread fingers hook over the outer rim.
   - The thumb rests flat against the face of the disc for balance (it does not grip the rim).
   - Centrifugal force generated during the swing holds the disc against the hand!
2. **Preliminary Swings:**
   - Standing at the back of the 2.50-meter circle facing away from the sector, the athlete swings the arm back and forth across the body to establish rhythm and stretch the chest muscles.
3. **Turn and Delivery:**
   - The thrower pivots on the balls of the feet, executing a rotational turn across the circle.
   - The throwing arm trails far behind the hips, creating a tight elastic tension across the torso.
   - When the front foot plants, the hips whip forward, dragging the arm forward like a whip.
   - The discus is released off the **index finger** in a clockwise spinning motion (for right-handers), slicing through the air like a frisbee.
4. **Rules & Common Fouls:**
   - The implement must land entirely within the inner edges of the $34.92^\circ$ sector lines.
   - The athlete must not touch the ground outside the circle or the top of the metal rim or toe board during or after the throw.
   - The athlete must remain inside the circle until the implement has hit the ground, and must exit strictly through the **rear half of the circle**.

## Common Mistakes

| Common Mistake | Consequence / Rule Infraction | Correction Technique |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Resting the shot deep in the palm** | Decreases mechanical leverage and strains the wrist joint severely. | Rest the shot on the bases of the fingers with fingers slightly spread. |
| **Pulling the shot away from the neck before release** | Ruled an illegal "throw" rather than a "put"; high risk of elbow ligament strain. | Keep the shot glued firmly under the jaw/collarbone until the final explosive forward drive. |
| **Gripping the discus too tightly with thumb over the edge** | Destroys the aerodynamic spin and causes the disc to wobble and drop prematurely. | Hook only the outer fingertips over the rim; let centrifugal force hold the disc to the palm. |
| **Stepping on top of the stop board during follow-through** | Automatic foul throw! No distance will be measured. | Plant feet wide, bend knees low, and reverse feet quickly to absorb forward momentum. |
| **Walking forward out of the circle after throwing** | Instant foul! All throws require exiting through the back half of the circle. | Wait until the implement strikes the turf, turn calmly, and step behind the dividing white line. |

## Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Judging a Questionable Shot Put Attempt
- **Context:** Tunde enters the circle for his second trial. He executes a strong glide and puts the shot. The shot lands at 11.45 meters inside the sector. However, as Tunde celebrates, his shoe touches the top white edge of the curved wooden stop board.
- **Problem:** Is Tunde's throw valid or a foul?
- **Step 1:** Review the stop board rule: A competitor is legally allowed to touch the *inside inner face* of the stop board to stop their momentum.
- **Step 2:** However, touching the *top surface* or *outside edge* of the stop board or the ground beyond it constitutes an immediate infraction.
- **Step 3:** Because Tunde touched the *top edge* of the board, the attempt is illegal.
- **Verdict:** The field official raises the **red flag**. The throw is officially recorded as a **Foul (No Mark)**.

### Scenario 2: Determining Landing Sector Validity
- **Context:** In the discus event, Fatima throws a disc that lands directly on top of the white chalk boundary line demarcating the left border of the landing sector.
- **Problem:** Does landing on the sector line count as an in-bounds legal throw?
- **Step 1:** Recall the sector boundary rule: The white sector lines themselves are considered *outside* (out-of-bounds) the landing area.
- **Step 2:** The implement must land completely within the inner edge of the radial boundary lines.
- **Verdict:** A throw landing on the line or outside the line is an **out-of-sector foul**. The red flag is raised.

## Guardian Sync
- **Grip & Wrist Safety Demonstration:** Practice holding a soft rubber ball or tennis ball on the pads of the fingers (not resting in the palm) and tucking it under the jaw to understand the "clean palm" principle of the shot put grip.
- **Centrifugal Force Exploration:** Gently swing an empty plastic plate down by your side to feel how centrifugal momentum keeps the plate pressed against the fingers without gripping with the thumb.
- **Discussion:** Emphasize why sports equipment safety rules (such as clearing landing sectors) are essential to prevent severe sports injuries.
