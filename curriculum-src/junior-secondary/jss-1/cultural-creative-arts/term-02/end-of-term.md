## Hook

Over the past nine intensive weeks, your journey has crossed the vibrant studios of visual craft into the mathematical world of musical science and theory. You have torn and glued paper into modern Cubist collages, pounded wet paper pulp in mortars to sculpt lightweight papier-mâché masks, studied the royal coral beads of Nigerian monarchs and the fiery Nupe glass kilns of Bida, and laid shimmering stone tesserae into permanent architectural mosaics. ## Key Terms
- Term: Collage
- Definition: A 2D assembled artwork constructed by pasting varied paper, fabric, or mixed-media scraps onto a flat backing support.
- Term: Papier-mâché
- Definition: A sculptural medium made from pulped or layered paper bound with cassava starch or glue that hardens into a rigid solid.
- Term: Beadwork
- Definition: The craft of stringing, sewing, or assembling perforated beads into decorative, ceremonial, or royal regalia.
- Term: Mosaic
- Definition: An architectural craft in which small hard pieces (*tesserae*) of stone, glass, or tile are inlaid into mortar and sealed with grout.
- Term: Tesserae
- Definition: The small individual cut cubes or fragments of colored stone, marble, glass, or ceramic tile used to build a mosaic.
- Term: Music vs. Noise
- Definition: Music is organized sound with regular, periodic vibrations and definite pitch; noise is chaotic sound with irregular vibrations and indefinite pitch.
- Term: Pitch and Frequency
- Definition: Pitch is the highness or lowness of sound governed by frequency in Hertz (Hz); intensity is volume governed by wave amplitude.
- Term: Timbre (Tone Color)
- Definition: The unique acoustic fingerprint that distinguishes different instruments or voices playing the same note at the same volume.
- Term: Elements of Music
- Definition: The seven foundational components: Pitch, Melody, Harmony, Rhythm, Texture, Timbre, and Form.
- Term: Musical Textures
- Definition: Monophonic (single melody), Homophonic (melody with chords), and Polyphonic (multiple independent melodies).
- Term: Enharmonic Equivalents
- Definition: Notes with different written names that sound the identical pitch on the keyboard (e.g., C$\sharp$ and D$\flat$).
- Term: Musical Stave and Clefs
- Definition: A five-line, four-space grid numbered bottom to top, unlocked by the Treble (G) Clef for high registers and Bass (F) Clef for low registers.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Applied Crafts:** Mixing up collage (flat glued paper), papier-mâché (molded mashed paper), and mosaic (hard stone/glass tesserae in mortar).
2. **Confusing Musical Properties:** Conflating pitch (frequency Hz) with intensity (amplitude/loudness dB).
3. **Misreading Staves:** Counting stave lines from top to bottom instead of bottom to top.
4. **Misinterpreting Enharmonics:** Believing C$\sharp$ and D$\flat$ are different keys on the piano when they are the exact same black key.
5. **Miscalculating Note Values:** Thinking a minim is worth 4 beats and a semibreve is worth 2; remember: Semibreve = 4 beats, Minim = 2 beats, Crotchet = 1 beat.

## Worked Examples
### Comprehensive Problem Solving
*Scenario:* An artistic director is organizing a multi-disciplinary stage production that requires:
1. A durable set piece of a palace floor that will not cause dancers to trip.
   - *Craft Solution:* An architectural floor built with the **Indirect Mosaic Method**, ensuring all tesserae tops form a completely flat plane.
2. A choral arrangement featuring high female voices and low male voices singing in four-part harmony.
   - *Music Notation Solution:* A **Grand Stave** joining the Treble Clef (for Soprano and Alto reading EGBDF / FACE) and Bass Clef (for Tenor and Bass reading GBDFA / ACEG) linked by Middle C.
3. An acoustic sound effect resembling thunder using natural acoustic principles.
   - *Acoustic Solution:* A large orchestral bass drum struck with soft mallets, generating low-frequency, high-amplitude vibrations with dark timbre.
