## Hook

You have explored how modern masters Braque and Picasso shocked the world with collage, discovered how ancient artisans mashed paper into rock-hard papier-mâché sculptures, uncovered the sacred royal coral beads of Nigerian monarchs and the fiery glass kilns of Bida, and learned how ancient Roman and Byzantine builders created eternal stone tapestries with mosaics. ## Key Terms
- Term: Collage
- Definition: A 2D artwork created by pasting varied paper, fabric, or mixed-media scraps onto a flat backing support with glue.
- Term: Papier-mâché
- Definition: A composite sculptural medium made of pulped or layered paper bound with starch paste or glue that dries into a lightweight, rigid solid.
- Term: Beadwork
- Definition: The craft of stringing, sewing, or assembling perforated beads into jewelry, ceremonial regalia, or decorative ornaments.
- Term: Mosaic
- Definition: The architectural art of cementing small, hard colored fragments (*tesserae*) into mortar on a solid substrate to create patterns or pictures.
- Term: Tesserae
- Definition: The small individual cubes or fragments of stone, glass, ceramic tile, or pebbles used in mosaic construction.
- Term: Binder / Adhesive
- Definition: The sticky bonding agent (cassava starch paste, PVA glue, thin-set mortar) that adheres craft fibers or tesserae together.
- Term: Release Agent
- Definition: A slippery substance (such as petroleum jelly, oil, or plastic wrap) coated over a mold to prevent wet papier-mâché from sticking.
- Term: Bida Glass Guild
- Definition: The historic Nupe artisanal glassworkers (*Masaga*) in Niger State renowned for smelting recycled glass beads.
- Term: Grout
- Definition: A fine cement paste forced into the joints (*interstices*) between mosaic tesserae to lock them together and seal out moisture.
- Term: Direct Method
- Definition: The assembly technique in mosaic where tesserae are glued face-up directly onto the permanent backing.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Landmarks Reviewed
- **Collage:** Modern art revolution in Paris (1912); Pablo Picasso (*Still Life with Chair Caning*) and Georges Braque (*papier collé*).
- **Papier-mâché:** Originating in ancient China (Han Dynasty helmets); refined in France (*papier-mâché* = "chewed paper"); widely practiced in Nigeria for masks, theatrical props, and anatomy models.
- **Bead Work:** Name derived from Old English *bede* (prayer); royal coral regalia (*Iyun* in Yoruba, *Ivie* in Edo); famous Nupe glassmakers of Bida, Niger State; traditional waist beads (*Jigida*).
- **Mosaic:** Named after Greek/Latin *Muses*; Sumerian clay cone temples; Roman floor tesserae; Byzantine gold-leaf glass (*smalti*) cathedral domes; contemporary pebble and ceramic architectural murals.

### 3. Studio Safety and Execution Rules
- *Eye Protection:* Always wear safety goggles when cutting glass or ceramic tiles with tile nippers.
- *Mold Release:* Always apply petroleum jelly or plastic wrap to bowl molds before laying papier-mâché strips.
- *Glue Economy:* Never flood thin paper collage or paper beads with excess liquid glue to prevent wrinkling and color bleed.
- *Grout Joint Spacing:* Always maintain 1–2 mm gaps between mosaic tesserae to allow cement grout to lock pieces together.
- *Wire Fastening:* Always clamp crimp beads with flat-nose pliers rather than tying standard granny knots on tiger tail wire.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Dimensionality:** Stating that collage is 3D and papier-mâché is 2D. Remember: collage is flat (2D) on a backing board, while papier-mâché produces hollow sculptures in full round (3D).
2. **Omitting the Release Agent:** Laying wet papier-mâché pulp directly onto a ceramic bowl without petroleum jelly glues the paper permanently to the bowl.
3. **Placing Mosaic Tesserae Without Gaps:** Butt-joining tiles without 1–2 mm joints prevents grout from filling spaces, allowing water to loosen the tiles.
4. **Pulling Paper Bead Strips Loosely:** Rolling loosely around toothpicks results in squishy beads that collapse when strung.
