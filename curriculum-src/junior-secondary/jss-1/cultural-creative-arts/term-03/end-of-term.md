## Key Terms
- Term: Drama vs Theatre
- Definition: Drama is the written literary script; Theatre is the live collaborative stage performance before an audience.
- Term: Aristotle's Six Elements
- Definition: Plot, Character, Theme, Diction, Music, and Spectacle as formalized in *Poetics*.
- Term: Freytag's Pyramid
- Definition: The five dramatic plot phases: Exposition, Rising Action, Climax, Falling Action, and Denouement.
- Term: Hamartia and Hubris
- Definition: *Hamartia* is the tragic hero's fatal flaw; *Hubris* is excessive pride that defies divine and moral laws.
- Term: Soliloquy, Aside, and Dramatic Irony
- Definition: Soliloquy is speaking alone on stage; Aside is a secret whisper to the audience; Dramatic Irony is the audience knowing a secret hidden from stage characters.
- Term: Celebrated Nigerian Playwrights
- Definition: Wole Soyinka (Nobel Prize 1986), Zulu Sofola (first published female playwright), and Ola Rotimi (*The Gods Are Not to Blame*).
- Term: Four Iconic Nigerian Dances
- Definition: Bata (Yoruba, Shango), Atilogwu (Igbo, acrobatics), Koroso (Hausa/Fulani, Kawasara rattles), and Swange (Tiv, serpentine torso waves).
- Term: Choreography Principles
- Definition: The structured design of movement incorporating Action, Space (Levels/Pathways), Dynamics, Relationships (Unison/Canon), and Motif.
- Term: Advanced Musical Extensions
- Definition: Dotted notes (adding half the value), Ties (connecting identical pitches), Slurs (smooth *legato* across different pitches), and Fermatas (prolonged pause).

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Musical Ties and Slurs:** Remembering: A **Tie** joins identical pitches into one sustained tone; a **Slur** connects different pitches to be played smoothly (*legato*).
2. **Confusing Spatial Levels:** Thinking that walking upright is "high level"; standing and walking is **medium level**, leaping in the air is **high level**, and rolling/kneeling on the floor is **low level**.
3. **Miscalculating Dotted Notes:** Believing the dot doubles the note; the dot adds **half** of its base duration.
4. **Confusing Theatrical Roles:** Believing the Producer directs rehearsals (the Director directs rehearsals; the Producer manages the money and contracts).
5. **Confusing Traditional Nigerian Dances:** Mistaking *Atilogwu* (Igbo acrobatic) for *Swange* (Tiv serpentine torso wave).

## Worked Examples
### Sessional Comprehensive Analysis
*Scenario:* An integrated arts festival requires an ensemble presentation:
1. A 3-minute choreographed contemporary African dance inspired by the Benue River.
   - *Choreographic Design:* The dance utilizes **fluid, undulating serpentine torso ripples** inspired by the *Swange* dance, traveling along curved circular pathways on medium and low levels (floor work), culminating in an explosive high-level leap in **unison**!
2. A musical score in 4/4 time featuring a dramatic climax.
   - *Notation Design:* A melody composed on the **Treble Stave** (reading EGBDF lines and FACE spaces) featuring a **Dotted Minim** ($2 + 1 = 3\text{ beats}$) tied to a crotchet ($1\text{ beat}$) to sustain a 4-beat hold, concluding on a final chord crowned by a **Fermata** (pause) held at the conductor's discretion.
3. A dramatic monologue portraying King Odewale.
   - *Performance Design:* An actor in traditional Yoruba *Aso-Oke* with coral beads speaks an impassioned speech revealing his fatal temper (**Hamartia**), creating deep **Catharsis** as the audience recognizes his tragic fate.
