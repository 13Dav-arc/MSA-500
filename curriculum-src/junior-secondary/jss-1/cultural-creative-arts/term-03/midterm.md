## Key Terms
- Term: Drama vs Theatre
- Definition: Drama is the written literary script created by the playwright; Theatre is the live collaborative stage performance before an audience.
- Term: Aristotle's Poetics
- Definition: The ancient classical text formalizing the six elements of drama: Plot, Character, Theme, Diction, Music, and Spectacle.
- Term: Freytag's Pyramid
- Definition: The five-stage plot structure: Exposition, Rising Action, Climax, Falling Action, and Denouement.
- Term: Hamartia
- Definition: The tragic flaw, error of judgment, or internal blind spot that leads to the tragic hero's catastrophic downfall.
- Term: Soliloquy
- Definition: A speech delivered by an actor alone on stage, revealing their private inner thoughts directly to the audience.
- Term: Dramatic Irony
- Definition: A theatrical device where the audience knows a crucial secret that characters on stage are unaware of.
- Term: Farce
- Definition: A high-speed, slapstick subgenre of comedy built on improbable coincidences, slamming doors, and mistaken identities.
- Term: Producer vs Director
- Definition: The Producer manages finances, venue hire, and business logistics; the Director guides the artistic vision, acting, and staging.
- Term: Stage Manager
- Definition: The operational backstage coordinator who runs the prompt book and calls technical cues during live performances.
- Term: Straight vs Character Makeup
- Definition: Straight makeup enhances natural facial features under stage spotlights; Character makeup radically alters age, scars, or physical appearance.

### 2. Critical Distinctions to Master
1. **Script vs Performance:** A play script in a book is *drama*; the living performance on stage with living actors and audience is *theatre*.
2. **Soliloquy vs Monologue vs Aside:**
   - *Soliloquy:* Actor is alone on stage speaking to their own soul and audience.
   - *Monologue:* Actor delivers an extended speech to other characters present on stage.
   - *Aside:* Actor whispers a quick secret directly to the audience that other stage characters pretend not to hear.
3. **Producer vs Director:**
   - *Producer:* Financial controller, accountant, business manager, venue leaser.
   - *Director:* Artistic captain, coach of actors, blocking designer, visual unifier.
4. **Straight Makeup vs Character Makeup:**
   - *Straight:* Worn by all actors to counter harsh spotlight washout and accentuate natural contours.
   - *Character:* Worn to simulate old age, war wounds, traditional facial marks (*ila*), or mythical beasts.

## Comparative Matrix
| Dimension | Literary Text (Drama) | Stage Performance (Theatre) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin Word** | Greek *dran* (to act/do) | Greek *theatron* (place for seeing) |
| **Key Author** | Playwright (solitary author) | Collaborative company (Director, Cast, Designers, Crew) |
| **Audience Mode** | Solitary private reader | Collective communal spectators in an auditorium |
| **Core Medium** | Written dialogue, stage directions | Live human voice, physical motion, lighting, sets, sound |
| **Life Span** | Eternal on paper / in libraries | Transient, ephemeral live moment occurring in real time |

## Common Mistakes
1. **Spelling "Playwright" Incorrectly:** Writing "playwrite" instead of **playwright** (sharing the *-wright* suffix with shipwright).
2. **Confusing Farce with Serious Comedy:** Farce is driven by frantic physical slapstick and preposterous coincidences, not subtle intellectual wit.
3. **Assuming the Director Calls Cues During the Show:** Once opening night arrives, the **Stage Manager** is in total operational command backstage while the Director watches from the seats.
4. **Neglecting Straight Makeup for Male Performers:** All performers require basic foundation makeup to avoid washed-out ghostly faces under 1,000-watt theatre spotlights.

## Worked Examples
### Diagnostic Synthesis Exercise
*Problem:* Analyze the following production profile:
- A school stages an adaptation of Ola Rotimi's *The Gods Are Not to Blame*.
- The Producer secures ₦250,000 from the PTA for lumber, lighting rentals, and costumes.
- The Director blocks the scene where King Odewale confronts Baba Fakunle, positioning Odewale center stage standing over the kneeling seer.
- The actor playing Baba Fakunle is 13 years old; the makeup artist applies brown crease lines, gray hair spray, and spirit-gum-attached crepe beard.
- During the performance, when Odewale vows to hunt down the murderer, the audience groans because they already know Odewale himself committed the deed.
*Questions:*
1. What theatrical convention creates the audience's suspense when Odewale vows to catch the killer?
   - *Answer:* **Dramatic Irony** (the audience knows the fatal truth that the character does not yet realize).
2. What makeup division transformed the 13-year-old student into the elderly seer?
   - *Answer:* **Character (Transformative) Makeup**.
3. What is Odewale's fatal flaw that accelerates his downfall?
   - *Answer:* His explosive, uncontrollable temper and rash pride (**Hamartia** and **Hubris**).
