## Hook
What happens when an artist masters all the traditional rules of the past? They begin to invent the future! In the world of dance, 20th-century dancers grew tired of the stiff corsets, pink pointe shoes, and rigid rules of classical ballet. They wanted to feel the earth beneath their bare feet, roll on the floor, and express real raw human emotions! That revolutionary movement gave birth to **Contemporary Dance**—a style that today powers global African arts and dynamic Afrobeats choreography. Meanwhile, in music, composers realized that simple notes (semibreve, minim, crotchet) were not flexible enough to capture the intricate syncopations and soulful hesitations of human speech and song. They invented dots that stretch time, curved bridges called **Ties** that connect beats across bar lines, silky curves called **Slurs**, and mysterious bird-eye symbols called **Fermatas** that make time stand completely still! In this lesson, you will master the expressive vocabulary of Contemporary Dance, conquer advanced musical extensions, and cross the official Sessional 80% Mastery Gate to qualify for your Terminal Examination!

## Key Terms
- Term: Contemporary Dance
- Definition: A versatile, expressive modern dance genre that merges elements of classical, modern, and traditional cultural styles, emphasizing freedom of movement, floor work, and gravity.
- Term: Improvisation
- Definition: The spontaneous creation of physical movements or musical notes in real time without pre-planned choreography or written scores.
- Term: Floor Work
- Definition: Choreographic movements executed close to or on the floor (kneeling, sliding, rolling, and inverted balances) central to contemporary dance technique.
- Term: Dotted Note
- Definition: A musical note accompanied by a small dot placed immediately to its right, which increases its duration value by exactly half of its original value.
- Term: Augmentation Dot Rule
- Definition: The universal musical arithmetic rule stating that a dot adds $50\%$ ($1/2$) of the note's base beat value to itself.
- Term: Dotted Minim
- Definition: A minim (2 beats) with a dot ($2 + 1 = 3\text{ beats}$), sustaining a sound for three beats.
- Term: Dotted Crotchet
- Definition: A crotchet (1 beat) with a dot ($1 + 0.5 = 1.5\text{ beats}$), equal in duration to three quavers.
- Term: Tie
- Definition: A curved horizontal line connecting two notes of the identical pitch, combining their duration values into a single uninterrupted sound.
- Term: Slur
- Definition: A curved horizontal line placed over or under two or more notes of different pitches, instructing the musician to perform them smoothly without separation (Legato).
- Term: Legato
- Definition: An Italian musical performance direction meaning to play or sing notes smoothly and seamlessly connected with no silence in between.
- Term: Fermata (Pause)
- Definition: A notation symbol shaped like a bird's eye (a semicircle enclosing a dot) indicating that a note or rest should be held longer than its standard duration.
- Term: Chromatic Scale
- Definition: A twelve-note musical scale composed entirely of consecutive semitones, encompassing all black and white keys within an octave.

In the 20th century, dancers rebelled against the artificial rigidity of classical European ballet:
- **Core Philosophies:**
  - *Freedom & Emotional Honesty:* Movement springs from genuine human emotion, breath, and natural body weight rather than strict geometric poses.
  - *Embracing Gravity:* While classical ballet dancers attempt to look weightless and defy gravity, contemporary dancers embrace gravity—falling, tumbling, and using the floor as an artistic partner (**Floor Work**).
  - *Bare Feet:* Dancers often perform barefoot to maintain direct, tactile contact with the stage floor.
  - *Contraction and Release:* Pioneer **Martha Graham** developed a technique based on the natural cycle of breathing: tightening the abdominal core (contraction) and expanding outward (release).
- **Contemporary African Dance:**
  - Modern Nigerian choreographers seamlessly blend traditional dance steps (the staccato kicks of *Bata*, the serpentine torso waves of *Swange*, and the aerial flips of *Atilogwu*) with contemporary floor work and street Afrobeats grooves, creating an electrifying global performance style!

### 3. The Mathematics of Dotted Notes
The dot is a mathematical multiplier:
$$\text{Value of Dotted Note} = \text{Base Value} + \frac{1}{2}(\text{Base Value})$$

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        DOTTED NOTE CALCULATIONS                        │
├───────────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────────┤
│ NOTE TYPE         │ BASE BEATS   │ DOT VALUE (HALF) │ TOTAL BEAT VALUE │
├───────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Dotted Semibreve  │ 4 beats      │ + 2 beats        │ = 6 beats        │
├───────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Dotted Minim      │ 2 beats      │ + 1 beat         │ = 3 beats        │
├───────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Dotted Crotchet   │ 1 beat       │ + 0.5 beat       │ = 1.5 beats      │
├───────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Dotted Quaver     │ 0.5 beat     │ + 0.25 beat      │ = 0.75 beats     │
└───────────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────────┘
```

### 4. Tie versus Slur: A Vital Musical Distinction
Though ties and slurs look almost identical (both are curved lines), their musical functions are completely different:
- **The Tie:**
  - Connects notes of the **SAME PITCH** (e.g., G to G, or C to C).
  - The second note is **not struck again**; rather, the first note is held continuously across the bar line for the combined duration of both notes.
  - *Example:* A crotchet (1 beat) tied to a minim (2 beats) on the same note creates one unbroken tone lasting $1 + 2 = 3\text{ beats}$.
- **The Slur:**
  - Connects notes of **DIFFERENT PITCHES** (e.g., C to E to G).
  - Every note is played, but smoothly connected with no silence or tongue attack between them (**Legato**).
  - On stringed instruments (violin), a slur indicates all notes must be played in a single bow stroke; for vocalists, it indicates singing several notes on a single breath or vowel syllable.

---

### 5. Diatonic versus Chromatic Scales
- **Diatonic Scale:** An 8-note scale moving in a fixed pattern of whole tones and semitones within a specific key (e.g., C Major: C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C).
- **Chromatic Scale:** A 12-note scale composed entirely of **consecutive semitones**. It includes every white and black key on the keyboard within an octave (e.g., C, C$\sharp$, D, D$\sharp$, E, F, F$\sharp$, G, G$\sharp$, A, A$\sharp$, B, C).

## Common Mistakes
1. **Thinking the Dot Doubles Note Value:** Believing a dotted minim is worth 4 beats ($2 \times 2$). The dot adds **HALF** of the note's value ($2 + 1 = 3\text{ beats}$)!
2. **Confusing Ties and Slurs:** Looking at two notes connected by a curve and calling it a tie when the pitches are different. If the notes are different pitches, it is a **slur**; if they are identical pitches, it is a **tie**.
3. **Assuming Contemporary Dance Has No Technique:** Thinking modern dance is "anything goes." Contemporary dancers undergo intense classical training to master core balance, floor work, and muscular control.
4. **Miscounting a Dotted Crotchet:** Forgetting that a dotted crotchet ($1.5\text{ beats}$) equals exactly **three quavers** ($0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5$).

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Calculating Dotted Note Equations
*Problem:* Calculate the total beat sum for the following musical bar:
$$\text{Dotted Minim} + \text{Dotted Crotchet} + \text{Quaver} = ? \text{ beats}$$
- **Step 1: Calculate the value of each note:**
  - Dotted Minim: $2 + (2 \div 2) = 2 + 1 = 3\text{ beats}$
  - Dotted Crotchet: $1 + (1 \div 2) = 1 + 0.5 = 1.5\text{ beats}$
  - Quaver: $0.5\text{ beat}$
- **Step 2: Add all values together:**
  $$\text{Total} = 3 + 1.5 + 0.5 = 5\text{ beats}$$
- **Conclusion:** The total duration is exactly 5 beats.

### Example 2: Analyzing Tie vs Slur in a Musical Score
*Scenario:* A student choir conductor is rehearsing a score. In Measure 3, the Soprano line shows a High C crotchet connected by a curved line to a High C minim in Measure 4. In Measure 5, the Alto line shows an F crotchet connected by a curved line to an A crotchet.
- **Analysis:**
  - *Measure 3 to 4 (Soprano):* The notes share the identical pitch (High C to High C). This is a **Tie**. The sopranos must sing one continuous sound lasting $1 + 2 = 3\text{ beats}$ without taking a new breath or re-articulating the syllable.
  - *Measure 5 (Alto):* The notes have different pitches (F to A). This is a **Slur**. The altos must sing both notes on a single breath, transitioning smoothly and seamlessly (*legato*).
