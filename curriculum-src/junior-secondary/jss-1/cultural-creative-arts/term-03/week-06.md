## Hook
When you speak in daily conversation, your words vanish into thin air in less than a second. But when a master dramatist takes up a pen, their words can thunder across centuries and continents, changing how millions of human beings view justice, love, courage, and destiny! In ancient Athens, thousands of citizens wept together in open-air stone theaters listening to the tragedies of Sophocles. In Renaissance London, Queen Elizabeth I was spellbound by William Shakespeare's immortal verse at The Globe Theatre. And right here in Nigeria, our playwrights have achieved the highest pinnacles of world literature! Did you know that in 1986, Nigerian playwright and poet **Professor Wole Soyinka** made global history by becoming the very first African in human history to be awarded the **Nobel Prize in Literature**? Alongside trailblazing pioneers like **Professor Zulu Sofola** (Africa's first published female dramatist) and **Ola Rotimi**, Nigerian playwrights have made African storytelling a global phenomenon. In this lesson, you will meet the classical masters, explore Shakespeare's universe, and study the legendary Nigerian dramatists who brought African drama to the world stage!

## Key Terms
- Term: Playwright (Dramatist)
- Definition: A creative author who writes plays, dramatic scripts, and theatrical dialogue.
- Term: Aeschylus (c. 525–456 BC)
- Definition: The ancient Greek dramatist known as the "Father of Tragedy" who introduced the second actor to the stage and authored the *Oresteia* trilogy.
- Term: Sophocles (c. 496–406 BC)
- Definition: The celebrated Athenian tragic playwright who introduced the third actor, established fixed chorus numbers, and authored *Oedipus Rex* and *Antigone*.
- Term: Aristophanes (c. 446–386 BC)
- Definition: The ancient Greek master known as the "Father of Comedy" who pioneered political satire and farce in plays like *The Clouds* and *The Birds*.
- Term: William Shakespeare (1564–1616)
- Definition: The foremost English playwright and poet of the Elizabethan and Jacobean eras, author of 37 plays (including *Hamlet*, *Macbeth*, and *Romeo and Juliet*) and co-owner of The Globe Theatre.
- Term: Professor Wole Soyinka (Born 1934)
- Definition: The legendary Nigerian dramatist, poet, and essayist who made history in 1986 as the first African recipient of the Nobel Prize in Literature; author of *Death and the King's Horseman*.
- Term: Professor Zulu Sofola (1935–1995)
- Definition: Nigeria's first published female playwright and the first female professor of theatre arts in Africa, celebrated for *Wedlock of the Gods*.
- Term: Professor Ola Rotimi (1938–2000)
- Definition: Renowned Nigerian dramatist and theatre director famous for infusing traditional proverbs into dramatic masterworks like *The Gods Are Not to Blame* and *Kurunmi*.
- Term: John Pepper (J.P.) Clark (1935–2020)
- Definition: Acclaimed Nigerian poet and playwright from the Niger Delta who captured indigenous Ijaw mythology and poetic tragedy in *Song of a Goat* and *Ozidi*.
- Term: Professor Femi Osofisan (Born 1946)
- Definition: Prominent Nigerian second-generation dramatist and social critic who reinterprets traditional myths from the perspective of the common people in plays like *Morountodun*.

## Core Concept
### 1. The Classical Greek Masters
[IMAGE: alt="Educational diagram and authentic visual representation of Notable Playwrights and Dramatists" caption="Notable Playwrights and Dramatists: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration"]

In 5th-century BC Athens, four dramatists defined the foundations of Western theatre:
1. **Aeschylus (The Pioneer of Conflict):**
   - Before Aeschylus, plays featured only one actor conversing with a chorus.
   - Aeschylus revolutionized drama by introducing the **second actor**, allowing genuine dramatic tension and face-to-face conflict between characters.
   - *Famous Work:* The *Oresteia* trilogy (*Agamemnon*, *The Libation Bearers*, *The Eumenides*).
2. **Sophocles (The Master of Character and Tragic Irony):**
   - Added a **third actor**, reduced chorus size to 15, and introduced painted scene backdrops.
   - Explored deep moral dilemmas where good people clash with divine fate.
   - *Famous Work:* *Oedipus Rex* (the model of perfect dramatic plot) and *Antigone*.
3. **Euripides (The Rebel and Realist):**
   - Portrayed human beings as they really were, showing complex psychology, strong female heroines, and victims of war.
   - *Famous Work:* *Medea* and *The Trojan Women*.
4. **Aristophanes (The Father of Comedy):**
   - Used biting humor, caricature, and outrageous farce to mock warmongering politicians, corrupt philosophers, and foolish civic customs.
   - *Famous Work:* *The Clouds*, *The Frogs*, and *Lysistrata*.

### 2. William Shakespeare: The English Titan
Born in Stratford-upon-Avon, England, **William Shakespeare** is universally recognized as the greatest dramatist in the English language:
- **The Elizabethan Golden Age:** Writing during the reign of Queen Elizabeth I and King James I, Shakespeare was the resident playwright of the King's Men acting troupe at **The Globe Theatre** in London.
- **Master of Diverse Genres:**
  - *Tragedies:* *Hamlet*, *Macbeth*, *Othello*, *King Lear*, *Romeo and Juliet*.
  - *Comedies:* *A Midsummer Night's Dream*, *Twelfth Night*, *The Merchant of Venice*.
  - *Histories:* *Julius Caesar*, *Henry V*, *Richard III*.
- **Linguistic Genius:** Shakespeare wrote primarily in **blank verse** (unrhymed iambic pentameter) and coined hundreds of words and idiomatic expressions still used today across the English-speaking world.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Assuming Wole Soyinka Was the First Nigerian to Write:** Soyinka was the first **African to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1986)**, a global honor recognizing supreme literary achievement.
2. **Confusing Zulu Sofola with Other Playwrights:** Zulu Sofola was Nigeria's **first published female playwright**; she wrote *Wedlock of the Gods*, while Ola Rotimi wrote *The Gods Are Not to Blame*.
3. **Misspelling Dramatists' Names:** Misspelling "Aeschylus" or "Soyinka"; precision in academic attribution is essential in cultural arts.
4. **Thinking Classical Greek Theatre Featured Women Actors:** In ancient Greece, all roles (including female characters like Medea and Antigone) were played exclusively by masked male actors!

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Comparing Source and Adaptation
*Problem:* Compare Sophocles' Greek tragedy *Oedipus Rex* with Ola Rotimi's Nigerian drama *The Gods Are Not to Blame*.
- **The Original (*Oedipus Rex* by Sophocles):**
  - Set in ancient Thebes, Greece.
  - Protagonist: King Oedipus.
  - Seer: Tiresias (blind prophet of Apollo).
  - Cultural Context: Classical Greek mythology and Olympian oracles.
- **The Adaptation (*The Gods Are Not to Blame* by Ola Rotimi):**
  - Set in the Yoruba land of Kutuje, Nigeria.
  - Protagonist: King Odewale.
  - Seer: Baba Fakunle (blind priest of Ifa oracle).
  - Cultural Context: Yoruba chieftaincy traditions, ancestral proverbs, drumming, and *Ogun* warrior oaths.
- **Significance:** Illustrates how great Nigerian dramatists adapt universal human themes into authentic indigenous African cultural frameworks!

### Example 2: Recognizing Theatrical Milestones
*Problem:* Identify the historical significance of the year 1986 in Nigerian and African cultural history.
- **Historical Milestone:** In October 1986, the Swedish Academy awarded the **Nobel Prize in Literature** to Nigerian playwright and poet **Professor Wole Soyinka**.
- **Significance:** This marked the first time in the 85-year history of the Nobel Prize that an African writer was chosen for the world's most prestigious literary honor, validating the global supremacy of African dramatic literature.
