## Hook
Imagine standing before a massive, breathtaking mural in a modern art gallery in Lagos, depicting a vibrant traditional festival with dancers, drummers, and royal princes on horseback. The colors glow with electric intensity—the red robes seem to burst out from the canvas, the sky blends into a soft evening twilight, and if you lean in close, you can see thick, sculpted ridges of oil paint standing up like physical mountain ranges! Have you ever wondered how artists turn colored powders from rocks, plants, and chemical labs into liquid magic that can survive on canvas for hundreds of years? The supreme visual fine art that captures light, mood, and storytelling on a flat surface is **Painting**. From delicate, transparent water washes to rich, buttery, sculptural brushstrokes, painting is the ultimate language of color. In this lesson, you will master the four major painting media, understand the anatomy of paint brushes and palettes, explore painting techniques like *wash* and *impasto*, and learn to paint like an accomplished artist!

## Key Terms
- Term: Painting
- Definition: The visual fine art practice of applying colored pigment suspended in a liquid binder onto a two-dimensional supporting surface (such as canvas, paper, wood, or plaster) to create aesthetic imagery.
- Term: Pigment
- Definition: Finely ground mineral, biological, or synthetic colored powder that gives paint its characteristic color hue.
- Term: Binder (Medium / Vehicle)
- Definition: The liquid substance that holds pigment particles together in suspension and adheres the paint securely to the supporting surface upon drying.
- Term: Solvent (Thinner)
- Definition: A liquid used to dilute, thin the consistency of paint, and clean brushes (e.g., water for watercolor and acrylics; turpentine or white spirit for oil paint).
- Term: Watercolor
- Definition: A transparent painting medium consisting of fine pigment bound with gum arabic and thinned with water, celebrated for its lightness and luminous washes.
- Term: Poster Color (Gouache)
- Definition: An opaque, water-based paint bound with gum arabic and inert white chalk (calcium carbonate), producing flat, matte, solid color surfaces ideal for commercial graphic design.
- Term: Oil Paint
- Definition: A slow-drying, rich painting medium consisting of pigment ground in drying vegetable oils (primarily linseed oil), celebrated for its deep blending, glazes, and durability.
- Term: Acrylic Paint
- Definition: A versatile modern painting medium consisting of pigment suspended in an acrylic polymer emulsion; water-soluble when wet, but drying into a water-resistant, flexible plastic film.
- Term: Canvas
- Definition: A heavy, woven textile fabric (traditionally unbleached linen or cotton duck) stretched tightly over a wooden strainer frame and primed with white gesso to receive oil or acrylic paint.
- Term: Impasto
- Definition: A bold painting technique where thick, buttery paint is laid onto the canvas heavily with a brush or palette knife, leaving visible sculptural brush marks in relief.

### 3. Essential Tools and Equipment of the Painter
- **Brushes:**
  - *Round Brush:* Pointed tip used for fine delicate details, crisp outlines, and controlled curves.
  - *Flat Brush:* Square bristle edge used for spreading wide horizontal washes and sharp rectangular blocks of color.
  - *Filbert Brush:* Oval curved tip combining the broad coverage of a flat brush with the soft edges of a round brush.
  - *Bristle Types:* Stiff coarse hog bristles (for heavy oils and acrylics); soft synthetic or animal sable hair (for fluid watercolors).
- **Palette:** A flat oval or rectangular board (wood, plastic, or glass) held by the artist with a thumb-hole, used for laying out colors and mixing custom tints.
- **Easel:** A sturdy wooden tripod or standing H-frame that holds the canvas upright at eye level while the artist stands or sits to paint.
- **Palette Knife:** A flexible steel spatula used for scraping the palette clean, mixing paints, or directly applying thick, textural paint to canvas.
- **Supports (Surfaces):** Heavyweight watercolor paper (300 gsm), primed stretched canvas, Masonite wood panels, or plaster mural walls.

### 4. Master Painting Techniques
Professional painters utilize diverse application methods to achieve different visual textures:

```

├───────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

├───────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Flat Wash          │ Applying a thin, watery layer of watercolor    │
```

## Common Mistakes
1. **Adding White to Watercolor to Make it Lighter:** In traditional watercolor painting, there is no white paint! To make a color lighter, you add **more water**, allowing the brilliant white paper underneath to shine through.
2. **Thinning Oil Paint with Water:** Water and oil do not mix! Attempting to thin oil paint with water ruins the paint. Oil paint must be thinned with **turpentine** or **linseed oil**.
3. **Allowing Acrylic Paint to Dry on Brushes:** Acrylic paint dries into a tough, waterproof plastic. If an artist forgets to wash an acrylic brush in water immediately after painting, the bristles will harden into a permanent solid block of rock-hard plastic!
4. **Painting Thick Over Thin in Oil Painting:** In oil painting, the golden rule is **"Fat over Lean"** (flexible oily paint layered on top of fast-drying lean paint) to prevent the top surface from cracking.

## Worked Examples

### Scenario: Selecting the Best Medium and Technique for an Artwork
*Problem:* An artist in Osogbo is commissioned to create three different artworks:
1. A quick, spontaneous landscape study of the morning mist over the Osun River, capturing soft, blending clouds.
2. A large, durable outdoor historical portrait of the Ataoja of Osogbo in royal coronation robes with thick, textured, sculptural golden beads on the crown.
3. A crisp, flat, solid-colored commercial poster for a school athletic inter-house sports festival.
*Specify the optimal painting medium and primary technique for each assignment.*

*Solution:*
1. **Morning Mist River Study:** Use **Watercolor** using the **Wet-on-Wet** technique. The transparent water-soluble pigments will naturally bleed and diffuse on dampened paper, capturing the ethereal morning mist perfectly.
2. **Royal Coronation Portrait:** Use **Oil Paint** (or heavy-body **Acrylic Paint**) on primed canvas, applying the **Impasto** technique with a palette knife to sculpt the thick, physical 3D gold beads on the royal crown.
3. **Inter-House Sports Poster:** Use **Poster Color (Gouache)** on heavy cartridge paper using a **Flat Wash / Solid Opaque** technique to create uniform, bold, matte lettering and bright graphic shapes.

## Guardian Sync
- **The Watercolor Paper Transparency Check:** If your child has a watercolor set, show them that in watercolor painting, the white of the paper is the "white paint." Challenge them to paint a cloud simply by leaving the white paper untouched while washing blue sky around it!
- **Brush Cleaning Discipline:** Remind your child that professional artists always wash their paintbrushes with soap and water immediately after use, reshaping the bristle tips with their fingers and drying them flat.
- **Home Painting Discussion:** Point out any framed paintings or decorative prints hanging on your home walls. Discuss whether the painting has a flat smooth surface (like a wash) or thick, textured ridges of paint (like *impasto*)!
