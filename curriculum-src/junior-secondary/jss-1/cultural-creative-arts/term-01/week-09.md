## Hook
When European travelers and archaeologists first arrived in West Africa in the 19th and early 20th centuries, they believed the false racist myth that Africa was an uncivilized continent with no history of great art. But when the earth of Nigeria opened up, it shattered their prejudices and stunned the entire world! In Kaduna, miners unearthed 2,500-year-old terracotta sculptures with piercing triangular eyes crafted by the **Nok Culture**—the oldest iron-age sculpture civilization in sub-Saharan Africa. In Ile-Ife, archaeologists discovered royal bronze heads so breathtakingly realistic and lifelike that European scholars could not believe human hands had cast them! In Benin City, royal guilds produced thousands of bronze palace plaques and the legendary **Queen Mother Idia Ivory Mask** that became the global symbol of **FESTAC '77**. And in Igbo-Ukwu, villagers digging a water well unearthed 1,100-year-old bronze vessels so delicately wrapped in cast bronze rope cages that modern metalworkers still marvel at their genius! Nigeria is not just an oil nation; it is the cradle of African classical civilization! In this lesson, you will journey through these four ancient art cultures and unlock your **Term 1 80% Formative Mastery Gate**!

## Key Terms
- Term: Art Heritage
- Definition: The legacy of physical artworks, architectural monuments, sculptures, and cultural traditions inherited from past civilizations that define a nation's historic identity.
- Term: Nok Culture
- Definition: The ancient iron-smelting and terracotta sculpture civilization that flourished in central Nigeria (Kaduna, Plateau, and Niger States) from approximately **500 BC to 200 AD**.
- Term: Terracotta
- Definition: Coarse, porous brownish-red earthenware clay that is modeled by hand and baked in a bonfire or kiln until hard and durable.
- Term: Ife Art
- Definition: The classical Yoruba bronze, brass, and terracotta sculpture tradition of Ile-Ife (11th to 15th Century AD), renowned for its lifelike naturalism and royal striations.
- Term: Benin Art
- Definition: The royal court art tradition of the Kingdom of Benin (13th to 19th Century AD), famous for commemorative bronze heads, palace wall plaques, and royal ivory carvings.
- Term: Lost-Wax Casting (*Cire Perdue*)
- Definition: An intricate metallurgical casting technique where a sculpture is first modeled in beeswax, coated in clay, melted out with heat, and replaced with molten bronze.
- Term: Queen Mother Idia Mask
- Definition: A magnificent 16th-century Benin royal ivory pendant mask depicting the mother of Oba Esigie, adopted as the official global emblem of **FESTAC '77**.
- Term: Igbo-Ukwu Art
- Definition: An ancient 9th-century AD bronze-casting civilization discovered in Anambra State, renowned for highly ornate ritual vessels with delicate surface filigree and cast rope cages.
- Term: British Punitive Expedition of 1897
- Definition: The British military invasion of Benin City in 1897 that burned the royal palace, deposed Oba Ovonramwen, and looted over 3,000 priceless bronze and ivory royal treasures.
- Term: Term 1 Mastery Gate (80% Benchmark)
- Definition: The formal summative academic evaluation requiring learners to achieve an 80% competency benchmark across all Term 1 visual art domains before advancing to official terminal examination ranking.

### 2. In-Depth Study of the Four Civilizations

#### A. The Nok Culture (500 BC – 200 AD)
- **Discovery:** In 1928, tin miners washing gravel in the village of Nok near the Jos plateau uncovered strange terracotta heads. British archaeologist Bernard Fagg recognized their antiquity and led extensive excavations.
- **Sculptural Style:** Nok heads are stylized and expressive. Their most defining signature is the **triangular or D-shaped eyes** with pupils pierced right through the clay. The nostrils, ears, and mouths are also pierced with holes. This functional technique prevented thick clay from cracking during bonfire baking while creating dramatic, shadow-filled eyes.
- **Iron Smelting:** Nok people were among the earliest iron workers in Africa, operating bloomery furnaces alongside their sophisticated pottery!

#### B. Classical Ife Art (11th – 15th Century AD)
- **Spiritual Homeland:** Ile-Ife is revered as the ancestral cradle of the Yoruba people (*Ilé-Ifẹ̀ Ilé Ọlọ́jọ́*).
- **The Wonder of Naturalism:** While Nok art is stylized, Ife art is breathtakingly **naturalistic and lifelike**—the royal heads look like living human beings frozen in serene contemplation.
- **Key Features:**
  - Delicate vertical lines (**facial striations**) carved down the cheeks and forehead.
  - Dignified beaded crowns (*Ori Ade*).
  - Small holes drilled along the hairline and lower jaw, where ancient courtiers attached real human hair or bead veils to conceal the King's sacred face during ceremonies.

#### C. The Court Art of the Kingdom of Benin (13th – 19th Century AD)
- **Origin of Guild:** Oral tradition records that Oba Oguola (late 13th century) invited a master brass smith named **Iguegha** from Ile-Ife to establish the royal bronze casters' guild at **Igun Street** in Benin City.
- **Commemorative Oba Heads:** When an Oba passed away, his successor commissioned a heavy bronze memorial head to be placed on a royal ancestral altar. A massive, intricately carved elephant ivory tusk was inserted into the hollow top of the bronze head!
- **Palace Wall Plaques:** Hundreds of bronze relief rectangular plaques showing Obas, Portuguese soldiers with muskets, and palace warriors were nailed to the timber pillars of the royal palace.
- **The Queen Mother (Idia) Mask:** In the 16th century, Oba Esigie honored his brilliant military mother, Queen Idia, by commissioning an ivory hip-pendant mask. Carved with stylized Portuguese heads along the crown, it was selected in 1977 as the official global emblem of **FESTAC '77**.
- **The British Invasion (1897):** During the Punitive Expedition, British troops burned Benin City and looted thousands of royal bronzes and ivories, which remain the subject of international restitution demands today.

#### D. The Igbo-Ukwu Bronze Wonder (9th Century AD)
- **Accidental Discovery:** In 1938, a local man named **Isaiah Anozie** was digging a water cistern in his compound in Igbo-Ukwu (Anambra State) when his hoe struck a cache of ancient metal objects. Archaeologist Thurstan Shaw excavated the site in 1959.
- **Masterpieces of Leaded Bronze:**
  - *The Roped Pot:* An elegant globular bronze water vessel enclosed inside an intricate, separate bronze rope net with handles—all cast as a single technical tour de force!
  - *The Bronze Snail Shell:* A bronze vessel shaped like a natural African snail shell, surmounted by a miniature leopard resting on the rim.
- **Decorative Mastery:** Unlike the smooth surfaces of Ife, Igbo-Ukwu bronzes are covered in micro-ornamentation: tiny spirals, raised granules, crickets, flies, and geometric latticework, demonstrating peerless metallurgical sophistication in the 9th century!

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Nok Eyes with Ife Eyes:** Nok eyes are geometric, triangular, or D-shaped with pierced holes; Ife eyes are almond-shaped, naturalistic, and lifelike.
2. **Believing Benin Bronze Casting was Taught by Europeans:** The Portuguese did not arrive in Benin until 1485, whereas the Igun Street bronze casting guild was established centuries earlier by master Iguegha from Ile-Ife!
3. **Assuming Igbo-Ukwu Art Dates to Recent Centuries:** Radiocarbon tests confirmed that Igbo-Ukwu flourished in the **9th century AD**—over 1,100 years ago, pre-dating European contact by more than 500 years!
4. **Thinking Terracotta is Unbaked Clay:** Terracotta is baked in fire; raw unbaked clay dissolves in water, whereas terracotta is permanent ceramic rock!

## Worked Examples

### Scenario: Identifying Ancient Nigerian Art Civilizations
*Problem:* Identify which ancient Nigerian art culture created each of the following archaeological artifacts:
1. A 16th-century carved ivory pendant mask of a Queen Mother, which served as the official mascot of FESTAC '77.
2. A 2,300-year-old terracotta head with hollow pierced pupils, nostrils, and mouth, accompanied by iron smelting slag.
3. An 1,100-year-old leaded bronze pot intricately wrapped inside a cast bronze rope cage, discovered in Anambra State.
4. A highly naturalistic cast bronze royal head with fine vertical linear striations across the face and a beaded diadem.

*Solution:*
1. **Queen Mother Ivory Mask:** **Kingdom of Benin Art** (depicting Queen Idia).
2. **Terracotta Head with Pierced Pupils:** **Nok Culture** (Kaduna/Plateau).
3. **Bronze Roped Pot:** **Igbo-Ukwu Art** (excavated in Anambra State).
4. **Naturalistic Striated Head:** **Ancient Ife Art** (Ile-Ife, Osun State).
