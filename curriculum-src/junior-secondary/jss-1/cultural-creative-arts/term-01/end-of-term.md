## Key Terms
- Term: Visual Arts
- Definition: Art forms that produce physical objects or images appreciated primarily through the sense of sight, divided into Fine Arts and Applied/Industrial Arts.
- Term: Fine Arts
- Definition: Visual art created primarily for intellectual and aesthetic appreciation rather than practical utility, including painting, drawing, and non-functional sculpture.
- Term: Applied (Industrial) Arts
- Definition: The integration of artistic design into functional, everyday utilitarian objects, including textile design, ceramics, graphic design, and interior decoration.
- Term: Graphite Pencil Scale
- Definition: The standardized grading system measuring core hardness and darkness, ranging from hard, light H-grades (`9H`–`H`) to soft, black B-grades (`B`–`9B`).
- Term: Shading
- Definition: The artistic application of varying tonal values of light and darkness to create the illusion of three-dimensional volume, mass, and depth on a flat surface.
- Term: Elements of Art
- Definition: The seven foundational visual ingredients used by artists: Line, Shape, Form, Color, Texture, Value, and Space.
- Term: Principles of Design
- Definition: The governing organizational rules that dictate how elements are arranged: Balance, Proportion, Harmony, Rhythm, Variety, Contrast, and Dominance.
- Term: Color Wheel
- Definition: A circular organizational diagram displaying the chromatic relationships among Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary colors.
- Term: Indigenous Crafts
- Definition: Skilled manual handiwork producing functional and decorative articles using local sustainable raw materials (such as *Aso-Oke*, *Akwete*, *Adire*, pottery, and leatherwork).
- Term: Classical Nigerian Heritage
- Definition: The glorious lineage of ancient archaeological sculpture civilizations, including Nok (500 BC), Ife (11th–15th C.), Benin (13th–19th C.), and Igbo-Ukwu (9th C.).

### 2. Systematic Synthesis of Term 01 Core Disciplines

#### A. Scope of CCA and Visual Arts
- **Three Branches:** Visual Arts (Fine & Applied), Performing Arts (Music, Dance, Drama), Literary Arts (Poetry, Prose, Plays).
- **Fine vs. Applied:** Fine art is contemplative/aesthetic (a framed canvas painting); Applied art is utilitarian/functional (a woven *Aso-Oke* robe or ceramic tea mug).
- **Careers:** Graphic designers, fashion creators, animators, painters, sculptors, ceramicists, and art curators.

#### B. The Science of Drawing & Pencil Grading
- **Pencil Scale:** Hard `H` pencils (`2H`, `4H`) have high clay content and produce faint, precise technical lines; Soft `B` pencils (`2B`, `4B`, `6B`) have high graphite content and produce dark, velvety lines for artistic shading.
- **Shading Techniques:** Hatching (parallel lines), Cross-hatching (intersecting lines), Stippling (dot density), and Blending (smooth smudging with a tortillon).
- **Drawing Genres:** Still Life (inanimate objects), Nature (botanical plants), Figure/Life (human anatomy), Portrait (human face), Landscape (outdoor scenery).

#### C. Elements of Art & Principles of Design
- **Elements (The Ingredients):** Line, Shape (2D flat), Form (3D mass/volume), Color, Texture (Tactile real touch vs. Visual implied illusion), Value (lightness/darkness), Space (Positive subject vs. Negative background).
- **Principles (The Recipe):** Balance (Symmetrical formal mirror, Asymmetrical informal equilibrium, Radial circular), Proportion (Western mathematical vs. African spiritual *Ori* emphasis), Rhythm/Movement, Harmony/Unity, Variety, Contrast, Dominance/Focal Point.

#### D. Color Theory and Harmonies
- **Newton 1666:** White sunlight splits through a prism into **ROYGBIV**.
- **Color Wheel:** Primary (Red, Yellow, Blue); Secondary (Orange, Green, Purple); Tertiary (Primary named first: Yellow-Green, Blue-Green, Red-Orange).
- **Temperature:** Warm colors (Red, Orange, Yellow) advance forward; Cool colors (Blue, Green, Violet) recede backward.
- **Tints & Shades:** Tint = Color + White; Shade = Color + Black.
- **Harmonies:** Monochromatic (single hue variations), Complementary (opposites on wheel: Red/Green, Blue/Orange), Analogous (neighbors on wheel).

#### E. Traditional Nigerian Crafts
- **Weaving:** *Aso-Oke* on narrow horizontal strip-looms in Iseyin/Oyo; *Akwete* on wide vertical frame-looms by Ndoki women in Abia State.
- **Pottery:** Traditional hand-coiling and open pit-firing; legendary pioneer **Dr. Hadiza Ladi Kwali** of Suleja, depicted on the **₦20 banknote**.
- **Leatherwork:** Tanning Red Sokoto goatskins in ancient Kano tanneries (*Kurmi Market*).
- **Adire Dyeing:** Abeokuta (Ogun State); *Adire Oniko* (tied with raffia) vs. *Adire Eleko* (cassava starch paste resist).
- **Calabash Decoration:** Pyrography (hot-iron poker burning) in Oyo, Adamawa, and Plateau.
- **Bronze Guild:** Hereditary royal casters on **Igun Street** in Benin City.

#### F. Painting Media & Techniques
- **Chemistry:** Pigment (powdered color), Binder (adhesive vehicle), Solvent (thinner).
- **Media:** Watercolor (gum arabic / water; transparent), Poster Color (gum arabic + chalk / water; opaque matte), Oil Paint (linseed oil / turpentine; slow-drying, rich), Acrylic Paint (synthetic polymer emulsion / water; waterproof plastic film when dry).
- **Techniques:** Flat wash, Wet-on-wet, Dry brush, Impasto (thick 3D sculptural ridges), Glazing.

#### G. Classical Nigerian Art Heritage
- **Nok Culture (500 BC – 200 AD):** Oldest in sub-Saharan Africa (Kaduna/Plateau); fired terracotta; iconic triangular/D-shaped pierced pupils, nostrils, and mouth; early iron smelting.
- **Ancient Ife (11th–15th C. AD):** Spiritual cradle of Yoruba; cast bronze and terracotta royal portraits; supreme lifelike naturalism; fine vertical facial striations.
- **Kingdom of Benin (13th–19th C. AD):** Court art for the Oba; Igun Street guild established by Iguegha; lost-wax (*cire perdue*) commemorative altar heads; palace wall plaques; **Queen Mother Idia Ivory Mask** (emblem of **FESTAC '77**); looted in 1897 British Punitive Expedition.
- **Igbo-Ukwu (9th C. AD):** Excavated in Anambra State (discovered by Isaiah Anozie); 1,100-year-old leaded bronzes; The Roped Pot; exquisite micro-ornamentation and raised granules.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Confusing Elements of Art with Principles of Design:** Lines, shapes, and colors are *Elements* (ingredients); Balance, contrast, and rhythm are *Principles* (the organizational recipe).
2. **Using a Hard Pencil to Shade Dark Shadows:** Hard pencils like 2H scratch paper; dark shading requires soft B pencils like **4B** or **6B**.
3. **Thinning Oil Paint with Water:** Water and oil do not mix! Oil paint must be thinned with **turpentine** or **linseed oil**.
4. **Believing African Sculptural Proportions Were Mistakes:** Traditional African artists intentionally sculpted large heads to celebrate *Ori* (spiritual destiny and intellect).
5. **Thinking Benin Bronze Casting Began with the Portuguese:** The bronze casters' guild at Igun Street was established in the 13th century by master Iguegha from Ife, long before Europeans arrived in 1485.
