## Hook
Imagine walking through a bustling sports stadium in London, Paris, or Tokyo during an international athletics championship. The stadium announcer’s voice echoes across sixty thousand spectators: *"Ladies and gentlemen, please rise for the National Anthem of the Federal Republic of Nigeria!"* Instantly, sixty thousand people of all nationalities stop talking, rise to their feet, and stand in total, reverent silence. Even foreign heads of state remove their hats and stand at rigid attention out of absolute respect for our sovereign country! Now imagine an ordinary citizen in a local marketplace who hears the national anthem over the radio, laughs, kicks a muddy green-white-green flag into a gutter, and throws Naira notes on the ground to be stomped on by party guests. What a heartbreaking contrast! Why do laws exist that treat the desecration of national symbols as a crime? Because disrespecting your national symbols is disrespecting your motherland, your heritage, and yourself! Let us master the sacred etiquette of our national emblems!

## Key Terms
- Term: Desecration
- Definition: The act of treating a sacred object, state emblem, or national symbol with violent disrespect, contempt, defacement, or irreverence.
- Term: Flags and Coats of Arms Act
- Definition: The federal statutory legislation in Nigeria that regulates the display, proportions, protocols, and penalties for the misuse or desecration of the National Flag and Coat of Arms.
- Term: Hoisting at Sunrise
- Definition: The ceremonial protocol of raising the National Flag swiftly and briskly at 6:00 AM (dawn) on official buildings and ships.
- Term: Lowering at Sunset
- Definition: The dignified protocol of lowering the National Flag slowly, solemnly, and ceremoniously at 6:00 PM (dusk).
- Term: Flying at Half-Mast
- Definition: The universal protocol of flying a flag halfway down the flagpole as a profound symbol of national mourning, sorrow, or remembrance following the death of an eminent state leader.
- Term: Standing at Attention
- Definition: The mandatory physical posture of standing completely upright, motionless, with heels together, eyes forward, and arms resting straight at the sides during the rendition of the national anthem.
- Term: Currency Mutilation
- Definition: Any unlawful act of spraying, stepping on, writing on, stapling, tearing, or defacing legal tender banknotes, prohibited under Section 21 of the Central Bank of Nigeria Act 2007.
- Term: Position of Honor
- Definition: The pre-eminent flagpole position (the extreme right from the viewer's perspective, or the center in a cluster of flags) legally reserved strictly for the National Flag.
- Term: Retirement of the Flag
- Definition: The dignified, private ceremony of destroying a torn, faded, or unserviceable national flag (traditionally by dignified private burning) so it is not subjected to dishonor.
- Term: Civic Etiquette
- Definition: The established code of polite, reverent, and lawful behavior observed by citizens toward their sovereign state and its national emblems.

## Core Concept
### Why Must National Symbols Be Respected?
National symbols are the tangible embodiment of Nigeria's sovereignty, dignity, and collective independence. Disrespecting a national flag, stomping on currency, or mocking the national anthem is an attack on the sovereign authority of the state.

### Strict Protocols for Handling the National Flag
Under the **Flags and Coats of Arms Act**, strict regulations govern the display and handling of the green-white-green banner:
1. **Timing:** The flag is hoisted briskly at sunrise (**6:00 AM**) and lowered slowly and ceremoniously at sunset (**6:00 PM**). It should not remain flying overnight in the dark unless illuminated by dedicated spotlights.
2. **Never Touch the Ground:** The flag must never touch the earth, floor, or water. When lowering the flag, ceremonial handlers must catch it carefully and fold it neatly.
3. **Flown on the Highest Mast:** When flown alongside state flags (e.g., Lagos or Kano State flag), corporate flags, or school banners, the Nigerian National Flag must occupy the **position of honor** (the highest pole, or the center pole). No foreign or private flag may fly higher than the Nigerian flag on Nigerian soil!
4. **Flying at Half-Mast:** The flag is flown at half-mast (first raised to the peak of the pole, then solemnly lowered to the halfway mark) only upon an official proclamation by the **President of Nigeria** during periods of national mourning or upon the death of an eminent head of state.
5. **Retirement of Damaged Flags:** A faded, soiled, or torn flag must never be displayed outside any public building. It must be reverently retired and destroyed in private, usually by dignified burning, so that its pieces are not scattered in trash heaps.

### Etiquette During the National Anthem and Pledge
Whenever the National Anthem is played or sung:
- **Immediate Halt:** Every citizen within hearing distance must stop walking, riding, or engaging in conversations.
- **Physical Stance:** Stand at rigid attention with your body straight, chest out, eyes looking forward, and hands held straight along the seams of your trousers or skirt.
- **Removal of Headgear:** Gentlemen and boys must remove non-religious caps or hats.
- **Reciting the Pledge:** When reciting the National Pledge, place your right hand flat across the left side of your chest (over your heart), symbolizing sincerity and personal loyalty.

---

### Legal Penalties for Desecrating the National Currency
The **Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Act 2007 (Section 21)** strictly outlaws the abuse and mutilation of Naira banknotes:
- **Prohibited Conduct:**
  - Spraying Naira notes during parties, weddings, or concerts.
  - Stepping on, dancing on, or trampling banknotes on dance floors.
  - Writing notes, drawing caricatures, or stamping telephone numbers on currency.
  - Folding currency into tight balls, squeezing, or stapling banknotes with metal staples.
  - Hawking, selling, or trading new currency notes for higher amounts.
- **Statutory Penalties:** Any person who commits currency mutilation or abuse is guilty of a criminal offense and, upon conviction, is liable to a **fine of not less than ₦50,000**, imprisonment for a term of not less than **six months**, or both!

---

### Prohibitions Regarding the National Coat of Arms
Under Section 5 of the Flags and Coats of Arms Act:
- It is a criminal offense to use the National Coat of Arms or state seals for unauthorized private commercial branding, private business letterheads, or marketing advertisements without written presidential authorization.
- The Coat of Arms is reserved exclusively for the President, Governors, the National Assembly, courts of law, ministries, and diplomatic missions.

## Common Mistakes
- **Assuming Spraying Naira is Just a Cultural Celebration:** While celebrating is fine, spraying and dancing on Naira notes is a severe criminal offense under Section 21 of the CBN Act. Citizens should place cash gifts neatly in gift envelopes or use electronic money transfers!
- **Thinking Only Soldiers Must Stand for the Anthem:** Every single citizen—students, traders, teachers, bankers, and visitors—is legally and morally required to stand at attention when the national anthem is performed.
- **Leaving the National Flag Flying in Heavy Rains:** Flapping in violent rainstorms ruins flag fabric. Flags should be lowered before severe storms or properly secured.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Prosecuting Currency Abuse
**Scenario:** A popular socialite attends a high-society birthday party in Lagos, stands on a table, and sprays bundles of crisp ₦1,000 notes onto the dance floor, where hundreds of guests trample the notes with high heels. The video goes viral on social media.
- **Legal Enforcement:** The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) and the Central Bank of Nigeria track the offender, effect an arrest, and arraign them in Federal High Court.
- **Court Verdict:** The defendant is convicted under Section 21 of the CBN Act 2007 and sentenced to six months imprisonment or a heavy fine.
- **Civic Lesson:** The law protects our sovereign currency from public humiliation!

### Example 2: Hoisting Flags on National Assembly Grounds
**Scenario:** A delegation from a foreign parliament visits the National Assembly in Abuja. The ceremonial team must hoist the Nigerian flag, the foreign country's flag, and the ECOWAS flag.
- **Correct Protocol:** The Nigerian National Flag must occupy the central, most prominent position and must be hoisted first and lowered last.
- **Rule of Precedence:** On Nigerian territory, the sovereign national flag never defers to any foreign flag.
