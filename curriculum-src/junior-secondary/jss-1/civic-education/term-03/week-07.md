## Hook
Imagine a giant passenger bus traveling down the highway from Abuja to Kaduna. Three people share control of the vehicle: one person holds the steering wheel to guide the direction, another person controls the fuel accelerator and brakes, and a third person holds the official road safety navigation map, checking that the bus does not exceed the legal speed limit or drive off the cliff! What would happen if a single person tried to steer, pump the gas, read the map, serve drinks, and inspect tickets all at the same time at 120 kilometers per hour? Catastrophe! The bus would crash within minutes. In the governance of a great nation, that is exactly why the 1999 Constitution separates government power among three distinct, co-equal branches: the **Legislature**, the **Executive**, and the **Judiciary**! How do these three branches work together to steer the Federal Republic of Nigeria? Let us step inside the halls of power!

## Key Terms
- Term: Arms of Government
- Definition: The three distinct, autonomous organs of state authority established by the constitution to perform the functions of lawmaking (Legislature), law enforcement (Executive), and law interpretation (Judiciary).
- Term: Bicameral Legislature
- Definition: A legislative body consisting of two separate chambers or houses (in Nigeria: the Senate and the House of Representatives forming the National Assembly).
- Term: Unicameral Legislature
- Definition: A lawmaking parliament consisting of only one single chamber (such as the State Houses of Assembly in each of the 36 states of Nigeria).
- Term: Bill
- Definition: A formal, written legislative proposal for a new law or an amendment to an existing law presented before parliament for debate and enactment.
- Term: Act of Parliament
- Definition: A bill that has been successfully passed by both chambers of the legislature and received presidential assent (or had a veto overridden), becoming statutory law.
- Term: Presidential Veto
- Definition: The constitutional power of the President to refuse to sign or approve a bill passed by the National Assembly, returning it with objections.
- Term: Veto Override
- Definition: The constitutional power of the National Assembly to pass a bill into law despite a presidential veto by a two-thirds (2/3) majority vote in both chambers.
- Term: Supreme Court of Nigeria
- Definition: The apex, highest, and final appellate court of record in the Nigerian judicial hierarchy, headed by the Chief Justice of Nigeria (CJN).
- Term: Judicial Review
- Definition: The power of superior courts of law to examine the actions of the legislative and executive arms and invalidate any act or policy that breaches the Constitution.
- Term: Impeachment
- Definition: A formal constitutional process by which the legislature investigates, charges, and may remove a serving President or Governor from office for gross misconduct.

## Core Concept
### The Constitutional Architecture of State Power
Under Sections 4, 5, and 6 of the **1999 Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria**, government power is deliberately distributed across three independent arms. This institutional design ensures that no single individual or body can accumulate tyrannical authority:

### In-Depth Analysis: The Legislature (Section 4)
The lawmaking arm of the federation:
- **Structure:** Nigeria operates a **Bicameral National Assembly** in Abuja:
  1. *The Senate (Upper Chamber):* 109 Senators (3 from each of the 36 states plus 1 from the Federal Capital Territory), presided over by the **Senate President**.
  2. *The House of Representatives (Lower Chamber):* 360 Honorable Members representing federal constituencies across Nigeria, presided over by the **Speaker of the House**.
- **How a Bill Becomes Law (The 5 Stages):**
  1. *First Reading:* Formal introduction and distribution of the bill.
  2. *Second Reading:* General debate on the core principles and merits of the bill.
  3. *Committee Stage:* Detailed clause-by-clause scrutiny, public hearings with citizens, and expert amendments.
  4. *Report Stage:* The committee presents the refined bill to the whole house.
  5. *Third Reading & Passage:* Final vote; identical text must be passed by both chambers.
  6. *Presidential Assent:* The President signs the bill into an Act. If the President vetoes, the National Assembly can override the veto with a **two-thirds majority**.
- **Key Functions:**
  - Making laws on the Exclusive and Concurrent Legislative Lists.
  - Passing the annual national budget (**Appropriation Act**).
  - Conducting investigative **oversight** on executive ministries to prevent corruption.
  - Screening and confirming presidential nominees (ministers, ambassadors, judges).

---

### In-Depth Analysis: The Executive (Section 5)
The law-implementing and administrative arm:
- **Leadership:** The **President of Nigeria**, who is both Head of State, Chief Executive of the Federation, and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces.
- **Support Machinery:** The Vice-President, the Federal Executive Council (Ministers), Special Advisers, the Federal Civil Service, the Nigeria Police Force, and the Armed Forces.
- **Key Functions:**
  - Implementing statutory laws enacted by the National Assembly.
  - Executing national development projects (building highways, railways, power plants).
  - Maintaining internal law and order and defending the territorial integrity of Nigeria.
  - Conducting foreign policy and representing Nigeria internationally.

---

### In-Depth Analysis: The Judiciary (Section 6)
The law-interpreting and dispute-resolving arm:
- **Hierarchical Structure of Courts:**
  1. **Supreme Court of Nigeria:** The apex court; final authority on constitutional appeals; decisions are binding on all other courts in Nigeria.
  2. **Court of Appeal:** Intermediate appellate court sitting across divisions in Nigeria.
  3. **High Courts:** Federal High Court, State High Courts, and National Industrial Court.
  4. **Customary & Sharia Courts of Appeal:** Specialized appellate courts on customary and Islamic personal law.
  5. **Lower Courts:** Magistrate Courts, Customary Courts, and Area Courts.
- **Key Functions:**
  - Interpreting the provisions of the Constitution and statutory acts.
  - Adjudicating disputes between citizens, between businesses, and between states and the federal government.
  - Safeguarding fundamental human rights against executive lawlessness.
  - Exercising **Judicial Review** to strike down unconstitutional laws or presidential actions.

## Common Mistakes
- **Thinking the President Can Dismiss a Supreme Court Justice at Will:** The President cannot arbitrarily fire a judge. Removal of superior court judges requires a recommendation by the National Judicial Council (NJC) and approval by a two-thirds majority of the Senate!
- **Confusing State Houses of Assembly with the National Assembly:** The National Assembly in Abuja is bicameral (Senate + House of Reps) and makes federal laws. Each of the 36 states has a **unicameral** State House of Assembly that makes state laws.
- **Believing the Supreme Court Makes Laws:** The Supreme Court interprets laws; it cannot write new statutes. Lawmaking is strictly reserved for the Legislature.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Overriding a Presidential Veto
**Scenario:** The National Assembly passes a bill requiring public transparency in petroleum revenues. The President refuses to sign the bill, returning it without assent after 30 days.
- **Constitutional Remedy:** Under Section 58(5) of the 1999 Constitution, the Senate and House of Representatives vote again on the bill.
- **Outcome:** If supported by a **two-thirds majority** in both chambers, the bill automatically becomes an Act of Parliament without the President’s signature!

### Example 2: Resolving Inter-Governmental Disputes
**Scenario:** A state government sues the Federal Government over the distribution of revenues from coastal offshore crude oil wells.
- **Judicial Jurisdiction:** Under Section 232 of the Constitution, the **Supreme Court of Nigeria** possesses original jurisdiction to hear and decide legal disputes between a State and the Federation.
- **Resolution:** The Supreme Court delivers a final, binding judgment that both the President and the Governor must obey immediately.
