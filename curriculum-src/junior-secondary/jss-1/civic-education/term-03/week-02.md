## Hook
Every single morning in secondary schools across all thirty-six states of Nigeria and the Federal Capital Territory, thousands of school bells ring simultaneously. Instantly, over ten million energetic students stop walking, freeze in their tracks, raise their heads, and stand at rigid attention as powerful, solemn music swells across the assembly grounds. Voices unite in harmonious thunder: *"Nigeria, we hail thee, our own dear native land! Though tribe and tongue may differ, in brotherhood we stand!"* Next, every right hand is placed solemnly over the left chest: *"I pledge to Nigeria my country, to be faithful, loyal and honest..."* Why do these sacred melodies and solemn pledges send shivers down our spines? Why do paper currency notes in your pocket carry portraits of our historic heroes, and why does an electronic green passport grant you international passage across foreign borders? Let us explore the living anthems, pledges, and credentials of the Federal Republic of Nigeria!

## Key Terms
- Term: National Anthem
- Definition: An official patriotic hymn or musical composition formally adopted by a sovereign nation, sung on ceremonial occasions to evoke national identity, pride, and solidarity.
- Term: National Pledge
- Definition: A solemn, public oath of loyalty, honesty, and service recited by citizens to affirm their personal commitment to the unity, honor, and defense of Nigeria.
- Term: Professor Felicia Adebola Adedoyin
- Definition: The renowned Nigerian educationist and professor (1938–2021) who authored the Nigerian National Pledge in 1976.
- Term: The National Currency (Naira and Kobo)
- Definition: The official legal tender and monetary unit of Nigeria, issued exclusively by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), introduced on January 1, 1973.
- Term: Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN)
- Definition: The apex monetary authority and central banking institution of Nigeria responsible for managing money supply, currency issuance, and financial stability.
- Term: Chief Obafemi Awolowo
- Definition: The distinguished Nigerian elder statesman and Federal Commissioner for Finance who coined the word "Naira" in 1973 by shortening "Nigeria".
- Term: Nigerian International Passport
- Definition: The official electronic travel document issued by the Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS) certifying the holder's identity and Nigerian citizenship abroad.
- Term: Biometric e-Passport
- Definition: A modern security passport embedded with an electronic microchip containing the bearer’s facial features, fingerprints, and personal demographic data.
- Term: Legal Tender
- Definition: Any officially recognized currency that a creditor or merchant is legally obligated to accept in settlement of a debt or payment for goods within a nation.
- Term: National Heroes
- Definition: Prominent historical figures commemorated on Nigerian banknotes and monuments for exceptional leadership, sacrifice, and contributions to nation building.

### Detailed Examination: The National Anthem
Under the **National Anthem Act 2024**, Nigeria officially re-adopted its historic inaugural independence anthem: **"Nigeria, We Hail Thee"**:

#### Stanza 1: Brotherhood Across Diversity
> *Nigeria, we hail thee,*  
> *Our own dear native land,*  
> *Though tribe and tongue may differ,*  
> *In brotherhood we stand,*  
> *Nigerians all, are proud to serve*  
> *Our sovereign Motherland.*  

*Core Message:* Celebrates our rich cultural diversity (over 250 ethnic groups) and emphasizes that ethnic differences are not barriers, but rather a source of collective brotherhood and shared patriotism.

#### Stanza 2: The Banner of Justice
> *Our flag shall be a symbol*  
> *That truth and justice reign,*  
> *In peace or battle honour’d,*  
> *And this we count as gain,*  
> *To hand on to our children*  
> *A banner without stain.*  

*Core Message:* Binds citizens to uphold truth, justice, and moral integrity so that future generations inherit an honorable, stainless nation.

#### Stanza 3: Prayer for Peace and Plenty
> *O God of all creation,*  
> *Grant this our one request,*  
> *Help us to build a nation*  
> *Where no man is oppressed,*  
> *And so with peace and plenty*  
> *Nigeria may be blessed.*  

*Core Message:* A profound prayer to the Almighty for divine guidance, eradication of injustice and oppression, and economic abundance ("plenty").

### Detailed Examination: The National Pledge
The National Pledge was written in **1976 by Professor (Mrs.) Felicia Adebola Adedoyin**, a mother and scholar whose children asked her why Nigeria had an anthem but no national pledge like American schools. Her patriotic composition was published in newspapers and subsequently adopted by the Supreme Military Council under General Olusegun Obasanjo, making it mandatory for all school assemblies:

> *"I pledge to Nigeria my country,*  
> *To be faithful, loyal and honest,*  
> *To serve Nigeria with all my strength,*  
> *To defend her unity,*  
> *And uphold her honour and glory.*  
> *So help me God."*

- **The Stance:** Recited standing rigidly at attention with the right hand placed flat over the left chest (over the heart), symbolizing sincere personal commitment.

### The National Currency: Naira (₦) and Kobo (k)
On **January 1, 1973**, Nigeria abandoned the colonial British West African Pound and introduced the **Naira and Kobo** (100 Kobo = 1 Naira). The name "Naira" was coined by Chief Obafemi Awolowo.
- **Portraits of National Icons on Banknotes:**
  - *₦5:* Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa (Nigeria's first and only Prime Minister).
  - *₦10:* Dr. Alvan Ikoku (pioneering educationist).
  - *₦20:* General Murtala Ramat Muhammed (military head of state and pan-African hero) and celebrated pottery artist Ladi Kwali.
  - *₦50:* Peoples of Nigeria (symbolizing agricultural and cultural diversity).
  - *₦100:* Chief Obafemi Awolowo (Premier of Western Region, visionary educational leader).
  - *₦200:* Sir Ahmadu Bello (Premier of Northern Region, Sardauna of Sokoto).
  - *₦500:* Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe (Nigeria's first President, "Zik of Africa").
  - *₦1000:* Alhaji Aliyu Mai-Bornu and Dr. Clement Isong (first two indigenous Central Bank Governors).

### The Nigerian International Passport
Issued exclusively by the **Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS)** under the Ministry of Interior:
- Features the Coat of Arms embossed in gold on a rich green cover.
- Serves as the supreme diplomatic proof of citizenship when traveling abroad.
- Contains high-security biometric microchips preventing identity theft, counterfeiting, and human trafficking.

## Common Mistakes
- **Fidgeting or Talking During the National Anthem:** Shuffling feet, making jokes, or eating snacks during the anthem is disrespectful. Citizens must stand silently at attention until the final note ends.
- **Mutilating or Spraying Naira Notes at Parties:** Spraying, stepping on, writing on, or squeezing Naira notes is a criminal offense under the CBN Act punishable by a ₦50,000 fine, six months in prison, or both!
- **Assuming the National Pledge is an Ancient British Document:** The Nigerian National Pledge was created entirely by a patriotic Nigerian woman (Prof. Felicia Adedoyin) in 1976.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: Practicing Constitutional Currency Respect
**Scenario:** At a wedding celebration, guests throw bundles of crisp ₦1,000 banknotes on the dance floor, and guests stomp on them with muddy shoes.
- **Civic Evaluation:** This is criminal currency mutilation under Section 21 of the Central Bank of Nigeria Act 2007.
- **Lawful Protocol:** Currency is an official sovereign symbol. The Central Bank encourages citizens to use alternative monetary transfer systems (e-banking, gift envelopes, point-of-sale transfers) rather than defacing legal tender.

### Example 2: Analyzing Stanza 1 of the National Anthem
**Scenario:** Two students from different ethnic backgrounds have a heated argument over tribal origins in a school dormitory.
- **Civic Application:** The school prefect reminds them of Stanza 1: *"Though tribe and tongue may differ, in brotherhood we stand!"*
- **Takeaway:** Ethnic diversity is a beautiful mosaic; our brotherhood as Nigerians transcends linguistic and tribal differences.

## Guardian Sync
- **Anthem & Pledge Recitation at Home:** Have your child recite the three stanzas of the National Anthem and the National Pledge from memory. Discuss the meaning of "a banner without stain."
- **Currency Inspection:** Examine different Naira banknotes with your child. Teach them about the life and historic achievements of icons like Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe, Chief Obafemi Awolowo, and Sir Ahmadu Bello.
- **Proper Handling of Currency:** Teach your child to store paper money neatly in wallets rather than folding them into tight balls, explaining that preserving currency saves the nation billions in printing costs.
