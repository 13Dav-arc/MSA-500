## Key Terms
- Term: Citizen
- Definition: A legal member of a recognized sovereign state who owes allegiance to the state and is entitled to full constitutional protection, rights, and privileges.
- Term: Citizenship
- Definition: The legal status, social bond, and political relationship existing between an individual and a state, embodying rights, privileges, and reciprocal duties.
- Term: Chapter III of 1999 Constitution
- Definition: The constitutional chapter establishing the legal framework and criteria for acquiring Nigerian citizenship by birth, registration, and naturalization.
- Term: Chapter IV of 1999 Constitution
- Definition: The constitutional Bill of Rights guaranteeing fundamental human rights (such as life, dignity, personal liberty, fair hearing, and expression) to all persons.
- Term: Naturalization
- Definition: The statutory legal procedure by which a foreign resident of at least 15 years, certified good character, and community assimilation acquires Nigerian citizenship.
- Term: Oath of Allegiance
- Definition: A solemn constitutional declaration sworn by an individual pledging fidelity, loyalty, and defense of the Federal Republic of Nigeria and its Constitution.
- Term: Alien
- Definition: A person residing in a country who is not a legal citizen of that country, owing primary allegiance to a foreign sovereign state.
- Term: Inalienable Rights
- Definition: Inherent moral and constitutional entitlements belonging to human beings from birth that cannot be lawfully surrendered or taken away without due process.
- Term: Presumption of Innocence
- Definition: The universal legal principle that an accused individual is considered innocent until proven guilty beyond reasonable doubt in a lawful court.
- Term: Deprivation of Citizenship
- Definition: The constitutional power of the President (Section 30) to revoke the citizenship of a naturalized citizen for treason, crime, or wartime disloyalty.

### Core Review Summary
1. **The Legal Nature of Citizenship:** A citizen owes permanent allegiance and receives state protection. Aliens reside conditionally and do not enjoy political franchise (voting or running for office).
2. **Pathways to Citizenship:** Nigeria recognizes five types: Birth (indigenous parentage), Descent (born abroad to Nigerian parents), Registration (foreign wives of Nigerian citizens), Naturalization (aliens of 15+ years residency and good character), and Honorary (presidential award).
3. **The Naturalization Gate:** Naturalization requires being at least 18 years old, verified good character, community assimilation, 15 years residency, public newspaper notice, presidential approval, and the sacred **Oath of Allegiance**. It can be revoked under Section 30 for treason or felony convictions.
4. **Fundamental Rights Shield:** Chapter IV protects inalienable human dignity:
   - *Section 33:* Right to Life (no arbitrary execution).
   - *Section 34:* Right to Dignity (zero torture, forced labor, or slavery).
   - *Section 35:* Right to Liberty (court arraignment within 24–48 hours).
   - *Section 36:* Fair Hearing (*Audi Alteram Partem* and presumption of innocence).
   - *Section 39:* Freedom of Expression and the Press.
   - *Section 45:* Permits restrictions for defense, public order, and public health.

## Common Mistakes
- **Confusing "Birth in Nigeria" with "Automatic Citizenship":** Nigeria adheres to *Jus Sanguinis*. Children of foreign tourists born in Nigeria are not Nigerian citizens; at least one parent or grandparent must belong to a Nigerian indigenous community or be a citizen.
- **Thinking Fundamental Rights Mean Freedom to Break Laws:** Freedom of expression does not protect libel, slander, or hate speech; personal liberty does not shield a suspect from lawful arrest under a judicial warrant.
- **Assuming the Police Can Hold Suspects Without Charge Indefinitely:** Section 35 strictly commands that an arrested person must be brought before a court of competent jurisdiction within 24 hours (or 48 hours where courts are distant).

## Worked Examples
### Diagnostic Problem 1: Categorizing Citizenship Entitlements
- **Task:** Determine which of the following individuals is a Nigerian citizen:
  1. *A child born in Houston, Texas, to a Nigerian mother from Abia State and an American father:* Nigerian Citizen **by descent** (Section 25).
  2. *A British engineer residing in Lagos for three years on a work permit:* **Alien** (foreign resident).
  3. *A Spanish woman married to a Nigerian banker who completed registration vetted by the Ministry of Interior:* Nigerian Citizen **by registration** (Section 26).
  4. *A child born in Kano whose parents are both Chadian nationals visiting on vacation:* **Alien** (no Nigerian parentage or indigenous ancestry).

### Diagnostic Problem 2: Assessing Human Rights Infractions
- **Task:** Identify the constitutional right violated in each scenario:
  1. *Police officers beat a detained suspect with iron cables to force a confession:* Violation of Section 34 (Right to Dignity of Human Person).
  2. *A magistrate convicts an accused student without allowing them to speak or call a lawyer:* Violation of Section 36 (Right to Fair Hearing).
  3. *A suspect is detained in a police lockup for forty days without court arraignment:* Violation of Section 35 (Right to Personal Liberty).
