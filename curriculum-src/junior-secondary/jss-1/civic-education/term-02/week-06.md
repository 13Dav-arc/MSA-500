## Hook
Imagine moving into a beautiful, brand-new estate with paved roads, streetlights, twenty-four-hour clean water, and manicured green parks. Every resident is thrilled to enjoy these wonderful facilities! But at the end of the first month, when the estate management sends out the maintenance bill for electricity, diesel, waste disposal, and security guards, every single resident throws the invoice into the trash, shouting, "It is my constitutional right to live here for free!" What happens by month three? The security guards pack their bags and leave, the water pumps shut down, darkness blankets the streets, garbage piles up attracting rodents, and armed robbers break in! Society works exactly like that estate: you cannot enjoy rights without paying the bills of civic responsibility! That is why the law balances your constitutional rights with your **Duties and Obligations**! Let us discover the responsibilities that keep a nation running!

## Key Terms
- Term: Civic Duty
- Definition: A mandatory moral and legal obligation that a citizen is required by law and the constitution to perform for the survival, order, and progress of the sovereign state.
- Term: Obligation
- Definition: A duty, requirement, or course of action to which an individual is morally or legally bound (e.g., obeying laws, paying taxes).
- Term: Financial Obligations
- Definition: The mandatory requirement for citizens to pay legitimate taxes, levies, and rates to provide public revenue for state infrastructure and services.
- Term: Economic Obligations
- Definition: The duty of citizens to protect, preserve, and safeguard public utilities, state assets, and government infrastructure from vandalism and theft.
- Term: Civic Obligations
- Definition: The responsibility of citizens to maintain public peace, obey national laws, respect constituted authorities, and prevent civil disorder or anarchy.
- Term: Political Obligations
- Definition: The civic responsibility to participate actively in democratic governance by registering, voting in elections, and holding leaders accountable.
- Term: Social Obligations
- Definition: Responsibilities aimed at promoting community welfare, national defense, environmental sanitation, and assisting law enforcement agencies.
- Term: Taxation
- Definition: The compulsory financial contribution levied by the government on workers' income, business profits, and goods to finance public expenditures.
- Term: Vandalism
- Definition: The deliberate, malicious destruction, damage, or defacement of public or private property and infrastructure.
- Term: Anarchy
- Definition: A catastrophic state of lawlessness, disorder, and chaos resulting from the absence, breakdown, or complete defiance of government authority.

## Core Concept
### What are Duties and Obligations?
If **Rights** are what the nation gives to the citizen, **Duties** are what the citizen must give back to the nation. Rights and duties are the two sides of a coin: you cannot flip one side without moving the other. In Civic Education, a **duty** is an action expected of a citizen in exchange for the constitutional freedoms and security guaranteed by the sovereign state.

### Detailed Classification of Citizen Obligations:

#### 1. Financial Obligations (Section 24f)
The state needs immense financial resources to construct asphalt highways, build public schools, fund police forces, and provide clean pipe-borne water. Citizens who earn income must pay **Personal Income Tax (PAYE)**, business levies, and property rates promptly. Tax evasion is a serious criminal offense.

#### 2. Economic Obligations
Citizens must respect and actively safeguard public property. Public utilities—such as oil pipelines, electrical transformers, railway tracks, telecommunications masts, and public school buildings—belong to all Nigerians. Citizens must never vandalize these assets or buy stolen public cables.

#### 3. Civic Obligations
The foundational duty to obey the laws of the land. When citizens obey traffic wardens, observe building codes, and respect courts, the community enjoys order. The alternative to law obedience is **anarchy**—a terrifying state of total chaos, violence, and destruction.

#### 4. Political Obligations
A democracy cannot survive if citizens refuse to participate. Qualified citizens (18 years and above) have an obligation to register with the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), vote during elections, resist vote-buying, and constructively monitor elected leaders.

#### 5. Social and Security Obligations
- **National Defense:** When the nation faces external aggression or invasion, citizens must be ready to defend the fatherland or serve in the military.
- **Reporting Criminals:** Assisting the Nigeria Police, Civil Defence (NSCDC), and military by providing credible intelligence about kidnappers, armed robbers, and terrorists.
- **Environmental Sanitation:** Keeping drainage gutters clean, disposing of garbage in approved bins, and halting the illegal dumping of refuse that causes catastrophic urban flooding.

### Why Rights and Duties are Vital to Society
- **Social Control:** They provide clear legal guardrails that prevent human conflict and curb criminal impulses.
- **Guaranteed Peace and Stability:** Respect for mutual rights and performance of duties creates harmonious coexistence across ethnic and religious groups.
- **Due Process:** Ensures that disputes are resolved lawfully through independent courts rather than jungle justice.
- **Societal Discipline:** Instills a culture of orderliness, punctuality, and civic pride in both young and old.

## Common Mistakes
- **The "Tax is Theft" Fallacy:** Believing that paying taxes is throwing money away. Without taxes, there would be no fire service to extinguish burning homes, no soldiers to defend borders, and no public roads.
- **Assuming Public Property Belongs to "Nobody":** Some people vandalize government property saying, "It is government property, not my father's house." In reality, government property was paid for with citizens' collective tax money—destroying it is stealing from yourself!
- **Believing Only Adults Have Duties:** Junior secondary students have vital civic duties right now: obeying school regulations, protecting classroom desks, respecting teachers, keeping the compound clean, and singing the national anthem with solemn dignity.

## Worked Examples
### Example 1: The Tragedy of Tax Evasion
**Scenario:** A commercial transport company earns ₦50 million in annual profit but uses forged accounting books to pay zero taxes to the State Internal Revenue Service.
- **Analysis:** This is criminal tax evasion. The missing revenue deprives the state of funds to repair potholes on the highway the company's buses use daily.
- **Outcome:** A bus hits an unrepaired pothole, overturns, and costs the company ₦80 million in damages. 
- **Civic Lesson:** When citizens evade taxes, public infrastructure collapses, harming everyone!

### Example 2: Safeguarding Community Infrastructure
**Scenario:** In an estate in Benin City, teenagers notice two men with hacksaws cutting copper cables from the community electricity transformer at 2:00 AM.
- **Irresponsible Action:** The teenagers ignore it, go back to sleep, and say, "It is NEPA's problem." (Result: The neighborhood stays in pitch darkness for six months).
- **Dutiful Civic Action:** The teenagers immediately alert their parents, who quietly phone the local police and estate vigilante patrol. (Result: The vandals are caught, the transformer is saved, and power flows uninterrupted).

## Guardian Sync
- **Family Civic Audit:** Discuss the various taxes and public bills paid in your household (e.g., income tax, property tenement rates, waste management bills). Help your child understand how these contributions finance local services.
- **Environmental Stewardship Day:** Designate a family clean-up morning where parents and children clear the compound, sweep public sidewalks, and unblock the frontage gutter.
- **Community Safety Awareness:** Teach your child emergency contact numbers for the local police and emergency services, emphasizing that reporting crime is a patriotic civic duty.
