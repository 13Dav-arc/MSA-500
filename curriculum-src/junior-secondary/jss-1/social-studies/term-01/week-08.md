## Hook
Have you ever wondered why a Yoruba boy prostrates flat on the ground with his chest touching the mat to greet his grandfather, while a young Hausa boy in Kano kneels down politely with lowered eyes, and an Edo maiden in Benin curtsies gracefully while gently lifting the hem of her wrapper? Or think about the rich diversity of our delicious Nigerian dishes: why is *Amala* with *Gbegiri* and *Ewedu* the pride of Ibadan, while *Ofe Owerri* with pounded yam is celebrated across Imo State, and *Tuwo Shinkafa* with *Miyan Kuka* is beloved in Kaduna? No baby is born with a craving for *Egusi* soup or knowing how to tie a majestic royal *Gele* headgear! We acquire these customs, languages, art forms, foods, and celebrations because we grow up surrounded by a glorious human treasure called **Culture**! What is culture? What makes it alive, constantly evolving, and uniquely human? Let us dive into the fascinating world of human culture!

## Key Terms
- Term: Culture
- Definition: The total way of life of a group of people, comprising their knowledge, beliefs, art, morals, laws, customs, habits, technology, and modes of thinking.
- Term: Material Culture
- Definition: The physical, tangible, touchable artifacts and human-made objects created and used by a society (such as tools, clothing, housing, food, pottery, and musical instruments).
- Term: Non-Material Culture
- Definition: The intangible, abstract creations of human society that cannot be touched physically (such as language, religious beliefs, philosophical values, folklore, music, etiquette, and social norms).
- Term: Learned Culture
- Definition: The characteristic of culture showing that it is not biologically inherited through genetics, but acquired through socialization and observation.
- Term: Shared Culture
- Definition: The characteristic that culture is held in common by members of a social group rather than being an individual's private invention.
- Term: Dynamic Culture
- Definition: The quality of culture being flexible and constantly changing over time in response to new technologies, historical contacts, and environmental realities.
- Term: Adaptive Culture
- Definition: The capacity of culture to adjust to environmental challenges and human needs to ensure group survival and well-being.
- Term: Cultural Diffusion
- Definition: The spread of cultural beliefs, social activities, food traditions, and technologies from one ethnic group or society to another.
- Term: Artifacts
- Definition: Physical objects made or modified by human beings that reflect the technology and artistic traditions of a particular culture (e.g., Igbo-Ukwu bronzes, Nok terracottas).
- Term: Ethnocentrism
- Definition: The biased tendency to evaluate other cultures using the standards of one's own culture, often assuming one's own customs are superior.

### Five Universal Characteristics of Culture
1. **Culture is Learned (Not Innate):**
   - No human infant is born knowing how to speak Yoruba, Tiv, or Kanuri.
   - We learn culture through socialization from parents, teachers, elders, and peers. If a Nigerian infant is adopted at birth and raised in Tokyo, Japan, the child will grow up speaking fluent Japanese and adopting Japanese bowing etiquette naturally.

2. **Culture is Shared:**
   - Culture cannot exist for only one isolated person. An eccentric habit invented by one individual is an idiosyncratic quirk, not culture.
   - For an idea, fashion, or language to be cultural, it must be widely accepted, practiced, and recognized by members of a community.

3. **Culture is Dynamic (Ever-Changing):**
   - Culture is not a frozen museum statue; it grows, modifies, and evolves continuously.
   - Fifty years ago, traditional Nigerian town criers beat metal gongs (*Ogene*) to summon villagers to the village square. Today, royal palaces and community leaders use WhatsApp group chats, radio announcements, and mobile phone calls to broadcast news.

4. **Culture is Adaptive:**
   - Cultural practices help humans survive in specific physical environments.
   - People living in cold highland plateaus wear heavy woven garments and build insulated huts, while coastal creek dwellers build stilt houses on water and master canoe navigation.

5. **Culture is Symbolic:**
   - Culture communicates through symbols—national flags, royal crowns, language scripts, traditional walking sticks, and wedding rings all convey profound shared meanings.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Equating culture exclusively with traditional dances, festivals, and old rituals.
  - *Correction:* Culture is the *entire way of life* of a people, including modern science, legal systems, street food, smartphone communication, and contemporary business practices.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming that some ethnic groups have "more culture" or "superior culture" compared to others.
  - *Correction:* Every human society has a complete and valid culture designed to meet human needs. Ranking cultures as "superior" or "inferior" is ethnocentric bias.
- **Mistake 3:** Believing that culture never changes and that modern modifications "destroy" our identity.
  - *Correction:* All healthy cultures are dynamic. Adopting modern sanitation, medicine, electricity, and equal education for girls enhances culture while preserving cherished core values.
- **Mistake 4:** Confusing *Material Culture* with *Non-Material Culture*.
  - *Correction:* A wooden drum is *material culture*; the rhythm, musical meaning, and religious reverence associated with beating that drum are *non-material culture*.

## Worked Examples
- **Example 1: Classifying Elements of Culture**
  - *Task:* Categorize each of the following cultural items from a traditional Nigerian festival into *Material* or *Non-Material* culture:
    1. A hand-woven *Aso-Oke* agbada.
    2. The traditional greeting: "E ku odun" (wishing happy celebrations).
    3. The carved wooden royal throne of the Oba.
    4. The belief that ancestral spirits protect the village from epidemics.
  - *Solution:*
    - *Material Culture:* The hand-woven *Aso-Oke* and the carved wooden royal throne (both are tangible physical objects).
    - *Non-Material Culture:* The spoken greeting "E ku odun" and the metaphysical belief in ancestral protection (both are abstract ideas/beliefs).
- **Example 2: Demonstrating the Dynamic Nature of Culture**
  - *Scenario:* In 1930, sending invitations to a traditional wedding in an Edo community required sending messengers on foot with kola nuts to each extended family compound over three weeks. In 2026, the family designs a digital graphic wedding flyer and distributes it to 500 relatives worldwide in two seconds via messaging applications.
  - *Analysis:* This illustrates the *dynamic* and *adaptive* characteristic of culture. The non-material value (honoring kin through celebration and invitations) remains constant, but the material medium has modernized through technological innovation.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Discussion Point:** Take out traditional family clothing (such as an embroidered Agbada, George wrapper, or Babariga). Ask your child: *“Which ethnic culture does this garment represent, and what occasion is it worn for?”*
- **Language Connection:** Teach your child three proverbs or traditional greetings in your native language, explaining the deep moral wisdom behind them.
- **Cultural Appreciation Exercise:** Discuss the danger of looking down on other people's ethnic customs. Emphasize that respecting Nigeria’s cultural diversity makes our country uniquely vibrant and strong.
