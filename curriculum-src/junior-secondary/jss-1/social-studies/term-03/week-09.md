## Hook
Imagine walking to school on a misty morning along a busy multi-lane highway in Lagos, Ibadan, or Abuja. Cars, articulated fuel tankers, and commercial buses (*Danfos*) roar past at 100 kilometers per hour, blasting their air horns! You arrive at a major junction where you need to cross to reach your school gate. Do you just shut your eyes, scream, and sprint blindly across four lanes of speeding metal monsters? If you do, a two-ton vehicle traveling at highway speed cannot stop in time—it takes a speeding car more than 50 meters just to brake to a halt! Or imagine riding your bicycle home at dusk wearing a pitch-black sweater with no reflectors, completely invisible to oncoming truck drivers! Thousands of precious lives—schoolchildren, parents, cyclists, and motorists—are cut short on Nigerian roads every year due to careless, avoidable road accidents! How can pedestrians cross roads safely? What do traffic signs and zebra stripes mean, and what life-saving road rules must you obey as a pedestrian, cyclist, and future driver? Let us find out!

## Key Terms
- Term: Road Safety
- Definition: The methods, measures, and behavioral practices used to prevent road users (pedestrians, cyclists, motorcyclists, motorists, and passengers) from being killed or seriously injured in vehicular collisions.
- Term: Pedestrian
- Definition: Any person walking, running, jogging, or moving on foot along a road, sidewalk, or highway corridor.
- Term: Cyclist
- Definition: An individual riding a two-wheeled human-powered bicycle or tricycle on a roadway or designated cycle track.
- Term: Motorist
- Definition: A licensed driver who operates a motorized four-wheeled vehicle (such as a car, bus, truck, or van) on public roads.
- Term: Zebra Crossing
- Definition: A marked pedestrian crossing zone across a roadway characterized by alternating black and white painted stripes, giving pedestrians the legal right-of-way when traffic stops.
- Term: Footbridge (Overhead Pedestrian Bridge)
- Definition: An elevated bridge built across a busy multi-lane highway allowing pedestrians to cross overhead safely without interacting with high-speed vehicular traffic.
- Term: Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC)
- Definition: The lead Nigerian federal law enforcement agency responsible for road traffic administration, preventing road traffic crashes, clearing highway obstructions, and educating the public.
- Term: Kerb (Curb)
- Definition: The raised stone or concrete edging along a street or road separating the vehicular road surface from the elevated pedestrian pavement or sidewalk.
- Term: Defensive Driving
- Definition: A method of driving where a motorist actively anticipates hazards and potential mistakes by other road users, driving carefully to prevent crashes regardless of conditions.
- Term: Sessional 80% Mastery Gate
- Definition: The cumulative academic benchmark requiring students to demonstrate at least 80% factual mastery of all JSS 1 Social Studies concepts across Terms 1, 2, and 3 before writing the sessional examination.

1. **Safety Guidelines for Pedestrians:**
   - **The Golden Walking Rule:** Always walk on elevated sidewalks or pavements. If there is NO sidewalk, **walk on the LEFT shoulder of the road, facing oncoming traffic**. This allows you to see approaching vehicles with your own eyes and step aside if a driver loses control.
   - **The Kerb Drill (How to Cross Safely):**
     1. Stop near the edge of the road, keeping clear of moving vehicles.
     2. **Look LEFT, look RIGHT, and look LEFT again.**
     3. Listen carefully for approaching engines or horns.
     4. If the road is clear, walk straight across—**DO NOT RUN**, as running increases the risk of tripping and falling in front of traffic.
   - **Use Designated Crossings:** Always cross busy expressways using elevated **Footbridges** or **Zebra Crossings**. Never scale highway concrete road dividers!
   - **Visibility at Night:** Wear white, yellow, or fluorescent clothing at night, or carry a flashlight. A driver cannot stop for what they cannot see!
   - **Eliminate Distractions:** Never walk across a road while looking at a smartphone screen or wearing noise-canceling headphones.

2. **Safety Guidelines for Cyclists:**
   - Always wear a fastened, certified safety helmet to protect the brain against concussions.
   - Ride on the **RIGHT side of the road, moving in the SAME direction as vehicular traffic** (unlike pedestrians who face traffic).
   - Equip the bicycle with a functional bell, a white front lamp, and a red rear reflector.
   - Use clear hand signals before turning: extend the left arm straight for left turns, or point right for right turns.
   - Never carry multiple friends on handlebars or perform stunt acrobatics on public roads.

3. **Safety Guidelines for Motorists and Commercial Drivers:**
   - **Seatbelts are Compulsory:** Every driver and passenger must fasten their seatbelt before the vehicle moves. Seatbelts reduce the risk of fatal head injury by 50%.
   - **Obey Speed Limits:** Maximum 100 km/h on expressways, 80 km/h on federal highways, and 40–50 km/h in built-up urban/school zones.
   - **Zero Phone Distraction:** Making phone calls or typing text messages while driving increases crash probability by 400%.
   - **Never Drink and Drive:** Alcohol impairs motor reflexes, causes blurred vision, and destroys judgment.
   - **Vehicle Roadworthiness:** Regularly inspect tires, hydraulic brakes, headlights, turn indicators, and wipers.

### Recognizing Major Traffic Signs and Signals
- **Regulatory Signs (Circular, Red Border):** Mandatory prohibitions (e.g., "NO ENTRY", "SPEED LIMIT 50").
- **Warning Signs (Triangular, Red Border):** Warn of road hazards ahead (e.g., "BEND AHEAD", "PEDESTRIAN CROSSING", "SLIPPERY ROAD").
- **Informative Signs (Rectangular, Blue/Green):** Provide navigational information (e.g., "HOSPITAL", "FIRST AID STATION").
- **Traffic Light Sequence:**
  - **RED:** Complete stop behind the stop line.
  - **AMBER (YELLOW):** Prepare to stop; clear the junction if already past the line.
  - **GREEN:** Proceed with caution if the junction is clear.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Walking on the right side of the road with traffic approaching behind you.
  - *Correction:* Pedestrians must walk on the LEFT shoulder **facing oncoming traffic**. Walking with traffic behind you leaves you blind to runaway vehicles approaching from your rear!
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming that cars will automatically stop the second you step onto a zebra crossing.
  - *Correction:* Even on a zebra crossing, wait until cars come to a complete, visible standstill before stepping onto the road. An inattentive driver cannot stop instantly!
- **Mistake 3:** Running across a highway because it looks faster.
  - *Correction:* Running causes tripping, shoe loss, and falls. Always walk briskly and purposefully in a straight line.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking cyclists should move against traffic like pedestrians.
  - *Correction:* Cyclists are vehicle operators; they must travel in the SAME direction as traffic, following all traffic lights and hand signals.

## Worked Examples
- **Example 1: Safe Road Crossing on an Urban Highway**
  - *Scenario:* Tobi, a JSS 1 student, steps off a bus in Ikeja. His school is on the other side of an eight-lane highway. There is an overhead footbridge 40 meters away, but two older boys jump over the concrete road median divider to run across the highway.
  - *Decision Analysis:*
    - The highway traffic moves at 80 km/h. Jumping the median is extremely dangerous and illegal.
    - Tobi walks the extra 40 meters to the pedestrian footbridge, climbs the stairs, and crosses completely separated from traffic.
    - *Outcome:* One of the boys jumping the road median trips on a stone and is narrowly missed by a speeding tanker, scraping his knees and tearing his trousers. Tobi arrives at school on time, unharmed, and peaceful. Tobi obeyed the golden rule of road safety.
- **Example 2: Night Cycling Visibility Protocol**
  - *Scenario:* Haruna is riding his bicycle home from evening lessons in Zaria at 7:00 PM. The road has no streetlights.
  - *Safety Measures:*
    1. Haruna wears a white reflective sash over his school uniform.
    2. His bicycle has a working front dynamo lamp and red rear reflector.
    3. He rides on the extreme right side of the road with traffic, keeping both hands firmly on the handlebars.
    4. *Outcome:* Drivers see him 150 meters away in their headlights and give him wide, safe clearance.
