## Hook
Imagine walking through a bustling Nigerian city. You see magnificent glass-walled commercial banks, flyover expressways buzzing with modern cars, and high-tech telecommunication towers beaming fast 5G internet signals into millions of smartphones. Nigeria has made breathtaking strides in technology and urban development! But look a little closer beneath the glittering surface: why are secondary school principals caught sneaking leaked test question sheets to students in exam halls? Why do young university undergraduates join violent secret groups that terrorize campuses in the dead of night? Why are young lives tragically cut short by preventable diseases like HIV/AIDS and substance addiction? These painful challenges that plague modern human coexistence are known as **Contemporary Social Problems**! What are they, why did they emerge with rapid urbanization and foreign cultural contact, and what role can our generation play in confronting them? Let us find out!

## Key Terms
- Term: Contemporary Social Problems
- Definition: Current, widespread societal challenges, vices, and negative conditions affecting many people in modern society that disrupt peace, threaten health, and hinder national development.
- Term: Urbanization
- Definition: The rapid growth and physical expansion of towns and cities, driven by mass migration of people from rural villages in search of jobs and modern amenities.
- Term: Examination Malpractice
- Definition: Any unauthorized, illegal, or unethical conduct or practice employed by a candidate or their collaborators before, during, or after an examination to gain unmerited grades.
- Term: Cultism
- Definition: Membership, participation, or allegiance to a secret, oath-bound fraternity whose activities are concealed, ritualistic, violent, and disruptive to public order.
- Term: HIV/AIDS
- Definition: Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), a retrovirus that attacks and destroys the human immune system, which if untreated progresses to Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS).
- Term: Substance (Drug) Abuse
- Definition: The habitual, non-medical consumption of illicit drugs, prescription medications, or dangerous chemical substances for mood alteration, resulting in physical and psychological harm.
- Term: Terrorism
- Definition: The systematic, unlawful use of calculated violence, intimidation, and fear against civilian populations or government targets to achieve ideological or political goals.
- Term: Human Trafficking
- Definition: The illegal recruitment, transportation, harboring, or receipt of human beings by force, fraud, or coercion for the purpose of exploitation (forced labor or prostitution).
- Term: Advance Fee Fraud (419)
- Definition: An unlawful financial confidence trick where a perpetrator deceives a victim into paying upfront fees on the false promise of receiving a massive financial fortune.
- Term: Social Deviation
- Definition: Behavioral actions and lifestyles that depart significantly from the established cultural norms, laws, and moral expectations of a society.

1. **Examination Malpractice:**
   - One of the most destructive academic vices in Nigeria. Involves bringing unauthorized notes ("micro-chips" or "expo") into exam halls, impersonation, collusion with corrupt supervisors, and operating illegal "miracle centers."
   - Destroys academic integrity, devalues Nigerian educational certificates internationally, and produces unqualified professionals (e.g., incompetent doctors or collapsing building engineers).

2. **Cultism:**
   - Originated in 1952 at University College, Ibadan, as a peaceful, non-violent student club (the Pyrates Confraternity) advocating social justice and African cultural pride.
   - Degenerated in later decades into violent, ritualistic, armed secret gangs that engage in turf wars, extortion, political thuggery, assassinations, and terror on campuses and street corners.

3. **HIV/AIDS Epidemic:**
   - Human Immunodeficiency Virus attacks the body's immune system, specifically CD4 helper T-lymphocytes.
   - Transmitted through unprotected sexual intercourse with an infected partner, transfusion of unscreened blood, sharing unsterilized sharp objects (needles, razor blades, clippers), and mother-to-child transmission during pregnancy, delivery, or breastfeeding.
   - Stigmatization and discrimination against carriers remain serious social challenges, hindering testing and treatment.

4. **Drug and Substance Abuse:**
   - Rampant abuse of prescription opioids (Tramadol, codeine cough syrups), cannabis, synthetic chemical mixtures ("Colorado", "Arizona"), and volatile inhalants (glue, fuel).
   - Leads to acute psychosis, brain damage, kidney and liver failure, family violence, and criminal delinquency.

5. **Terrorism, Militancy, and Human Trafficking:**
   - Insurgencies disrupting peace and displacing millions of innocent farmers into Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) camps.
   - Human traffickers deceiving vulnerable rural girls with promises of foreign hairdressing jobs, only to trap them in sexual exploitation and forced labor across desert routes.

## Comparative Matrix
| Contemporary Social Problem | Nature of Vice | Primary Driving Factor | Direct Negative Impact | Statutory Legal Consequence in Nigeria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Examination Malpractice** | Academic dishonesty & fraud | Laziness, desperation for certificates, corrupt supervisors | Devaluation of certificates; incompetent workforce | Up to 21 years imprisonment under Exam Malpractice Act / Decree 18 of 1984 |
| **Cultism** | Secret ritualistic violence & intimidation | Craving for false power, peer pressure, political godfathers | Campus bloodshed, loss of young lives, fear, rustication | Minimum 10 to 21 years imprisonment under state anti-cultism laws |
| **HIV/AIDS** | Infectious public health disease | Unscreened transfusions, unsterilized needles, unprotected sex | Physical illness, orphan crisis, social stigmatization | Protected under Anti-Discrimination Act of 2014 against workplace bias |
| **Drug Abuse** | Toxic substance addiction | Peer pressure, depression, curiosity, idle frustration | Mental psychosis, organ failure, violent criminality | Arrest, prosecution, and rehabilitation by the NDLEA |
| **Advance Fee Fraud (419)** | Financial cyber deception | Greed, get-rich-quick mentality, moral decay | National reputational damage, victim bankruptcy | Strict imprisonment under S. 419 of Criminal Code & EFCC Act |

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Viewing examination malpractice as a "victimless shortcut" that hurts nobody.
  - *Correction:* Exam cheating produces fake professionals: fake doctors who kill patients, fake engineers whose bridges collapse, and fake teachers who misinform youth. It poisons national development.
- **Mistake 2:** Believing that cultism confers real respect, invincibility, or career success.
  - *Correction:* Cultism delivers bloodshed, terror, physical arrest, emotional paranoia, prison sentences, or premature death in street gutters.
- **Mistake 3:** Believing that HIV/AIDS can be contracted by shaking hands, hugging, or sharing a classroom desk with an infected person.
  - *Correction:* HIV is NOT transmitted through saliva, sweat, tears, casual handshakes, hugs, sharing spoons, or using the same toilet seat. Stigmatizing carriers is medically ignorant and cruel.
- **Mistake 4:** Assuming that only street touts abuse dangerous drugs.
  - *Correction:* Substance abuse affects students across all economic classes. Misusing prescription cough syrups or painkillers is just as dangerous as consuming illegal street narcotics.

## Worked Examples
- **Example 1: Classifying and Deconstructing a Contemporary Social Problem**
  - *Scenario:* A candidate sitting for a national examination writes biological answers on a handkerchief and hides it in their socks. The invigilator notices the student copying answers and confiscates the cheat sheet.
  - *Analysis:*
    - *Vice:* Examination Malpractice (Academic fraud).
    - *Underlying Cause:* Inadequate academic preparation, lack of self-confidence, and laziness.
    - *Consequence:* Cancellation of the candidate's paper, reporting to the examination council, and potential prosecution under the Examination Malpractice Act.
- **Example 2: Dispelling HIV/AIDS Stigmatization in School**
  - *Scenario:* A JSS 1 student learns that their classmate's mother tested positive for HIV. The student refuses to sit next to the classmate, claiming: *"I don't want to catch HIV through the air!"*
  - *Educational Intervention:* The Social Studies teacher explains that HIV cannot be airborne. The virus lives inside body fluids and cannot spread through sitting together, sharing textbooks, or playing football. The class apologizes, and the student warmly welcomes their classmate back to the desk.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Discussion Point:** Discuss the dangers of get-rich-quick mindsets with your child. Ask: *“Why is honest, patient labor with integrity far better than cheating on an exam or engaging in cyber fraud?”*
- **Health and Hygiene Vigilance:** Review personal hygiene rules at home: never share toothbrushes, razor blades, or sharp instruments. Discuss how staying informed about public health protects the family.
- **Open Communication:** Encourage your child to share any strange peer groups or secret invitations they encounter at school, assuring them of parental love and protection.
