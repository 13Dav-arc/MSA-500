## Key Terms
- Term: Socialization
- Definition: The lifelong interactive process by which individuals learn, internalize, and practice the culture, values, roles, and behavioral expectations of their society.
- Term: Primary Socialization
- Definition: The foundational phase of socialization that takes place in early childhood within the loving, intimate circle of the family.
- Term: Secondary Socialization
- Definition: The ongoing phase of socialization that occurs outside the family throughout life in schools, peer groups, religious bodies, and workplaces.
- Term: Agents of Socialization
- Definition: The organized social groups, institutions, and communication channels that facilitate and transmit societal culture and knowledge.
- Term: Social Stratification
- Definition: The hierarchical ranking and division of individuals and groups into different socio-economic strata based on wealth, status, power, or occupation.
- Term: Ascribed Status
- Definition: A social standing or rank assigned to an individual at birth or involuntarily through lineage, biological sex, or age.
- Term: Achieved Status
- Definition: A social standing attained through personal choice, hard work, education, talent, and demonstrated competence.
- Term: Social Role
- Definition: The expected rights, duties, and pattern of behaviors associated with a particular social position in a group.
- Term: Peer Pressure
- Definition: The psychological and social influence exerted by age-mates to conform to their behavioral norms and attitudes.
- Term: Assertiveness
- Definition: The confident, calm, and respectful communication of one's personal convictions and boundaries without being timid or aggressive.

1. **The Nature and Scope of Socialization (Weeks 01–02):**
   - Distinguishes primary socialization (early childhood, family-centered, deeply emotional) from secondary socialization (lifelong, institutional, objective).
   - Examines the specific roles of the five primary agents: the family provides mother tongue and foundational ethics; the school transmits academic literacy and the hidden curriculum; peer groups offer egalitarian social negotiation; religious bodies nurture spiritual and moral consciousness; and the mass media broadens global awareness.

2. **Social Stratification and Roles (Week 03):**
   - Contrasts *Ascribed Status* (inherited by birth: royalty, sex, age) with *Achieved Status* (earned through study, skill, and effort: doctor, engineer, teacher).
   - Explores how modern education enables upward social mobility, allowing youth from humble backgrounds to attain high leadership positions based on merit.
   - Highlights the modernization of gender roles in Nigeria, demonstrating that domestic survival skills belong to all genders, and that girl-child education unlocks immense national productivity.

3. **Friendship and Peer Dynamics (Week 04):**
   - Identifies the hallmarks of healthy friendship: loyalty, respect for boundaries, constructive honesty, and mutual celebration of success.
   - Differentiates positive peer pressure (collaborative studying, sportsmanship) from destructive peer pressure (smoking, truancy, exam cheating).
   - Equips students with assertiveness skills (the CLEAR model) to resist negative coercion calmly, firmly, and courageously.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing that an *Ascribed Status* like being born into wealth makes a person more intelligent or honorable than someone with an *Achieved Status*.
  - *Correction:* True honor and competence come from character, hard work, and achieved merit, not from unearned accidents of birth.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming that aggressive shouting is the only way to resist peer pressure.
  - *Correction:* True assertiveness is quiet, calm, polite, and unshakeable. Looking someone in the eye and saying *"No, thank you, I don't cheat"* carries far more power than angry arguments.
- **Mistake 3:** Treating the mass media as a neutral, harmless mirror of reality.
  - *Correction:* Mass media and digital networks are commercial systems designed to influence minds. Students must practice *media literacy* to filter harmful, violent, or deceptive content.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking that boys doing domestic chores reduces their masculine dignity.
  - *Correction:* Cooking, cleaning, and laundry are essential life survival skills that make both men and women capable, independent, and responsible adults.

## Worked Examples
- **Example 1: Dissecting an Adolescent's Social Trajectory**
  - *Scenario:* Obinna grew up in a rural fishing village in Rivers State. His parents taught him honesty and hard work (Primary Socialization). His primary and secondary teachers noticed his aptitude for mathematics and encouraged him (School Agent). His friends formed a study group to prepare for the National Science Olympiad (Positive Peer Pressure). He won a full scholarship to study Electrical Engineering at the University of Lagos (Upward Social Mobility).
  - *Analysis:* Obinna's success illustrates the harmonious alignment of primary family values, secondary educational nurture, constructive peer influence, and achieved status powering upward social mobility.
- **Example 2: Applying the CLEAR Model in an Examination Crisis**
  - *Scenario:* Ten minutes before the Basic Technology exam, a classmate approaches Zainab with a leaked cheat sheet and whispers: *"Copy this on your palm or you are not one of us!"*
  - *Application:*
    - *C (Calm):* Zainab stands tall, breathes slowly, and looks directly at the classmate.
    - *L (Loud & Clear):* *"No, I am not cheating."*
    - *E (Explain):* *"I studied all weekend, and I will not risk my education and integrity."*
    - *A (Alternative):* *"You still have ten minutes; let me quiz you on the major hand tools instead."*
    - *R (Remove):* When the student laughs and curses, Zainab walks directly into the exam hall to take her designated seat.
  - *Outcome:* The exam invigilator catches the cheater with the paper, leading to exam cancellation. Zainab scores 86% legitimately.
