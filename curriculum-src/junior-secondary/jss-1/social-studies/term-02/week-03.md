## Hook
Imagine walking into a grand royal palace in ancient Kano, Oyo, or Benin City: the Emir, Alaafin, or Oba sits upon an elevated, elaborately adorned throne, surrounded by hereditary titled noble chiefs, royal advisers, court musicians, palace guards, and common citizens. Every single person in that palace has a clearly recognized rank, title, and specific duty! Now jump forward to a modern hospital in Abuja: you see the Chief Medical Director, specialist consultant surgeons, registered nurses, laboratory scientists, administrative clerks, security guards, and patients. Why are human societies structured into layers, ranks, and specialized roles rather than everyone doing the exact same thing at the exact same level? Even more fascinating: fifty years ago in many Nigerian villages, girls were told their place was strictly in the kitchen, while boys were expected to clear farmlands and inherit property. Today, Nigerian women are commanding commercial aircraft, heading international financial banks, performing open-heart surgeries, and serving as deputy governors! How do social positions, status, and gender roles change over time? Let us explore!

## Key Terms
- Term: Social Stratification
- Definition: The hierarchical ranking and categorization of individuals and groups in a society into different socio-economic layers or strata based on wealth, power, prestige, occupation, or birth.
- Term: Social Status
- Definition: The social standing, position, or rank that an individual occupies within a society or group.
- Term: Ascribed Status
- Definition: A social position assigned to an individual at birth or involuntarily later in life based on biological or ancestral factors (such as royalty, gender, age, race, or caste) without regard to personal effort or talent.
- Term: Achieved Status
- Definition: A social position that an individual attains through personal effort, merit, education, hard work, skills, and choices (such as becoming a medical doctor, engineer, or lawyer).
- Term: Social Role
- Definition: The expected behavior, rights, duties, and responsibilities associated with a particular social status.
- Term: Role Conflict
- Definition: A situation occurring when an individual faces incompatible or contradictory expectations from two or more simultaneous social roles (e.g., being a caring mother and a demanding full-time company CEO).
- Term: Gender Roles
- Definition: The culturally and socially learned behaviors, expectations, and activities that a society considers appropriate for males and females.
- Term: Gender Stereotyping
- Definition: Rigid, oversimplified, and biased generalizations about the abilities, characteristics, and appropriate behaviors of boys and girls based purely on sex.
- Term: Social Mobility
- Definition: The movement of individuals, families, or groups upward or downward between different socio-economic strata or status levels in a society.
- Term: Meritocracy
- Definition: A social system where advancement, status, and rewards are based strictly on individual talent, effort, and demonstrated competence rather than hereditary privilege.

1. **Ascribed Status vs. Achieved Status:**
   - **Ascribed Status:** Imposed upon a person regardless of their wishes. You did not choose to be born a boy or a girl; a crown prince did not earn his royalty through an exam—it was bestowed by birth lineage.
   - **Achieved Status:** Gained through individual diligence, character, academic excellence, and perseverance. A child from a humble farming background in Benue State who studies hard, scores 340 in JAMB, and becomes an international neurosurgeon has attained an *achieved status*.

2. **Social Mobility in Modern Nigeria:**
   - In traditional feudal systems, people were locked into their parents' social strata with little chance of rising.
   - Modern Nigeria is increasingly an **open-class society** where **education is the great equalizer**. Through formal schooling, vocational skill acquisition, and hard work, a young person can rise from extreme poverty to the highest strata of national and global leadership.

3. **Gender Roles: Traditional vs. Modern Nigerian Society:**
   - **Traditional Gender Expectations:**
     - *Males:* Assigned roles as physical defenders of the community, heavy agricultural clearers, hunters, lineage heads, and primary decision-makers in village councils.
     - *Females:* Assigned domestic homemaking, food processing, water fetching, childcare, and market petty trading. Female education was often de-prioritized under the mistaken belief that "a woman’s education ends in the kitchen."
   - **Modern Realities and Gender Parity:**
     - Modern technology, education, and human rights have dismantled old gender restrictions.
     - Nigerian women hold major corporate executive positions, command military units, govern departments, invent scientific technologies, and run major industrial conglomerates (e.g., Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala heading the World Trade Organization).
     - Modern men actively participate in childcare, cooking, and emotional family nurture, recognizing that domestic responsibilities are shared human life skills.

## Common Mistakes
- **Mistake 1:** Believing that *Ascribed Status* is superior to *Achieved Status*.
  - *Correction:* An achieved status represents personal effort, competence, and character, whereas ascribed status is an accident of birth. Modern civilized society honors merit and achievement over mere hereditary privilege.
- **Mistake 2:** Assuming that cooking and sweeping are biological duties hardwired only into female bodies.
  - *Correction:* Preparing food and maintaining personal cleanliness are essential human survival skills. Every boy and girl should master basic cooking, laundry, and cleaning to become self-reliant adults.
- **Mistake 3:** Confusing *Gender* with *Sex*.
  - *Correction:* *Sex* refers to biological and anatomical differences between male and female bodies. *Gender* refers to the socially constructed roles, behaviors, and cultural expectations assigned to boys and girls by society.
- **Mistake 4:** Thinking that advancing women's education diminishes or oppresses men.
  - *Correction:* Educating girls empowers entire communities, doubles household income, improves child nutrition, and drives national prosperity for both men and women.

## Worked Examples
- **Example 1: Analyzing Status Acquisition**
  - *Scenario:* Chief Oladipo was born into a royal family in Abeokuta (Status A). However, he studied hard at the University of Ibadan, earned a degree in civil engineering, and built an engineering firm that constructed 20 major bridges across Nigeria (Status B).
  - *Analysis:*
    - Status A (being born into a royal family) is an **Ascribed Status** (inherited by birth).
    - Status B (becoming a certified civil engineer and successful firm owner) is an **Achieved Status** (earned through personal study, intellect, and hard work).
- **Example 2: Overcoming Gender Stereotyping in Academic Choices**
  - *Scenario:* In a JSS 1 class, an introductory technology teacher asks for volunteers to wire an electrical circuit. Two boys shout: "Circuits are for boys! Girls should stick to Home Economics and baking cakes!"
  - *Analysis:* The boys are exhibiting destructive gender stereotyping. The teacher intervenes, explaining that intellectual and technical aptitude has no gender. A female student, Fatima, steps up, wires the circuit with precision, and illuminates the test bulb in record time, demonstrating that girls can excel in STEM disciplines.

## Guardian Sync
- **Home Discussion Point:** Discuss the roles in your own home with your child. Ask: *“Do we assign household chores fairly, or do we assume certain jobs are only for boys or girls?”*
- **Aspiration Encouragement:** Encourage your daughter or son to dream without gender limitations—whether in aviation, software engineering, culinary arts, business management, or governance.
- **Diligence Value Check:** Reinforce that true societal respect comes from *achieved status*—one's own integrity, work ethic, and contributions to society—rather than entitlement based on family background.
