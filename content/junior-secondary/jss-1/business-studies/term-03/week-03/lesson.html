Why This Matters to You

Imagine walking into the headquarters of a major Nigerian conglomerate like Dangote Group or a massive retail chain in Lagos. They manage hundreds of delivery trucks, dozens of factories, thousands of employees, hundreds of thousands of suppliers, and millions of retail customers. If all these financial records were mixed together inside one giant notebook, how could the chief accountant instantly tell how much money a specific distributor in Kano owes, how much the company spent on diesel fuel last month, or what the company’s factory machinery is worth? The accountant would be lost in a sea of numbers! To bring perfect order to this vast ocean of data, accountants organize financial accounts into specialized collections called Ledgers, and systematically divide all accounts into Personal, Real, and Nominal accounts. In this lesson, you will master the architecture of the ledger—the supreme "Principal Book of Account"—and discover how every transaction in the commercial world finds its exact, classified home!

Physical Ledger Formats:

Bound Book Ledger: Traditional hardbound books with pre-numbered pages. Rigid and difficult to expand.
Loose-Leaf Ledger: Heavy-duty binders holding detachable, pre-printed ledger sheets. New sheets can be inserted anytime an account expands.
Card Ledger: Stiff pre-printed index cards filed alphabetically in metal cabinets or trays. Highly flexible for businesses with thousands of credit customers.

3. Classification of Accounts

Every account opened in an accounting system belongs to one of three primary families:

```
│

┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐

┌─────────────┴─────────────┐

▼ ▼

REAL ACCOUNTS NOMINAL ACCOUNTS

(Tangible Assets) (Expenses & Income)

```

A. Personal Accounts

Accounts that record commercial dealings with specific named persons or entities.
Examples:
Debtors: Alhaji Aliko Dangote Account, Mrs. Bimbo Adeleke Account.
Creditors: Unilever Nigeria Plc Account, Chi Farms Ltd Account.
Banking Institutions: Zenith Bank Plc Account.
Golden Rule for Personal Accounts: Debit the receiver, Credit the giver.

B. Impersonal Accounts: Real Accounts

Accounts that record physical, tangible property or material assets that the business owns, can see, and touch.
Examples: Land & Buildings Account, Plant & Machinery Account, Motor Vehicles Account, Office Furniture Account, Computer Hardware Account, Cash in Hand Account.
Golden Rule for Real Accounts: Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out.

C. Impersonal Accounts: Nominal Accounts

Accounts that record intangible commercial events, operations, gains, and losses. They do not represent physical objects you can touch; they represent reasons why money was spent or earned.
Examples:
Expenses & Losses: Rent Account, Wages & Salaries Account, Electricity & Power Account, Carriage Inwards, Bad Debts, Discounts Allowed.
Revenues & Gains: Sales Account, Discount Received Account, Commission Received Account, Rent Received Account.
Golden Rule for Nominal Accounts: Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all gains and revenues.
