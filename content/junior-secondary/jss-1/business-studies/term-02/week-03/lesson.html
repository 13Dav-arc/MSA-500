Why This Matters to You

Imagine a multi-million Naira legal contract arriving at your company's head office in a sealed envelope marked "URGENT: Submit within 24 hours to secure ₦50,000,000 project." The mail clerk carelessly tosses the envelope into an overflowing cardboard box behind the door, where it sits buried under old newspapers for three weeks. By the time the Managing Director discovers the letter, the deadline has passed, the lucrative project has been awarded to a fierce competitor, and the company has suffered a catastrophic financial blow! In the corporate world, an organization's lifeblood flows through its mail and correspondence. Every day, hundreds of critical business documents arrive and depart: bank payment drafts, customer purchase orders, government tax queries, supplier bills, and legal notices. If incoming mail is misdirected or outgoing letters are delayed, businesses collapse into legal disputes and financial ruin. In this lesson, you will master the seven sequential steps of processing incoming mail, explore outgoing mail dispatch systems, and learn how to maintain professional mail registers!

1. Importance of Efficient Mail Handling

Handling Mail and Office Correspondence: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

Managing correspondence with speed, accuracy, and security is vital for commercial success:

Preserves Legal Evidence: Date-stamped letters and dispatch books serve as admissible legal proof in court demonstrating when notices were received or mailed.
Protects Financial Assets: Inward mail often contains high-value payment cheques, bank drafts, and bills of exchange that must be accounted for immediately.
Promotes Customer Satisfaction: Prompt handling ensures that customer purchase orders and complaints receive swift responses, building corporate goodwill.

2. The Seven Steps of Handling Inward (Incoming) Mail

1.
Receiving the Mail: Mail arrives via postal delivery, collection from the post office box/private mail bag (PMB), or through physical delivery by courier dispatch riders (DHL, FedEx, NIPOST).
2.
Sorting the Mail:
The mail clerk sorts envelopes into three distinct categories:
Category A: Official general mail addressed to the organization.
Category B: Urgent / Express correspondence.
Category C: Mail clearly marked "Confidential," "Private," "Personal," or "Secret."

> [!IMPORTANT]

> The Cardinal Rule of Mail Handling: Letters marked "Confidential" or "Private" must NEVER be opened by the mail clerk! They must be delivered intact, sealed, and unopened directly to the named officer.

1.
Opening the Mail: Non-confidential official letters are slit open along the upper flap using a smooth letter opener. Slicing roughly with fingers or ripping envelopes tears important enclosed documents.
2.
Checking for Enclosures: The clerk removes all contents and checks the letter for the notation "Enc." or "Encl." (e.g., "We enclose our cheque for ₦50,000"). If an enclosure is missing, the clerk immediately writes "Enclosure Missing" in red ink on the letter margin and attaches the envelope.
3.
Date-Stamping: Every incoming letter is stamped with the official Inward Date Stamp in the top right-hand corner, permanently recording the exact date and time of arrival.
4.
Recording in the Inward Mail Register: The clerk logs the details in the Inward Mail Book:
Date received, Date on the letter, Name of sender and company, Subject matter, Action department/officer assigned, and File reference number.
1.
Distribution to Action Officers: Letters are placed in dedicated departmental dispatch folders or circulation trays and delivered to the relevant officers for immediate action.

---

3. Steps in Handling Outward (Outgoing) Mail

1.
Collection: Collecting signed letters and duplicate office file copies from executive desks.
2.
Verification of Enclosures: Checking that all attachments mentioned in the letter (cheques, catalogues, vouchers) are physically pinned behind the covering letter.
3.
Folding & Inserting into Envelopes: Folding the letter neatly in thirds and inserting it into a matching envelope. If a window envelope is used, the letter must be folded so that the recipient's typed address aligns perfectly with the clear plastic window.
4.
Weighing & Stamping: Weighing the envelope on a postal scale to determine the correct postage rate. The envelope is then stamped with adhesive stamps or processed through a franking machine.
5.
Recording in the Outward Mail Register (Despatch Book):
Date, Name and address of recipient, Department originating the letter, Postage cost, and Method of dispatch (hand delivery, registered post, courier).
1.
Dispatch: Depositing mail at the post office, handing it to a courier agent, or dispatching an office messenger with an internal delivery book.
