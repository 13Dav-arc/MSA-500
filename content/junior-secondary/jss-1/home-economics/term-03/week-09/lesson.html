The CLEAR Refusal Formula

When confronted with negative peer pressure or offered illicit drugs, execute the CLEAR Formula:

1.
C – Calm: Maintain steady composure; take a deep, quiet breath.
2.
L – Look directly into their eyes: Unwavering eye contact conveys unwavering confidence and self-respect.
3.
E – Enunciate a firm, clear "NO": Use definitive language: "No, I don't use drugs." Never use hesitant words like "Maybe next time" or "I am not sure."
4.
A – Alternative Suggestion: Pivot the conversation: "Let's go play basketball instead," or "I'm going to the library."
5.
R – Remove Yourself: If the peer persists or mocks you, immediately walk away to a safe, public environment. Never stay to debate!

2. Nigerian Regulatory and Enforcement Architecture

The war against substance abuse in Nigeria is led by specialized government agencies operating under statutory mandates:

A. NDLEA (National Drug Law Enforcement Agency)

Established by Decree No. 48 of 1989 (now Act Cap N30 Laws of the Federation of Nigeria 2004).
Core Enforcement Functions:
Supply Reduction: Policing international airports, seaports, border crossings, and major highways to intercept imported cocaine, heroin, and illicit synthetic opioids.
Forest Interdiction: Raiding and setting ablaze clandestine cannabis (Indian hemp) plantations in deep forest reserves (e.g., in Ondo, Edo, and Osun states).
Prosecution: Investigating, arresting, and prosecuting drug barons, couriers, and street-corner dealers in the Federal High Court.
Drug Demand Reduction: Running youth awareness campaigns in secondary schools, providing counseling services, and operating drug treatment and rehabilitation centers across Nigeria.

B. NAFDAC (National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control)

Controls the legal pharmaceutical supply chain to prevent legitimate prescription drugs from leaking into the black market.
Enforces strict bans on over-the-counter sales of codeine-containing cough syrups and controls the importation and distribution of high-potency opioids like Tramadol.

3. Rehabilitation, Recovery, and Social Reintegration

Addiction is a chronic medical disease of the brain, not a hopeless character flaw. Recovering addicts can be successfully rehabilitated through a four-phase medical model:

1.
Clinical Assessment & Diagnosis: Evaluating physical damage, mental health state, and specific chemical dependencies.
2.
Medical Detoxification: Supervised withdrawal management in a hospital setting to purge chemical toxins safely while managing painful symptoms with medications.
3.
Psychotherapy & Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT): Identifying the emotional trauma and psychological triggers that led to drug use and rewiring healthy thinking patterns.
4.
Vocational Training & Community Reintegration: Teaching trade skills (tailoring, computer programming, carpentry, agriculture) and providing family counseling to eliminate social stigma and prevent relapse.
