Foundational curriculum module in Home Economics covering "Rights and Responsibilities of the Child" for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Have you ever walked past a busy traffic intersection in Lagos, Kano, or Onitsha at ten o'clock on a Tuesday morning and seen a twelve-year-old child carrying a heavy tray of cold soft drinks or roasted plantains on their head, dodging moving cars in the scorching heat while other children are sitting safely in classrooms learning mathematics and science? Or have you heard of a brilliant young girl forced to drop out of school to become a domestic child maid or forced into early marriage? Is that fair? Does a child simply exist to be exploited, or does every child possess legal protections guaranteed by national and international law? In 2003, the Federal Republic of Nigeria enacted a historic law called the Child's Rights Act (CRA 2003), declaring that every child has inalienable rights to education, health, protection, and dignity! But rights do not exist in a vacuum; every right comes with corresponding responsibilities. In this lesson, we will explore your fundamental rights as a child, uncover the legal laws that protect you, and examine the vital duties you owe to your parents, your school, and your nation!

1. The Legal Definition of a Child

Rights and Responsibilities of the Child: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

Under both the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) and Nigeria's Child's Rights Act (CRA 2003):

A child is any individual who has not yet attained the age of eighteen (18) years.
Childhood is recognized as a precious, protected developmental period requiring special legal protection, parental care, education, and social nurturing.

2. Fundamental Rights of the Child (Child's Rights Act 2003)

A. Survival Rights

Right to Life: Every child has an inherent right to life; infanticide or denial of medical survival care is a capital crime.
Right to Adequate Standard of Living: Entitlement to balanced nutritious food, potable drinking water, weather-appropriate clothing, and secure, sanitary shelter provided by parents and supported by the state.
Right to Health and Healthcare Services: Free access to life-saving childhood immunizations (against polio, measles, tuberculosis), primary healthcare, and medical treatment when sick.

B. Development Rights

Right to Free, Compulsory Basic Education: Every Nigerian child has the legal right to free, compulsory primary and junior secondary education under the Universal Basic Education (UBE) framework. Keeping a child out of school is a punishable offense for parents.
Right to a Name and Legal Nationality: Every child must be registered at birth with the National Population Commission (NPC), receiving a legal birth certificate, a recognized name, and Nigerian citizenship.
Right to Leisure, Rest, and Recreation: Children are entitled to age-appropriate play, physical recreation, sports, and cultural arts to foster healthy emotional and physical growth.
Right to Parental Care and Upbringing: The right to live with and be nurtured by one's parents, except when separation is legally determined to be in the child's best interest.

C. Protection Rights

The Child's Rights Act strictly shields children from harmful societal practices:

Freedom from Child Labor: Prohibits hazardous economic exploitation, including street hawking during school hours, child domestic servitude, quarry mining, and industrial factory labor.
Protection from Child Abuse: Bans all physical brutality, harsh corporal injury, emotional cruelty, and sexual abuse.
Prohibition of Child Marriage: Section 21 of the CRA (2003) explicitly declares: "No person under the age of 18 years is capable of contracting a valid marriage." Any adult who marries a child or arranges the marriage of a child commits a serious criminal offense.
Protection from Harmful Traditional Practices: Prohibits harmful practices such as Female Genital Mutilation (FGM), forced tribal scarification, and witchcraft accusations against children.
Protection of Children with Disabilities: Children living with physical, sensory, or intellectual disabilities have the right to special dignity, inclusive education, assistive devices, and barrier-free public facilities.

D. Participation Rights

Right to Freedom of Thought, Conscience, and Expression: Children are entitled to express their thoughts and opinions respectfully in matters affecting their lives, appropriate to their age and maturity.

3. Responsibilities and Duties of the Child

A healthy society requires balance: Rights cannot exist without corresponding responsibilities! A child who demands their rights must fulfill their moral, domestic, and civic obligations:

A. Responsibilities to Parents and Family

1.
Respect, Honor, and Obedience: Treating parents, guardians, and older family members with deep respect, politeness, and gratitude.
2.
Assisting with Household Chores: Willingly and cheerfully performing age-appropriate domestic tasks (washing dishes, sweeping floors, making beds, weeding gardens, caring for younger siblings).
3.
Upholding Family Honor: Behaving with integrity, honesty, and moral dignity in public so as not to bring shame, embarrassment, or dishonor to the family name.

B. Responsibilities to Oneself

1.
Dedication to Education: Attending classes punctually, completing homework assignments diligently, listening to teachers, and studying hard to achieve academic excellence.
2.
Personal Hygiene and Health Care: Keeping the body, clothing, and living space clean, eating healthy food, and exercising regularly.
3.
Avoiding Harmful Vices: Resisting negative peer pressure; saying an absolute "NO" to illicit drugs, alcohol, smoking, cultism, sexual misconduct, and examination malpractice.

C. Responsibilities to the School and Peers

1.
Obeying School Regulations: Adhering to school uniform rules, attending assemblies, and respecting teachers and school prefects.
2.
Kindness and Anti-Bullying: Treating classmates with empathy and fairness; standing up against bullying, harassment, and discrimination.
3.
Protecting School Property: Caring for school desks, library books, laboratory apparatus, and campus gardens.

D. Responsibilities to the Community and Nation

1.
Patriotism and Civic Duty: Respecting Nigerian national symbols (the National Flag, National Anthem, and National Pledge); obeying civil laws.
2.
Community Service: Participating in environmental sanitation, keeping drainage gutters clear of plastic waste, and promoting peaceful coexistence across ethnic and religious lines.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Family Responsibilities Rota: Create a simple weekly chore chart together with your child. Assign age-appropriate tasks (setting the dinner table, making their bed, feeding pets) and celebrate their contribution to the family's welfare! - Child's Rights Discussion: Discuss the Child's Rights Act at the dinner table. Reassure your child that your home is a safe space where they are loved, protected from harm, and supported in their education. - Civic Awareness Walk: When you encounter a child working or begging on the street during school hours, take the opportunity to explain to your child why universal basic education is vital and how society must work to protect every child's future. ---
