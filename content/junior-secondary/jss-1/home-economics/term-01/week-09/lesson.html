Why This Matters to You

Have you ever tried to sweep a room using a broom whose bristles were bent, crushed, and squashed flat because someone shoved it head-first into a dark corner? Instead of sweeping away the dust, it just scratches the floor and kicks dirt into the air! Or have you tried to scrub a greasy, burnt aluminum cooking pot using only cold water, scrubbing until your arms ache, while an experienced homemaker steps in, sprinkles a little wood ash and crushed eggshells, rubs gently with a sponge, and makes the pot gleam like brand new silver in two minutes? Cleaning is not about brutal, exhausting physical labor; it is about science, proper tools, and the right cleaning agents! Knowing which broom to use, how to store a mop so it never smells like sour mildew, and how to harness both modern detergents and traditional local abrasives can turn hours of frustrating housework into quick, satisfying work. In this lesson, we will examine household cleaning equipment, master tool maintenance, explore commercial and homemade cleaning agents, and unlock the Term 1 80% Mastery Gate!

A. Brooms and Mops

Soft Brooms (Hair, Nylon, or Soft Grass): Used for sweeping smooth indoor surfaces (terrazzo, glazed tiles, polished hardwood floors) without scratching delicate finishes.
*Hard / Broomstick Brooms (Palm Frond Ribs - Igbale):* Stiff, rugged brooms made from oil palm or coconut frond midribs. Used for sweeping rough outdoor compounds, cement courtyards, verandas, and driveways.
Cobweb Brooms (Wall Brooms): Long-handled brooms with rounded bristle heads designed to reach high ceiling corners, ceiling fans, and cornices to remove spiderwebs and accumulated dust.
Floor Mops (String / Sponge Mops): Consist of absorbent cotton cords or cellulose sponge attached to a handle. Used with a wringer bucket for washing, damp-mopping, and disinfecting hard floors.

B. Brushes

Scrubbing Brushes: Possess short, stiff nylon or plant fibers mounted on a wooden or plastic block. Used for vigorous scouring of unpolished cement floors, tiled bathroom floors, and stubborn outdoor stains.
Toilet Brushes: Curved or angled bristle brushes mounted on a long handle, designed specifically to reach beneath the rim and down into the trap of the water closet (WC) bowl.

C. Dusters, Cloths, and Receptacles

Dusters: Soft, lint-free cotton or microfiber cloths that attract and hold dust particles without scratching polished wooden furniture or glass.
Floor Cloths: Heavy, coarse cotton cloths used for drying or scrubbing floors.
Dustpans: Plastic or metal shallow trays with a flat edge used to gather and transfer swept dirt into a waste bin.
Waste Bins: Plastic or metal bins fitted with tight-fitting lids and foot pedals, lined with polythene bags to contain refuse hygienic.

2. Care, Maintenance, and Storage of Cleaning Equipment

Proper maintenance extends the working life of tools and ensures sanitary conditions:

1.
Care of Brooms:
After sweeping, shake out trapped fluff, hair, and dust.
Storage Rule: Store brooms hanging upside down on a wall hook by a cord through the handle, or resting upside down with the handle on the floor. Never store a broom resting on its bristles! The weight of the broom bends, curls, and crushes the bristles permanently.
1.
Care of Brushes:
Wash dirty brushes in warm soapy water, comb out tangled fibers with an old comb, and rinse thoroughly.
Dry brushes with the bristles facing downwards so water drains out of the wooden backing, preventing wood rot and bristle loosening.
1.
Care of Mops:
Wash the mop head thoroughly with soap and warm water after every mopping session.
Squeeze or wring out excess water completely.
Hang the mop outside in the sun and fresh air to dry thoroughly. Never leave a damp, unwashed mop bunched inside a dark cupboard or dirty bucket, as it breeds foul-smelling mildew and fungal bacteria!
1.
Care of Dustpans and Buckets:
Wash with soapy water, rinse, wipe dry, and store upside down on storage shelves.

3. Cleaning Agents and Materials

Cleaning agents are chemical or natural compounds that loosen grime and eliminate germs:

A. Water

The universal cleaning solvent. Dissolves water-soluble dirt:

Cold Water: Suitable for rinsing soap away and soaking blood or protein stains (hot water coagulates protein stains).
Hot Water: Melts, emulsifies, and dissolves cooking oils, animal fats, and heavy grease much faster than cold water.

B. Soaps and Synthetic Detergents

Soaps: Made from natural animal fats or vegetable oils reacted with an alkali (caustic soda/potash). Ideal for gentle cleaning, personal bathing, and washing delicate fabrics.
Synthetic Detergents: Formulated from petrochemical surfactants. Unlike soap, detergents lather freely in both soft water and hard water (water containing dissolved calcium and magnesium salts) without forming sticky scum (curd).

C. Abrasives (Scouring Agents)

Clean surfaces by mechanical friction to wear away stubborn deposits:

Commercial Abrasives: Formulated scouring powders (such as Vim, Ajax) containing finely ground silica, detergents, and mild bleaching agents. Excellent for stainless steel sinks, ceramic basins, and aluminum pans.
Traditional / Homemade Local Abrasives in Nigeria:
1.
Clean River Sand: Fine, sifted sand used to scrub greasy iron pots and firewood soot.
2.
Finely Ground Eggshells: Crushed into fine powder, providing gentle abrasive scouring that removes stains without deeply scratching aluminum cookware.
3.
Wood Ash: Cold ash gathered from traditional firewood stoves. Contains alkaline potassium carbonate (potash), which chemically reacts with grease to form soap while providing gentle abrasive scouring for blackened cooking pots.
4.
Sandpaper Leaves (Ficus exasperata - Ewe Epin in Yoruba): Coarse, rough leaves with natural abrasive microscopic silica hairs, traditionally used to polish and scrub wooden mortars and cooking utensils.

D. Disinfectants and Bleaches

Disinfectants (e.g., Izal, Dettol, Pine Oil): Chemical compounds added to cleaning water to destroy pathogenic bacteria, viruses, and fungi on floors, drains, and latrines.
Bleaches (e.g., Sodium Hypochlorite / Jik): Powerful oxidizing agents used to whiten yellowed white cotton fabrics, remove stubborn organic food stains, and sanitize drinking water tanks.
