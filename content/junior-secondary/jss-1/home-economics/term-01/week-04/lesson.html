Why This Matters to You

Have you ever heard someone in your community whisper fearful rumors about a neighbor, claiming that you could catch HIV just by sitting next to them in a commercial bus, sharing a bench in church or mosque, or being bitten by a mosquito that bit them earlier? Or have you heard teenagers spreading false myths that washing with strong soft drinks or salt water after sexual activity can prevent infections? These dangerous myths and superstitious fears have caused immense suffering, unnecessary panic, and unfair discrimination across our society! In reality, science has unlocked exactly how microscopic pathogens attack our bodies, how infections are transmitted, and how they can be 100% prevented. In this lesson, we will uncover the factual truth about Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) and HIV/AIDS, learn how to protect our health through knowledge and personal values, and discover how empathy and compassion defeat stigma!

2. Common Sexually Transmitted Infections in Nigeria

A. Gonorrhea

Causative Agent: The bacterium Neisseria gonorrhoeae.
Symptoms:
In Males: Burning, stinging pain during urination; thick, creamy yellowish-green pus discharge from the penis; painful swelling of the testes.
In Females: Often mild or asymptomatic initially; burning urination, increased vaginal discharge, pelvic pain, and abnormal bleeding.
Consequences of Neglect: Leads to Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID), blocked fallopian tubes, permanent ectopic pregnancy, and infertility in both men and women. If an infected mother gives birth vaginally, the bacteria can infect the newborn's eyes, causing permanent blindness (ophthalmia neonatorum).
Treatment: Curable with doctor-prescribed antibiotics.

B. Syphilis

Causative Agent: The spirochete bacterium Treponema pallidum.
Progression in Stages:
Primary Stage: A single, firm, painless round sore called a chancre appears at the infection site (genitals or mouth) and heals naturally after a few weeks, giving a false impression that the infection has gone.
Secondary Stage: Non-itchy reddish-copper skin rashes appear across the body (especially on palms and soles), accompanied by fever, swollen lymph nodes, and sore throat.
Latent & Tertiary Stages: If untreated, the bacteria multiply secretly for years, eventually attacking the brain, spinal cord, heart, and bones, causing blindness, mental paralysis (dementia), and death.
Congenital Syphilis: Transmitted from infected mother to fetus, causing stillbirth, deformities, or infant death.
Treatment: Completely curable with penicillin antibiotics if diagnosed early.

C. Chlamydia and Trichomoniasis

*Chlamydia (Chlamydia trachomatis):* Known as the "silent epidemic" because up to 70% of infected individuals show no outward symptoms while the bacteria silently damage reproductive organs, causing scarring and infertility.
*Trichomoniasis (Trichomonas vaginalis):* A microscopic protozoan parasite causing frothy, greenish-yellow, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, intense itching, and discomfort during urination.

3. The Biology and Transmission of HIV/AIDS

HIV is a virus that specifically attacks the immune system:

How It Works: HIV penetrates CD4 T-helper cells, hijacks their genetic machinery to replicate millions of new virus particles, and bursts out, destroying the host immune cell.
As the CD4 count drops from a healthy 1,000 cells/µL down below 200 cells/µL, the body loses its ability to fight common germs.
The person enters the stage of AIDS, becoming vulnerable to lethal opportunistic infections such as tuberculosis (TB), chronic diarrhea, fungal pneumonia (Pneumocystis), and Kaposi's sarcoma (skin cancer).

The Four Verified Routes of HIV Transmission

1.
Unprotected Sexual Contact: Sexual intercourse with an infected partner where infected semen or vaginal fluid enters the bloodstream through mucosal micro-tears.
2.
Mother-to-Child Transmission (MTCT): From an infected mother to her baby during pregnancy (through the placenta), during labor and vaginal delivery, or after birth through breast milk.
3.
Contaminated Blood Transfusion: Receiving blood or blood products that have not been screened for HIV antibodies.
4.
Shared Sharp Skin-Piercing Instruments: Using unsterilized hypodermic needles, syringes, razor blades, traditional circumcision blades, scarification tools, or tattooing equipment contaminated with infected blood.

4. Prevention, Treatment, and Destigmatization

Prevention Strategies (The ABC Rule)

A — Abstinence: For young adolescents and unmarried students, total abstinence from all sexual activity is the only 100% effective, zero-risk method to avoid sexually transmitted infections, HIV, and unwanted teen pregnancy.
B — Be Faithful: For married adults, remaining mutually faithful to one uninfected partner.
C — Condom Use: Correct and consistent use of latex barrier condoms during every sexual encounter reduces STI and HIV transmission risk for non-abstinent adults.
Safe Blood & Injections: Screen 100% of donor blood in certified hospitals; always insist on brand-new, sterile, disposable needles opened from fresh packaging in your presence.
PMTCT Programs: Pregnant mothers living with HIV take daily antiretroviral medication, which suppresses the virus so effectively that babies are born completely HIV-free!

Modern Management: Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)

While there is currently no permanent cure or preventive vaccine for HIV, Antiretroviral Drugs (ARVs) have transformed HIV from a fatal death sentence into a manageable chronic condition.
When taken faithfully every day, ARVs suppress the viral load in the blood to undetectable levels.
Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U): A person living with HIV whose viral load is undetectable cannot transmit the virus to their sexual partners and can live a long, active, normal lifespan!

Ending Stigmatization and Discrimination

People living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA) deserve love, respect, dignity, and equal social rights.
Denying someone school admission, housing, employment, or friendship because of their HIV status is unjust, cruel, and illegal under Nigerian federal anti-discrimination legislation.
Showing empathy, offering encouragement, and sharing meals with affected persons fosters a compassionate, healthy society.
