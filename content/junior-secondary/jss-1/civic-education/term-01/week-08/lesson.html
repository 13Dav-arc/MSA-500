Why This Matters to You

When Nigerian track star Tobi Amusan was a young secondary school student in Ijebu-Ode, Ogun State, she was already fast, but raw speed was not enough to conquer the world. She woke up before sunrise every morning, endured grueling hurdle drills under the watchful eyes of dedicated coaches, perfected her stride technique, and battled through agonizing defeats. In 2022, in Eugene, Oregon, she shattered the World Record in the 100-meter hurdles with a breathtaking 12.12 seconds, bringing tears of joy to millions of Nigerians! Now compare that with countless young people who possessed magical singing voices or lightning football skills in our streets, but spent their afternoons smoking, gambling, or wasting time on street corners. Today, their names are forgotten. What makes the difference between a world champion and a forgotten street legend? The answer is Nurturing Talents and Skills! A seed buried in dry dust dies, but a seed watered, pruned, and protected becomes a mighty iroko tree!

Core Concept

What Does It Mean to Nurture Talents and Skills?

Having raw talent is like finding an unpolished diamond buried in the mud: it looks like an ordinary, dull grey rock until an expert diamond cutter cleans, saws, grinds, and polishes its facets. Only then does it sparkle with blinding brilliance and immense value! Nurturing talents means converting raw biological potential into master-level competence through education, discipline, practice, and moral guidance.

Channels for Nurturing Talents and Skills

Young people have access to diverse platforms to develop their abilities:

1.
School Co-Curricular Clubs and Societies:
Literary & Debating Society: Cultivates public speaking, logical argumentation, and linguistic eloquence.
Junior Engineers, Technicians, and Scientists (JETS) Club: Sharpens scientific inquiry, robotics, invention, and engineering.
Creative Arts & Music Club: Refines visual painting, sculpture, vocal pitch, and musical instrumentation.
Sports and Athletics Clubs: Builds speed, stamina, tactical teamwork, and Olympic athletic discipline.
1.
Vocational and Technical Training Centers:
Formal technical colleges and trade schools offering accredited courses in mechatronics, web development, coding, and culinary arts.
1.
The Traditional Apprenticeship System:
Invaluable informal vocational training (e.g., the famous Igbo Apprenticeship Scheme or Igba-Boi), where youths learn business management, sourcing, sales, and supply chain logistics under master merchants.
1.
Competitions and Talent Showcases:
Regional science fairs, National Mathematics Olympiads, essay competitions, and inter-school sports festivals (e.g., National School Sports Festival) that test abilities against the best peers.
1.
Digital Learning Platforms:
Utilizing educational YouTube tutorials, open-source coding bootcamps, language-learning apps, and online graphic design platforms.

---

The Vital Role of Mentorship

A mentor is someone who has walked the path you are just starting. Mentors accelerate your growth by:

Sharing Practical Wisdom: Revealing shortcuts, professional standards, and unwritten rules that textbooks do not cover.
Providing Honest Feedback: Pointing out flaws in your technique lovingly so you can correct them before critical competitions.
Building Emotional Resilience: Encouraging you when you feel like quitting after painful defeats or rejections.
Opening Networks and Opportunities: Introducing you to universities, scholarship boards, sports scouts, and employers.

---

Severe Consequences of Wasted or Undeveloped Talents

When a young person fails to nurture their talents, the damage ripples across their entire life and society:

Personal Frustration and Bitter Regret: Living with the painful awareness that one had great gifts but lacked the discipline to fulfill them.
Poverty and Economic Stagnation: Inability to earn premium income in a competitive global knowledge economy.
Vulnerability to Anti-Social Vices: Idle hands and unchallenged minds easily fall prey to substance abuse, street gangs, cultism, and cybercrime.
National Loss of Innovation and Prestige: When thousands of potential doctors, inventors, authors, and athletes waste away, Nigeria loses technological breakthroughs and global medals.
