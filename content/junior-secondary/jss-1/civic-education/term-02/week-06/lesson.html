Core Concept

What are Duties and Obligations?

If Rights are what the nation gives to the citizen, Duties are what the citizen must give back to the nation. Rights and duties are the two sides of a coin: you cannot flip one side without moving the other. In Civic Education, a duty is an action expected of a citizen in exchange for the constitutional freedoms and security guaranteed by the sovereign state.

Detailed Classification of Citizen Obligations:

1. Financial Obligations (Section 24f)

The state needs immense financial resources to construct asphalt highways, build public schools, fund police forces, and provide clean pipe-borne water. Citizens who earn income must pay Personal Income Tax (PAYE), business levies, and property rates promptly. Tax evasion is a serious criminal offense.

2. Economic Obligations

Citizens must respect and actively safeguard public property. Public utilities—such as oil pipelines, electrical transformers, railway tracks, telecommunications masts, and public school buildings—belong to all Nigerians. Citizens must never vandalize these assets or buy stolen public cables.

3. Civic Obligations

The foundational duty to obey the laws of the land. When citizens obey traffic wardens, observe building codes, and respect courts, the community enjoys order. The alternative to law obedience is anarchy—a terrifying state of total chaos, violence, and destruction.

4. Political Obligations

A democracy cannot survive if citizens refuse to participate. Qualified citizens (18 years and above) have an obligation to register with the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), vote during elections, resist vote-buying, and constructively monitor elected leaders.

5. Social and Security Obligations

National Defense: When the nation faces external aggression or invasion, citizens must be ready to defend the fatherland or serve in the military.
Reporting Criminals: Assisting the Nigeria Police, Civil Defence (NSCDC), and military by providing credible intelligence about kidnappers, armed robbers, and terrorists.
Environmental Sanitation: Keeping drainage gutters clean, disposing of garbage in approved bins, and halting the illegal dumping of refuse that causes catastrophic urban flooding.

---

Why Rights and Duties are Vital to Society

Social Control: They provide clear legal guardrails that prevent human conflict and curb criminal impulses.
Guaranteed Peace and Stability: Respect for mutual rights and performance of duties creates harmonious coexistence across ethnic and religious groups.
Due Process: Ensures that disputes are resolved lawfully through independent courts rather than jungle justice.
Societal Discipline: Instills a culture of orderliness, punctuality, and civic pride in both young and old.
