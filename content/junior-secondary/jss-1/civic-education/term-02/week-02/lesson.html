Why This Matters to You

Consider this fascinating family story: Mr. and Mrs. Okonkwo are Nigerian citizens working as medical doctors in London, United Kingdom. While living in London, they give birth to a baby girl named Chiamaka. Chiamaka has never set foot on Nigerian soil, yet the moment she was born, she became an authentic, legal citizen of the Federal Republic of Nigeria! How could a baby born five thousand kilometers away in England be a Nigerian citizen from the very second she drew her first breath? At the same time, an Italian engineer who has lived peacefully in Kano for twenty-five years, speaks fluent Hausa, and loves eating Tuwo Shinkafa, is not automatically a citizen unless he submits a formal application to the President! Why does the law treat these two situations so differently? The answer lies in the Types of Citizenship established under the 1999 Constitution of Nigeria! Let us unlock this constitutional mystery!

Core Concept

How Does One Acquire Nigerian Citizenship?

Under Chapter III (Sections 25, 26, 27, and 32) of the 1999 Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, citizenship is not acquired in one single way. The constitution provides five clear, distinct legal avenues for acquiring citizenship:

The Five Constitutional Types of Nigerian Citizenship: Understanding Section 25 to 27 of the 1999 Constitution

---

Detailed Examination of the Five Types:

1. Citizenship by Birth (Section 25)

This is the most common form of citizenship in Nigeria. A person is a citizen by birth if:

They were born in Nigeria before October 1, 1960, and either of their parents or grandparents belonged to an indigenous Nigerian community.
They were born in Nigeria after October 1, 1960, and either of their parents or any of their grandparents is a citizen of Nigeria.
Crucial Rule: Simply being born on Nigerian soil to two foreign tourists or temporary aliens does not automatically make that child a Nigerian citizen. Nigeria follows primarily Jus Sanguinis (blood lineage) rather than unconditional Jus Soli (soil birth).

2. Citizenship by Descent

This applies to individuals born outside the territory of Nigeria:

If a child is born in London, New York, Johannesburg, or Beijing, but their father, mother, or any grandparent was a Nigerian citizen by birth, that child is automatically a Nigerian citizen by descent.
Such individuals only need to present their birth certificates and their parents' Nigerian passports at a Nigerian Embassy to receive their green Nigerian passport.

3. Citizenship by Registration (Section 26)

This route is specifically designed for foreign spouses and persons of Nigerian heritage:

Foreign Women Married to Nigerian Citizens: A French, Ghanaian, or Japanese woman who marries a Nigerian male citizen can apply to the President to be registered as a Nigerian citizen.
Foreign-born Adults of Nigerian Heritage: A person of full age and capacity born outside Nigeria whose grandparents were Nigerian citizens.
Requirements: The applicant must satisfy the President of good character, show clear intention to reside permanently in Nigeria, and take the constitutional Oath of Allegiance.

4. Citizenship by Naturalization (Section 27)

This route allows qualified foreign nationals (aliens) living in Nigeria to voluntarily become citizens:

Conditions for Naturalization:
1.
The applicant must be of full age (at least 18 years old) and sound mind.
2.
The applicant must be a person of good character, certified by reputable citizens in their local community.
3.
The applicant must have lived in Nigeria continuously for at least 15 years (or 12 consecutive months plus 15 aggregate years).
4.
The applicant must show genuine assimilation into the customs, language, and culture of the local community where they reside.
5.
The applicant must be acceptable to the local community in which they live.
6.
The applicant must be capable of contributing positively to the economic and social advancement of Nigeria.
7.
The applicant must officially renounce their previous foreign citizenship (unless the President permits an exception) and take the Oath of Allegiance.

5. Honorary Citizenship

This is a distinguished, symbolic status conferred by the President and the Federal Executive Council upon an eminent foreigner (such as a foreign head of state, Nobel laureate, or global philanthropist) who has rendered outstanding, heroic, or extraordinary service to Nigeria.
