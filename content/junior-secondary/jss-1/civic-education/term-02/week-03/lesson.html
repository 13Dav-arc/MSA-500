Core Concept

The Constitutional Framework: How an Alien Becomes a Citizen

Acquiring the citizenship of a sovereign nation is a profound legal privilege, not an automatic right. Under Section 27 of the 1999 Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, the President may grant a certificate of naturalization to any alien who submits a formal application and satisfies seven non-negotiable statutory requirements:

Step-by-Step Procedure of Application

1.
Filing the Application: The alien submits a formal petition through the Federal Ministry of Interior with certified documentation (passports, residence permits, tax clearance certificates for the preceding three years).
2.
Community & Security Vetting: The Department of State Services (DSS), the Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS), and the Police conduct thorough background checks to ensure zero criminal record, zero involvement in terrorism, and verified good moral character.
3.
Public Notice in National Newspapers: The applicant must publish a public notice of intent in two national daily newspapers, inviting any citizen who knows a reason why the applicant should not become a citizen to submit objections within a specified period.
4.
Local Government & Community Endorsement: Traditional rulers (such as an Emir, Oba, or Obi) and local government chairmen must certify that the applicant has assimilated peacefully into the community.
5.
Presidential Recommendation & Approval: The Minister of Interior presents vetted applications to the Federal Executive Council (FEC) and the President for final approval.
6.
Taking the Oath of Allegiance: Upon presidential approval, the candidate appears physically to swear or affirm the sacred Oath of Allegiance.

---

The Text of the Constitutional Oath of Allegiance

As codified in the Seventh Schedule of the 1999 Constitution of Nigeria:

> "I, [Full Name of Applicant], do solemnly swear that I will be faithful and bear true allegiance to the Federal Republic of Nigeria and that I will preserve, protect and defend the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria. So help me God."

Upon signing this oath, the individual is presented with their official Certificate of Naturalization, conferring the rights and duties of a Nigerian citizen.

---

Deprivation of Citizenship (Section 30)

Unlike a citizen by birth whose citizenship can never be taken away, a naturalized or registered citizen can be deprived of their citizenship by the President if:

1.
Criminal Conviction: Within seven years of being naturalized, the person is sentenced to imprisonment for a term of not less than three years by any court of law.
2.
Acts of Disloyalty: The person has, through speech or actions, demonstrated disloyalty or hostility toward the Federal Republic of Nigeria.
3.
Aiding an Enemy in Wartime: During a time of war, the person unlawfully trades with, communicates with, or assists an enemy nation engaged in hostilities against Nigeria.
4.
Fraud in Application: It is discovered that the citizenship was obtained through false documentation, impersonation, or bribery.
