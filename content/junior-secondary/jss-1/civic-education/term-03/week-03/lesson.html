Why This Matters to You

Imagine walking through a bustling sports stadium in London, Paris, or Tokyo during an international athletics championship. The stadium announcer’s voice echoes across sixty thousand spectators: "Ladies and gentlemen, please rise for the National Anthem of the Federal Republic of Nigeria!" Instantly, sixty thousand people of all nationalities stop talking, rise to their feet, and stand in total, reverent silence. Even foreign heads of state remove their hats and stand at rigid attention out of absolute respect for our sovereign country! Now imagine an ordinary citizen in a local marketplace who hears the national anthem over the radio, laughs, kicks a muddy green-white-green flag into a gutter, and throws Naira notes on the ground to be stomped on by party guests. What a heartbreaking contrast! Why do laws exist that treat the desecration of national symbols as a crime? Because disrespecting your national symbols is disrespecting your motherland, your heritage, and yourself! Let us master the sacred etiquette of our national emblems!

Core Concept

Why Must National Symbols Be Respected?

National symbols are the tangible embodiment of Nigeria's sovereignty, dignity, and collective independence. Disrespecting a national flag, stomping on currency, or mocking the national anthem is an attack on the sovereign authority of the state.

Strict Protocols for Handling the National Flag

Under the Flags and Coats of Arms Act, strict regulations govern the display and handling of the green-white-green banner:

1.
Timing: The flag is hoisted briskly at sunrise (6:00 AM) and lowered slowly and ceremoniously at sunset (6:00 PM). It should not remain flying overnight in the dark unless illuminated by dedicated spotlights.
2.
Never Touch the Ground: The flag must never touch the earth, floor, or water. When lowering the flag, ceremonial handlers must catch it carefully and fold it neatly.
3.
Flown on the Highest Mast: When flown alongside state flags (e.g., Lagos or Kano State flag), corporate flags, or school banners, the Nigerian National Flag must occupy the position of honor (the highest pole, or the center pole). No foreign or private flag may fly higher than the Nigerian flag on Nigerian soil!
4.
Flying at Half-Mast: The flag is flown at half-mast (first raised to the peak of the pole, then solemnly lowered to the halfway mark) only upon an official proclamation by the President of Nigeria during periods of national mourning or upon the death of an eminent head of state.
5.
Retirement of Damaged Flags: A faded, soiled, or torn flag must never be displayed outside any public building. It must be reverently retired and destroyed in private, usually by dignified burning, so that its pieces are not scattered in trash heaps.

Etiquette During the National Anthem and Pledge

Whenever the National Anthem is played or sung:

Immediate Halt: Every citizen within hearing distance must stop walking, riding, or engaging in conversations.
Physical Stance: Stand at rigid attention with your body straight, chest out, eyes looking forward, and hands held straight along the seams of your trousers or skirt.
Removal of Headgear: Gentlemen and boys must remove non-religious caps or hats.
Reciting the Pledge: When reciting the National Pledge, place your right hand flat across the left side of your chest (over your heart), symbolizing sincerity and personal loyalty.

Legal Penalties for Desecrating the National Currency

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Act 2007 (Section 21) strictly outlaws the abuse and mutilation of Naira banknotes:

Prohibited Conduct:
Spraying Naira notes during parties, weddings, or concerts.
Stepping on, dancing on, or trampling banknotes on dance floors.
Writing notes, drawing caricatures, or stamping telephone numbers on currency.
Folding currency into tight balls, squeezing, or stapling banknotes with metal staples.
Hawking, selling, or trading new currency notes for higher amounts.
Statutory Penalties: Any person who commits currency mutilation or abuse is guilty of a criminal offense and, upon conviction, is liable to a fine of not less than ₦50,000, imprisonment for a term of not less than six months, or both!

---

Prohibitions Regarding the National Coat of Arms

Under Section 5 of the Flags and Coats of Arms Act:

It is a criminal offense to use the National Coat of Arms or state seals for unauthorized private commercial branding, private business letterheads, or marketing advertisements without written presidential authorization.
The Coat of Arms is reserved exclusively for the President, Governors, the National Assembly, courts of law, ministries, and diplomatic missions.
