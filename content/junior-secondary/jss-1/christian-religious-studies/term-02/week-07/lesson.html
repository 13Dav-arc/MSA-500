Foundational curriculum module in Christian Religious Studies covering "Call to Service I: Meaning of Service, Joseph Sold to Egypt, and Joseph's Faithful Service in Egypt" for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine being seventeen years old, gifted, beloved by your father, and having dreams of doing great things. Then, out of sheer jealousy, your own brothers throw you into a dry pit in the desert, strip you of your beautiful coat of many colors, and sell you for twenty pieces of silver to slave traders bound for a foreign country! In an instant, you go from being a favored prince to a penniless slave who does not speak the language. What would you do? Would you become bitter, plot revenge, and refuse to work? Or would you serve with such diligence, honesty, and godliness that your masters put you in charge of everything they own? That was the breathtaking life of Joseph in Egypt. In this lesson, you will discover that true Christian service is not about title or privilege—it is about honoring God through integrity, hard work, and loyalty wherever life places you!

Betrayal and the Pit at Dothan (Genesis 37:12–36)

The Seeds of Jealousy: Jacob loved Joseph more than all his children because he was the son of his old age, and gave him a magnificent robe of many colors. When Joseph shared his prophetic dreams—where his brothers' sheaves of wheat bowed to his sheaf, and the sun, moon, and eleven stars bowed to him—his brothers hated him even more.
The Ambush at Dothan: Jacob sent seventeen-year-old Joseph from the Valley of Hebron to check on the welfare of his brothers pasturing flocks in Shechem and Dothan. Seeing him from afar, his brothers plotted: "Here comes this dreamer. Come now, let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits... and we shall see what will become of his dreams."
Reuben and Judah Intervene:
Reuben, the eldest, sought to rescue him and return him to Jacob, persuading them: "Shed no blood; throw him into this pit here in the wilderness, but do not lay a hand on him."
While Reuben was absent, a caravan of Ishmaelite/Midianite merchants approached, bearing spices and balm to Egypt. Judah proposed: "What profit is it if we kill our brother and conceal his blood? Come, let us sell him to the Ishmaelites."
The Sale into Slavery: The brothers dragged Joseph out of the dry cistern and sold him for twenty shekels of silver. They dipped Joseph's beautiful robe in the blood of a slaughtered goat and brought it to Jacob, deceiving him into believing a fierce wild animal had devoured his son. Jacob tore his clothes and wept inconsolably.

Joseph's Service in Egypt (Genesis 39:1–41:57)

Faithful Service in Potiphar's House: Joseph was sold in Egypt to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard. The secret to Joseph's extraordinary success is repeated four times in Genesis 39: "The Lord was with Joseph, and he was a successful man."
Potiphar saw that the Lord was with Joseph and that God caused all that he did to prosper. Potiphar made Joseph overseer of his house, putting all his possessions under Joseph's charge.
The Test of Moral Integrity: Potiphar's wife repeatedly tempted Joseph to commit adultery. Joseph refused with moral brilliance:
"How then can I do this great wickedness and sin against God?" (Genesis 39:9).
When she grabbed his cloak, Joseph fled the room, leaving the garment behind. Falsely accused of assault, Joseph was unjustly cast into Pharaoh's royal prison.
Diligent Service in the Prison: Even in the dark prison dungeon, "the Lord was with Joseph and showed him steadfast love and gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison" (Genesis 39:21). The warden put Joseph in charge of all the prisoners. Joseph cared for his fellow prisoners with empathy, successfully interpreting the dreams of Pharaoh's royal cupbearer and baker.
Interpretation of Pharaoh's Dreams: Two full years later, Pharaoh had two troubling dreams: seven sleek, fat cows consumed by seven ugly, gaunt cows; and seven plump ears of grain swallowed by seven thin, scorched ears.
Joseph was brought from the dungeon. He gave glory to God: "It is not in me; God will give Pharaoh a favorable answer."
Joseph explained that seven years of bumper agricultural abundance across Egypt would be followed by seven years of catastrophic famine. He recommended appointing a discerning, wise administrator to store twenty percent of grain during the years of plenty.
Promotion to Prime Minister (Vizier): Pharaoh recognized the Spirit of God in Joseph: "Can we find a man like this, in whom is the Spirit of God?" (Genesis 41:38). Pharaoh placed his own signet ring on Joseph's finger, dressed him in fine linen, placed a gold chain around his neck, and gave him the royal chariot.
At age thirty, Joseph became ruler over all Egypt under Pharaoh, successfully managing grain reserves that saved Egypt, Canaan, and his own repentant family from starvation.
