Week 06: "Call to Obedience II: The Birth of Isaac and The Supreme Test of Abraham's Faith on Mount Moriah"

Foundational curriculum module in Christian Religious Studies covering "Call to Obedience II: The Birth of Isaac and The Supreme Test of Abraham's Faith on Mount Moriah" for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine waiting twenty-five long years for a promised gift that you longed for with all your heart. Year after year passes until everyone around you says, "Give up, it is biologically impossible now!" Then, suddenly, at age 100, the miracle baby arrives! Your joy is uncontainable; laughter fills your home, and you name the boy "Laughter" (Isaac). But then, several years later, God asks you the hardest question imaginable: "Will you place this most precious gift back on the altar for Me?" That was the supreme test faced by Abraham on Mount Moriah. It was not a test to destroy him, but a test to prove that he loved the Giver even more than the gift. In this lesson, you will discover the extraordinary heights of biblical obedience, the meaning of Jehovah Jireh ("The Lord will provide"), and how radical trust unlocks God's greatest covenant blessings!

The Miracle of Isaac's Birth (Genesis 21:1–8)

Call to Obedience II: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration
The Long-Awaited Promise: God had promised Abraham a biological son when he was seventy-five years old. For twenty-five years, Abraham and Sarah waited through doubts, cultural mistakes (Ishmael), and physical aging.
The Fulfillment of God's Word: "The Lord visited Sarah as he had said, and the Lord did to Sarah as he had promised. And Sarah conceived and bore Abraham a son in his old age at the time of which God had spoken to him" (Genesis 21:1–2).
Ages and Joy: Abraham was one hundred years old when his son Isaac was born, and Sarah was ninety. Sarah exclaimed: "God has made laughter for me; everyone who hears will laugh over me."
Covenant Obedience on the 8th Day: Abraham circumcised Isaac when he was eight days old, exactly as God had commanded in the covenant of Genesis 17.

The Supreme Test on Mount Moriah (Genesis 22:1–19)

The Heart of the Test: After some years, God tested Abraham, saying: "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains of which I shall tell you" (Genesis 22:2).
Notice the emotional weight: "your son... your only son... whom you love." Isaac was the sole bearer of all the covenant promises!
Prompt, Early Morning Obedience: Abraham did not delay, argue, or hold a family council to debate God's command. He rose early in the morning, saddled his donkey, took two young men and his son Isaac, chopped the wood for the burnt offering, and set out.
The Three-Day Journey: For three agonizing days, Abraham walked with Isaac, wrestling with his faith. Hebrews 11:19 reveals Abraham's inner reasoning: "He considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead, from which, figuratively speaking, he did receive him back."
The Innocent Question: Reaching the base of the mountain, Abraham told the servants: "Stay here with the donkey; I and the boy will go over there and worship and come again to you." (Note his prophetic statement of faith: we will come again!). He placed the wood on Isaac's back while carrying the fire and the knife.
Isaac asked: "Behold, the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?"
Abraham replied with unwavering faith: "God will provide for himself the lamb for a burnt offering, my son."
The Altar and the Divine Intervention: On the mountain summit, Abraham built the altar, arranged the wood, bound his son Isaac, and laid him on the altar atop the wood. As Abraham reached out his hand and took the knife to slaughter his son, the Angel of the Lord called from heaven:
"Abraham, Abraham! Do not lay your hand on the boy or do anything to him, for now I know that you fear God, seeing you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me."
The Provision of Jehovah Jireh: Abraham looked up and saw behind him a ram caught in a thicket by its horns. Abraham went and took the ram and offered it up as a burnt offering instead of his son!
Abraham named the place The Lord Will Provide (Yahweh Yireh / Jehovah Jireh), giving rise to the proverb: "On the mount of the Lord it shall be provided."
The Unconditional Covenant Sworn by Oath: Because Abraham did not withhold his only son, God swore by Himself:
"I will surely bless you, and I will surely multiply your offspring as the stars of heaven and as the sand that is on the seashore... and in your offspring shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because you have obeyed my voice" (Genesis 22:16–18).
