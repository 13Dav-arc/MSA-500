Living in God's Service (1 Peter 2:18–25)

The Apostle Peter explains that serving God in a fallen world often brings unfair treatment:

1.
Enduring Injustice Graciously: "For this is a gracious thing, when, mindful of God, one endures sorrows while suffering unjustly. For what credit is it if, when you sin and are beaten for it, you endure? But if when you do good and suffer for it you endure, this is a gracious thing in the sight of God" (1 Peter 2:19–20).
2.
Jesus Christ as the Ultimate Archetype: "For to this you have been called, because Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example, so that you might follow in his steps. He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth. When he was reviled, he did not revile in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but continued entrusting himself to him who judges justly" (1 Peter 2:21–23).
3.
The Redemptive Purpose: Christ bore our sins in His body on the tree that we might die to sin and live to righteousness; by His wounds we are healed.

Ways Christians are Called to Serve Today

Christian youth are called to serve across three distinct environments:

1. Service in the Home and Family:
Honoring, respecting, and promptly obeying parents and guardians (Ephesians 6:1–3).
Cheerfully undertaking household chores: sweeping, washing dishes, caring for younger siblings or aging grandparents, just as Ruth cared for Naomi.
Fostering peace, avoiding sibling rivalry and jealousy, unlike Joseph's brothers.
2. Service in the School and Peer Community:
Assisting teachers with classroom materials, cleaning chalkboards, and organizing desks.
Tutoring struggling classmates who find mathematics, languages, or sciences challenging.
Standing up against school bullying, cultism, and exam malpractice with Joseph-like integrity.
3. Service in the Church and Broader Society:
Participating actively in youth choir, ushering, sanitation, and technical ministries in church.
Engaging in community sanitation, visiting orphanages, and helping the physically disabled.
Practicing professional and civic honesty in future careers as model Nigerian citizens.

Problems and Obstacles Encountered in Christian Service

When young people decide to serve God faithfully, they face real-world challenges:

1.
Misunderstanding and False Accusations: Just as Potiphar's wife falsely accused Joseph, students doing good may be misunderstood or accused of bad motives.
2.
Mockery, Peer Pressure, and Jealousy: Jealous peers may tease or ostracize a diligent student as an "eye-servant" or "teacher's pet."
3.
Physical Exhaustion and Burnout: Serving others without proper rest, balanced study, and prayer can lead to weariness.
4.
Lack of Appreciation or Ingratitude: Serving people who never say "thank you," like the royal cupbearer who forgot Joseph in prison for two full years.
5.
The Christian Response: Never lose heart! Galatians 6:9 promises: "And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up."
