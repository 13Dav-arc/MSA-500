Week 08: "Call to Service II: Ruth's Devoted Service to Naomi and Divine Reward"

Foundational curriculum module in Christian Religious Studies covering "Call to Service II: Ruth's Devoted Service to Naomi and Divine Reward" for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine moving to a foreign country with your family to escape a severe famine. While there, tragedy strikes: your father-in-law dies, and ten years later, both your husband and his brother die as well! You are left in a desolate home with an aging, heartbroken mother-in-law who has no money, no land, and no sons to protect her. She turns to you and says, "Go back to your parents' comfortable home, find another husband, and live your own life. I have nothing left to give you." What would you do? Most people would say a tearful goodbye and return to their comfort zone. But a young Moabite widow named Ruth made one of the most stunning declarations of love, service, and loyalty ever recorded in human history: "Where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God!" In this lesson, you will discover the beauty of self-sacrificing service, the blessing of loyalty, and how a humble gleaner in the barley fields became the great-grandmother of King David and an ancestor of Jesus Christ!

Call to Service II: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

True Christian service is defined by steadfast love (hesed in Hebrew)—loyal, self-giving commitment that expects nothing in return. Ruth's life exemplifies how humble, daily service to family and community aligns with God's sovereign redemptive plans.

The Background of Loss and the Covenant Vow (Ruth 1:1–22)

Famine and Bereavement: In the days when the judges ruled, a severe famine struck Bethlehem ("House of Bread"). Elimelech, his wife Naomi, and their two sons, Mahlon and Chilion, migrated across the Jordan to Moab.
Ten Years of Heartbreak: Elimelech died. The two sons married Moabite women—Orpah and Ruth. About ten years later, both Mahlon and Chilion died, leaving Naomi completely childless and destitute in a foreign land.
The Crossroads of Loyalty: Hearing that the Lord had visited Bethlehem with food, Naomi prepared to return. She urged her daughters-in-law to remain in Moab: "Go, return each of you to her mother's house. May the Lord deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me... No, my daughters, for it is exceedingly bitter to me for your sake that the hand of the Lord has gone out against me" (Ruth 1:8, 13).
Orpah's Choice: Orpah wept, kissed her mother-in-law, and returned to her people and her idols.
Ruth's Immortal Vow: Ruth clung to Naomi and spoke words that echo down the centuries:

"Do not urge me to leave you or to return from following you. For where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God. Where you die I will die, and there will I be buried. May the Lord do so to me and more also if anything but death parts me from you" (Ruth 1:16–17).

Return to Bethlehem: Seeing Ruth's determined devotion, Naomi spoke no more of leaving. They arrived in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest. Naomi lamented: "Do not call me Naomi [Pleasant]; call me Mara [Bitter], for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me... I went away full, and the Lord has brought me back empty."

Faithful Service in the Fields and Divine Elevation (Ruth 2:1–4:22)

Humble Labor as a Gleaner: Ruth did not sit in the house grieving or complaining. She asked Naomi: "Let me go to the field and glean among the ears of grain after him in whose sight I shall find favor."
The Field of Boaz: Scripture notes that "she happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz, who was of the clan of Elimelech" (Ruth 2:3). Boaz was a wealthy, righteous man of Bethlehem.
Boaz Honors Ruth's Character: Boaz noticed Ruth and asked his overseer about her. Discovering her identity, Boaz showed her extraordinary protection and generosity:
He commanded her to stay in his field beside his maiden workers.
He instructed young men not to touch her and let her drink water from the reapers' jars.
At mealtime, he invited her to dip her bread in wine vinegar and gave her roasted grain until she was satisfied.
He secretly instructed his reapers to let fall some bundles on purpose for her to glean.
Why Boaz Favored Ruth: When Ruth fell on her face asking why a foreigner should receive such kindness, Boaz answered:

"All that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband has been fully told to me, and how you left your father and mother and your native land and came to a people that you did not know before. The Lord repay you for what you have done, and a full reward be given you by the Lord, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge!" (Ruth 2:11–12).

The Kinsman-Redeemer and Divine Reward: Under Naomi's wise guidance, Ruth approached Boaz according to the Mosaic redemption law. Boaz recognized Ruth as "a worthy woman" (a woman of noble character) and legally bought Elimelech's estate, marrying Ruth as kinsman-redeemer.
The Sovereign Fruit of Service:
Ruth conceived and gave birth to a son named Obed.
The women of Bethlehem rejoiced with Naomi: "He shall be to you a restorer of life and a nourisher of your old age, for your daughter-in-law who loves you, who is more to you than seven sons, has given birth to him!"
Obed fathered Jesse, and Jesse fathered David, the greatest king of Israel.
Through Ruth's selfless service, this Gentile Moabite widow entered the direct human lineage of Jesus Christ, the Savior of the world (Matthew 1:5).
