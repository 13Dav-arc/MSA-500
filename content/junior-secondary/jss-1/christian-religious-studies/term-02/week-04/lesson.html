Why This Matters to You

Imagine your parents suddenly waking you up tomorrow morning and saying: "Pack your school bags and say goodbye to your neighborhood, your friends, and everything familiar. We are moving to a new land. We do not have an address yet, but God has told us to pack up and start walking, and He will guide our steps!" How would you feel? You would probably feel nervous, full of questions, and reluctant to leave your comfort zone. Yet, over four thousand years ago, an elderly man named Abram heard that exact call from the living God. Without arguing, hesitating, or demanding a GPS map, he packed his tents, took his family, and set out into the unknown. In this lesson, you will discover the transformative power of biblical obedience through the inspiring call of Abraham, the father of faith!

The Foundations of Biblical Obedience

Call to Obedience I: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

Obedience in Christian faith is not blind or forced compliance driven by terror. It is the loving, active response of trusting faith. To obey God means to hear His voice, surrender personal control, and take concrete action in alignment with His will.

The Call of Abram (Genesis 12:1–9)

The Setting: Abram was living comfortably in Haran, a thriving ancient city, after migrating from Ur of the Chaldeans with his father Terah. When Terah died at age 205, God spoke directly to Abram.
The Radical Mandate: God commanded Abram: "Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you" (Genesis 12:1). This command required three costly separations:
1.
Separation from his geographical homeland (country).
2.
Separation from his extended clan and social safety net (kindred).
3.
Separation from his ancestral heritage and domestic security (father's house).
The Sevenfold Covenant Promise: God accompanied this daunting command with extraordinary sovereign promises:
1.
"I will make of you a great nation."
2.
"I will bless you."
3.
"And make your name great."
4.
"So that you will be a blessing."
5.
"I will bless those who bless you."
6.
"And him who dishonors you I will curse."
7.
"And in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed." (The ultimate Messianic promise pointing to Jesus Christ).
Abram's Unhesitating Obedience: Scripture records the decisive simplicity of faith: "So Abram went, as the Lord had told him; and Lot went with him. Abram was seventy-five years old when he departed from Haran" (Genesis 12:4).
Journey into Canaan: Abram took Sarai his wife, Lot his brother's son, all their accumulated wealth, and the persons they had acquired in Haran, and they journeyed toward Canaan.
Erecting Altars of Worship:
Upon reaching the oak of Moreh at Shechem, the Lord appeared to Abram and said: "To your offspring I will give this land." Abram built an altar there to the Lord.
Moving east of Bethel, he pitched his tent between Bethel on the west and Ai on the east; there he built another altar and called upon the name of the Lord.
