Foundational curriculum module in Christian Religious Studies covering "Relationship in the Church: The Body of Christ, Church Leadership, and Maintaining Loving Fellowship" for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

When you hear the word "church," what is the first picture that comes to your mind? Do you picture a towering cathedral with stained-glass windows, a large auditorium with musical instruments, or a simple village chapel built of mud bricks and corrugated iron sheets? In Christian theology, the church is not a physical building made of mortar, cement, and zinc! The true Church is the living community of all baptized believers in Jesus Christ—the sacred spiritual family called the Body of Christ. Just as your physical human body has eyes, ears, hands, and feet working together in complete harmony, every member of the church has a unique spiritual role to play. In this lesson, you will discover the biblical nature of the Church, learn how to identify and honor diverse church leaders (such as the Pope, Archbishops, Bishops, Priests, Pastors, and General Overseers), and master the divine art of maintaining genuine, loving fellowship through Romans 12 and 1 Peter 3!

Relationship in the Church: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

The New Testament Greek word for church is Ekklesia, meaning "the called-out ones." The Church is not a stone structure; it is the living spiritual people of God:

Christ is the Sole Head: "He is the head of the body, the church. He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in everything he might be preeminent" (Colossians 1:18).
Unity in Diversity (1 Corinthians 12:12–27): Just as the human body is one and has many members, and all the members of the body, though many, are one body, so it is with Christ.
The eye cannot say to the hand, "I have no need of you."
The ear cannot say to the foot, "Because I am not an eye, I do not belong to the body."
If one member suffers, all suffer together; if one member is honored, all rejoice together.

Identification of Church Leaders

God appoints diverse spiritual leaders to guide, protect, and feed the flock:

1.
The Papacy and Episcopal Orders (Catholic, Anglican, Methodist):
The Pope: The worldwide leader of the Catholic Church.
Archbishops and Bishops: Oversee regional territories (dioceses), ordain priests, and safeguard orthodox Christian doctrine.
Priests / Curates / Deacons: Minister in parishes, celebrating Holy Communion (Eucharist), preaching the Word, and visiting the sick.
1.
Pentecostal, Evangelical, and Charismatic Leadership:
General Overseers (G.O.): Lead large church denominations, casting visionary direction and training pastors.
Senior Pastors and Assistant Pastors: Provide weekly pulpit teaching, shepherd local branch congregations, and counsel families.
Elders and Deacons / Deaconesses: Assist pastors in administrative tasks, distribution of welfare, and maintaining orderly worship.
1.
Biblical Mandate Toward Church Leaders:
"Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls, as those who will have to give an account" (Hebrews 13:17).
"Let the elders who rule well be considered worthy of double honor, especially those who labor in preaching and teaching" (1 Timothy 5:17).

Sustaining Loving Fellowship in the Congregation (Romans 12:9–21; 1 Peter 3:8–13)

The Apostle Paul and the Apostle Peter provide explicit blueprints for vibrant church fellowship:

Marks of Genuine Christian Love (Romans 12:9–16):
1.
"Let love be genuine. Abhor what is evil; hold fast to what is good."
2.
"Love one another with brotherly affection. Outdo one another in showing honor."
3.
"Do not be slothful in zeal, be fervent in spirit, serve the Lord."
4.
"Rejoice in hope, be patient in tribulation, be constant in prayer."
5.
"Contribute to the needs of the saints and seek to show hospitality."
6.
"Rejoice with those who rejoice, weep with those who weep."
7.
"Live in harmony with one another. Do not be haughty, but associate with the lowly. Never be wise in your own sight."
Overcoming Conflict and Persecution (Romans 12:17–21):
"Repay no one evil for evil, but give thought to do what is honorable in the sight of all. If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all."
"Beloved, never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God... Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good."
The St. Peter Standard of Church Unity (1 Peter 3:8–9):
"Finally, all of you, have unity of mind, sympathy, brotherly love, a tender heart, and a humble mind. Do not repay evil for evil or reviling for reviling, but on the contrary, bless, for to this you were called, that you may obtain a blessing."
