Why This Matters to You

Think about the moment you wake up in the morning. Who is the first person who greets you, prepares your breakfast, reminds you to iron your school uniform, or helps you pack your books? For almost every student in Nigeria, that first circle of support is the family. Whether you live in a bustling apartment in Lagos, a compound in Enugu, or an extended family home in Kano, the family is the foundational brick upon which your entire life and society are built. But why do some families live in beautiful peace and harmony, while others face bitter fighting, distrust, and heartbreak? The answer lies in the quality of their relationships and their obedience to God's divine guidelines. In this lesson, you will discover the true biblical meaning of family relationships, explore the structure of nuclear and extended families, and uncover the sacred roles God has assigned to children, parents, husbands, and wives!

Types of Families in Our Society

1.
The Nuclear Family:
Comprises the father, mother, and their children.
Characterized by close daily proximity, joint decision-making, and primary parental nurture.
1.
The Extended Family:
Encompasses several generations and kinship branches living in close contact or within a shared family compound.
In Nigerian and African culture, the extended family provides a crucial social safety net: grandparents pass down wisdom, aunts and uncles support education, and cousins share companionship and mutual protection.
1.
The Single-Parent and Foster Family:
Arises through the death of a spouse, abandonment, or legal adoption. Scripture commands the community of faith to care for widows, widowers, and fatherless children with special compassion (James 1:27).

Biblical Guidelines for Family Harmony (Ephesians 6:1–9; Colossians 3:18–21; 1 Peter 3:1–7)

The New Testament outlines specific, reciprocal ethical responsibilities for every family member:

Duties of Children (Ephesians 6:1–3; Colossians 3:20):
"Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right" (Ephesians 6:1).
"Honor your father and mother (this is the first commandment with a promise), that it may go well with you and that you may live long in the land" (Ephesians 6:2–3).
Obedience must be sincere, respectful, and prompt, avoiding backtalk, deceit, or eye-service.
Duties of Parents and Fathers (Ephesians 6:4; Colossians 3:21):
"Fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, lest they become discouraged, but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord."
Parents must not abuse their authority through harsh cruelty, unfair favoritism, or constant verbal degradation. They are called to nurture children with patience, godly example, and loving instruction.
Duties of Husbands (Ephesians 5:25–28; 1 Peter 3:7):
"Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her" (Ephesians 5:25).
Husbands must treat their wives with understanding, honor, and gentle protection, recognizing them as joint heirs of the grace of life, so that their prayers may not be hindered (1 Peter 3:7).
Duties of Wives (Ephesians 5:22–24; Colossians 3:18):
"Wives, submit to your own husbands, as to the Lord."
Wives are called to respect, support, and cooperate with their husbands in building a peaceful, God-fearing home, rejecting strife and rebellion.
Duties in Domestic Labor / Masters and Servants (Ephesians 6:5–9):
Domestic helpers and house workers should serve with integrity and singleness of heart as unto Christ; employers/masters must treat them with justice and kindness, knowing that God in heaven shows no partiality.
