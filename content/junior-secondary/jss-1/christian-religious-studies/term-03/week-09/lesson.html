The Multi-Religious Landscape of Nigeria

Living in Harmony with People of Other Faiths: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

Nigeria is blessed with rich cultural and religious diversity. The three recognized religious traditions in our society are:

1.
Christianity: Centered on faith in the Triune God, salvation through Jesus Christ, the Holy Bible, and active love for neighbor.
2.
Islam: Centered on faith in Allah (the One God), the prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), the Holy Qur'an, prayer, and charity (Zakat).
3.
African Traditional Religion (ATR): The indigenous ancestral heritage centered on the Supreme God (Olodumare, Chukwu, Ubangiji), veneration of ancestors, moral taboos, and respect for nature.

Three Common Elements Across Religions in Nigeria

As highlighted in official NERDC curriculum studies, religious tolerance is grounded in recognizing three fundamental shared convictions:

1.
Belief in the Supreme Being: All three religions acknowledge one Supreme Creator who made the heavens and the earth and sustains human existence.
2.
The Reality of Moral Breakdown: All three recognize that human beings often fall into sin, disobedience, selfishness, and moral failure, damaging their relationship with God and fellow humans.
3.
The Necessity of Reconciliation and Moral Restoration: All three emphasize that broken relationships between humanity and God must be restored through prayer, repentance, righteous conduct, and peaceful living.

Biblical Principles for Living with People of Other Faiths

The New Testament provides clear, non-negotiable guidelines for Christian engagement in a diverse society:

1. The Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12):
"So whatever you wish that others would do to you, do also to them, for this is the Law and the Prophets."
If you wish Muslims or traditional believers to respect your church services and Christian holidays, you must respect their mosques, prayer times, and cultural rights.
2. The Parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37):
When a Jewish man was beaten by armed robbers on the Jericho road, the priest and the Levite (his own religious leaders) walked past him on the other side.
A Samaritan—a man from a despised, rival ethnic and religious group—stopped, felt deep compassion, bandaged his wounds with oil and wine, set him on his own beast, brought him to an inn, and paid all expenses.
Jesus asked: "Which of these three, do you think, proved to be a neighbor to the man who fell among the robbers?" The lawyer replied: "The one who showed him mercy." And Jesus said: "You go, and do likewise."
3. Pursuing Universal Peace (Romans 12:18; Hebrews 12:14):
"If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all" (Romans 12:18).
"Strive for peace with everyone, and for the holiness without which no one will see the Lord" (Hebrews 12:14).
4. Gentle, Respectful Witness (1 Peter 3:15):
"Always be prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect."
Christians share their faith through godly character, loving service, and respectful dialogue—never through insults, violence, or coercion.

Practical Steps to Promote Religious Peace in Schools and Communities

1.
Never Mock or Insult Other Faiths: Avoid insulting the sacred texts, prayers, or clothing of other religions.
2.
Collaborate on Common Community Projects: Work together in school study clubs, neighborhood environmental sanitation, and sports teams regardless of faith.
3.
Stand Together Against Social Injustice: Christians and Muslims in Nigeria can jointly oppose drug abuse, cultism, exam malpractice, corruption, and kidnapping.
4.
Practice Hospitality During Festivals: Share meals and send goodwill greetings during Christmas, Easter, Eid al-Fitr, and Eid al-Kabir.
