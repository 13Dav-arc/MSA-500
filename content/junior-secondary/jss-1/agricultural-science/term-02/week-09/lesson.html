Why This Matters to You

Imagine walking into two neighboring poultry farms on the outskirts of Abeokuta or Kaduna. On Farm A, the birds are crammed into a dark, suffocating room with damp, foul-smelling litter; water troughs are coated with green slime, and wild pigeons fly freely in and out of broken windows, spreading germs. Half the flock is coughing, drooping their wings, and dying daily. On Farm B, the air is clean and breezy; the concrete floor is covered with dry, golden wood shavings; automated nipple drinkers provide crystal-clear water; and any visitor must step through a chemical disinfectant foot-bath before entering. The birds on Farm B are robust, energetic, and laying shiny brown eggs every morning! What makes the difference between catastrophic bankruptcy on Farm A and massive commercial profit on Farm B? The answer is scientific animal husbandry! In this lesson, you will master the principles of livestock housing, balanced nutrition, biosecurity, and health management that keep farm animals thriving—and unlock the Term 2 80% Mastery Gate!

2. Housing Requirements for Livestock

Proper animal housing provides five fundamental conditions:

Environmental Protection: Shields animals from scorching tropical sun, torrential downpours, strong winds, and dampness.
Predator and Theft Security: Prevents access by snakes, stray dogs, rodents, and livestock rustlers.
Cross-Ventilation: Continuous flow of fresh air that removes noxious ammonia gas produced by decaying urine and manure, preventing chronic respiratory infections.
Sanitary Flooring and Drainage: Sloped concrete floors allow quick washing, disinfection, and drainage, preventing mud accumulation and crippling bacterial foot-rot.
Adequate Floor Space: Eliminates overcrowding, preventing aggressive vices such as cannibalism and feather pecking in poultry, or ear biting in pigs.

Specific Housing Facilities

Poultry: Raised in Deep-Litter Houses (floors covered with 5–10 cm of dry wood shavings that absorb droppings) or multi-tier Battery Cages (wire cages where eggs roll safely onto collection trays).
Pigs: Housed in concrete pens equipped with separate feeding, sleeping, and dunging areas, plus shallow water wallows or mist sprinklers to cool down, since pigs lack sweat glands.
Rabbits: Kept in raised hutches with wire-mesh floors that allow droppings and urine to fall through cleanly into collection trays below.
Ruminants (Cattle/Goats/Sheep): Kept in well-ventilated sheds, fenced paddocks, or open-sided barns with shaded feed mangers and clean water troughs.

3. Livestock Nutrition: Roughages vs. Concentrates

A balanced animal ration must supply carbohydrates, proteins, fats, minerals, vitamins, and unlimited water:

Roughages (For Ruminants & Equines):
High in crude fiber (>18%) and low in digestible energy.
Pasture Grasses: Elephant grass (Pennisetum purpureum), Guinea grass (Panicum maximum).
Pasture Legumes: Centro (Centrosema pubescens), Stylo (Stylosanthes gracilis), which fix atmospheric nitrogen and provide protein.
Hay: Sun-dried green pasture grass stored for dry-season feeding.
Silage: Succulent green forage preserved through anaerobic microbial fermentation in an airtight silo pit.
Concentrates (For Monogastrics & High-Yield Dairy/Beef):
Low in fiber (<18%) and high in digestible energy and protein.
Energy Concentrates: Crushed maize, guinea corn (sorghum), wheat offal, rice bran, cassava meal.
Protein Concentrates: Soybean meal, groundnut cake (GNC), fish meal, blood meal.
Minerals and Water:
Salt licks, dicalcium phosphate, and crushed oyster shells (for eggshell strength).
Animals must have access to cool, fresh, pathogen-free water at all times (ad libitum).

4. Routine Health Management and Biosecurity

Sanitation: Daily sweeping of pens, scrubbing feed and water troughs, and applying lime or virucidal disinfectants.
Foot-Baths: Disinfectant trays positioned at every pen entrance to destroy disease-causing microbes on workers' footwear before entry.
Parasite Control:
Endoparasites (Roundworms, Tapeworms, Liver flukes): Controlled through scheduled oral administration of dewormers (anthelmintics).
Ectoparasites (Ticks, Mange mites, Lice): Controlled through periodic dipping in chemical plunge dips, knapsack spraying with acaricides, or pour-on medications.
Immunization (Vaccination): Strict vaccination schedules against deadly contagious epizootics:
Poultry: Newcastle disease, Gumboro (Infectious Bursal Disease), Fowl Pox.
Goats and Sheep: Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR).
Cattle: Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia (CBPP), Anthrax, Foot-and-Mouth Disease.
Quarantine: Isolating any newly brought animal in a dedicated quarantine pen for 21 to 30 days to ensure it is disease-free before mixing with the resident flock or herd.
