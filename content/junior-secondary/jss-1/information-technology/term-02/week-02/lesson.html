Week 02: Computer Hardware I: Central Processing Unit & System Unit

Foundational curriculum module in Information Technology covering Computer Hardware I: Central Processing Unit & System Unit for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

When you look at a car, the steering wheel allows you to steer and the headlights allow you to see at night, but what actually drives the vehicle forward? The powerful engine hidden underneath the hood! In a computer system, the keyboard lets you type words and the monitor lets you see vibrant images, but where does the actual "thinking," calculating, and decision-making occur? It takes place inside a protective metallic casing called the System Unit, resting on the main circuit board on a thumbnail-sized silicon chip known as the Central Processing Unit (CPU). Often celebrated as the "brain of the computer," this microscopic powerhouse executes billions of calculations every second without making a single computational error. How does this tiny piece of silicon coordinate millions of complex operations simultaneously? Let us step inside the System Unit and discover the inner anatomy of the CPU!

Key Terms

System Unit The main rectangular protective enclosure (chassis or tower) that houses the vital internal electronic components of a computer, including the motherboard, power supply, and CPU.
Central Processing Unit (CPU) The primary hardware microprocessor that interprets, directs, coordinates, and executes program instructions, functioning as the computational brain of the computer system.
Control Unit (CU) The supervisory subunit inside the CPU that manages, directs, and coordinates the flow of data, instructions, and timing signals between the processor, memory, and peripheral devices.
Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) The operational subunit inside the CPU that executes all mathematical computations (addition, subtraction, multiplication) and logical comparisons (AND, OR, greater than, equal to).
Register A tiny, high-speed temporary storage location directly inside the CPU that holds data, memory addresses, and instructions currently undergoing immediate processing.
Machine Cycle (Instruction Cycle) The continuous four-step operational sequence executed by the CPU to process every single program instruction: Fetch, Decode, Execute, and Store.
System Clock An internal quartz crystal timing circuit that emits electrical pulses at precise, steady intervals to synchronize all processing operations inside the CPU, measured in Gigahertz (GHz).

Core Concept

Inside the System Unit: The Motherboard & Core Components

The large rectangular box standing beside or underneath a desktop monitor is the System Unit (often mistakenly called "the CPU" by beginners). The System Unit is the protective fortress of the computer, shielding delicate microcircuits from dust, physical shock, and electrical interference.

Inside the System Unit, you will find:

1.
The Motherboard (System Board): The primary printed circuit board that physically connects and permits communication between the CPU, RAM, storage drives, and expansion cards.
2.
The Power Supply Unit (PSU): Converts high-voltage alternating current (AC) electricity from the wall outlet into low-voltage direct current (DC) required by internal chips.
3.
The Central Processing Unit (CPU): Mounted securely in a zero-insertion-force (ZIF) socket on the motherboard, covered by a metallic heat sink and an active cooling fan.
4.
Primary Memory (RAM Slots): High-speed electronic memory sticks plugged into vertical slots adjacent to the CPU.
5.
Secondary Storage Drives: Solid-State Drives (SSDs) and Hard Disk Drives (HDDs) mounted in internal drive bays.
Anatomy of the Computer System Unit: The motherboard serves as the central highway linking the CPU, RAM, and internal peripherals

1. The Control Unit (CU) — The Traffic Director

The Control Unit acts as the manager or "traffic police officer" of the computer:

It does not calculate numbers or process data itself.
It retrieves (fetches) instructions from primary memory (RAM) in proper sequence.
It interprets (decodes) what each instruction means.
It sends electronic command signals to the ALU, memory, and input/output devices telling them exactly when and how to respond.

2. The Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) — The Calculator & Judge

The Arithmetic Logic Unit is the mathematical powerhouse and logical brain of the CPU. It performs two categories of operations:

Arithmetic Operations: Basic mathematical calculations including addition ($+$), subtraction ($-$), multiplication ($\times$), and division ($\div$).
Logical Operations: Comparisons between two data items to evaluate conditions and make decisions:
Relational tests: Is $A = B$? Is $A > B$? Is $A < B$?
Logical evaluations: Boolean operations such as `AND`, `OR`, and `NOT`.

3. Internal Registers — The Scratchpad

Registers are special, microscopic high-speed memory cells embedded directly within the CPU circuitry:

They hold data values and memory addresses that the ALU needs right at that exact microsecond.
Key registers include:
Program Counter (PC): Holds the memory address of the next instruction to be fetched.
Instruction Register (IR): Holds the current instruction while it is being decoded.
Accumulator (ACC): Temporarily stores the immediate results of arithmetic calculations produced by the ALU.

---

The Machine Cycle (Instruction Cycle)

Every single task executed by a computer—whether typing a character, loading a video game, or applying an Instagram photo filter—is broken down into millions of basic instructions. The CPU processes each instruction through the four-stage Machine Cycle:

1.
Fetch: The Control Unit retrieves the instruction from a designated memory address in RAM and loads it into an internal register.
2.
Decode: The Control Unit translates the instruction into binary electrical control signals that the processor's circuitry understands.
3.
Execute: The Arithmetic Logic Unit carries out the requested operation (e.g., adding two numbers or evaluating a comparison).
4.
Store (Write-back): The resulting data from the execution is written back into a register or returned to RAM memory for future use.

Processor Speed & Clock Rate:

The speed of the machine cycle is dictated by the System Clock. Each tick of the clock allows the CPU to advance through operational steps. A modern CPU rated at $3.5\text{ GHz}$ (Gigahertz) ticks 3.5 billion times every single second!

Home Application & Guardian Sync

Together with your parent or guardian, locate a desktop or laptop computer in your home or school office: 1. Locate the System Unit: If it is a desktop, point out the metal tower casing (the System Unit) and explain that the CPU is safely mounted inside. If it is a laptop, explain that the System Unit is built directly underneath the keyboard! 2. Feel the Heat Sink Vent: Put your hand near the small exhaust vent on the side or back of the computer. Feel the warm air blowing out. 3. Explain the Heat: Explain to your guardian that when the CPU executes billions of machine cycles per second, electricity passing through microscopic silicon transistors creates heat, which is why computers require cooling fans and metal heat sinks to prevent overheating.
