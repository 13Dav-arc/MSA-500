Week 04: Computer Hardware III: Output Devices

Foundational curriculum module in Information Technology covering Computer Hardware III: Output Devices for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine writing a brilliant, award-winning essay or composing an inspiring national anthem on a computer. The CPU calculates every word, letter, and musical chord with flawless precision, storing the data as microscopic electrical charges inside silicon registers. But what good is all that brilliant digital work if it remains locked invisibly inside the computer's circuitry? For human beings to see, read, evaluate, hear, or physically hold the computer's results, we need specialized hardware ambassadors that translate binary electrical signals into glowing light pixels on a screen, ink markings on paper, and audible sound waves in the air! These essential components are called Output Devices. Whether it is the desktop screen in front of your eyes, a laser printer producing your term report card, or speakers playing an audio lecture, output devices bring the invisible digital universe into the physical human world!

Key Terms

Output Device Any peripheral hardware device that receives processed digital data from the Central Processing Unit and converts it into human-perceptible forms (visual displays, physical paper prints, or audible sounds).
Soft Copy Output Intangible, transient digital output presented on a visual display screen or heard through audio speakers, which cannot be physically held and disappears when the device is powered off.
Hard Copy Output Permanent, tangible physical output printed onto paper, cardstock, or transparency film, which can be physically held, read, and preserved without electrical power.
Visual Display Unit (VDU / Monitor) The primary visual output hardware device that displays computer-generated text, graphics, and video on a pixel-based screen.
Impact Printer A mechanical printer that forms characters and images on paper by physically striking an inked ribbon against the page using mechanical pins or hammers (e.g., Dot Matrix printer).
Non-Impact Printer A modern, quiet printer that prints text and graphics onto paper without physical striking, utilizing microscopic sprays of liquid ink droplets (Inkjet) or electrostatic dry toner powder (Laser printer).
Plotter A specialized high-precision output device that uses automated mechanical pens or blades to draw continuous, large-scale vector graphics, architectural blueprints, and engineering schematics.

Visual Output Devices (Monitors & Displays)

The Monitor (or Visual Display Unit - VDU) is the most immediate output device. It allows users to view data as they type and observe software feedback in real time.

1.
Evolution of Monitor Technology:
Cathode Ray Tube (CRT): Heavy, bulky monitors with deep glass vacuum tubes used in early computing. They consumed significant electrical power, generated heat, and caused eye fatigue from screen flickering.
Liquid Crystal Display (LCD): Lightweight, slim, flat-panel monitors that use liquid crystal molecules sandwiched between polarizing glass panels, illuminated by a fluorescent backlight.
Light Emitting Diode (LED): Modern flat-panel displays that replace fluorescent bulbs with an array of tiny, energy-efficient Light Emitting Diodes (LEDs). They produce brighter colors, sharper contrast, consume minimal power, and feature ultra-slim profiles.
Organic LED (OLED): Advanced displays where each individual pixel produces its own light without any backlight, delivering true pitch blacks, ultra-wide viewing angles, and flexible screens.
1.
Display Projector: An optical output device that projects computer screen displays onto a large auditorium wall or projection canvas, used for classroom teaching and boardroom presentations.

Printed Output Devices (Printers)

Printers transform digital files into physical hard copies. They are divided into two distinct technological families:

1. Impact Printers

Impact printers operate on the same mechanical principle as traditional typewriters: a mechanical component physically strikes an inked fabric ribbon against paper to leave an ink mark.

Dot Matrix Printer:
Mechanism: Uses a moving print head containing a vertical column of tiny pins (typically 9 or 24 pins). The pins strike an inked ribbon against paper to form letters out of patterns of dots.
Characteristics: Very noisy, slow print speed, low print resolution, and coarse graphic rendering.
Crucial Real-World Advantage: It is the only type of printer that can produce carbon duplicate copies through multipart continuous paper (e.g., three-part bank deposit vouchers, official government tax receipts, and warehouse dispatch notes).

2. Non-Impact Printers

Non-impact printers create images without any mechanical parts striking the paper, making them virtually silent and remarkably fast.

Inkjet Printer:
Mechanism: Sprays microscopic droplets of liquid ink from hundreds of microscopic nozzles directly onto the paper fibers.
Characteristics: Produces excellent, high-resolution color photographs and smooth graphics. Moderate speed; ink cartridges can dry up if unused for long periods. Widely used in homes and photography studios.
Laser Printer:
Mechanism: Uses a rotating electrostatic drum, a precision laser beam, and dry powdered plastic ink called toner. The laser draws an invisible electrostatic charge pattern on the drum, which attracts the toner powder. The toner is transferred to the paper and permanently fused into the paper fibers using high-heat pressure rollers.
Characteristics: Extremely high speed (measured in Pages Per Minute - PPM), razor-sharp text quality, low cost per printed page, and smudge-proof output. The standard workhorse of modern schools, universities, and commercial offices.
Thermal Printer:
Mechanism: Uses special chemically treated, heat-sensitive paper. Heated electrical pins activate the chemical coating to create dark letters without any ink or ribbon.
Characteristics: Compact, silent, and maintenance-free. Universally used in Point of Sale (POS) payment terminals and bank Automated Teller Machines (ATMs).

---

Specialized & Audio Output Devices

1.
Plotters:
Unlike standard printers that print line-by-line using dots, plotters use automated pens, markers, or blades to draw smooth, continuous, precision vector lines on massive sheets of paper.
Used by architects, civil engineers, and surveyors to produce architectural building blueprints, bridge engineering drawings, geographical maps, and massive roadside advertising banners.
1.
Audio Speakers & Headphones:
Convert digital audio signals into physical sound pressure waves. Used to play educational video narration, listen to pronunciation lessons in English Studies, and conduct video conferencing calls.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

This evening, sit down with your parent or guardian to examine two printed documents in your household: 1. Examine an ATM or POS Receipt: Pull out a small receipt slip from a bank withdrawal or supermarket purchase. Notice that it was printed on smooth, heat-sensitive paper without ink or ribbon (thermal printing). Try scratching it gently with your fingernail—a gray friction mark appears due to heat sensitivity! 2. Examine a School Report Card or Utility Bill: Look closely at the text. Is it crisp and dark without dot patterns? That indicates a laser printer! If it has rich colors, it was likely printed by an inkjet printer. Discuss why different printers are chosen for different jobs.
