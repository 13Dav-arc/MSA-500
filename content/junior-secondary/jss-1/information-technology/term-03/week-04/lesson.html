Foundational curriculum module in Information Technology covering Evolution of Communication Media (From Postal to Electronic Mail) for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

If you wanted to send an urgent letter from Lagos to a business partner in London in the year 1880, you would write your message with fountain pen ink on heavy parchment paper, melt red wax over the folded edge to seal it, and walk to the colonial harbor post office. Your letter would be packed into a heavy canvas sack, loaded into the cargo hold of an ocean steamship, and spend four long weeks tossed on the waves of the Atlantic Ocean! If your partner replied immediately, you might receive their response two months later! Fast forward to today: you open your laptop or smartphone, type a message, attach a colorful photograph, and click "Send." Before you can even blink your eyes, your message has arrived safely in London! How did humanity make the quantum leap from canvas mailbags on steamships to instantaneous digital Electronic Mail (E-mail)? The Evolution of Communication Media is the thrilling story of how human beings conquered the barriers of time and space.

Key Terms

Communication Media The physical channels, technical systems, and electronic pathways through which information, messages, and signals are transmitted between senders and receivers.
Postal System An organized public or commercial service responsible for physically collecting, sorting, transporting, and delivering paper letters, postcards, and parcels.
Electric Telegraph A 19th-century telecommunication system that used electrical pulses transmitted over metallic wires to send text messages across long distances almost instantaneously.
Morse Code An alphanumeric character-encoding system invented by Samuel Morse that translates alphabet letters and numbers into patterns of short electrical pulses ("dots") and long pulses ("dashes").
Electronic Mail (E-mail) A digital communication service that enables users to compose, send, store, and receive text messages and computer file attachments over the Internet.
Blind Carbon Copy (Bcc) An email addressing feature that sends a copy of an email to secondary recipients while keeping their email addresses completely hidden and invisible to other recipients.

The Grand Milestones of Communication Media

Humanity’s ability to transmit thoughts across distances evolved through four distinct historical eras:

```

ERA 1: Physical Era (Postal Mail & Couriers)

Letters carried by foot, horses, stagecoaches, and ocean steamships
Delivery measured in days, weeks, or months

│

▼

ERA 2: Electro-Mechanical Era (The Electric Telegraph)

1830s/1840s: Samuel Morse invents the electric telegraph and Morse Code
First time information traveled faster than human physical transportation!

│

▼

ERA 3: Electronic Voice & Audiovisual Era (Telephone, Radio, TV)

1876: Alexander Graham Bell patents the telephone (voice travels across wires)
1900s: Radio broadcasts audio through the air; 1930s: Television broadcasts live video

│

▼

ERA 4: Digital Network Era (The Internet & Electronic Mail)

1971: Ray Tomlinson invents network email, introducing the "@" symbol
Multimedia messages and documents delivered globally in milliseconds

```

Key Historical Milestones

1. The Postal System (Courier & Post Offices)

Before electricity, all long-distance messages were physical objects. Governments established organized postal agencies (such as the Nigerian Postal Service - NIPOST).
Senders purchased adhesive postage stamps as proof of payment, affixed them to envelopes, and dropped letters into red postboxes.
Mail was sorted in central post offices and transported by rail, road, and ship.
Limitation: Very slow delivery times, high cost of overseas postage, and packages could be damaged or lost in transit.

2. The Electric Telegraph & Morse Code (1837 – 1844)

American inventor Samuel Morse and his colleagues developed the electric telegraph.
By pressing an electrical switch called a telegraph key, an operator completed a circuit that sent short and long electric pulses along a wire.
Morse Code: Represented every character using dots ($\cdot$) and dashes ($-$). For example, the universal distress emergency signal is SOS:

$$\text{S } (\cdot\cdot\cdot) \quad \text{O } (---) \quad \text{S } (\cdot\cdot\cdot)$$

Messages called telegrams were received at distant telegraph offices, decoded, printed on paper strips, and hand-delivered to homes by bicycle couriers.

3. The Digital Revolution: Electronic Mail (E-mail)

In 1971, computer engineer Ray Tomlinson, working on the early internet precursor (ARPANET), authored the first software program that allowed users on different computers to send messages to one another:

Tomlinson chose the `@` ("at") symbol to separate the user's personal name from the name of the host computer.
Structure of an Email Address:

$$\underbrace{\text{amina.musa}}_{\text{Username}} \quad \mathbf{@} \quad \underbrace{\text{msaglobal}}_{\text{Mail Server Domain}} \quad \underbrace{\text{.edu.ng}}_{\text{Top-Level Domain}}$$

Anatomy of an Email Message:
`From:` The sender's email address (populated automatically).
`To:` The primary recipient's address who is expected to read and act on the email.
`Cc:` (Carbon Copy): Secondary recipients who receive a copy of the email for their information; all other recipients can see their names.
`Bcc:` (Blind Carbon Copy): Secret recipients who receive a copy of the email; their email addresses are completely concealed from all other recipients.
`Subject:` A concise summary of the email's topic (e.g., "JSS 1 Term 03 Exam Timetable").
`Body:` The main written message content.
`Attachment:` Computer files (such as PDFs, photos, spreadsheets, or videos) clipped to the message.

Why Electronic Mail Transformed the World

1.
Blinding Speed: Delivered to any continent within 1 to 5 seconds.
2.
Minimal Cost: Sending an email across the street or across the world costs virtually nothing beyond standard internet access.
3.
Multimedia Attachments: Users can attach high-resolution photos, architectural designs, audio clips, and large PDF textbooks.
4.
Permanent Digital Archiving: Emails can be easily searched, categorized into folders, and retrieved years later without fading paper ink.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

Together with your parent or guardian, explore the evolution of correspondence in your family: 1. Ask About Letters and Telegrams: Ask your guardian if they ever received a physical telegram in the past. Explain that because telegrams charged money per word, people wrote in funny, short sentences (e.g., "ARRIVING WEDNESDAY MORNING STOP SEND CAR STOP"). 2. Review an Email Inbox: Open an email app together. Identify the Inbox, the Sent Items, the Drafts, and the Spam / Junk folder. Discuss why it is critical never to open suspicious email attachments or click unknown links from strange senders.
