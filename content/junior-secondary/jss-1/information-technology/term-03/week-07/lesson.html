Week 07: ICT and Society: Impact, The Digital Divide, and Ethical Responsibilities

Foundational curriculum module in Information Technology covering ICT and Society: Impact, The Digital Divide, and Ethical Responsibilities for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine two Nigerian students of the exact same age: Amina lives in Ikeja, Lagos, with 5G fiber-optic internet in her home, a personal laptop on her study desk, and 24-hour electricity. She studies online, watches video lectures on science topics, and learns computer programming after school. Meanwhile, three hundred kilometers away in a rural agricultural village, Emeka has no electricity, no computer lab in his school, and the nearest cell phone network tower is a seven-kilometer walk down a dirt road! Both children are equally brilliant, curious, and ambitious, yet one has the entire knowledge of human civilization at her fingertips, while the other is locked out of the digital age. This profound global inequality is known as the Digital Divide. As ICT transforms every corner of our society, we must confront an urgent question: how do we harness the incredible benefits of technology while protecting our communities from inequality, cybercrime, and ethical abuses?

Key Terms

Digital Divide The social, economic, and educational gap between individuals, households, and geographical regions that have access to modern ICT infrastructure and the Internet, and those who do not.
Information Society A modern society in which the creation, distribution, manipulation, and economic utilization of information and digital knowledge is the primary economic and cultural driver.
Cybercrime Any illegal, unlawful criminal activity carried out using computers, digital devices, the Internet, or telecommunication networks as either a tool, a target, or a platform.
Intellectual Property (IP) Legal rights that protect the original intellectual creations of the human mind, including software code, books, musical compositions, scientific inventions, and artistic designs.
Software Piracy The illegal, unauthorized copying, distribution, duplication, commercial sharing, or use of copyrighted software programs without purchasing a valid legal license.
Netiquette (Network Etiquette) The established code of polite, respectful, and ethical behavioral standards expected of responsible citizens when communicating on the Internet and digital social networks.

The Social Impact of ICT: Balancing digital empowerment against inequality and ethical risks

---

1. Positive Impacts of ICT on Society

Democratization of Knowledge: High-quality digital textbooks, scientific research papers, and interactive learning tools are accessible to anyone with an internet connection, breaking down traditional educational barriers.
Financial Inclusion: Through mobile banking, digital wallets, and POS terminals, millions of unbanked citizens in semi-urban and rural communities can now save money, receive payments, and build businesses without traditional brick-and-mortar bank branches.
Economic Innovation & Employment: ICT has created entire new industries in Nigeria—software development, telecommunications engineering, graphic design, digital marketing, and e-commerce logistics.
Enhanced Government Transparency (E-Governance): Online passport application portals, automated tax filing systems, and digital voter accreditation systems reduce corruption by eliminating human middle-men and bribery.

---

2. Societal Challenges and Disadvantages

The Digital Divide: The gap between urban and rural populations. In Nigeria, while major cities like Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt enjoy high-speed 4G and 5G networks, many rural agrarian communities lack basic mobile connectivity and electricity, deepening educational and economic inequality.
Job Displacement (Technological Unemployment): Automation and software systems replace human workers in repetitive occupations. For example, automated bank teller machines (ATMs) and online banking apps reduce the need for human bank tellers; electronic tollgates replace manual toll ticket sellers.
Spread of Disinformation (Fake News): Unverified, sensational, or malicious false stories can be spread to millions of users across social media platforms within minutes, causing social panic and communal unrest.
Privacy Invasion: The unauthorized tracking, harvesting, and commercial sale of personal user data without informed consent.

---

3. Legal and Ethical Responsibilities

Living in a digital society requires strict moral discipline and adherence to the law:

1.
Combating Cybercrime: In Nigeria, the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act of 2015 imposes severe criminal penalties (including heavy fines and multi-year prison sentences) for electronic financial fraud (phishing/advance-fee scams), identity theft, system hacking, and online harassment.
2.
Respect for Intellectual Property & Anti-Piracy: Software developers, authors, and musicians spend years of hard work creating programs, books, and songs. Downloading cracked software, copying commercial music without payment, or using pirated software is illegal theft under copyright law.
3.
Plagiarism versus Academic Honesty: Plagiarism is the unethical act of copying another person's written text, computer code, or scientific ideas and presenting them as your own work without proper citation. Students must always cite their sources.
4.
Practicing Good Netiquette:
Never post cruel, abusive, or defamatory comments (cyberbullying).
Respect other people's privacy; never share private photos or confidential conversations without explicit permission.
Avoid typing messages in all capital letters (`LIKE THIS`), which is universally interpreted in digital communication as shouting or anger!

Home Application & Guardian Sync

Together with your parent or guardian, discuss the social impact of technology on your family and community: 1. Discuss the Digital Divide: Ask your guardian: "How does internet access in our city compare to internet access in our ancestral hometown or village?"
