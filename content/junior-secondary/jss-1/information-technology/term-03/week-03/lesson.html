Week 03: Modern Methods of Transmitting Information

Foundational curriculum module in Information Technology covering Modern Methods of Transmitting Information for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine you are sitting in your living room in Kaduna, Benin City, or Lagos on a Saturday evening. On your television screen, you are watching a live Premier League football match being played at a stadium in London, England. When a striker kicks the ball into the net, you jump up and cheer at the exact same split-second as the seventy thousand spectators sitting physically in the stadium four thousand miles away! How is it possible for live pictures, roaring stadium sounds, and match statistics to travel across the Sahara Desert and the Mediterranean Sea to reach your television in less than a quarter of a second? The answer lies in the incredible technological revolution of Modern Methods of Information Transmission. From printing presses to satellites in outer space and the global Internet, modern technology has dismantled the barriers of time and distance, turning our vast planet into a tiny, interconnected global village!

Key Terms

Modern Methods of Information Transmission Advanced electronic, optical, and electromagnetic communication technologies used to capture, encode, transmit, and receive data, text, audio, images, and video instantaneously across global distances.
Printing The mechanical or digital process of reproducing text, graphics, and books on paper using printing presses, typewriters, laser printers, or commercial offset presses.
Telephone A telecommunication device invented by Alexander Graham Bell that converts human voice into electrical or optical signals for transmission across copper wires or wireless radio networks.
Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM) A digital cellular telecommunication standard that enables mobile handheld phones to make voice calls, send SMS text messages, transfer multimedia data, and roam across international cellular networks.
Facsimile (Fax) A telecommunication system that scans a physical paper document or photograph, converts it into audio-frequency electronic tones, and transmits it over telephone lines to a remote fax machine that reconstructs and prints an exact paper copy.
Communications Satellite An artificial electronic spacecraft placed into geostationary orbit around the Earth that relays radio, television, telephone, and digital internet signals between distant ground stations.
The Internet The vast, global network of millions of interconnected computer networks linked together by undersea fiber optic cables, satellite uplinks, and wireless routers to share information worldwide.

While ancient methods relied on physical stamina and acoustic reach within a few kilometers, modern transmission methods harness the power of electricity, light, and radio frequencies traveling at 300,000 kilometers per second (the speed of light!).

Key Modern Methods of Transmitting Information

1. Printing (The First Mass Communication Medium)

In the 1440s, German goldsmith Johannes Gutenberg invented the movable type printing press.
Function: Books, newspapers, magazines, and educational textbooks are mass-produced on paper. For the first time in human history, knowledge was preserved in permanent, tamper-proof physical form and distributed to millions of citizens.

2. The Traditional Telephone & Telephony

Invented by Alexander Graham Bell in 1876.
Function: Converts human speech into electrical analog currents traveling through copper telephone lines. A receiver diaphragm at the other end vibrates to reconstruct the caller's audible voice.

3. Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM)

Introduced commercially in the early 1990s and deployed nationwide across Nigeria in 2001 (by operators like MTN, Airtel, Globacom, and 9mobile).
Function: Replaces fixed wall wires with a cellular network of radio base transceivers (cell towers).
Features: Allows users to carry a single handheld phone with a SIM card (Subscriber Identity Module), enabling voice calls, text messaging (SMS), multimedia messaging (MMS), and high-speed mobile internet (3G, 4G, 5G) from virtually any location.

4. Telex (Teleprinter Exchange)

A historical networked system of electromechanical teletypewriters linked over telegraph and telephone channels.
Function: Allowed businesses and government embassies to type text messages on a keyboard in one country, which were typed out automatically and printed on a receiving teletypewriter across the world.

5. Facsimile (Fax)

Function: Acts like a "long-distance photocopier."
How It Works: A user feeds a paper document into the sender's fax machine. An optical scanner reads the text and drawings, converting the image into a bitmap of pixels and translating them into audio tones transmitted over standard telephone lines. The receiving machine decodes the audio tones and uses an internal printer to generate an identical paper copy.

6. Radio & Television Broadcasting

Radio (Audio): Broadcasts sound and voice through electromagnetic radio waves (AM/FM bands) from radio transmission towers to millions of household and automobile receivers. Used widely in Nigeria for news, educational lessons, and agriculture weather advisories in diverse local languages.
Television (Audiovisual): Transmits both moving pictures (video) and synchronized audio. Allows viewers to witness historic news events, live sports matches, and educational documentaries in rich color.

7. Communications Satellites

Function: Artificial spacecraft orbiting in high geostationary orbit (approximately 35,786 kilometers above the equator).
How It Works: A ground station beams an uplink signal up to the satellite transponder. The satellite amplifies the signal and beams it back down as a downlink signal covering an entire continent.
Applications: Direct-to-home satellite television (such as DSTV or Startimes in Nigeria), international telephone routing, GPS vehicle navigation, weather monitoring (NiMet), and maritime ship communication.

8. The Global Internet (The "Net")

The largest and most powerful computer network in human history, linking billions of computing devices across every continent via undersea fiber-optic cables and wireless routers.
Capabilities: Electronic mail (Email), video streaming, search engines (Google), online education (LMS portals like MSA), digital banking, and instant multimedia messaging across the World Wide Web.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

Together with your parent or guardian, conduct a "Modern Media Inventory" of your household: 1. Identify the Media Channels: Look at your home television, mobile phones, home Wi-Fi router, or satellite dish (such as a DSTV or Startimes dish mounted outside). 2. Explore the Shift from Landlines to GSM: Ask your guardian if your family or grandparents ever owned a fixed NITEL landline telephone with a rotary dial and a curled cord in the 1980s or 1990s. Contrast that with how everyone in the family carries an individual GSM smartphone today. 3. Discuss the Fax Machine: Ask if they have ever used a fax machine at work, and discuss how modern email PDF scanning has gradually replaced older thermal fax paper machines.
