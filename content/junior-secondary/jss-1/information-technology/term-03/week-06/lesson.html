Week 06: Information and Communication Technology (ICT): Meaning, Tools, and Applications

Foundational curriculum module in Information Technology covering Information and Communication Technology (ICT): Meaning, Tools, and Applications for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Think about how your life unfolded over the past twenty-four hours. When your family woke up, your parent checked an instant weather forecast on a smartphone; a student opened an educational portal on a tablet to review a science diagram; a local business owner paid for goods from a supplier in Aba using an instant mobile banking transfer; and a doctor at a federal medical center reviewed a digital blood test report sent directly from the hospital laboratory computer. In the past, each of these actions would have required physical travel, handwritten paper receipts, days of waiting, and manual ledger books. Today, they happen in seconds because of a powerful convergence of computers, telecommunications, and digital media known as Information and Communication Technology (ICT). How does ICT tie together computing, broadcasting, and telecommunications to transform human society? Let us step into the engine room of modern digital life!

Key Terms

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) An integrated suite of technological tools, hardware, software, telecommunication networks, and digital resources used to create, process, store, manage, transmit, and exchange information.
Computer-Assisted Learning (CAL) / Computer-Aided Instruction (CAI) The use of interactive educational software, multimedia simulations, tutorials, and computer drills to teach, reinforce, and evaluate academic curriculum concepts.
Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) The electronic transfer of money from one bank account directly to another through computerized financial telecommunication networks without the physical exchange of paper cash.
E-Governance (Electronic Governance) The application of ICT systems and digital portals by government ministries and agencies to deliver public services, enhance administrative transparency, and interact efficiently with citizens.
Telemedicine The remote provision of clinical medical healthcare services, specialist consultations, and patient diagnosis using telecommunication technologies and networked medical devices.
Timing and Control Systems Automated ICT systems that use sensors and microprocessors to monitor, time, and regulate mechanical operations, such as traffic lights, industrial assembly lines, and aircraft navigation.

Core Concept

What is Information and Communication Technology (ICT)?

ICT is an umbrella term that represents the modern fusion of two previously separate technologies:

1.
Information Technology (IT): Computing hardware, data processing software, databases, and digital storage.
2.
Communication Technology (CT): Telecommunications infrastructure, telephone networks, radio/television broadcasting, and internet connectivity.

The Universal Reach of ICT: Driving modern education, banking, healthcare, government, and industry

Major Sectors of Everyday ICT Application

1. ICT in Education

ICT has revolutionized traditional teaching and learning beyond chalkboards and paper notebooks:

Computer-Aided Instruction (CAI) & Computer-Assisted Learning (CAL):
Drills & Practice: Interactive exercises that allow students to practice mathematical and grammatical concepts with instant automated feedback.
Tutorials: Self-paced digital learning modules that guide students through new curriculum materials step-by-step.
Simulations: Digital "what-if" models that replicate real-world phenomena (such as planetary orbits or chemical reactions) safely on a screen.
Computer-Based Testing (CBT): Secure, automated testing systems (such as the JAMB UTME in Nigeria) that eliminate examination paper leaks and calculate objective scores instantaneously.
Learning Management Systems (LMS): Digital platforms like MSA that deliver verified NERDC curriculum notes, track student performance metrics, and synchronize learning with guardians.

2. ICT in Banking and Financial Services

Automated Teller Machines (ATMs): Cash dispensing and account inquiry kiosks operating 24 hours a day across banking networks.
Point of Sale (POS) Terminals: Handheld card terminals that allow retail shoppers, filling stations, and neighborhood merchants to accept digital debit card payments.
Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT): Instant inter-bank settlement engines (such as NIBSS in Nigeria) that move funds between accounts in seconds using mobile apps and USSD codes.
Automated Account Management: Core banking software that updates millions of customer balances simultaneously without manual paper ledgers.

3. ICT in Healthcare and Medical Sciences

Diagnostic Imaging: Advanced computerized scanning equipment—such as Computed Tomography (CT) scanners, Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), and digital ultrasound—revealing internal organ pathology without exploratory surgery.
Electronic Medical Records (EMR): Digital patient folders that store patient medical history, allergies, and laboratory results securely for instant access by attending doctors.
Telemedicine: Enabling specialist physicians in major teaching hospitals to examine, consult with, and prescribe medications for patients living in remote rural villages via video and telemetry links.

4. ICT in Governance and Public Administration

Biometric Identity Management: Systems implemented by the National Identity Management Commission (NIMC) using fingerprint and iris scans to issue unique National Identification Numbers (NIN).
E-Voting & Accreditation: Handheld biometric devices like the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) deployed by INEC to eliminate fraudulent multiple voting.
Electronic Revenue & Tax Filing: Portals created by agencies like the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) that allow corporate organizations and citizens to file tax assessments online.

5. ICT in Timing, Control, and Industry

Traffic Light Sequencing: Microprocessors running automated timer algorithms that regulate road traffic flow at busy street intersections.
Industrial Automation: Robotic assembly arms guided by computer sensors in vehicle manufacturing plants and bottling factories.
Air Traffic Management: Centralized radar and flight tracking networks that schedule safe aircraft takeoff and landing sequences at international airports.
