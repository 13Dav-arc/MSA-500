Core Concept

The Universal Four Phases of Jumping

Every jumping event in track and field—whether vertical (High Jump) or horizontal (Long Jump)—consists of four sequential, interconnected phases:

```

┌──────────────────┐ ┌──────────────────┐ ┌──────────────────┐ ┌──────────────────┐

│ APPROACH RUN │ ──▶ │ TAKE-OFF │ ──▶ │ FLIGHT PHASE │ ──▶ │ LANDING │

│ (Rhythmic Speed) │ │ (Single-Foot Lift)│ │ (Aerial Action) │ │ (Safe Descent) │

└──────────────────┘ └──────────────────┘ └──────────────────┘ └──────────────────┘

```

Long Jump: Mechanics & Styles

In the long jump, the goal is to travel as far horizontally as possible into the sandpit from behind the legal take-off line.

The Four Phases in Long Jump:

1.
Approach Run:
Usually 16 to 24 strides of smooth, progressive acceleration.
Jumpers establish a checkmark to ensure their take-off foot hits the board accurately without looking down at their feet.
1.
Take-off:
The athlete drives the take-off foot firmly flat onto the board.
The non-take-off knee (lead knee) punches upward violently while the arms drive forward and up.
Crucial Rule: The jumper must take off from one foot only! Taking off from two feet is an automatic foul. Touching any part of the plasticine line ahead of the board is also ruled a foul jump (no distance measured).
1.
Flight Styles:
Sail Technique: The jumper brings both legs forward in a sitting posture immediately after lift-off (best for beginners).
Hang Style: The athlete arches their torso backward, dropping their legs and extending arms overhead to create an arched body posture, snapping forward just before landing.
Hitch-Kick (Running in the Air): The athlete continues pedaling their legs in a running motion while airborne, preserving balance and preventing early forward rotation.
1.
Landing:
The jumper thrusts both feet as far forward as possible into the soft sand.
The knees bend to absorb shock, and the body rolls forward or sideways.
Measurement Rule: The jump is measured from the front edge of the take-off board to the nearest mark in the sand made by any part of the athlete’s body or clothing. If an athlete touches the sand behind their feet with their hand, they lose valuable distance!

---

High Jump: Mechanics & Clearance Styles

In the high jump, the athlete must clear a horizontal crossbar set at progressive heights without dislodging it from its upright supports onto the ground.

High Jump Techniques:

1.
Scissors Jump:
A basic upright style where the jumper approaches at an angle, kicks the inner leg straight over the bar first, followed by the trailing leg in a scissor-like motion, landing on their feet.
1.
Western Roll:
The athlete approaches from a 45-degree angle, takes off from the foot closer to the bar, and rolls over the bar with their side or chest facing downward, landing on the same take-off foot and hands.
1.
Eastern Cut-off:
An evolution of scissors where the torso turns horizontal along the bar during clearance.
1.
Fosbury Flop:
The standard modern technique used worldwide.
The athlete runs a J-shaped curved approach, turning their back to the crossbar at take-off.
The athlete drives upward, arches their back completely over the bar (allowing the hips to clear while head and shoulders drop), and snaps the legs over before landing flat on the upper back and shoulders on a thick, cushioned foam landing mat (crash mat).
Safety Warning: The Fosbury Flop must never be attempted into sandpits; it requires a standardized, deep foam crash bed!

Common High Jump Rules:

The jumper must take off from one foot only.
A trial is deemed a failure (foul) if:
1.
The crossbar falls off the supports due to athlete contact.
2.
The athlete touches the landing mat or ground beyond the vertical plane of the uprights before clearing the bar.
3.
The athlete fails to begin their attempt within the designated time limit (usually 1 minute).
Three consecutive failures at any given height eliminate the competitor.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Home Physical Activity: Set up two soft markers (e.g., shoes or towels) in a safe, open yard. Practice the Standing Broad Jump (landing with soft bent knees) to build explosive leg power. - Vertical Reach Test: Measure standing vertical jump against a smooth wall with chalk on the fingertips to assess lower-body explosive power. - Discussion Prompt: Discuss why jumping safely onto proper landing surfaces (sand or foam mats) prevents sprains and fractures.
