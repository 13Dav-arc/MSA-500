Week 01: Physical Education: Meaning, Objectives, Scope, and Historical Development in Nigeria

Foundational curriculum module in Physical and Health Education covering Physical Education: Meaning, Objectives, Scope, and Historical Development in Nigeria for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Think about the explosive celebration that erupts across Nigerian streets when the Super Eagles score a breathtaking winning goal in the Africa Cup of Nations, or when Nigerian world-record holder Tobi Amusan glides gracefully over hurdles on the global Olympic stage! Have you ever wondered what enables an elite athlete to sprint like a cheetah, or why your own heart beats with exhilarating energy and your mind feels crystal clear after a spirited game of football on the school field? Physical activity is not merely casual playtime or a distraction from classroom studies; it is an essential, scientifically structured academic discipline known as Physical and Health Education (PHE). From ancient traditional wrestling festivals held under village moonlight to modern Olympic tracks, physical training has always been the cornerstone of human survival, vitality, and national unity. Let us explore the foundational science and history of Physical Education!

Key Terms

Physical Education An integral part of the total educational process that utilizes structured physical activities, movement, games, and sports to develop the physical, mental, emotional, and social capacities of the individual.
Health Education The educational process of imparting verified scientific knowledge, positive attitudes, and healthy lifestyle habits that motivate individuals to maintain personal, family, and community wellness.
Physical Fitness The physiological capacity of the human body to perform daily physical tasks with vigor, alertness, and efficiency, without undue exhaustion, while retaining reserve energy to enjoy leisure and meet unexpected emergencies.
Traditional Physical Education Indigenous, non-formal physical activities, martial arts, survival skills, and cultural dances practiced by ancestral African societies for hunting, community defense, and ceremonial festivals before Western formal schooling.
Neuromuscular Coordination The harmonious, synchronized interaction between the nervous system and muscular system to execute smooth, precise, and balanced physical movements.
Sportsmanship The ethical attitude of fairness, courtesy, mutual respect, self-control, and graceful acceptance of both victory and defeat during sports competitions.

Core Objectives of Physical Education

Modern curriculum frameworks define five foundational objectives for physical education:

1.
Physical & Organic Development: Builds strong bones, muscular endurance, flexibility, good posture, and efficient respiratory and circulatory systems.
2.
Neuromuscular Skill Development: Trains the brain and reflex pathways to coordinate complex motor skills—such as kicking a football, catching a high fly ball, or landing safely from a jump.
3.
Cognitive & Intellectual Development: Teaches students game rules, strategic decision-making under time pressure, mechanical movement principles, and spatial judgment.
4.
Emotional Stability & Resilience: Teaches students how to handle intense competition, channel adrenaline positively, manage anger, celebrate victory humbly, and accept defeat with dignity.
5.
Social Competence & Character Building: Cultivates cooperation, teamwork, respect for opponents and game officials, leadership, and national unity across diverse cultural backgrounds.

The Broad Scope of Physical Education

Physical Education encompasses a rich variety of human movement domains:

Athletics (Track & Field): Sprints, distance races, relays, hurdles, high jump, long jump, shot put, javelin, and discus throw.
Ball Games & Team Sports: Football (soccer), basketball, volleyball, handball, rugby, and cricket.
Racket Games: Table tennis, badminton, tennis, and squash.
Gymnastics: Floor stunts, tumbling, vaulting box, balance beam, and parallel bars.
Combat & Martial Sports: Traditional African wrestling, modern wrestling, judo, and karate.
Aquatics (Swimming): Water safety, floating, breaststroke, backstroke, freestyle crawl, and lifesaving.
Rhythmic & Dance Activities: Traditional Nigerian folk dances (e.g., Atilogwu, Bata, Koroso), calisthenics, and modern aerobics.
Recreation & Outdoor Pursuit: Hiking, camping, cycling, and leisure pursuits.

Historical Evolution of Physical Education in Nigeria

1. The Pre-Colonial Era (Indigenous Physical Culture)

Long before European contact, Nigerian ethnic nations possessed rich, organized physical cultures embedded in daily life:

Survival Skills: Boys were trained in spear throwing, bow and arrow marksmanship (archery), canoeing across delta creeks, climbing palm trees, and horseback riding across northern savannas.
Traditional Wrestling: Widely practiced as a rite of passage, festival highlight, and military preparation:
Kokawa among the Hausa and Fulani.
Gidigbo among the Yoruba.
Igba Mgba among the Igbo.
Festival Dances: Rhythmic drumming and aerobic dances developed incredible muscular endurance, flexibility, and community cohesion during new yam and harvest festivals.

2. The Colonial Era (The British Military Influence)

Formal Western schooling introduced physical education in the late 19th and early 20th centuries through Christian mission schools (such as CMS Grammar School Lagos [1859], Hope Waddell Training Institution Calabar [1895], and King's College Lagos [1909]).
Early colonial physical education was called Physical Training (P.T.), consisting of rigid British military marching drills, calisthenics, and empire games (cricket, track and field, and football).
The 1933 Education Syllabus formally recognized Physical Education as an official school subject.

3. The Post-Independence Era (National Pride & Unity)

After Nigerian independence in 1960, Physical Education transitioned from colonial military drills into an academic and scientific discipline.
Key Milestones:
Establishment of the National Institute for Sports (NIS) at the National Stadium in Surulere, Lagos, to train certified coaches and sports managers.
Creation of the biennial National Sports Festival (NSF) in 1973 by the Federal Government to heal national wounds following the Civil War and promote inter-ethnic harmony among Nigerian youth.
Inclusion of Physical and Health Education (PHE) by the Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council (NERDC) as a compulsory core subject in the national 9-year Basic Education curriculum.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

Together with your parent or guardian, explore the sports and physical heritage of your family: 1. Ask About Traditional Games: Ask your guardian: "What traditional games or physical sports did you play in your youth?" (e.g., traditional wrestling, Ayo Olopon, Suwe, skipping competitions, or climbing). 2. Discuss School Inter-House Sports: Ask them about their favorite memories of Inter-House Sports days during their secondary school years. Discuss why schools place so much importance on annual sports competitions. 3. Plan a Family Exercise Routine: Agree on a simple physical fitness habit you can practice together at home—such as a 15-minute morning brisk walk or evening stretching session!
