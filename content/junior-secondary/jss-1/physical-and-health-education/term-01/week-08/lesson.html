Origins & Inventors of Court Games

Both volleyball and basketball were invented in the state of Massachusetts, USA, within four years of each other:

1.
Basketball (1891): Invented by Canadian physical education instructor Dr. James Naismith in Springfield, Massachusetts, using two peach baskets nailed to a gymnasium balcony and a soccer ball to keep students active indoors during harsh winters.
2.
Volleyball (1895): Invented by William G. Morgan in Holyoke, Massachusetts, as a less strenuous, non-contact recreational game for businessmen, initially christened "Mintonette".

Volleyball: Court, Rules & Fundamentals

Court Dimensions: Rectangular, measuring $18\text{ meters}$ long by $9\text{ meters}$ wide, divided into two $9\text{m} \times 9\text{m}$ halves by a centerline and a net. An Attack Line is drawn $3\text{ meters}$ from the net on each side.
Net Height: Standard regulation net height is $2.43\text{ meters}$ for men and $2.24\text{ meters}$ for women (calibrated slightly lower for junior secondary classes).
Team Size & Rotation: 6 players per team on the court. When a receiving team wins a rally, its players rotate one position clockwise before serving!

Core Volleyball Skills:

1.
The Service: Putting the ball into play from behind the end line. Techniques include the Underhand Serve (safe for beginners), Overhand/Tennis Serve (aggressive spin), and Jump Serve.
2.
The Forearm Pass (Dig): Used to receive hard serves and spikes. Hands are clasped, thumbs aligned side-by-side, elbows locked rigid, and knees bent to absorb and cushion the ball.
3.
The Overhead Pass (Set): Shaping hands like a triangle above the forehead, contacting the ball gently with the finger pads to float it up for an attacker.
4.
The Spike & Block: The spike drives the ball down violently; the defensive block features front-row players leaping with upright outstretched hands at the net to deflect the spike.
Rally Point Scoring: The first team to score 25 points (with at least a 2-point lead) wins a set. Matches are played to best of 3 or best of 5 sets. A team is allowed a maximum of three touches before returning the ball over the net.

Basketball: Court, Rules & Fundamentals

Court Dimensions: Standard FIBA court measures $28\text{ meters}$ long by $15\text{ meters}$ wide.
Ring Height: The rim is suspended horizontally at exactly $3.05\text{ meters}$ ($10\text{ feet}$) above the floor.
Players on Court: 5 active players per team (Point guard, Shooting guard, Small forward, Power forward, Center).
Court Dimensions and Playing Zones: Volleyball 18m x 9m court layout vs Basketball 28m x 15m court layout

Core Basketball Skills:

1.
Dribbling: Bouncing the ball continuously against the floor using the finger pads (not slapping with the palm!), keeping the head up and body low.
2.
Passing:
Chest Pass: Quickest pass, snapping from chest to chest.
Bounce Pass: Snapped to hit the floor two-thirds of the distance to the teammate, ideal for slipping under the outstretched arms of defenders.
Overhead Pass: Held above the head with both hands, used for skip passes over a zone defense.
1.
Shooting:
Lay-up: Approaching the rim, taking a legal two-step cadence, leaping high off one foot, and softly kissing the ball off the backboard.
Jump Shot: Leaping vertically into the air and releasing the ball at the highest point with high arch and backward wrist rotation.
Free Throw: An unguarded shot from the 4.60-meter foul line awarded after fouls, worth 1 point.
Scoring Breakdown:
Field goal scored from inside the three-point arc = 2 points.
Field goal scored from behind the three-point arc = 3 points.
Free throw = 1 point.
Key Violations: Traveling, Double Dribble, Backcourt Violation (returning ball to backcourt once established in frontcourt), 24-second shot clock expiration.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Wall Set & Volley Drill: Stand facing an exterior wall about 1 meter away. Practice continuous overhead finger-pad tapping (setting) against the wall to build finger strength and coordination. - Basketball Low-Dribble Drill: Practice bouncing a tennis or basketball at knee height 50 times with the right hand and 50 times with the left hand without looking down at the ball. - Discussion: Discuss with the student how team rotation and communication in court games develop social responsibility, trust, and cooperative leadership.
