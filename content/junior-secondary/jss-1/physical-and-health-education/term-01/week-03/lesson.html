Why This Matters to You

Imagine the breathless silence that grips eighty thousand spectators in an Olympic stadium just moments before the 100-meter sprint final. Eight of the fastest human beings on the planet are down on their knees in the running lanes, their fingers spread like stone bridges right behind the crisp white starting line. The starter raises the starting pistol: "On your marks... Set..." BANG! Within ten seconds of ferocious, blinding speed, a champion storms across the finish line! And what about the heart-pounding drama of the 4x100-meter relay race, where four lightning-fast runners combine their powers, passing a smooth metal cylinder at full running speed—where dropping the baton by a millimeter can break a nation's heart, but a flawless handoff brings immortal Olympic gold? Welcome to the high-octane world of Track Athletics!

Key Terms

Athletics An ancient collection of competitive sporting disciplines that encompass running, walking, jumping, and throwing contests conducted on standardized tracks and sports fields.
Track Events Running and race-walking athletic competitions conducted on a measured running track (usually a standard 400-meter synthetic oval).
Sprint Races Short-distance athletic running events (such as 100m, 200m, and 400m) where athletes run at maximum velocity over their entire course within dedicated lanes.
Crouch Start The low-profile four-point starting posture used by sprint athletes in starting blocks, executed through three progressive commands: "On your marks", "Set", and the starter's gun ("Go").
Relay Race A team track event in which four athletes run sequential equal stages (legs) of a race, transferring a smooth hollow cylinder called a baton within a strictly marked changeover zone.
Baton A smooth, hollow, one-piece cylinder made of metal, wood, or plastic (measuring 28–30 cm long) passed from one runner to the next in a relay race.
Non-Visual Baton Exchange A blind baton transfer technique used in 4x100m sprint relays where the outgoing runner does not look back, keeping their eyes focused forward while extending their receiving hand backwards.
Visual Baton Exchange A baton passing technique used in longer relays (such as the 4x400m) where the outgoing runner turns their head to watch the incoming runner and visually guide their hand onto the baton.

Sprinting Mechanics & The Crouch Start

In all sprint races ($100\text{m}, 200\text{m}, 400\text{m}$), runners are legally required to start from a Crouch Start using metallic starting blocks:

The Three Starting Commands:

1.
"On Your Marks":
The athlete steps into the starting blocks, placing the dominant foot on the front block pedal and the other foot on the rear pedal.
The athlete drops the rear knee onto the track surface.
Hands are placed just behind the starting line, spaced shoulder-width apart, forming a bridge or high arch with the thumbs and fingers (hands must not touch or cross the line).
1.
"Set":
At the command, the athlete raises the hips smoothly to a level slightly higher than the shoulders.
The rear knee lifts off the track; body weight shifts forward over the hands.
The head remains in natural alignment with the spine, with eyes focused approximately one meter down the track.
Crucial Rule: Every athlete must remain completely motionless until the pistol fires!
1.
The Gun / "Go" (BANG!):
Both feet push explosively against the block pedals simultaneously.
The rear leg drives forward rapidly, while the opposite arm swings vigorously forward to initiate forward momentum.
The torso rises gradually over the first 15 to 20 meters (the Drive Phase).

Relay Racing (4x100m and 4x400m)

A relay team consists of four runners, each running one "leg" of the race:

4x100m Relay: Each runner sprints 100 meters at full velocity.
4x400m Relay: Each runner runs a full 400-meter lap of the track.

The Changeover (Take-over) Zone

The baton pass cannot happen just anywhere on the track! It must take place strictly inside a 20-meter or 30-meter changeover zone marked by yellow or white lines:

If the baton is handed over before entering the zone, or after passing the end line, the team is disqualified!
If the baton is accidentally dropped, the athlete who dropped it must pick it up without obstructing other teams.

Baton Passing Techniques:

1.
Non-Visual Exchange (Blind Pass):
Where Used: Strictly in 4x100-meter sprint relays.
How It Works: When the incoming runner reaches a designated checkmark on the track, the outgoing runner sprints forward at maximum acceleration. When the incoming runner enters the changeover zone, they yell a loud verbal cue ("Hand!" or "Hop!"). The outgoing runner blindly extends their receiving hand backward (palm facing up or down) while keeping their head and eyes looking straight ahead!
Advantage: Maximum sprinting speed is maintained without slowing down.
1.
Visual Exchange:
Where Used: In 4x400-meter relays.
How It Works: Because 400-meter runners are physically exhausted at the end of their lap, their speed is unpredictable. The outgoing runner turns their head, looks directly at the incoming runner's hand, and visually grasps the baton before accelerating.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

Together with your parent or guardian, practice starting techniques and baton passing in an open yard: 1. Practice the Crouch Start: Mark a line on the ground. Have your guardian act as the starter, giving the official commands: "On your marks!", "Set!", and clapping their hands for "Go!" Focus on holding completely still in the "Set" position without wobbling! 2. Practice the Non-Visual Relay Pass: Take an empty cardboard paper towel roll or a clean rolled-up newspaper as a baton. Walk in front of your guardian. When your guardian shouts "Hand!", extend your right hand backward with your palm facing open and your thumb pointing down, keeping your eyes looking straight ahead. Feel the baton press into your palm and wrap your fingers securely!
