Origins & Scope of Gymnastics

The word gymnastics derives from the ancient Greek word gymnos (meaning "unclad" or "naked"), because athletes in ancient Greece trained stripped of heavy clothing in facilities called gymnasia. In modern Physical Education, gymnastics is divided into two primary disciplines:

1.
Floor Activities (Floor Stunts & Tumbling): Executed directly on padded mats or floor areas.
2.
Apparatus Activities: Executed using elevated gymnastic equipment (such as the vaulting box, balance beam, parallel bars, and horizontal bar).

Basic Floor Gymnastics: Forward roll sequence, tripod headstand triangle, and side cartwheel execution

1. Forward Roll:

Phase 1 (Preparation): Stand at the edge of the mat, lower into a deep squat with knees together, and place hands shoulder-width apart flat on the mat with fingers pointing forward.
Phase 2 (Execution): Tuck the chin tightly onto the chest (look at the belly button!), push off the balls of the feet, raise hips, and bend elbows so the back of the neck and shoulders (never the top of the crown!) touch the mat softly.
Phase 3 (Follow-through): Round the spine like a ball, hold knees into the chest (tuck), roll smoothly forward along the spine, and rock onto the feet without touching hands to the floor to stand upright.

2. Backward Roll:

Start in a deep crouch with palms held up beside the ears (fingers pointing backward over shoulders).
Sit backward close to the heels, tuck chin tightly to chest, roll smoothly backward along the rounded spine.
When the hands contact the mat beside the ears, push vigorously against the floor to relieve pressure on the neck, flip the legs over, and land softly on the feet in a squat.

3. Headstand (The Tripod Base):

Place two hands flat on the mat, shoulder-width apart.
Place the hairline / upper forehead on the mat ahead of the hands to form a stable equilateral triangle (Tripod). If your head and hands are in a straight line, you will lose balance and topple sideways!
Walk feet toward the chest until the hips are over the shoulders.
Slowly lift one leg, then the other, into a vertical alignment, squeezing abdominal core and glute muscles.

4. Cartwheel:

Face forward, step into a lunge with the lead foot.
Reach forward and sideways to place the lead hand on the mat, followed by the second hand, kicking legs overhead sequentially in a wide straddle (hand-hand-foot-foot cadence).
Land on the first foot, followed by the second foot, returning to an upright standing posture.

Mat Safety Rules and Spotting

Always Use Safety Mats: Never practice floor stunts, rolls, or balances on bare concrete, terrazzo, or gravel!
Tuck the Chin: The single most critical rule in rolling stunts is tucking the chin to the chest so the curved spine absorbs impact rather than the fragile cervical vertebrae (neck).
The Spotter’s Duty:
The spotter stands at the side of the performer to stabilize hips, catch the legs during headstands/handstands, and cushion unexpected falls.
Spotters must never look away or joke during a stunt execution!

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Basic Flexibility & Spinal Warm-up: Guide the learner through gentle cat-cow stretches and seated forward bends on a rug or carpet at home to improve spinal flexibility. - Plank Stability Challenge: Practice holding an upright push-up plank on hands or elbows for 30 to 45 seconds to build the core stability required for gymnastics. - Safety Rule Reinforcement: Reiterate to the learner that gymnastic tumbling should never be attempted on hard cement floors, verandahs, or without proper supervision.
