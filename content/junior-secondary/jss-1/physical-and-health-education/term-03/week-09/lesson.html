Detailed Organ Care Protocols

1. Care of the Skin:

Bathe at least once or twice daily with clean water and mild toilet soap, scrubbing with a clean sponge.
Pay close attention to skin folds (armpits, groin, behind ears, between toes) where warm sweat and sebum oil accumulate, breeding bacteria that produce foul body odor (bromhidrosis) and fungal ringworm.
Dry thoroughly with a clean personal towel; never share damp towels with classmates.

2. Care of the Teeth:

Brush teeth twice daily: once in the morning after waking up, and once at night before going to bed (this stops mouth bacteria from feeding on food sugars for eight uninterrupted hours while you sleep!).
Use a soft or medium nylon-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste (or a clean traditional chewing stick like neem / pako / miswak, chewed into a soft fibrous brush).
Brush with gentle circular and vertical sweeping motions from the gums toward the tooth crowns.
Rinse the mouth with clean water after every meal to dislodge trapped meat and starch particles.

3. Care of the Eyes:

Wash the face daily with clean, cool water.
Never rub the eyes with dirty hands or soiled handkerchiefs; rubbing transfers bacteria and viruses causing acute conjunctivitis ("Apollo") or trachoma.
Avoid reading in dim, flickering light or inside moving vehicles; never look directly at the intense midday sun or welding torches without protective eyewear.

4. Care of the Hair and Scalp:

Wash hair regularly with soap or mild shampoo to remove scalp oils, dust, and dandruff.
Keep hair neatly trimmed, combed, or styled in clean braids.
Avoid sharing combs, brushes, or caps, which can spread Head Lice (Pediculus humanus capitis) and fungal scalp ringworm (Tinea capitis).

5. Care of the Hands and Nails:

Keep fingernails and toenails trimmed short and rounded with clean clippers. Long nails trap black filth, fecal bacteria, and microscopic roundworm (Ascaris) eggs that enter the mouth when eating with hands!
Handwashing: Wash hands with clean running water and soap for at least 20 seconds:
1.
After using the toilet or latrine.
2.
Before preparing, serving, or eating food.
3.
After physical sports, gardening, or handling animals.

Community Health & Immunization

No individual can remain healthy in a sick, filthy community. Community health relies on collective responsibility:

1. Immunization & The National Programme on Immunization (NPI):

Vaccines introduce safe, harmless biological antigens into the body. The immune system's white blood cells produce targeted antibodies and develop cellular memory.
If the wild pathogen ever attacks in the future, the trained immune system destroys it immediately before illness can develop!
Key Nigerian Routine Childhood Vaccines:
BCG: Protects against Tuberculosis (given at birth).
OPV / IPV: Protects against crippling Poliovirus.
Pentavalent Vaccine: Protects against 5 deadly diseases: Diphtheria, Whooping Cough (Pertussis), Tetanus, Hepatitis B, and Haemophilus influenzae type b.
Measles Vaccine: Protects against measles (administered at 9 months).
Yellow Fever & Meningitis Vaccines: Protects against tropical vector and airborne epidemics.

2. Environmental Sanitation & Communal Cleanliness:

Unblocking neighborhood stormwater gutters to eliminate stagnant water where malaria mosquitoes breed.
Cutting overgrown bushes and elephant grass around school compounds to deter venomous snakes, scorpions, and rodents.
Participating actively in monthly environmental sanitation exercises.

Check your child’s toothbrush to ensure bristles are not splayed and worn out. - Nail Inspection & Handwashing Station: Inspect your child’s fingernails together.
