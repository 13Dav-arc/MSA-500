1.
Origins at University College, Ibadan (1952):
Founded by seven young undergraduate scholars known as "The Magnificent Seven" (Wole Soyinka, Ralph Opara, Pius Oleghe, Ikpehare Aig-Imoukhuede, Nathaniel Oyelola, Olumuyiwa Awe, and Sylvanus Egbuche).
Their original objectives were noble and intellectual: fighting colonial snobbery, defending African cultural pride, eliminating ethnic tribalism on campus, and serving as a chivalrous, fun-loving student fraternity. They engaged in open debates, sang pirate songs, and organized voluntary blood donations for hospitals. They had zero weapons, zero blood oaths, and zero violence.
1.
The Descent into Violent Modern Cultism (1980s–Present):
In subsequent decades, splinter groups broke away (e.g., Buccaneers, Black Axe, Eiye, Vikings).
Under military rule, discipline collapsed. Unscrupulous politicians and military administrators began arming and funding these groups to suppress student union activism and eliminate political rivals.
Confraternities degenerated into vicious, armed secret cults characterized by demonic rituals, machete attacks, gang rapes, hired assassinations, and extortion. Today, cultism has spread beyond universities into polytechnics, secondary schools, and artisan street groups.

Why Young People Join Cults (Causes)

Search for False Security and Power: Weak-willed students mistakenly believe a cult will protect them from campus bullies or lecturers.
Negative Peer Pressure and Intimidation: Cultists stalk, threaten, and lure naive students with false promises of "connections" and academic marks.
Parental Neglect and Moral Breakdown: Children growing up in homes devoid of love, supervision, and discipline seek a sense of belonging in delinquent gangs.
Greed and Political Thuggery: Financial handouts from corrupt politicians looking for armed muscle during elections.

Devastating Consequences of Cultism

Premature Death and Physical Disability: Hundreds of youth are hacked to death, shot, or blinded in turf clashes annually.
Academic Destruction: Rustication, expulsion, and prison records permanently wipe out academic and professional careers.
Trauma for Families: Grieving parents waste millions of Naira on school fees only to receive their child’s corpse in a hospital morgue.
National Insecurity: Cultists graduate into highway robbery, kidnapping, and pipeline vandalism, sabotaging national peace.

Practical Ways to Eradicate Cultism

1.
Public Denunciation and Undertakings: Students upon admission must sign legally binding undertakings and renounce secret societies publicly.
2.
Strict Law Enforcement: Applying the Anti-Cultism Act: prosecuting members, sponsors, and godfathers, giving zero bail for violent clashes.
3.
Parental Vigilance and Care: Parents must monitor changes in their children's clothing habits, unusual night outings, unexplained scars, and sudden possession of strange emblems (berets, axes, rings).
4.
Campus Security & Lighting: Installing CCTV surveillance, illuminating dark corners, and establishing rapid-response campus security patrols.
5.
Moral and Religious Campaign: Churches, mosques, and mass media must systematically expose the demonic deception and grim reality of cultism.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Home Discussion Point: Talk openly with your child about cultism. Ask: “Do you know what cultism is, and why should anyone who tries to talk to you about secret groups be reported immediately?” - Inspect Behavioral Shifts: Watch for warning signs: sudden fascination with specific color combinations (red/black, yellow, blue), secret handshakes, unusual cuts or cigarette burn scars, and defensive secrecy. - Affirm Family Love: Cult recruiters target insecure, lonely young people. When children feel deeply cherished, valued, and listened to at home, they are immune to the false promises of cult brotherhood.
