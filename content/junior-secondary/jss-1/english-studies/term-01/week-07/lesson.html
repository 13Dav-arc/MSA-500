Foundational curriculum module in English Studies covering Plosives /p/ and /b/, Prepositions, Expository Writing, and Introduction to Poetry for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Hold your fingertips gently against your Adam's apple (larynx) and say the word "pin", then say "bin". Did you feel that distinct buzz of vibration during "bin" that was completely absent during "pin"? That subtle vocal cord vibration is the magic of voicing, separating voiceless /p/ from voiced /b/! In the architecture of sentences, small prepositions like under, between, and across function like precise GPS locators, pinpointing exact positions in time and space. When you unite this mechanical precision with the step-by-step clarity of expository writing and the rhythmic, musical beauty of poetry, you unlock both practical instruction and artistic expression.

Key Terms

Plosive (Stop) Consonant A consonant speech sound produced by completely blocking the airflow in the vocal tract, allowing compressed air to build up behind the closure, and then releasing it suddenly with an audible explosive burst.
Bilabial Consonant A consonant sound articulated using both the upper and lower lips pressed tightly together (such as /p/, /b/, and /m/).
Preposition A grammatical function word placed before a noun or pronoun to show its relationship (such as location, time, direction, or agency) to another word in the sentence.
Expository Composition A piece of non-fiction continuous writing designed to explain, inform, describe a step-by-step process, or clarify an idea logically and objectively.
Stanza A grouped set of lines within a poem, separated from other groups by a blank space, functioning analogously to a paragraph in prose.

Core Concept

Aspect 1: Speech Work — Bilabial Plosives /p/ and /b/

Both /p/ and /b/ are bilabial plosive consonants produced with the same place of articulation (the two lips), but they contrast sharply in vocal cord vibration (voicing).

The Three Stages of Plosive Production:
1.
Closing Stage: The upper and lower lips come together to form an airtight seal, while the soft palate is raised to prevent air from escaping through the nose.
2.
Compression Stage: Air expelled from the lungs builds up behind the closed lips under pressure.
3.
Release Stage: The lips part suddenly, and the compressed air explodes outward with an audible sound.
/p/ (Voiceless Bilabial Plosive):
Articulated with the two lips firmly closed. The vocal cords do not vibrate (voiceless). When followed by a vowel in an initial stressed syllable, it is pronounced with a puff of air (aspiration).
Common spelling: `p` (pen, pack, pig, open, cup), `pp` (apple, happen). (Note: `p` is silent in psychology, receipt, pneumonia, corps).
/b/ (Voiced Bilabial Plosive):
Articulated identically with closed lips, but the vocal cords vibrate energetically (voiced). It is never aspirated.
Common spelling: `b` (bed, bin, book, rubber, cab). (Note: `b` is silent in climb, comb, doubt, debt, lamb, tomb).

| Word Position | Voiceless Plosive /p/ | Voiced Plosive /b/ |

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Initial | pin, pack, pear, pest, pie | bin, back, bear, best, buy |

| Medial | staple, simple, caper, supper | stable, symbol, cable, rubber |

| Final | cap, rope, rip, cup, mop | cab, robe, rib, cub, mob |

Aspect 2: Structure — Prepositions and Prepositional Phrases

A preposition links its noun or pronoun object to the rest of the sentence, creating a Prepositional Phrase (Preposition + Object):

1.
Prepositions of Place (Location):
In: Enclosed spaces, towns, states, countries (in the classroom, in Ibadan, in Nigeria).
At: Specific exact points or institutions (at the gate, at the bus stop, at Federal Unity College).
On: Surfaces (on the table, on the wall, on the floor).
1.
Prepositions of Time:
At: Exact clock time and festival names (at 8:00 AM, at noon, at Christmas).
On: Days of the week and specific calendar dates (on Monday, on October 1st).
In: Months, years, centuries, and seasons (in January, in 2026, in the dry season).
1.
Prepositions of Direction / Movement:
To, toward, into, across, through, onto, out of (walked into the hall, ran across the field).
1.
Key Prepositional Distinctions:
Between vs Among: Use between for exactly two persons, items, or groups ("Divide the mango between Chidi and Tunde"). Use among for three or more entities ("Share the books among all the students").
Beside vs Besides: Beside means next to / by the side of ("She sat beside her mother"). Besides means in addition to / apart from ("Besides English, he speaks Yoruba and French").

Aspect 3: Comprehension & Vocabulary — "Modern Transportation and Highway Safety"

Reading Passage:

> The rapid expansion of modern transport infrastructure has revolutionized national commerce and human mobility across Nigeria. Express highways, commuter rail systems, and modern international airports now seamlessly connect dispersed agrarian hinterlands to thriving commercial metropolises like Lagos, Kano, and Port Harcourt. Perishable agricultural harvests that once rotted on rural farms can now be transported swiftly to bustling urban supermarkets within twenty-four hours, bolstering national food security and expanding domestic trade.

>

> Regrettably, this expansion in vehicular mobility has been accompanied by a distressing surge in preventable highway casualties. Careless motorists frequently ignore fundamental road safety regulations, engaging in reckless speeding, dangerous overtaking at sharp blind corners, and operating unroadworthy commercial vehicles with worn tires and defective brakes. Compounding these hazards, some commercial drivers consume intoxicating substances and operate mobile phones while navigating complex dual-carriageway expressways.

>

> In response to this grave epidemic of road traffic accidents, the Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC) has intensified its nationwide public enlightenment campaigns and highway enforcement patrols. Patrol marshals routinely utilize digital speed-radar guns and breathalyzers to apprehend traffic violators, while strict vehicle inspection officers impound mechanically deficient trucks. Ultimately, however, achieving genuine highway safety requires personal responsibility from every road user. Pedestrians must utilize designated overhead footbridges, passengers must politely caution reckless bus drivers, and motorists must recognize that driving with defensive vigilance is an indispensable civic obligation to preserve precious human lives.

Target Vocabulary in Context:

Infrastructure (Noun): The basic physical and organizational structures and facilities (roads, railways, power) needed for the operation of a society.
Hinterland (Noun): The remote, undeveloped rural areas situated away from the coast or major urban centers.
Defective (Adjective): Imperfect, faulty, or lacking an essential component needed for safe operation.
Intoxicating (Adjective): Capable of causing loss of physical or mental control; drunk or impaired.
Enforcement (Noun): The act of compelling observance of or compliance with a law, rule, or obligation.
Vigilance (Noun): The action or state of keeping careful watch for possible danger, difficulties, or hazards.

Aspect 4: Composition — Expository Writing I: "How to Prepare a Local Dish"

Expository writing explains a subject, informs an audience, or outlines a sequence of procedural steps logically, objectively, and accurately.

Key Characteristics of Procedural Expository Writing:
1.
Clarity and Objectivity: Straightforward, factual language without exaggerated fictional drama.
2.
Present Simple Tense / Imperative Mood: Uses imperative verbs (wash, slice, boil, stir) and present tense.
3.
Chronological Transitional Markers: Words that signal step order (first, secondly, subsequently, meanwhile, thereafter, finally).
4.
Materials / Ingredients List: A clear itemization of all necessary ingredients and utensils before explaining the preparation process.
Model Outline: "How to Prepare Delicious Nigerian Jollof Rice"
Paragraph 1 (Introduction): Brief background on Jollof rice as Nigeria's celebrated national party delicacy; summary of required ingredients (long-grain parboiled rice, fresh plum tomatoes, red bell peppers, onions, vegetable oil, tomato paste, chicken stock, curry, thyme, bay leaves, and salt).
Paragraph 2 (Preparation of the Base): Blending tomatoes, peppers, and onions into a smooth puree; boiling the puree to evaporate excess water; parboiling and washing the rice.
Paragraph 3 (Frying the Stew Base): Heating vegetable oil in a pot; frying sliced onions and tomato paste until fragrant; pouring in the blended pepper puree and frying until oil floats to the top.
Paragraph 4 (Cooking the Rice): Seasoning with curry, thyme, seasoning cubes, and rich chicken stock; adding bay leaves; stirring in the parboiled rice; covering with tight aluminum foil to lock in steam over low heat until the grains absorb the savory broth.
Paragraph 5 (Conclusion & Serving): Fluffing the steaming orange-red grains with a wooden spoon; serving hot with crispy fried chicken and golden fried plantains (dodo).

Aspect 5: Literature-in-English — Introduction to Poetry

Poetry is an imaginative form of literature that expresses deep emotions, ideas, and sensory experiences through rhythmic, concentrated language arranged in verses.

Poetry Terminology Distinction:
Poetry: The general genre or art form.
Poem: An individual, specific piece of poetic writing.
Poet: The creative person who authors a poem.
Essential Structural Elements of Poetry:
1.
Line: A single unit of language arranged in one row of text within a poem.
2.
Stanza: A group of lines forming a distinct structural unit in a poem (analogous to a paragraph in prose).
Couplet: A two-line stanza.
Tercet: A three-line stanza.
Quatrain: A four-line stanza.
1.
Rhythm: The musical beat or recurring pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables.
2.
Rhyme and Rhyme Scheme: The repetition of identical or similar sounds at the ends of poetic lines (end-rhyme). The pattern of rhymes is designated using letters (e.g., ABAB, AABB).
3.
Sensory Imagery: Descriptive language that appeals vividly to the five human senses (sight, hearing, touch, taste, smell) to evoke mental pictures.

Comparing Key Sound, Grammar, and Poetic Distinctions

Plosive /p/ vs Plosive /b/

Voicing State and Aspiration

/p/ is voiceless with zero vocal cord vibration and is aspirated initially (as in 'pin'); /b/ is voiced with vocal cord vibration and is never aspirated (as in 'bin').

Preposition 'Between' vs 'Among'

Numerical Number of Reference Entities

'Between' is used when referring to exactly two individuals or distinct entities; 'among' is used when referring to three or more individuals within a group.

'Beside' vs 'Besides'

Physical Proximity vs Conceptual Addition

'Beside' means located by the side of / next to (e.g., 'sitting beside me'); 'besides' means in addition to / apart from (e.g., 'besides English').

Prose Paragraph vs Poetic Stanza

Structural Unit of Composition

Prose is composed in sentences grouped into paragraphs; poetry is composed in rhythmic lines grouped into stanzas.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Pronouncing the voiceless plosive /p/ as the voiced plosive /b/, confusing "pest" with "best" or "pack" with "back".

Scientific Fact

Ensure the vocal cords remain completely silent without vibrating when articulating /p/.

Common Mistake

Saying *"Divide the money between the three boys"* instead of *"Divide the money among the three boys"*.

Scientific Fact

When distributing items among more than two entities, standard English grammar requires *among*, reserving *between* for two.

Common Mistake

Writing instructions in expository process essays without chronological transitional linkers.

Scientific Fact

Procedural writing must use sequential linkers like *first*, *thereafter*, *subsequently*, and *finally* to ensure logical clarity.
