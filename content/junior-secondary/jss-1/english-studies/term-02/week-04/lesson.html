Core Concept

Aspect 1: Speech Work — Consonants /ʃ/ and /ʒ/

Consonants /ʃ/ and /ʒ/ are palato-alveolar fricative sounds. During production, the front of the tongue moves close to the hard palate behind the alveolar ridge, creating a narrow channel through which air escapes with friction:

/ʃ/ is a voiceless palato-alveolar fricative produced without vocal cord vibration.
Common spelling symbols for /ʃ/: `sh` (shoe, sheep, dish, bash), `ss` (passion, mission), `s` (sure, sugar), `ch` (champagne, chauffeur, Chicago), `ti` (nation, mention), and `ci` (special, precious).
/ʒ/ is a voiced palato-alveolar fricative articulated with the exact same tongue posture as /ʃ/, but with the vocal cords vibrating.
Consonant /ʒ/ does not typically occur at the beginning or absolute end of native English words, occurring mostly medially or in loanwords borrowed from French.
Common spelling symbols for /ʒ/: `s` (measure, pleasure, leisure, usual), `si` (vision, erosion, decision, confusion, cohesion), `z` (seizure), and `g`/`ge` in French loanwords (genre, beige, rouge, barrage, garage).

Aspect 2: Structure — Future Tenses

The future tense expresses events, conditions, or actions that have not yet occurred. In English, future time is indicated using the auxiliary verbs shall and will:

Traditionally, shall is used with first-person pronouns (I and we), while will is used with second- and third-person pronouns (you, he, she, it, they). In contemporary English, will is widely used across all persons.

The Four Subdivisions of the Future Tense:

1.
Simple Future Tense: Expresses an action that will occur at a specified or unspecified time ahead.
I shall travel next week.
We shall meet next year.
He will arrive next Saturday.
They will pay your money.
1.
Future Continuous Tense: Indicates an ongoing action that will be taking place at a particular point in the future.
Formed using will/shall be + -ing verb.
He will be waiting for us when the train arrives.
They will be arriving tomorrow morning.
1.
Future Perfect Tense: Used to show that an action will be completed before another specified future time or event.
Formed using will/shall have + past participle.
She will have finished the work by Wednesday.
They will have eaten breakfast by the time he gets up.
1.
Future Perfect Continuous Tense: Shows that an action will have been in progress for a continuous period up to a certain point in the future.
Formed using will/shall have been + -ing verb.
By next January, she will have been living here for a whole year.
He will have been working for ten months by the time he takes his vacation.

Aspect 3: Comprehension & Vocabulary — An Indelible Lesson

Reading Passage:

> There is a day that will continue to remain indelible in my memory throughout my earthly existence. It is the memorable day that I learnt a bitter lesson which I have resolved to pass down to my own children. I was then an eleven-year-old boy living with my family in the tranquil outskirts of Zaria. My father and I woke up very early on an overcast Saturday morning to tend to our vegetable garden before the morning sun became scorching. As we harvested ripe tomatoes, my father handed me a substantial sum of money, commanding me strictly to walk directly to the village agro-allied depot to purchase special fertilizer for our maize seedlings.

>

> On my way to the depot, however, I met a group of boisterous neighborhood playmates rolling bicycle tyres and betting small coins on an improvised game of chance. Temptation overwhelmed my sense of obedience; I abandoned my father's stern instructions and joined the wager, convinced that I could swiftly multiply the money and pocket an illicit profit. Within forty-five minutes of reckless gambling, every single kobo of the fertilizer money had vanished into the pockets of the older boys. Stricken by sheer terror, I loitered aimlessly in the town square until nightfall, concocting transparent lies about armed highway pickpockets.

>

> When I finally sneaked into our living room, my father's piercing gaze dismantled my fabricated story within seconds. He did not spare the rod; the severe flogging that followed left painful welts upon my legs, but it was the deep sorrow in his eyes and the disappointment in his voice that inflicted the most lasting wound. Later that evening, after my tears had dried, my father sat beside my bed and gently treated my bruises, explaining that dishonesty destroys trust faster than fire consumes dry grass. From that traumatic incident, I learnt that deceit carries unavoidable humiliation, and that integrity is the greatest wealth an individual can ever possess.

Aspect 4: Continuous Writing — Formal Letters: Guided Writing and Logical Sequencing

Every effective piece of formal letter writing requires logical sequencing—arranging ideas, facts, and requests in a clear, step-by-step order so the reader can follow the writer's reasoning without confusion.

Types of Formal Letters:

Applications for employment or admission into educational institutions
Petitions to government ministries or traditional rulers
Official letters of complaint to law enforcement agencies or companies
Letters of inquiry regarding services or goods
Letters to the editors of national newspapers commenting on public policies

Step-by-Step Logical Sequence of a Formal Body Paragraph Layout:

1.
Paragraph 1: Purpose of Writing (Opening Statement)
State directly and courteously why you are writing without rambling pleasantries: "I am writing to apply for admission into JSS 1 in your reputable institution for the upcoming academic session".
1.
Paragraph 2: Background and Core Justification
Provide concrete details, qualifications, or evidence supporting your application or petition: "My younger brother completed his primary education at Delight Nursery and Primary School, achieving distinction in all subjects".
1.
Paragraph 3: Additional Details or Addressing Requirements
Supply relevant dates, age verification, character references, or specific requests: "He will be ten years of age this September, and copies of his official birth certificate and testimonial are enclosed herewith".
1.
Paragraph 4: Conclusion and Polite Action Request
Conclude with an expression of gratitude and an anticipation of favorable administrative action: "I would be deeply grateful if his application receives your kind consideration. Thank you for your assistance".

Aspect 5: Literature-in-English — Features and Themes of Myths and Legends

Traditional oral literature uses powerful recurring motifs and thematic devices:

Myths attempt to explain the fundamental mysteries of the universe—how the earth was formed, why the sun rises, how mortality entered the world, and how divine beings interact with humanity.
Legends recount the historical deeds of human heroes and heroines, though embellished over centuries of oral retelling with folklore elements.

Major Features and Themes:

1.
Magic and Enchantment: Heroes frequently employ enchanted weapons, shape-shift into animals, or make themselves invisible to defeat formidable adversaries.
2.
The Supernatural: The active intervention of deities, ancestral spirits, river goddesses, ghosts, and mythical monsters (such as dragons or werewolves) that alter the course of human destiny.
3.
Superstition: Deep-seated cultural beliefs in omens, taboos, divinations, and curses that dictate human behavior and warn communities against defying the cosmic order.
4.
Heroic Quests and Moral Standards: Legends often feature a noble hero (such as Moremi of Ile-Ife or Sunjata of Mali) who sacrifices personal comfort to liberate an oppressed community, teaching modern audiences courage, selflessness, and communal loyalty.

Comparing Core Linguistic, Grammatical, and Literary Contrasts

Phonetic Voicing

Consonant /ʃ/ vs Consonant /ʒ/

Both are palato-alveolar fricatives, but /ʃ/ is voiceless without vocal cord vibration (*sure*, *mission*), whereas /ʒ/ is voiced with active vocal cord vibration (*leisure*, *vision*)[cite: 107].

Future Projection

Simple Future vs Future Perfect

Simple future predicts an event that will occur (*She will clean the room*), while future perfect indicates that an action will be completed prior to a specific future moment (*She will have cleaned the room before the guests arrive*)[cite: 107].

Letter Formats

Formal Letter vs Informal Letter

Formal letters require two addresses, a formal salutation (*Dear Sir*), a concise letter heading, and *Yours faithfully,* with a signature[cite: 107]; informal letters use one address, informal salutations (*Dear Segun*), no heading, and *Yours sincerely*[cite: 107].

Folklore Characterization

Mythic Deities vs Legendary Heroes

Characters in myths are largely immortal gods, goddesses, and cosmological spirits[cite: 107], while characters in legends are mortal human kings, queens, and warriors who performed extraordinary historical deeds[cite: 107].

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Pronouncing the voiced fricative /ʒ/ as the affricate /dʒ/ (e.g., saying *vi-jon* or *mea-jure* instead of /vɪʒn/ and /ˈmɛʒə/)[cite: 107].

Scientific Fact

The sound /ʒ/ is a smooth, continuous fricative glide without the plosive tongue blockage that characterizes the affricate /dʒ/[cite: 107].

Common Mistake

Writing conversational greetings like *"How are you and your family?"* in the opening paragraph of an official formal letter[cite: 107].

Scientific Fact

Official letters must open directly with a clear statement of purpose and maintain an objective, businesslike register throughout[cite: 107].

Common Mistake

Using the future perfect tense without a future time reference marker[cite: 107].

Scientific Fact

The future perfect tense (*will have finished*) requires a reference point introduced by prepositions like *by* or *before* (*by next week*, *before the guests arrive*)[cite: 107].

Common Mistake

Believing that myths are simply "childish lies" without cultural value[cite: 107].

Scientific Fact

Myths represent profound philosophical and religious attempts by traditional societies to explain natural phenomena, morality, and human origins[cite: 107].
