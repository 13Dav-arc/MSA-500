Week 02: Uses and Specifications of Drawing Instruments and Materials

Foundational curriculum module in Basic Technology covering Uses and Specifications of Drawing Instruments and Materials for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

When an engineer prepares a working plan for an engine component or a house foundation, every tool selected must match the exact physical specifications of the drawing sheet. Using the wrong grade of pencil can smudge delicate dimensions, while picking an incorrect scale rule can cause a builder to misjudge dimensions by several meters. Knowing the standard dimensions of drawing boards, the paper series from A0 to A4, the pencil hardness scale from 8B to 8H, and the exact roles of instruments like dividers and compasses transforms simple sketching into professional engineering drafting.

Key Terms

Drawing Board Specifications The standardized physical dimensions of the wooden platforms used for drafting, primarily categorized into Full Imperial ($812\text{ mm} \times 585\text{ mm}$) and Half Imperial ($585\text{ mm} \times 452\text{ mm}$)[cite: 2].
ISO Paper Series An international standard sizing system for paper where each size is half the area of the preceding size, maintaining a constant aspect ratio of $1:\sqrt{2}$ (ranging from A0 to A4)[cite: 2].
Pencil Grade The standardized classification of drafting pencils based on the proportion of graphite to clay in the core, ranging from soft black grades (8B) to hard grades (8H)[cite: 2].
Scale Ratio The mathematical relationship between the linear dimensions of an object represented on a drawing sheet and the actual dimensions of the physical object[cite: 2].
Measurement Transfer The mechanical process of taking an exact linear dimension from a metric rule and stepping it off onto a drawing surface using a pair of dividers[cite: 2].

Core Concept

Aspect 1: Specifications of Drawing Boards and Tee-Squares

The drawing board and tee-square work together as a synchronized system to provide true horizontal references across the drafting surface:

1.
Drawing Board Types and Dimensions:
The drawing board is a flat wooden platform made of seasoned, knot-free softwood that supports the drawing sheet without flexing.
Full Imperial Board: Measures $812\text{ mm} \times 585\text{ mm}$. It accommodates large-scale architectural floor plans and major engineering assemblies.
Half Imperial Board: Measures $585\text{ mm} \times 452\text{ mm}$. It is the standard portable size utilized in secondary school technical drawing laboratories for A3 and A4 paper sheets.
1.
The Working Edge and Tee-Square Integration:
The left-hand edge of the board is machined to form a working edge.
The wooden or plastic stock of the tee-square slides along this edge, maintaining the blade at an angle of $90^\circ$.
The blade spans the width of the board to draw horizontal parallel lines and provide a stable base for set-squares.

Aspect 2: Standard Drawing Paper Sizes (The 'A' Series)

Technical drawing paper sizes follow international standardization (ISO) based on an surface area ratio where folding a sheet in half produces the next smaller standard size:

A0: $841\text{ mm} \times 1189\text{ mm}$ (Surface area of approximately $1\text{ m}^2$, used for large municipal maps and major structural engineering designs).
A1: $594\text{ mm} \times 841\text{ mm}$ (Used for civil engineering layouts and building plans).
A2: $420\text{ mm} \times 594\text{ mm}$ (Used for medium-sized mechanical component drawings).
A3: $297\text{ mm} \times 420\text{ mm}$ (The primary standard size for school studio work and technical examinations).
A4: $210\text{ mm} \times 297\text{ mm}$ (Standard size for component details, workshop exercise sheets, and technical reports).

Aspect 3: Technical Pencil Grading Scales and Applications

Drafting pencils are manufactured with cores made of a mixture of graphite powder and clay binder. The hardness and darkness of the pencil mark depend strictly on this ratio:

1.
The Hard (H) Series (Hard & Light):
Contains a higher proportion of clay, producing fine, crisp, light-gray lines that do not smudge easily.
Range: H, 2H, 3H, 4H, 5H, 6H, 7H, up to 8H (with 8H being the hardest and faintest).
Applications: 2H and 3H are used for technical construction lines, projection lines, center lines, and geometric layout work.
1.
The Soft (B) Series (Black & Soft):
Contains a higher proportion of graphite, producing dark, thick, bold lines.
Range: B, 2B, 3B, 4B, 5B, 6B, 7B, up to 8B (with 8B being the softest, darkest, and most easily smudged).
Applications: Used for freehand sketching, shading, rendering, and art work.
1.
The Medium (HB & F) Grades:
Balanced cores providing moderate darkness and hardness.
Applications: HB is used for lettering, dimension numerals, and darkening final visible outlines.

Aspect 4: Angular, Linear, and Scaling Instruments

1.
Set-Squares:
Right-angled triangular instruments made of clear plastic.
$45^\circ$ Set-Square: Contains angles of $45^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $90^\circ$.
$30^\circ/60^\circ$ Set-Square: Contains angles of $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$.
Used sitting squarely on the tee-square blade to draw vertical lines ($90^\circ$) and diagonal lines at angles of $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $60^\circ$. Combining both set-squares allows draftsmen to draw lines at $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$.
1.
Protractors:
Graduated in degrees from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$ (semi-circular) or $0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$ (circular).
Used for measuring and laying off angles that cannot be constructed directly with set-squares.
1.
Metric Rules and Scale Rules:
Metric Rule: Used for direct measurement of linear distances in millimeters and centimeters.
Scale Rule: Triangular or flat rule carrying graduated scale ratios ($1:1$, $1:2$, $1:5$, $1:10$, $1:50$, $1:100$).
Function: Used to produce reduced drawings of large objects (like houses or furniture) or enlarged drawings of small components (like watch gears) directly on paper without manual arithmetic calculations.

Aspect 5: Dividers, Compasses, Fasteners, and Erasers

1.
Compasses vs. Dividers:
Compass: Features one sharp steel point and one pencil leg; used specifically for drawing circles and arcs.
Divider: Features two sharp steel needle points; used for transferring dimensions from the metric rule to the drawing and stepping off equal lengths.
1.
Fasteners (Clips and Adhesive Tape):
Drawing paper must be held securely on the board to prevent displacement during drafting.
Sheets are held using drafting clips or pieces of masking tape applied across the four corners.
Thumb pins should never be used because they leave permanent holes in the board surface.
1.
Erasers:
A drafting eraser must be soft and pliable.
It must erase cleanly without smudging or tearing the delicate surface fibers of the drawing paper.

Comparing Core Drawing Tools and Standard Specifications

Board Format

Full Imperial vs Half Imperial Board

Full Imperial measures $812\text{ mm} \times 585\text{ mm}$ for large engineering plans[cite: 2], while Half Imperial measures $585\text{ mm} \times 452\text{ mm}$ for classroom A3/A4 work[cite: 2].

Paper Dimension

A3 Paper vs A4 Paper

A3 paper measures $297\text{ mm} \times 420\text{ mm}$ (twice the area of A4)[cite: 2], while A4 paper measures $210\text{ mm} \times 297\text{ mm}$[cite: 2].

Pencil Core

Hard Grade (2H) vs Soft Grade (2B)

2H has more clay binder, producing faint, clean lines for construction work[cite: 2], while 2B has more graphite, producing soft, dark lines for art and sketching[cite: 2].

Instrument Function

Divider vs Scale Rule

A divider physically transfers measurements from a ruler to paper using steel points[cite: 2], while a scale rule provides scaled ratio markings to draw objects larger or smaller than life-size[cite: 2].

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Using a 2B or soft pencil for drawing faint construction lines[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Soft pencils deposit loose graphite that smudges across the sheet; always use a 2H or 3H hard pencil for construction lines[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Using a scale rule as a straightedge to guide pencil lines[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Pencil graphite and pressure wear away the beveled measuring edges of the scale rule; use a metric rule, tee-square, or set-square as a drawing guide[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Using the needle point of a compass or divider to punch holes through the drawing board[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Compasses and dividers are precision instruments; piercing the board dulls the steel points and damages the timber[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Fastening paper to the drawing board using drawing pins[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Drawing pins make permanent punctures in the wood that derail pencil lines; use masking tape or metal clips[cite: 2].
