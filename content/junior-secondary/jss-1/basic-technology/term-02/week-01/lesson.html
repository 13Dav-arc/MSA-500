Week 01: Drawing Instruments and Materials

Foundational curriculum module in Basic Technology covering Drawing Instruments and Materials for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

When an architect in Lagos designs a bridge, a builder in Kaduna can construct it to the exact millimeter without speaking a single spoken word to the designer. This is possible because technical drawing serves as a universal graphical language understood by engineers, builders, and technicians across the globe. Just as a master surgeon relies on sterilized scalpels and forceps, a draftsman relies on precision tools—such as the tee-square, compasses, set-squares, and graded pencils—to turn abstract technical ideas into exact physical realities.

Key Terms

Technical Drawing A universal graphical language of communication used among technical personnel that conveys shapes, dimensions, and specifications using standardized lines, curves, symbols, and conventions[cite: 2].
Drawing Board A smooth, flat wooden platform upon which drawing paper is fastened and aligned for accurate drafting work[cite: 2].
Tee-Square A T-shaped drawing instrument consisting of a stock and a blade, used primarily for drawing horizontal parallel lines and guiding set-squares[cite: 2].
Set-Square A triangular drafting instrument made of transparent plastic, available in $30^\circ/60^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ angles, used to draw vertical and inclined lines[cite: 2].
Compass An instrument with two legs joined by a pivot (one fitted with a pointed steel needle and the other holding a pencil lead), used for drawing circles and arcs[cite: 2].

Core Concept

Aspect 1: Definition and Purpose of Technical Drawing

Technical drawing is the universal graphical language used for communication among technical people, such as engineers, architects, technicians, and craftsmen.

Unlike artistic drawing, which relies on personal expression, feelings, and aesthetic perception, technical drawing demands mathematical accuracy, dimensional fidelity, and adherence to standardized engineering conventions.
It communicates the exact shape, size, surface texture, and assembly details of physical objects using lines, curves, symbols, and standard notations.
A reliable and accurate drawing can only be achieved through continuous practice with the correct selection and application of drawing instruments and materials.

Aspect 2: Board Platform and Alignment Tools

1.
The Drawing Board:
Provides a flat, rigid wooden surface upon which drawing paper is placed and secured before drafting begins.
Constructed from well-seasoned timber to avoid warping and ensure a perfectly straight working edge on the left-hand side.
Standard sizes include:
Full Imperial Size: $812\text{ mm} \times 585\text{ mm}$
Half Imperial Size: $585\text{ mm} \times 452\text{ mm}$
1.
The Tee-Square:
Consists of two main parts joined rigidly at a right angle: the stock (which slides along the left edge of the drawing board) and the blade (which lies across the paper).
Primarily used for drawing horizontal parallel lines in conjunction with the drawing board working edge.
Functions as a moving base upon which set-squares are supported to draw vertical and inclined lines.
Essential Technical Drawing Setup: Drawing Board, Tee-Square, and Set-Squares

Aspect 3: Line Generation, Angular, and Scaling Tools

1.
Set-Squares:
Triangular drafting instruments made of clear plastic.
Come in two standard angular varieties:
The $45^\circ$ Set-Square: Contains angles of $45^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $90^\circ$.
The $30^\circ/60^\circ$ Set-Square: Contains angles of $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$.
Used sitting squarely on the blade of the tee-square to draw vertical lines ($90^\circ$) and inclined/diagonal lines at specified angles ($30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$).
1.
Protractor:
A semi-circular ($180^\circ$) or circular ($360^\circ$) transparent instrument graduated in degrees.
Used for measuring, setting, and marking out arbitrary angles on drawing paper.
1.
Metric Rule and Scale Rule:
Metric Rule: Used for direct linear measurements in millimeters and centimeters.
Scale Rule: Contains graduated ratios ($1:1$, $1:2$, $1:5$, $1:100$, etc.) used to produce reduced or enlarged representations of physical objects on paper without manual calculation.

Aspect 4: Precision Transfer and Curved Drafting Tools

1.
Compasses:
An instrument fitted with one pointed metal pivot leg and one pencil-lead holder.
Used for drawing circles, circular arcs, and stepped curve radii.
1.
Dividers:
Resembles a compass but possesses two sharp, pointed steel legs.
Used for transferring measurements accurately from the metric rule to the drawing paper, or for stepping off equal distances along a straight or curved line.
1.
French Curves and Flexible Curves:
Specially contoured templates made of smooth plastic or bendable lead-core rubber.
Used to draw smooth, irregular curved lines that cannot be formed with a standard circular compass.

Aspect 5: Media, Fasteners, and Consumables

1.
Drawing Paper:
The paper substrate on which technical representations are drafted. Standard international ISO sizes include:
$\text{A0}$: $841\text{ mm} \times 1189\text{ mm}$
$\text{A1}$: $594\text{ mm} \times 841\text{ mm}$
$\text{A2}$: $420\text{ mm} \times 594\text{ mm}$
$\text{A3}$: $297\text{ mm} \times 420\text{ mm}$
$\text{A4}$: $210\text{ mm} \times 297\text{ mm}$
1.
Paper Fasteners:
Adhesive Drafting / Masking Tape: Applied across the four corners to secure the sheet without damaging board timber.
Metal Drawing Clips: Clamped along paper borders to hold sheets firmly in position without adhesive residue.
1.
Pencils, Erasers, and Sharpeners:
Drawing Pencils: Classified into hard grades (H to 8H) for faint technical construction lines, medium grades (HB, F) for outlines and lettering, and soft grades (B to 8B) for artistic work.
Erasers: Soft rubber blocks designed to erase pencil lines cleanly without picking, tearing, or smudging the paper surface.
Sharpeners or Razor Blades: Used to maintain clean conical points or chisel edges on pencil cores.

Comparing Core Drawing Instruments and Drafting Tools

Functional Form

Pair of Compasses vs Pair of Dividers

Compasses have one steel point and one pencil lead to construct circles and arcs[cite: 2], while dividers have two steel points to transfer exact dimensions from rules to paper[cite: 2].

Angular Geometry

$45^\circ$ Set-Square vs $30^\circ/60^\circ$ Set-Square

The $45^\circ$ set-square features angles of $45^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $90^\circ$[cite: 2], whereas the $30^\circ/60^\circ$ set-square features angles of $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$[cite: 2].

Measurement Mode

Metric Rule vs Scale Rule

A metric rule measures absolute full-size physical distances in millimeters[cite: 2], while a scale rule produces proportionally enlarged or reduced drawings according to set mathematical ratios[cite: 2].

Curvature Drafting

Compass vs French Curve

A compass produces regular circular curves with a constant radius[cite: 2], while a French curve draws irregular, non-circular smooth curves through varied guide points[cite: 2].

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Using thumb pins to fasten drawing paper onto the drawing board[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Thumb pins puncture and ruin the smooth surface of the wooden board; always use masking tape or metal drawing clips[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Using a tee-square to draw vertical lines on the board[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

The tee-square is designed strictly for horizontal lines; vertical lines must be drawn using a set-square sitting on the tee-square blade[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Using soft pencils (such as 2B or 4B) for technical construction lines[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Soft pencils leave dark, smudgy, thick graphite lines; use hard pencils like 2H for faint, precise construction lines[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Using a scale rule as a straightedge for drawing heavy pencil lines[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Drawing directly against the beveled edges of a scale rule damages its graduated calibration marks; use a standard straightedge or set-square instead[cite: 2].
