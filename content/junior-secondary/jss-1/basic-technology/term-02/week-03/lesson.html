Week 03: Handling and Care of Drawing Instruments and Materials

Foundational curriculum module in Basic Technology covering Handling and Care of Drawing Instruments and Materials for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

A precision drafting tool is only as reliable as the care given to it by the draftsman. A single nick on the straightedge of a tee-square can distort every horizontal line across an engineering plan, while a bent divider point or a scarred drawing board surface can ruin delicate paper and destroy millimeter accuracy. Drawing instruments are precision investments designed to last for decades if handled with disciplined craftsmanship. Learning how to clean, handle, protect, and store your drawing boards, acrylic curves, compasses, and blades ensures that every line you draw remains clean, precise, and professional.

Key Terms

Instrument Maintenance The systematic care, cleaning, and proper storage of drafting equipment to preserve accuracy and prolong service life[cite: 2].
Working Edge The perfectly straight, true edge along the left side of a drawing board or along the blade of a tee-square that guides straight lines[cite: 2].
Acrylic Instruments Drafting tools manufactured from transparent plastic or acrylic resin (such as set-squares, protractors, and French curves) that are sensitive to heat, scratching, and impact[cite: 2].
Alignment True The state of an instrument remaining free from warping, bending, or angular distortion so that lines drawn with it remain mathematically accurate[cite: 2].
Fitted Case A dedicated protective box or pouch lined with compartments designed to secure compasses, dividers, and spare parts away from dust and mechanical damage[cite: 2].

Core Concept

Aspect 1: Importance of Caring for Drawing Instruments and Materials

Technical drawing instruments are precision-engineered devices manufactured to strict dimensional tolerances.

Prolonging Service Life: Quality drawing equipment is costly; diligent care prevents premature damage and extends the useful life of the tools over many school years.
Preserving Accuracy: Even microscopic dents on a tee-square blade or a bent compass pivot will introduce angular and linear errors into technical drawings.
Cleanliness and Presentation: Dusty, greasy, or graphite-coated instruments smudge drawing paper, resulting in messy, unreadable drawings.

Aspect 2: Handling and Care of the Drawing Board

The drawing board provides the flat foundation for all manual drafting:

1.
Never Use Thumb Pins: Do not use drawing pins or thumbtacks to fasten paper to the wooden surface. Pins puncture permanent holes into the wood, creating an uneven surface that snags pencil leads during future work. Always use adhesive masking tape or metal drawing clips.
2.
Never Cut on the Board Surface: Do not use razor blades, penknives, or craft cutters to slice paper directly on the face of the board. Blade cuts create deep grooves that ruin the smooth drawing surface.
3.
Surface Protection: Keep the board surface covered with clean cardboard or thick wrapping paper when not in use to protect it from dust, ink spills, and scratches.
4.
Safe Storage: Store drawing boards flat on shelves or vertically in dedicated slotted storage racks inside an equipment cupboard when practical sessions conclude.
Preventive Maintenance and Safe Handling of Drawing Equipment

Aspect 3: Handling and Care of the Tee-Square

The tee-square maintains the primary horizontal axis across the drawing sheet:

1.
Never Misuse as a Walking Stick or Cane: The tee-square is a delicate instrument, not a plaything or discipline cane. Dropping it, striking desks with it, or leaning on it as a walking stick loosens the joint between the stock and blade, ruining its $90^\circ$ alignment.
2.
Avoid Cutting Along the Blade Edge: Never draw a utility knife or razor blade along the transparent working edge of the tee-square blade. Slicing against the edge causes nicks and irregularities, making it impossible to draw true straight lines.
3.
Hanging for Storage: Always hang the tee-square vertically by the hole punched at the end of its blade when it is not in use. Leaving it lying flat on crowded desks or leaning against walls causes the blade to warp over time.
4.
Routine Cleaning: Regularly wipe down the blade with a clean, lint-free cloth to remove graphite residue and dust before placing it across fresh paper.

Aspect 4: Handling and Care of Set-Squares, Protractors, Scale Rules, and French Curves

These transparent acrylic instruments require gentle handling to preserve their clear edges and graduated scales:

1.
Protect Edges from Sharp Blades: Never run a craft knife or razor blade along the plastic edges of set-squares, scale rules, or French curves. Sharp blades shave away plastic, destroying straightness and bevel angles.
2.
Keep Away from Heat and Open Flames: Acrylic plastics warp, soften, and blister when exposed to high temperatures, sunlight on hot windowsills, or open flames. Warped instruments cannot lie flat on the drawing sheet.
3.
Safe Storage: Return these instruments to their protective plastic envelopes or folders immediately after use to prevent surface scratching and accidental breakage.
4.
Cleaning: Wash acrylic tools with lukewarm water and mild soap when they become soiled with graphite; avoid using harsh solvents like thinner, petrol, or acetone, which dissolve plastic.

Aspect 5: Handling and Care of Compasses, Dividers, and Storage Systems

Compasses and dividers possess hardened steel points that demand safety precautions and regular maintenance:

1.
Dedicated Container Storage: When not in use, always store compasses and dividers inside their velvet-lined cases or fitted containers. This shields the needle points from becoming blunted and prevents accidental injury.
2.
Never Misuse as Paper Holders or Punches: Never use compasses or dividers as clamps, paper holders, or tools to punch holes through cardboard, desks, or walls. Forcing the legs apart strains the central friction joint, while punching blunts or bends the needle points.
3.
Joint Tension Maintenance: Periodically check and gently tighten the small pivot screws at the compass joint using a watchmaker's screwdriver so the legs do not slip open while drawing circles.
4.
Classroom Cupboard Storage: The drawing laboratory should maintain a dedicated, lockable wooden or metal cupboard where drawing boards, tee-squares, and boxed instrument sets are systematically arranged by class and set number.

Comparing Maintenance Rules Across Different Drafting Tools

Surface Protection

Drawing Board Care vs Tee-Square Care

Drawing boards must be protected from pin punctures and blade gouges by using tape and paper covers[cite: 2], whereas tee-squares must be protected from joint dislocation and warping by hanging them vertically by the blade hole[cite: 2].

Edge Preservation

Acrylic Tools vs Steel Pivot Tools

Acrylic instruments (set-squares, curves) must be kept away from heat and cutting blades to prevent melting and chipping[cite: 2], while steel pivot tools (compasses, dividers) must be kept in fitted cases to protect their needle points from blunting[cite: 2].

Fastener Safety

Masking Tape vs Thumb Pins

Drafting tape holds paper firmly and peels off cleanly without leaving surface scars[cite: 2], whereas thumb pins puncture holes that permanently destroy the board's smoothness[cite: 2].

Storage Method

Fitted Cases vs Laboratory Cupboards

Small precision tools (compasses, dividers) are housed in individual fitted cases[cite: 2], while bulky items (drawing boards, tee-squares) are organized on dedicated shelves within laboratory cupboards[cite: 2].

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Using a razor blade or penknife to trim drawing paper directly on the drawing board[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Cutting on the board leaves deep score lines in the wood that derail pencil tips during future drawings; always trim paper on a dedicated cutting mat[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Using the tee-square as a walking stick, play sword, or cane[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Leaning on or hitting objects with a tee-square breaks the joint between the stock and blade, destroying its $90^\circ$ perpendicularity[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Using thumb pins to secure paper to preserve tape[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Pins leave permanent holes in the wooden board that catch pencil tips; use drafting tape or metal clips instead[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Storing compasses and dividers loose inside bags or pencil cases[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Loose storage allows needle points to blunt, pierce bags, or bend; always store them secured in their fitted cases[cite: 2].
