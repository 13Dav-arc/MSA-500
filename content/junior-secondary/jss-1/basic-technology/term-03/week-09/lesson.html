Week 09: Concept, Need, and Types of Maintenance

Foundational curriculum module in Basic Technology covering Concept, Need, and Types of Maintenance for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine a motorist setting out on a five-hundred-kilometer highway journey without checking the engine oil, radiator water, or brake fluid. A breakdown in the middle of nowhere is practically guaranteed, bringing costly repairs, ruined schedules, and severe safety hazards. In engineering and technology, whether dealing with a simple hand plane, a motorized workshop lathe, or an automobile, physical machines do not stay in working condition on their own. Maintenance is the vital pulse of engineering—it ensures that tools operate smoothly, catastrophic failures are prevented, and valuable machinery lasts for decades.

Key Terms

Maintenance The systematic work done on any engineering equipment, machine, or tool to keep it in good operating condition[cite: 2].
Preventive Maintenance Scheduled servicing and care carried out on equipment before breakdown occurs to keep it running smoothly and avoid sudden failure[cite: 2].
Predictive Maintenance Maintenance carried out using computers, sensors, and diagnostic devices to monitor equipment performance and predict impending breakdowns before they happen[cite: 2].
Corrective Maintenance Unscheduled emergency actions taken to repair, overhaul, or restore broken-down equipment back to a functional state after a failure has occurred[cite: 2].
Breakdown A complete stoppage or functional failure of a machine or tool that halts operations and requires technical intervention[cite: 2].

Core Concept

Aspect 1: The Concept of Maintenance

Maintenance is defined as the work done on any engineering equipment to keep it in good operating condition.

Everything used in our schools, workshops, factories, and homes must be properly cared for so that it can serve us reliably and efficiently.
Everyday maintenance is practiced all around us; for example, a responsible driver routinely checks the oil level, radiator water, battery terminals, tyres, and brakes before hitting the road to verify that there are no underlying mechanical problems.
Neglecting maintenance allows minor wear to develop into major, dangerous component failures.

Aspect 2: The Need for Maintenance

Systematic maintenance is essential across all technical fields for several reasons:

1.
Ensuring Proper Function: To make the equipment function properly and achieve its intended design output.
2.
Preventing Failures: To avoid preventable breakdowns that disrupt operations.
3.
Safety and Accident Prevention: To reduce the chances of workshop accidents and ensure the safe use of machinery by technicians.
4.
Eliminating Inconvenience: To avoid the frustration and operational disruptions caused by unexpected equipment failure.
5.
Extending Service Life: To make tools, machines, and infrastructure last significantly longer.
6.
Cost and Time Savings: To reduce downtime, minimize productivity loss, and avoid the heavy financial expense of purchasing replacement machinery.

Aspect 3: Preventive Maintenance

Preventive maintenance is the type of maintenance carried out on equipment before breakdown occurs. It is proactive, planned, and performed at regular calendar or operational intervals:

Regular Servicing: Routine engine servicing, changing oil filters, and tuning motor vehicles.
Lubrication: Regular oiling or lubricating of bearings, gears, slideways, and moving linkages to minimize friction and wear.
Corrosion Control: Regular painting or protective coating of corrosive metals exposed to moisture and air to prevent rust formation.
Cleaning and Hardware Inspection: Regular cleaning of equipment, routine checks on lubricant levels, safety guards, signal indicators, and tightening loose nuts, bolts, and studs.
Wear and Tension Checks: Routine inspections to measure levels of wear, verify shaft alignment, and check the tension of drive belts and transmission chains.
The Three Core Branches of Engineering Maintenance

Aspect 4: Predictive Maintenance

Predictive maintenance is an advanced maintenance strategy carried out using computers, sensors, and diagnostic monitoring devices to predict impending breakdowns before they occur:

Early Warning Analysis: Specialized sensors continuously track variables like vibration frequencies, heat build-up, and acoustic signatures in operating machinery.
Targeted Intervention: Once predictions of impending failure are detected, necessary remedies and repairs are designed and applied immediately, preventing actual breakdowns.
Minimizing Routine Interventions: Predictive maintenance optimizes workshop resources by reducing the need for arbitrary, excessive preventive teardowns and eliminating unexpected corrective work.
System Checks: To maintain effectiveness, technicians must conduct routine checks on the diagnostic facilities and sensors themselves to ensure they remain functional and properly calibrated.

Aspect 5: Corrective Maintenance

Corrective maintenance involves actions taken to correct or restore broken-down equipment to a functional state after a failure has taken place:

Reactive Nature: It is inherently reactive because work begins only after a machine has failed, stalled, or suffered mechanical damage during operation.
Scope of Work: The equipment may need a total overhaul, structural welding, or the complete replacement of damaged parts.
High Costs and Disruptions: Corrective maintenance is the least desirable maintenance approach because it requires substantial financial expenditure, causes prolonged downtime, and can damage surrounding components during catastrophic failures.

| Maintenance Category | Operational Timing | Primary Activities Involved | Relative Cost & Impact |

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Predictive Maintenance | Ongoing / Sensor-driven | Computer monitoring, vibration analysis, sensor tracking, forecasting impending faults | Highly efficient; reduces overall maintenance volume and eliminates sudden breakdowns |

| Preventive Maintenance | Scheduled / Pre-breakdown | Regular cleaning, oiling moving parts, painting metals, tightening bolts, checking belt tension | Cost-effective; prevents avoidable stoppages and extends service life |

| Corrective Maintenance | Reactive / Post-breakdown | Emergency repairs, replacing sheared components, total machine overhauls | Expensive; causes lost production time and unexpected repair costs |

Comparing the Three Branches of Engineering Maintenance

Timing of Action

Preventive vs Corrective Maintenance

Preventive maintenance is carried out proactively before breakdown occurs[cite: 2], whereas corrective maintenance is performed reactively after a machine has failed[cite: 2].

Technology Level

Preventive vs Predictive Maintenance

Preventive maintenance follows fixed schedules for cleaning, lubrication, and inspection[cite: 2], while predictive maintenance uses computers and sensors to forecast failures based on real-time data[cite: 2].

Financial & Time Impact

Planned Maintenance vs Emergency Repair

Planned preventive and predictive maintenance keep equipment operating smoothly at minimal cost[cite: 2], while emergency corrective maintenance involves expensive replacement parts and extended downtime[cite: 2].

Everyday Equivalents

Checking Oil vs Engine Replacement

Checking car oil and radiator coolant before driving represents preventive maintenance[cite: 2], whereas rebuilding a seized engine block after overheating represents corrective maintenance[cite: 2].

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Believing that maintenance is only necessary after a machine stops working[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Waiting for failure leads to expensive corrective overhauls; maintenance should be performed proactively to prevent breakdowns[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Confusing predictive maintenance with routine preventive maintenance[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Preventive maintenance follows fixed routine schedules (like oiling weekly)[cite: 2], while predictive maintenance uses computers and sensors to monitor machine condition and forecast impending faults[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Assuming that painting metal surfaces is purely for decoration[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Painting is an important preventive maintenance measure that seals corrosive metals from moisture and air to prevent rust[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Neglecting to verify diagnostic sensors used in predictive maintenance[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Technicians must perform routine checks on the diagnostic sensors and monitoring systems to ensure they remain calibrated and operational[cite: 2].
