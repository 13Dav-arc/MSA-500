Week 08: Algebraic Processes (II) — Simplification of Algebraic Expressions

Foundational curriculum module in Mathematics covering Algebraic Processes (II) — Simplification of Algebraic Expressions for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine going to the market to buy 5 baskets of tomatoes, 3 bunches of bananas, and 7 pineapples, and your friend buys another 2 baskets of tomatoes and 4 bunches of bananas. You would never mix the pineapples with the tomatoes because they are completely different items! In mathematics, this principle is called collecting like terms: grouping identical variables together so we can add, subtract, multiply, and expand expressions cleanly and without confusion.

Key Terms

Algebraic Expression A mathematical combination of constants, numbers, and variables connected by fundamental operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division[cite: 2].
Term A single number, variable, or number and variable multiplied together, separated from other parts of an algebraic expression by plus or minus signs[cite: 2].
Coefficient The numerical factor multiplying a variable in an algebraic term, such as the 5 in $5b$ or the 7 in $7d$[cite: 2].
Like Terms Terms that have the exact same variable letters raised to the exact same powers, which can be combined by adding or subtracting their coefficients[cite: 2].
Expansion of Brackets Multiplying every term inside a bracket by the number or variable outside the bracket to clear the grouping[cite: 2].

Core Concept

What is an Algebraic Expression?

An algebraic expression connects constants and variables using fundamental arithmetic operations ($+, -, \times, \div$). In algebra, letters of the alphabet represent numbers or physical objects.

For example, the expression $5b + 3c + 7d$ can represent 5 babies, 3 cats, and 7 dogs:

The quantities $5b$, $3c$, and $7d$ are called the terms of the expression. This expression contains 3 terms.
The numbers 5, 3, and 7 are called the coefficients of $b$, $c$, and $d$ respectively.
When a term has no visible number in front (like $a$ or $y$), its coefficient is understood to be 1 (e.g., $a = 1a$).

Addition and Subtraction of Similar (Like) Terms

When adding or subtracting like terms, we simply add or subtract their numerical coefficients while leaving the variable letter unchanged:

$4a + 5a + a = (4 + 5 + 1)a = 10a$
$9n - 5n - 3n = (9 - 5 - 3)n = 1n = n$

When an expression contains both positive and negative like terms, group positive terms together first before subtracting:

$11q + 5q - 2q + q = (11q + 5q + q) - 2q = 17q - 2q = 15q$
$20p - 9p - p + 2p = (20p + 2p) - (9p + p) = 22p - 10p = 12p$
Grouping Like Terms and Expanding Algebraic Brackets

Multiplication and Division of Similar Terms

Multiplication: When multiplying terms, multiply the numerical coefficients together and write the variable letters together in alphabetical order:
$3a \times b = 3ab$
$5y \times 2ab = (5 \times 2) \times (a \times b \times y) = 10aby$
$6p \times 3ap = (6 \times 3) \times a \times (p \times p) = 18ap^2$
Division: Express the numerator and denominator in factored form and cancel out identical common factors:
$6y^2 \div 2y = \frac{6 \times y \times y}{2 \times y} = 3y$
$36q \div 9q = \frac{36 \times q}{9 \times q} = 4$

Collection and Simplification of Like and Unlike Terms

Unlike terms cannot be added or subtracted together into a single term. To simplify an expression with mixed variables, collect like terms together with their preceding signs:

In $4a + 5b - a - 4b$:

$$\begin{aligned}

&= 4a - a + 5b - 4b \\

&= 3a + b

\end{aligned}$$

In $18a - 3a - 6a + 10b$:

$$\begin{aligned}

&= (18 - 3 - 6)a + 10b \\

&= 9a + 10b

\end{aligned}$$

The Use of Brackets

When brackets are present in an algebraic expression, the operations inside the brackets are expanded first by distributing the outside multiplier to every term inside:

$4(a + 3b) - 12b = 4a + 12b - 12b = 4a + 0 = 4a$
$x(x + 2) + x^2 + 3x = x^2 + 2x + x^2 + 3x = (x^2 + x^2) + (2x + 3x) = 2x^2 + 5x$

Comparing Algebraic Structures and Operations

Variable Nature

Like Terms vs Unlike Terms

Like terms share identical variable letters ($4a, 6a, -3a$) and can be combined into one term[cite: 2], whereas unlike terms have different letters ($4a, 5b$) and cannot be merged by addition[cite: 2].

Arithmetic Rules

Addition vs Multiplication of Variables

Adding like variables increases the coefficient ($a + a = 2a$)[cite: 2], whereas multiplying identical variables raises the power ($a \times a = a^2$)[cite: 2].

Operational Flow

Expanding Brackets vs Collecting Like Terms

Expanding brackets distributes an outside multiplier across grouped terms[cite: 2], while collecting like terms rearranges and combines matching letters across the entire expression[cite: 2].

Component Roles

Coefficient vs Constant

A coefficient is a number attached to and multiplying a variable (such as 6 in $6p$)[cite: 2], whereas a constant is an independent number standing without any variable (such as 7).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Combining unlike terms during addition (e.g., writing $3a + 2b = 5ab$).

Scientific Fact

Terms with different letters cannot be combined by addition or subtraction; $3a + 2b$ remains $3a + 2b$[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Forgetting that a variable written without a visible number has a coefficient of 1.

Scientific Fact

In an expression like $4a + a$, the term $a$ means $1a$, so the sum is $4a + 1a = 5a$, not $4a$[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Multiplying only the first term inside a bracket (e.g., writing $4(a + 3b) = 4a + 3b$).

Scientific Fact

The multiplier outside must multiply every single term inside the bracket: $4 \times a + 4 \times 3b = 4a + 12b$[cite: 2].
