Core Concept

1. Estimation and Unit Conversions (Weeks 01 & 02)

Linear Distance: Metric units increase by multiples of ten with the metre as the base unit ($10\text{ mm} = 1\text{ cm}$, $100\text{ cm} = 1\text{ m}$, $1000\text{ m} = 1\text{ km}$).
Capacity & Volume: Measured in litres and cubic units ($1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ dm}^3 = 1\text{ litre}$, $1000\text{ litres} = 1\text{ m}^3$).
Mass: Base unit is the kilogram ($1000\text{ mg} = 1\text{ g}$, $1000\text{ g} = 1\text{ kg}$, $1000\text{ kg} = 1\text{ tonne}$, $200\text{ mg} = 1\text{ carat}$).
Time: Follows the sexagesimal system (base 60) inherited from ancient Babylon ($60\text{ s} = 1\text{ min}$, $60\text{ min} = 1\text{ h}$, $24\text{ h} = 1\text{ day}$).

2. Approximation and Rounding Rules (Weeks 02 & 03)

Rounding Rule: Check the first digit to be ignored. If it is 5 or more, round up by adding 1 to the retained digit; if less than 5, round down (leave unchanged) and replace discarded whole-number places with zeros.
Significant Figures: Counted from the first non-zero digit on the left (e.g., $9,562,774$ to $2\text{ s.f.}$ is $9,600,000$; $768.45$ to $2\text{ s.f.}$ is $770$).
Everyday Applications: Rounding numbers to the nearest 10, 100, or 1000 simplifies market shopping, library shelf planning, and government budgeting.

3. Numbers in Base 2 — Addition, Subtraction, and Multiplication (Weeks 04 & 06)

Binary Addition: $0+0=0$, $1+0=1$, $0+1=1$, and $1+1=10_2$ (write down $0$, carry $1$).
Binary Subtraction: $0-0=0$, $1-0=1$, $1-1=0$, and $10_2-1=1$ (borrowing $1$ from the left column brings a value of $2_{10} = 10_2$ into the current column).
Binary Multiplication: $0 \times 0 = 0$, $1 \times 0 = 0$, $0 \times 1 = 0$, and $1 \times 1 = 1$, followed by long multiplication and binary addition.

4. Algebraic Processes and Simple Equations (Weeks 07 & 08)

Variables & Open Sentences: Letters represent unknown numbers. An open sentence (e.g., $x + 7 = 12$) becomes true when the correct value is substituted ($x = 5$).
Simplification: Combine like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients while keeping unlike terms separate ($4a + 5a + a = 10a$; $18a - 3a - 6a + 10b = 9a + 10b$).
Brackets & Division: Expand brackets by distributing the factor outside to all terms inside ($4(a + 3b) - 12b = 4a$); divide terms by simplifying coefficients and canceling matching variables ($36q \div 9q = 4$).
Simple Linear Equations: Maintain balance by performing the same operation on both sides of the equality sign ($x + 8 = 12 \implies x = 4$; $72 = 9x \implies x = 8$; $\frac{x}{2} = 5 \implies x = 10$).

Comparing Core Mathematical Domains of Second Term

Numerical Bases

Metric (Base 10) vs Time (Base 60) vs Binary (Base 2)

Metric length and mass progress by tens[cite: 2], time units follow sexagesimal base 60 multiples[cite: 2], and computer binary logic operates on powers of two using only 0 and 1[cite: 2].

Algebraic Grouping

Like Terms vs Unlike Terms

Like terms share identical variable letters and can be combined into a single term ($4a + 5a = 9a$)[cite: 2], whereas unlike terms have different letters and cannot be merged by addition ($4a + 5b$)[cite: 2].

Relational Statements

Open Sentences vs Simple Equations

An open sentence contains an unknown variable whose truth depends on substitution[cite: 2], while a simple equation uses an equality sign to balance two expressions that can be solved directly[cite: 2].

Place Value Retention

Whole Number Rounding vs Decimal Truncation

Whole number approximation replaces dropped places with zeros to preserve place magnitude[cite: 2], while decimal rounding drops trailing fractional places past the target precision[cite: 2].

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Combining unlike terms into a single product during addition (e.g., writing $3a + 2b = 5ab$)[cite: 2].

Scientific Fact

Unlike terms cannot be merged by addition or subtraction; $3a + 2b$ remains $3a + 2b$[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Treating borrowed digits in binary subtraction as decimal ten.

Scientific Fact

Borrowing $1$ from a higher binary column brings a value of $2_{10}$ ($10_2$), so $10_2 - 1 = 1$[cite: 2].

Common Mistake

Forgetting to maintain balance on both sides when solving simple equations.

Scientific Fact

Whatever operation is performed on the left-hand side (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) must be applied identically to the right-hand side[cite: 2].
