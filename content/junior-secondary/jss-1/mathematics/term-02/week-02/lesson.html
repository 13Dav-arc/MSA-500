Foundational curriculum module in Mathematics covering Approximation (I) — Rounding Off, Operations, and Significant Figures for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine walking into a supermarket to buy snacks with a ₦2,000 note in your pocket, picking up items priced at ₦495, ₦310, and ₦198. You do not need a calculator to know whether your money is enough; in a split second, your brain rounds the prices to ₦500, ₦300, and ₦200 to get a quick estimate of ₦1,000! That quick, sensible mathematical shortcut is called approximation, and it prevents calculation errors in both school exams and everyday trade.

Key Terms

Approximation Replacing a number with another number of nearly equal value that is simpler, shorter, or easier to calculate with[cite: 3].
Rounding Off The process of dropping unnecessary digits in a calculation result to express it to a specified number of significant figures or decimal places[cite: 3].
Rounding Up Increasing the last retained digit by 1 when the first digit to be ignored is 5 or greater[cite: 3].
Rounding Down Leaving the last retained digit unchanged when the first digit to be ignored is less than 5[cite: 3].
Significant Figures (s.f.) The meaningful digits in a number that carry precision, counted from the first non-zero digit on the left[cite: 3].

Core Concept

Rules for Rounding Off Numbers

When performing mathematical calculations, the result often produces more digits than necessary or practical to use. To round off a number:

1.
Locate the last digit that is to be kept.
2.
Look at the immediate next digit to its right (the first digit to be ignored):
Rounding Up: If the digit to be ignored is 5 or more ($5, 6, 7, 8, 9$), increase the last kept digit by $1$.
Rounding Down: If the digit to be ignored is less than 5 ($0, 1, 2, 3, 4$), leave the last kept digit unchanged.
1.
For whole numbers, replace all the dropped digit places with zeros so that the place value remains intact. For decimals, simply truncate the dropped places after rounding.

Significant Figures (s.f.)

Significant figures are counted starting from the first non-zero digit on the left:

In $9,562,774$, rounding to $2\text{ s.f.}$ gives $9,600,000$ (since the 3rd digit is 6, which is $\ge 5$, so 5 rounds up to 6).
In $768.45$, rounding to $2\text{ s.f.}$ gives $770$.
In $29,042$, rounding to $2\text{ s.f.}$ gives $29,000$.
Rules of Rounding Up and Rounding Down

Approximating Results of Addition and Subtraction

Before performing exact calculations, rounding each term to the nearest ten, hundred, or significant figure gives a quick benchmark to catch arithmetic errors:

Addition: To estimate $43 + 62 + 59$, round each to the nearest ten: $40 + 60 + 60 = 160$ (exact answer is $164$).
Decimals Addition: For $3.1 + 4.8 + 4.1$, approximate to the nearest whole number: $3 + 5 + 4 = 12$ (exact answer is $12.0$).
Subtraction: To estimate $53 - 38$, approximate to the nearest ten: $50 - 40 = 10$ (or $50 - 40 = 20$ depending on whether 38 rounds to 40). For $841 - 392$, rounding to the nearest ten gives $840 - 390 = 450$ (exact answer is $449$).

Approximating Results of Multiplication and Division

Approximation simplifies complex multiplication and division into easy mental calculations:

Multiplication: To estimate $63 \times 47$, round each number to $1\text{ s.f.}$: $60 \times 50 = 3,000$ (exact calculation is $2,961$).
Division: To estimate $703 \div 21$, round each number to convenient values: $700 \div 20 = 35$.

Comparing Exact Calculations and Approximations

Precision Level

Exact Value vs Approximated Value

An exact value represents the precise arithmetic outcome (e.g., $63 \times 47 = 2,961$), while an approximation represents a rounded estimate for rapid decision-making ($60 \times 50 = 3,000$)[cite: 3].

Rounding Direction

Rounding Up vs Rounding Down

Rounding up occurs when the test digit is 5 or greater, increasing the target digit by 1, whereas rounding down occurs when the test digit is 0 to 4, keeping the target digit unchanged[cite: 3].

Decimal Rounding

Decimal Places vs Significant Figures

Decimal places count positions strictly after the decimal point, while significant figures count all meaningful digits starting from the first non-zero digit on the left[cite: 3].

Arithmetic Checks

Addition Estimation vs Multiplication Estimation

Addition approximation rounds addends to the same nearest place value, whereas multiplication approximation rounds factors to 1 or 2 significant figures to simplify trailing zeros[cite: 3].

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Changing the place value of a whole number when rounding off (e.g., writing 48,057 rounded to the nearest hundred as 481 instead of 48,100)[cite: 3].

Scientific Fact

The dropped whole number places must be filled with zeros to preserve the magnitude of the number[cite: 3].

Common Mistake

Counting leading zeros as significant figures (e.g., assuming $0.0045$ has 4 significant figures).

Scientific Fact

Leading zeros only act as place holders; counting significant figures begins at the first non-zero digit ($4$), so $0.0045$ has only $2\text{ s.f.}$[cite: 3].
