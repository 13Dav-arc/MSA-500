Week 02: Perimeter of Plane Shapes — Circles, Triangles, Quadrilaterals, and Polygons

Foundational curriculum module in Mathematics covering Perimeter of Plane Shapes — Circles, Triangles, Quadrilaterals, and Polygons for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine your school principal decides to erect a security wire fence around the entire school compound or build a running track around the sports field. To know the exact length of wire or boundary marking required, nobody measures the space inside; instead, they measure the total distance around the outside edge. This total outer boundary of any two-dimensional figure is called its perimeter, and calculating it correctly saves time, materials, and money in construction, sports, and agriculture.

Key Terms

Perimeter The total distance around the outside boundary or outer edge of a closed plane shape[cite: 17].
Circumference The special name given to the perimeter or boundary distance around a circle, calculated using $2\pi r$ or $\pi d$[cite: 17, 24].
Regular Polygon A plane figure with all sides of equal length and all interior angles of equal size[cite: 19, 26].
Irregular Shape A geometric figure whose boundary sides or angles are of different lengths and sizes[cite: 17, 26].
Thread Method A practical measuring technique using a flexible piece of thread along curved edges, which is then straightened against a ruler to determine length[cite: 20].

Core Concept

What is Perimeter?

The perimeter is the outside boundary or edge of a plane shape. It also refers to the total linear length of that boundary. For example, walking along the entire boundary fence of a school compound for 200 paces gives an estimated perimeter of 200 paces. For straight-sided figures, the perimeter is simply the sum of all external side lengths.

Circumference of a Circle

The perimeter of a circle is called its circumference:

$$\text{Circumference } (C) = 2\pi r = \pi d$$

where $r$ is the radius, $d$ is the diameter ($d = 2r$), and $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$ or $3.142$.

When given the circumference, the radius is found by dividing: $r = \frac{C}{2\pi}$.
For example, if $C = 44\text{ cm}$ and $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$, then $r = \frac{44}{2 \times \frac{22}{7}} = 7\text{ cm}$, giving a diameter of $d = 2 \times 7 = 14\text{ cm}$.

Perimeter of Triangles and Regular Polygons

Triangles: For any triangle with sides $a$, $b$, and $c$, the perimeter is $P = a + b + c$.
An equilateral triangle with side $s$ has equal sides: $P = 3s$.
A right-angled triangle with legs $5\text{ cm}$ and $12\text{ cm}$ and hypotenuse $13\text{ cm}$ has $P = 5 + 12 + 13 = 30\text{ cm}$.
Regular Polygons: If a polygon has $n$ equal sides of length $s$, its perimeter is $P = n \times s$.
A regular hexagon with sides of $1.6\text{ cm}$ has $P = 6 \times 1.6\text{ cm} = 9.6\text{ cm}$.
Standard Perimeter Formulas for Common Plane Figures

Perimeter of Quadrilaterals

Square: A regular four-sided shape with equal sides of length $l$.

$$P = 4l \quad \implies \quad l = \frac{P}{4}$$

Rectangle: Has two equal lengths ($l$) and two equal breadths ($b$):

$$P = l + b + l + b = 2(l + b)$$

Parallelogram: Opposite sides are equal in length ($x$ and $y$):

$$P = 2(x + y)$$

Trapezium: Found by adding the four exterior boundary sides together.

Measuring Irregular Shapes

For shapes with straight edges, measure each edge directly with a ruler and add the lengths. If a shape has a curved boundary, lay a flexible thread along the curve, mark the ends, straighten the thread, and read off the measurement against a ruler.

Comparing Boundary Formulations Across Geometries

Boundary Formulation

Circle vs Straight-sided Polygons

A circle's perimeter is curved and governed by the irrational ratio $\pi$ ($C = 2\pi r$)[cite: 17, 27], whereas polygons have straight boundaries found by direct side summation ($P = \sum \text{sides}$)[cite: 17].

Formula Simplification

Square vs Rectangle

A square multiplies one side by 4 ($P = 4l$)[cite: 20], while a rectangle must group length and breadth ($P = 2(l + b)$)[cite: 21].

Side Congruence

Equilateral Triangle vs Scalene Triangle

An equilateral triangle uses multiplication ($P = 3s$)[cite: 19], whereas a scalene triangle requires adding three distinct lengths individually ($P = a + b + c$)[cite: 19].

Boundary Technique

Ruler Measurement vs Thread Method

Straight segments are measured directly with a rigid ruler[cite: 19, 20], while curved irregular boundaries require tracing with a thread before ruler measurement[cite: 20].

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Using the area formula ($\pi r^2$) instead of the circumference formula ($2\pi r$) when calculating distance around a circle[cite: 24, 29].

Scientific Fact

Circumference measures outer length ($2\pi r$ in $\text{cm}$ or $\text{m}$), while area measures enclosed surface space ($\pi r^2$ in $\text{cm}^2$ or $\text{m}^2$)[cite: 17, 24, 29].

Common Mistake

Including internal partition lines when calculating the perimeter of composite figures.

Scientific Fact

Perimeter strictly includes the outer enclosing edges of a figure, never internal dividing lines[cite: 17].

Common Mistake

Forgetting to multiply by 2 in the rectangle formula (e.g., computing only $l + b$)[cite: 21].

Scientific Fact

A rectangle has four sides; $l + b$ is only semi-perimeter, so it must be doubled: $P = 2(l + b)$[cite: 21].
