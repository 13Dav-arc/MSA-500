Week 08: Integers (Directed Numbers) — Meaning, Number Line, and Operations

Foundational curriculum module in Mathematics covering Integers (Directed Numbers) — Meaning, Number Line, and Operations for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

If a commercial market trader earns a profit of ₦5,000 on Monday but suffers a business loss of ₦8,000 on Tuesday, how is her financial balance represented mathematically? Or suppose an aircraft cruises at 10,000 metres above sea level while a marine submarine explores an oceanic trench 500 metres beneath the water surface. Ordinary positive numbers cannot distinguish between gaining money and owing debts, or rising above the clouds and plunging beneath the waves. Directed numbers, known formally as integers, introduce negative numbers into mathematics, providing a symmetrical number line that accounts for gains, losses, debts, depths, and freezing temperatures.

Key Terms

Integers (Directed Numbers) The complete set of whole numbers comprising positive integers, negative integers, and zero: $\{\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, +1, +2, +3, \dots\}$.
Positive Integers Whole numbers located strictly to the right of zero on the number line ($+1, +2, +3, \dots$), representing values greater than zero.
Negative Integers Whole numbers located strictly to the left of zero on the number line ($-1, -2, -3, \dots$), representing values less than zero and marked with an obligatory minus sign ($-$).
Number Line A continuous horizontal straight line on which points correspond to real numbers, with zero as the neutral origin, positive numbers increasing to the right, and negative numbers decreasing to the left.
Absolute Value (Modulus) The non-negative numerical distance of an integer from zero on the number line regardless of its directional sign, symbolized by vertical bars (e.g., $|-7| = 7$ and $|+7| = 7$).

Core Concept

1. Meaning and Real-World Applications of Directed Numbers

In basic science and mathematics, quantities have both magnitude (size) and direction:

Financial Balance:
Depositing money, receiving salary, or making a profit is positive ($+₦10,000$).
Withdrawing money, incurring debt, or recording a loss is negative ($-₦4,500$).
Temperature:
Temperatures above the freezing point of water ($0^\circ\text{C}$) are positive ($+28^\circ\text{C}$).
Temperatures below freezing (such as polar ice or a laboratory deep freezer) are negative ($-15^\circ\text{C}$).
Elevation and Altitude:
Mountain peaks above sea level are positive ($+1,200\text{ m}$).
Sea level is the neutral origin ($0\text{ m}$).
Submarine trenches or underground mines below sea level are negative ($-350\text{ m}$).
The Role of Zero ($0$):

Zero is the central reference point. It is neither positive nor negative.

2. The Integer Number Line

On a horizontal number line:

$$\longleftarrow \text{ Decreasing (Smaller)} \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad \text{Increasing (Larger)} \longrightarrow$$

$$\dots, -5, -4, -3, -2, -1, \quad 0, \quad +1, +2, +3, +4, +5, \dots$$

Any integer situated to the right of another integer has a greater value:
$+4 > +1$
$0 > -3$ ($0$ is greater than every negative integer)
$-1 > -6$ ($-1$ is to the right of $-6$, so owing $₦1$ is better than owing $₦6$)
$-2 > -100$
The Integer Number Line: Directional Vectors, Signed Arithmetic, and Thermometric Scales

3. Addition and Subtraction of Directed Numbers

Rules on the Number Line:
1.
Adding a positive number: Move to the right ($+5 + 3 = +8$).
2.
Adding a negative number: Adding a debt means moving to the left:

$$a + (-b) = a - b \quad \rightarrow \quad 7 + (-4) = 7 - 4 = 3$$

1.
Subtracting a positive number: Taking away a gain means moving to the left:

$$a - (+b) = a - b \quad \rightarrow \quad 2 - (+5) = 2 - 5 = -3$$

1.
Subtracting a negative number: Taking away a debt creates a gain; the two minus signs combine into a plus sign, moving to the right:

$$a - (-b) = a + b \quad \rightarrow \quad 4 - (-3) = 4 + 3 = 7$$

$$-6 - (-8) = -6 + 8 = 2$$

4. Multiplication and Division of Directed Numbers

The rules for signs in multiplication and division depend on whether the signs are identical or different:

Identical Signs (Like Signs) Produce a Positive Result ($+$):

$$\begin{aligned}

(+) \times (+) &= (+) \quad\rightarrow\quad (+4) \times (+3) = +12 \\

(-) \times (-) &= (+) \quad\rightarrow\quad (-4) \times (-3) = +12 \\

(+) \div (+) &= (+) \quad\rightarrow\quad (+20) \div (+4) = +5 \\

(-) \div (-) &= (+) \quad\rightarrow\quad (-20) \div (-4) = +5

\end{aligned}$$

Opposite Signs (Unlike Signs) Produce a Negative Result ($-$):

$$\begin{aligned}

(+) \times (-) &= (-) \quad\rightarrow\quad (+5) \times (-3) = -15 \\

(-) \times (+) &= (-) \quad\rightarrow\quad (-5) \times (+3) = -15 \\

(+) \div (-) &= (-) \quad\rightarrow\quad (+24) \div (-6) = -4 \\

(-) \div (+) &= (-) \quad\rightarrow\quad (-24) \div (+6) = -4

\end{aligned}$$

Comparing Directed Numbers and Operational Rules

Positive vs Negative

Greater than Zero vs Less than Zero

Positive integers lie to the right of zero and represent surplus, elevations, and gains; negative integers lie to the left of zero, requiring minus signs, and represent debts and sub-zero values.

Addition of Negative vs Subtraction of Negative

Subtracting Quantity vs Adding Opposite

Adding a negative integer reduces value ($7 + (-3) = 4$); subtracting a negative integer eliminates debt, effectively increasing value ($7 - (-3) = 10$).

Like Signs vs Unlike Signs

Positive Product/Quotient vs Negative Product/Quotient

Multiplying or dividing two numbers with identical signs always yields a positive result; operating on two numbers with different signs always yields a negative result.

Greater Negative vs Greater Absolute Value

Spatial Position vs Magnitude Distance

As negative numbers move further left, their absolute magnitude increases ($|-50| = 50$), but their actual numerical value decreases ($-50 < -5$).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Thinking that $-8$ is greater than $-2$ because $8$ is larger than $2$.

Scientific Fact

On the negative side of the number line, numbers decrease in value as they move left. $-2$ is greater than $-8$ ($-2 > -8$).

Common Mistake

Forgetting that subtracting a negative number produces an addition (e.g., evaluating $5 - (-3)$ as $2$ instead of $8$).

Scientific Fact

Double negative signs invert direction: $5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8$.

Common Mistake

Claiming that zero is a positive integer.

Scientific Fact

Zero ($0$) is the neutral origin; it is neither positive nor negative.
