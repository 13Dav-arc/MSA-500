Core Concept

1. Number Systems and Place Value (Week 01)

Decimal System (Base 10): Standard positional place value periods: Units ($10^0$), Thousands ($10^3$), Millions ($10^6$), Billions ($10^9$).
Roman Numerals: Seven core symbols: $\text{I}(1), \text{V}(5), \text{X}(10), \text{L}(50), \text{C}(100), \text{D}(500), \text{M}(1000)$.
Additive order: $\text{VI} = 6, \text{LX} = 60, \text{MC} = 1100$.
Subtractive order: $\text{IV} = 4, \text{IX} = 9, \text{XL} = 40, \text{XC} = 90, \text{CD} = 400, \text{CM} = 900$.
Note: Roman numerals contain no symbol for zero.

2. Factors, Multiples, HCF, and LCM (Week 02)

Primes vs. Composites: A prime number has exactly two factors ($1$ and itself); composite numbers have more than two factors. $2$ is the only even prime; $1$ is neither prime nor composite.
Prime Factorization: Expressing numbers in powers of primes (e.g., $180 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5^1$).
Highest Common Factor (HCF): Product of common prime factors with their lowest powers.
Lowest Common Multiple (LCM): Product of all prime factors with their highest powers.

3. Fractions and Operational Rules (Weeks 03 & 04)

Fraction Anatomy: $\frac{\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}}$ (Denominator $b \neq 0$).
Proper: $\frac{a}{b} < 1$ (e.g., $\frac{3}{5}$).
Improper: $\frac{a}{b} \geq 1$ (e.g., $\frac{7}{4} = 1\frac{3}{4}$).
Equivalent Fractions: Multiply or divide top and bottom by identical non-zero integers ($\frac{a \times k}{b \times k}$).
Simplest Form: Divide top and bottom by their HCF.
Fraction Arithmetic:
Addition/Subtraction: Convert to a common denominator using the LCM.
Multiplication: Multiply numerators together and denominators together; cancel common factors.
Division: Invert the divisor into its reciprocal and multiply ($\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}$).
BODMAS: Brackets $\rightarrow$ Of $\rightarrow$ Division $\rightarrow$ Multiplication $\rightarrow$ Addition $\rightarrow$ Subtraction.

4. Decimals and Percentage Mathematics (Weeks 06 & 07)

Decimal Positions: Tenths ($0.1$), Hundredths ($0.01$), Thousandths ($0.001$).
Decimals to Fractions: Write over power of 10 and reduce ($0.375 = \frac{375}{1000} = \frac{3}{8}$).
Fractions to Decimals:
Terminating: Denominator in lowest terms has prime factors of only $2$ and/or $5$ (e.g., $\frac{1}{4} = 0.25$).
Recurring: Denominator contains other prime factors like $3$ or $7$ (e.g., $\frac{1}{3} = 0.\dot{3}$).
Decimal Operations:
Addition/Subtraction: Align decimal points vertically.
Multiplication: Multiply as whole numbers, then insert the decimal point by counting total decimal places.
Powers of 10: Multiply $\rightarrow$ move point right; Divide $\rightarrow$ move point left.
Percentages ($x\% = \frac{x}{100} = 0.0x$):
Finding percentage of a quantity: $\frac{\%}{100} \times \text{Total}$.
Expressing part of whole as percentage: $\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}} \times 100\%$.
Discounts: Deduct discount amount from marked price.

5. Directed Numbers and Integer Operations (Week 08)

The Number Line: Positive numbers ($+n$) right of zero; negative numbers ($-n$) left of zero; zero is neutral. Numbers to the right are always greater ($-2 > -10$).
Signed Addition and Subtraction:
Adding a negative is subtraction: $a + (-b) = a - b$.
Subtracting a negative is addition: $a - (-b) = a + b$.
Signed Multiplication and Division Rules:
Like signs yield positive ($+$): $(-) \times (-) = (+)$, $(+) \times (+) = (+)$.
Unlike signs yield negative ($-$): $(+) \times (-) = (-)$, $(-) \times (+) = (-)$.

Sessional Core Mathematical Concept Comparisons

HCF vs LCM

Greatest Common Divisor vs Smallest Common Multiple

HCF takes lowest powers of common prime bases to find the largest dividing number; LCM takes highest powers of all prime bases to find the smallest shared multiple.

Fractions vs Decimals vs Percentages

Three Representations of Rational Numbers

All three represent broken quantities: 3/4 as a common fraction, 0.75 as a base-10 decimal fraction, and 75% as a standardized ratio out of 100.

Terminating vs Recurring Decimals

Finite Division vs Infinite Cyclic Pattern

Terminating decimals stop with remainder 0 because denominators factor into only 2 and 5; recurring decimals loop endlessly due to other prime divisors like 3 or 7.

Positive vs Negative Integers

Directional Sign and Spatial Position

Positive integers lie right of zero and represent gains/surplus; negative integers lie left of zero and represent debts/freezing points; zero is the neutral boundary.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Treating the number 1 as a prime number.

Scientific Fact

A prime number must possess exactly two distinct factors. The number 1 has only one factor (itself), making it neither prime nor composite.

Common Mistake

Adding denominators when adding fractions (e.g., $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{7}$).

Scientific Fact

Denominators cannot be added directly. Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions with a common denominator (LCM) first: $\frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$.

Common Mistake

Evaluating $-5 - (-8)$ as $-13$.

Scientific Fact

Subtracting a negative creates a positive addition: $-5 - (-8) = -5 + 8 = +3$.

Common Mistake

Thinking that $0.09$ is greater than $0.2$ because 9 is greater than 2.

Scientific Fact

Decimals are compared from the tenths column. $0.2 = 0.20$ ($2$ tenths), which is greater than $0.09$ ($0$ tenths).
