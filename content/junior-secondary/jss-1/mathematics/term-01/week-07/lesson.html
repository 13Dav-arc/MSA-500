Week 07: Decimals II & Percentages — Operations and Everyday Applications

Foundational curriculum module in Mathematics covering Decimals II & Percentages — Operations and Everyday Applications for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

When you score 42 out of 50 in an end-of-term continuous assessment test, your teacher calculates your result as 84%. When a department store offers a 20% discount on school sandals or a bank credits an annual interest rate to a savings account, they are using the universal financial language of percentages. Percentages and decimals are mathematical twins—both allow us to compare proportions, evaluate performance, and negotiate financial transactions on a standardized scale of one hundred. Mastering decimal arithmetic and percentage calculations gives you the practical mathematical power needed for everyday commerce and science.

Key Terms

Percentage A fraction whose denominator is fixed at 100, represented by the percent symbol (%), derived from the Latin phrase "per centum" meaning "out of one hundred."
Percent Symbol (%) The universal mathematical symbol denoting a hundredth part ($1\% = \frac{1}{100} = 0.01$).
Decimal Alignment The rule of arranging numbers vertically such that their decimal points form a straight vertical column before carrying out addition or subtraction.
Percentage Increase The relative measure of how much a quantity has grown compared to its original baseline value, expressed as a percentage: $\frac{\text{Actual Increase}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100\%$.
Discount A percentage reduction deducted from the original marked retail price of an item during a sales promotion.

Core Concept

1. Operations on Decimals

Addition and Subtraction of Decimals:

Line up the decimal points vertically, pad trailing blank columns with zeros if necessary, and add or subtract as whole numbers:

$$24.85 + 7.394 \rightarrow \begin{array}{r@{\quad}l} 24.850 \\ +\; 7.394 \\ \hline 32.244 \end{array}$$

$$15.2 - 6.84 \rightarrow \begin{array}{r@{\quad}l} 15.20 \\ -\; 6.84 \\ \hline 8.36 \end{array}$$

Multiplication of Decimals:
1.
Multiply the numbers as if they were whole numbers, completely ignoring decimal points.
2.
Count the total number of decimal places in both factors combined.
3.
Place the decimal point in the product so that it has that exact number of decimal places from the right:

$$3.2 \times 0.45 \rightarrow 32 \times 45 = 1440$$

Total decimal places = $1 + 2 = 3$. Insert decimal point 3 places from right $\rightarrow 1.440 = 1.44$.

Multiplying by Powers of 10 ($10, 100, 1000$): Shift the decimal point to the right by the number of zeros:

$4.825 \times 10 = 48.25; \quad 4.825 \times 100 = 482.5; \quad 4.825 \times 1000 = 4825$.

Division of Decimals:
Dividing by Powers of 10: Shift the decimal point to the left by the number of zeros:

$542.8 \div 10 = 54.28; \quad 542.8 \div 100 = 5.428; \quad 542.8 \div 1000 = 0.5428$.

Dividing by a Decimal Divisor: Multiply both the dividend and divisor by $10, 100,$ or $1000$ to turn the divisor into a whole number before dividing:

$$4.8 \div 0.12 = \frac{4.8 \times 100}{0.12 \times 100} = \frac{480}{12} = 40$$

2. Meaning and Conversions of Percentages

Meaning: A percentage is simply a fraction with a denominator of 100:

$$x\% = \frac{x}{100} = x \times 0.01$$

Converting Percentages to Fractions:

Write the percentage over 100 and simplify to lowest terms:

$$25\% = \frac{25}{100} = \frac{1}{4}, \quad 60\% = \frac{60}{100} = \frac{3}{5}, \quad 85\% = \frac{85}{100} = \frac{17}{20}$$

Converting Percentages to Decimals:

Divide by 100 (move the decimal point two places to the left):

$$45\% = 0.45, \quad 7\% = 0.07, \quad 12.5\% = 0.125, \quad 150\% = 1.50$$

Converting Fractions and Decimals to Percentages:

Multiply the fraction or decimal by $100\%$:

$$\frac{3}{8} \times 100\% = \frac{300\%}{8} = 37.5\%$$

$$0.85 \times 100\% = 85\%$$

3. Percentage Calculations in Real Life

Finding a Percentage of a Given Quantity:

Formula: $\text{Result} = \frac{\text{Percentage}}{100} \times \text{Total Quantity}$

Example: Calculate $15\%$ of $₦4,000$:

$$\frac{15}{100} \times 4000 = 15 \times 40 = ₦600$$

Expressing One Quantity as a Percentage of Another:

Formula: $\text{Percentage} = \frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}} \times 100\%$

Example: A student scores 35 marks out of 50. What is the percentage?

$$\frac{35}{50} \times 100\% = 35 \times 2\% = 70\%$$

Calculating Retail Discounts:

$\text{Discount Amount} = \frac{\text{Discount Rate}}{100} \times \text{Marked Price}$

$\text{Final Sale Price} = \text{Marked Price} - \text{Discount Amount}$

Example: A school bag marked at $₦12,000$ has a $10\%$ discount:

$$\text{Discount} = \frac{10}{100} \times 12000 = ₦1,200 \rightarrow \text{Sale Price} = 12000 - 1200 = ₦10,800$$

Comparing Percentages, Decimals, and Common Fractions

Percentage vs Decimal

Scale of 100 vs Base-10 Positional Form

Percentages display values explicitly out of 100 using the % symbol (e.g., 75%); decimals express the identical proportion through tenths and hundredths without symbols (0.75).

Multiplication vs Division by 10

Decimal Shift Right vs Decimal Shift Left

Multiplying by powers of ten increases magnitude, shifting the decimal point right; dividing by powers of ten decreases magnitude, shifting the point left.

Percent Part vs Percent Whole

Calculating Fraction of Amount vs Expressing Relative Ratio

Finding a percentage of an amount multiplies the percent by the total; expressing one quantity as a percent divides the part by the whole and multiplies by 100%.

Discount vs Price Increase

Deduction from Original vs Addition to Baseline

A discount subtracts a percentage amount from the marked retail price; an increase or tax adds a percentage amount to the original cost.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Forgetting to move the decimal point when dividing by a decimal divisor (e.g., evaluating $6.4 \div 0.2$ as $0.32$ instead of $32$).

Scientific Fact

Both numbers must be multiplied by 10 to turn the divisor into a whole number before dividing: $\frac{6.4 \times 10}{0.2 \times 10} = \frac{64}{2} = 32$.

Common Mistake

Writing $8\%$ as $0.8$ in decimal form instead of $0.08$.

Scientific Fact

$0.8 = \frac{8}{10} = 80\%$. $8\%$ represents 8 hundredths: $\frac{8}{100} = 0.08$.

Common Mistake

Misaligning decimal points during addition or subtraction (e.g., adding $12.3 + 4.56$ by aligning the last digits to the right).

Scientific Fact

Decimal points must be placed in a direct vertical line before adding or subtracting columns.
