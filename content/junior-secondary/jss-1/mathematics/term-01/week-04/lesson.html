Week 04: Fractions II — Operations on Fractions & BODMAS

Foundational curriculum module in Mathematics covering Fractions II — Operations on Fractions & BODMAS for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Suppose a Nigerian farmer allocates $\frac{1}{3}$ of his farmland to cassava cultivation, $\frac{2}{5}$ to maize, and leaves the remaining portion fallow for cattle grazing. How much of the total farm is under active cultivation, and what fraction remains unplanted? Or imagine sharing three-quarters of a bag of rice equally among six families in a community. Solving these everyday practical dilemmas requires performing the four fundamental arithmetic operations on fractions: addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. By mastering the rules of common denominators, reciprocals, and the universal hierarchy of BODMAS, you gain complete command over fractional mathematics.

Key Terms

Common Denominator A shared multiple of the denominators of two or more fractions, typically found using the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM), required to add or subtract fractions with unlike terms.
Reciprocal The multiplicative inverse of a number or fraction, obtained by swapping the numerator and the denominator ($\frac{a}{b} \rightarrow \frac{b}{a}$), such that their product equals 1.
Cross-Cancellation The mathematical simplification technique of dividing a numerator and a denominator by their common factor before carrying out fraction multiplication.
BODMAS The standardized mathematical order of operations determining calculation hierarchy: Brackets first, then Of (orders), Division, Multiplication, Addition, and Subtraction.
Unitary Method A problem-solving strategy that calculates the value of a single unit first before multiplying to find the required total quantity.

Core Concept

1. Addition and Subtraction of Fractions

Like Denominators: Simply add or subtract the numerators while keeping the common denominator:

$$\frac{3}{7} + \frac{2}{7} = \frac{3 + 2}{7} = \frac{5}{7}, \quad \frac{9}{11} - \frac{4}{11} = \frac{9 - 4}{11} = \frac{5}{11}$$

Unlike Denominators:
1.
Find the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of all denominators.
2.
Convert each fraction into an equivalent fraction with the LCM as its denominator.
3.
Add or subtract the resulting numerators and simplify to lowest terms:

$$\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{8}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{8 + 3}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$$

Mixed Numbers Addition and Subtraction:

Method: Add or subtract the whole numbers separately, then combine with the fractional part:

$$3\frac{1}{2} + 2\frac{1}{3} = (3 + 2) + \left(\frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6}\right) = 5 + \frac{5}{6} = 5\frac{5}{6}$$

When subtraction requires regrouping (borrowing):

$$4\frac{1}{4} - 1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{17}{4} - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{51 - 20}{12} = \frac{31}{12} = 2\frac{7}{12}$$

2. Multiplication of Fractions and the Meaning of "Of"

Multiplication Rule: Multiply numerators together and denominators together:

$$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}$$

Cross-Cancellation: Divide common factors before multiplying to keep numbers small:

$$\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{4 \div 4}{9 \div 3} \times \frac{3 \div 3}{8 \div 4} = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6}$$

The Word "Of": In mathematics, "of" signifies multiplication:

$$\frac{3}{4} \text{ of } 240 = \frac{3}{4} \times 240 = 3 \times 60 = 180$$

Fraction Arithmetic Pipeline: Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, and Division Rules

3. Division of Fractions and Reciprocals

The Reciprocal (Multiplicative Inverse):
The reciprocal of $\frac{3}{5}$ is $\frac{5}{3}$, because $\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{3} = 1$.
The reciprocal of a whole number $4$ (which is $\frac{4}{1}$) is $\frac{1}{4}$.
The reciprocal of a mixed number $2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$ is $\frac{3}{7}$.
Division Rule: Invert the divisor (the second fraction) to its reciprocal and multiply:

$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c} = \frac{a \times d}{b \times c}$$

Example: $\frac{5}{8} \div \frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{5 \times 1}{2 \times 3} = \frac{5}{6}$.

4. Order of Operations with Fractions (BODMAS)

When multiple operations appear in a single expression, execute them strictly according to BODMAS:

1.
Brackets: Evaluate expressions inside grouping symbols $(), [], \{\}$ first.
2.
Of: Execute "of" multiplications next.
3.
Division and Multiplication: Work from left to right.
4.
Addition and Subtraction: Work from left to right.
Example: Evaluate $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{3}$:
Multiplication before addition: $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{2}$.
Addition: $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1$.

Comparing Fraction Operations and Execution Rules

Addition vs Multiplication

Requires Common Denominator vs Direct Multiplication

Adding or subtracting fractions requires converting to a shared LCM denominator; multiplying fractions does NOT require a common denominator—simply multiply numerators and denominators.

Division vs Multiplication

Invert and Multiply vs Direct Cross-Cancellation

Division cannot be done directly across denominators; it must be converted into multiplication by inverting the second fraction into its reciprocal.

Mixed Addition vs Mixed Subtraction

Simple Summation vs Potential Whole Number Regrouping

Mixed addition always sums integers and fractional parts cleanly; mixed subtraction often requires borrowing 1 whole (as d/d) from the integer when the first fraction is smaller than the second.

"Of" vs Multiplication Sign (×)

Precedence in BODMAS vs Standard Product

In BODMAS, "Of" takes higher priority and must be calculated immediately after brackets, before regular division or multiplication.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Adding denominators together when adding fractions (e.g., writing $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1+1}{2+3} = \frac{2}{5}$).

Scientific Fact

Denominators cannot be added. Fractions must be converted to equivalent forms with a common denominator first: $\frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$.

Common Mistake

Inverting the first fraction instead of the second fraction during division (e.g., writing $\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{5} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{4}{5}$).

Scientific Fact

Always leave the first fraction unchanged; ONLY invert the divisor (the second fraction): $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{4} = \frac{5}{6}$.

Common Mistake

Adding before multiplying in an expression lacking brackets (e.g., evaluating $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2}$ by adding first).

Scientific Fact

BODMAS dictates that multiplication must be performed before addition.
