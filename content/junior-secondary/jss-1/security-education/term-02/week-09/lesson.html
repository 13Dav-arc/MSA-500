The Horror and Criminality of "Jungle Justice"

Jungle justice is a barbaric symptom of lawlessness that threatens every civilized person. Under Section 316 and 319 of the Criminal Code:

Everyone participating in a mob lynching is guilty of Murder, which carries a mandatory sentence of Capital Punishment (Death Penalty).
Destroying Forensics: Killing a suspect eliminates vital intelligence. A petty pickpocket may belong to a vast kidnapping syndicate that the police could have dismantled if he were interrogated lawfully.
Murdering Innocent People: History proves that victims of jungle justice are frequently innocent individuals falsely accused due to mistaken identity, personal vendettas, or panic (such as the tragic murder of four innocent university students in Aluu, Rivers State in 2012).
Subverting the Constitution: Section 36(5) of the Nigerian Constitution guarantees that every person charged with a criminal offense is presumed innocent until proven guilty in a court of law.

The Lawful Citizen's Arrest Protocol

Under Section 20 of the Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015, any citizen can execute a citizen's arrest under strict legal conditions:

1.
The suspect must be caught in the actual commission of a serious criminal offense (e.g., snatching a bag, breaking a lock, assaulting someone).
2.
The citizen must use only reasonable, non-lethal physical restraint (e.g., holding their arms).
3.
Beating, stripping naked, torturing, or burning the suspect is strictly illegal and criminal.
4.
The suspect must be handed over immediately to the nearest police officer or police station without delay.

The 80% Term 2 Mastery Gate Synthesis

Before sitting for the Terminal Examination, students must demonstrate mastery across the ten major security agencies mastered this term:

NPF: Primary civil policing and crime detection (S. 214; Bail is free).
Armed Forces (Army, Navy, Air Force): Defending territorial integrity (S. 217; Commander-in-Chief).
NSCDC: Safeguarding critical infrastructure (pipelines, power grids, railways) and licensing PGCs.
FRSC: Highway safety, reducing crashes, enforcing speed limiters, emergency line 122.
NIS: Controlling human migration, border posts, and issuing Nigerian passports.
NCS: Assessing import duties, facilitating trade, and seizing smuggled contraband.
EFCC: Combating cybercrime (Yahoo-Yahoo), financial fraud, and money laundering.
ICPC: Eradicating corruption, bribery, and contract inflation in public ministries.
DSS: Gathering domestic intelligence, counter-espionage, and VIP close protection.
NDLEA: Eradicating illicit drug trafficking (supply reduction) and substance rehabilitation (demand reduction).
NCoS & FFS: Reforming offenders through education/skills and executing fire rescues.
