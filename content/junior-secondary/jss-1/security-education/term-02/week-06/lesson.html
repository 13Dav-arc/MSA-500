The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC)

Established by the EFCC (Establishment) Act 2004, the EFCC investigates economic and financial crimes that threaten the financial stability and international reputation of Nigeria:

Core Responsibilities of the EFCC:

1.
Cybercrime and Digital Fraud: Combating phishing, unauthorized bank access, identity theft, and internet fraud ("Yahoo-Yahoo") under the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015.
2.
Money Laundering Interdiction: Tracking illicit funds funneled through banks, real estate, and bureau de change operators to conceal the proceeds of drug trafficking, kidnapping, or corruption.
3.
Advance Fee Fraud Enforcement: Prosecuting local and international scam artists under the Advance Fee Fraud and Other Fraud Related Offences Act 2006.
4.
Asset Recovery and Forfeiture: Securing court orders to confiscate illegally acquired properties, vehicles, luxury yachts, and bank accounts, auctioning them to remit proceeds to the federal treasury.

The Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC)

Established under the Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Act 2000, the ICPC focuses on systemic integrity within government ministries, departments, and agencies (MDAs):

Core Responsibilities of the ICPC:

1.
Public Sector Anti-Graft Enforcement: Investigating civil servants, ministers, and public officers who demand bribes, accept kickbacks, inflate government contracts, or siphon public payroll funds ("ghost workers").
2.
System and Administrative Reviews: Auditing financial processes in government ministries to plug corruption loopholes before theft occurs.
3.
Public Education & Grassroots Sensitization: Establishing Anti-Corruption Vanguards in tertiary institutions and Anti-Corruption Clubs in secondary schools to teach students honesty and ethical civic values.

The State Security Service (Department of State Services - DSS)

Established under the National Security Agencies Act 1986 (formerly the Nigerian Security Organisation - NSO), the DSS operates under the direct authority of the Presidency:

Core Responsibilities of the DSS:

1.
Domestic Intelligence and Counter-Subversion: Collecting non-military intelligence to detect, neutralize, and prevent acts of domestic terrorism, armed insurrection, assassination, and treasonable felony.
2.
Counter-Espionage and Counter-Sabotage: Neutralizing foreign spy networks, protecting classified state secrets, and shielding sensitive national installations from sabotage.
3.
Close Protection (VIP Security): Providing armed, elite close protection details to the President, Vice President, State Governors, visiting foreign dignitaries, and Supreme Court Justices.
