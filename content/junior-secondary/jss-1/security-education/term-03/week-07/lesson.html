Why This Matters to You

Imagine standing at a busy bus stop at 6:30 PM as twilight fades into darkness. You are tired after a long school day, and commercial buses are scarce. Suddenly, an unmarked mini-bus slows down right in front of you. The conductor screams: "Oshodi! Direct! Fifty naira only!"—half the normal fare! The bus looks nearly full, with four passengers inside chatting quietly. Eager to get home quickly, you squeeze into the last empty seat between two men. But the instant the bus accelerates, the side doors slam shut, the conductor pulls a sharp dagger, the "passengers" draw iron rods, and a cloth soaked in chemical chloroform is pressed against your face! You have just entered the deadly nightmare known across Nigerian cities as "One Chance"! How do these criminal syndicates operate, what are their hidden warning signs, and how can you navigate public transportation safely every single day? Let us find out!

Red Flags of a High-Risk Commercial Vehicle

Before you step your foot into any public commercial vehicle, scan for these critical warning indicators:

1.
Missing or Broken Interior Door Handles: If the inside door handle is missing, snapped off, or replaced with a piece of wire, DO NOT ENTER! The driver has modified the car so you cannot open the door from the inside during an emergency.
2.
Suspicious Passenger Distribution:
A 7-seater minibus where three or four large men are sitting separately, leaving only a middle seat open for you.
Genuine commercial passengers sit naturally; "One Chance" decoys often look tense, avoid eye contact, or sit in positions designed to box you in.
1.
Unusual Fares and Rushed Loading:
Shouting ridiculously low fares (e.g., charging ₦100 when all other buses are charging ₦300) to lure desperate, tired commuters.
The conductor aggressively pushing you to jump in quickly without giving you time to look inside.
1.
Boarding Outside Recognized Motor Parks:
Criminal vehicles rarely load inside official National Union of Road Transport Workers (NURTW) or Road Transport Employers Association of Nigeria (RTEAN) motor parks. They loiter on dark, unlit corners of expressways or under highway flyovers.

Safe Commuting Rules for Secondary School Students

Board Only at Official Terminals: Always board commercial vehicles inside designated, well-lit motor parks or registered bus terminals where vehicles are recorded and queued.
Inspect Before Boarding: Take three seconds to look at the tires, the driver’s face, the passengers, and the door handles before stepping inside.
Choose Strategic Seating: Sit near the window or next to the exit door whenever possible. Avoid being sandwiched between two unknown adults in the rear seat.
The "Zero Snack" Rule: NEVER accept water, biscuits, groundnuts, or chewing gum from fellow passengers! Criminals frequently lace treats with sedatives to knock out victims silently during transit.
Communicate Transit Details: Discreetly send a quick text message to your parents with the vehicle's registration number and destination before the vehicle departs.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Designate Fixed Commute Routes: Agree on a fixed, verified transit route between school and home. Instruct your child never to take unapproved detours or shortcuts with unknown drivers. - Check-In Protocol: Teach your child to send a quick text message whenever they board a commercial vehicle: e.g., "Boarded bus KJA-452-XY at Maryland park, heading home." - Provide Adequate Transport Fare: Ensure your child has sufficient funds for registered transport so they are never tempted to accept cheap rides from dangerous "One Chance" operators. ---
