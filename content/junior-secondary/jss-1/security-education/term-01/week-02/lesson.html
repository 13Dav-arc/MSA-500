Foundational curriculum module in Security Education covering Common Security Hazards (Domestic Hazards, Fire Outbreaks, Electrical Faults, Gas Leaks, and Transport Risks) for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Imagine a quiet, rainy Friday night in a suburban home in Ibadan or Enugu. The family is sound asleep under warm blankets. Outside in the backyard, an electrical generator chugs steadily, supplying power. But to keep the generator dry from the rain, someone dragged it onto the covered back veranda, just beneath the open louvers of the children's bedroom window! An invisible, completely odorless, and tasteless gas called Carbon Monoxide begins to drift quietly into the room. Within two hours, everyone inside is unconscious! Tragically, news headlines in Nigeria frequently report entire families perishing overnight from generator fumes or homes incinerated within minutes because a mother tried to light a match near a leaking cooking gas cylinder. Why are some of the most deadly security threats not armed bandits with guns, but invisible hazards hiding right inside our kitchens, living rooms, and streets? Let us learn how to identify and neutralize Security Hazards before they strike!

Core Concept

What is a Security Hazard?

A hazard is not the same thing as an accident or crime. A hazard is the danger source waiting to cause harm, while risk is the likelihood that the hazard will actually hurt someone. Security education trains individuals to spot hazards early, assess the danger, and remove the threat before tragedy strikes.

Security Hazard Prevention: Proper gas cylinder storage, generator placement, and fire safety systems

---

Detailed Examination of Major Security Hazards:

1. Fire and Gas Hazards:

Cooking Gas (LPG) Leaks: LPG is heavier than air; when it leaks, it sinks and pools along the floor.
Golden Rule: If you smell the rotten-egg odor of leaking gas, NEVER strike a match, NEVER turn on an electrical light switch, and NEVER use a mobile phone flashlight! The tiny electrical spark inside a light switch is enough to ignite the gas cloud and blow the roof off the building! Open all doors and windows gently to ventilate the room.
Best Practice: Gas cylinders must always be placed outside the kitchen in a shaded, well-ventilated metal cage with copper piping leading indoors.
Generator Fumes (Carbon Monoxide Poisoning):
Never run a generator indoors, in a hallway, inside a garage, or directly beneath bedroom windows. Generators must be positioned outdoors at least 6 meters (20 feet) away from any door, window, or ventilation louver.

2. Electrical Hazards:

Overloaded Multi-Sockets: Plugging an electric iron, microwave, electric kettle, and refrigerator into one cheap extension strip causes the plastic insulation to melt, creating electrical short-circuits and midnight fires.
Exposed Live Wires and Broken Sockets: Children touching frayed electrical cords with wet hands risk fatal electrocution.
Crucial Rule: Never use water to extinguish an electrical fire! Water conducts electricity and will electrocute the person holding the bucket. Use an ABC Dry Chemical Powder Fire Extinguisher or dry sand.

3. Domestic Chemical Hazards:

Storing kerosene or petrol in soft-drink plastic bottles inside the kitchen. Thirsty children frequently mistake fuel for water, suffering fatal chemical poisoning.
Storing rat poison, insecticides, and caustic soda on floor levels within reach of crawling toddlers.

4. Road and Transit Hazards:

Commercial Motorcycles (Okada): Riding without a standardized crash helmet exposes passengers to traumatic brain injury in minor collisions.
Commercial Minibuses (Danfo / Korope): Overcrowding, riding with limbs hanging out of doors, and vehicles operating with worn-out bald tires that blowout at high speeds.
Pedestrian Risks: Walking along highways wearing noise-canceling headphones, completely oblivious to approaching vehicular horns.

---

Safety Protocols and Hazard Elimination

Install Fire Extinguishers: Mount at least one multi-purpose (ABC Dry Chemical) fire extinguisher in the kitchen and living room corridor, checking the pressure gauge regularly.
Proper Generator Exhaust Direction: Ensure generator exhaust pipes point away from human dwellings.
Child-Proof Storage: Lock all medications, cleaning bleach, and pest chemicals in high, child-resistant cabinets.
Keep Drainage Gutters Clear: Never dump domestic trash or plastic bags into street gutters.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Home Hazard Walkthrough: Conduct a 15-minute home safety inspection with your child: 1. Inspect the kitchen: Is the cooking gas cylinder outside? Is the regulator hose firm and uncracked? 2. Inspect the generator: Is it positioned at least 20 feet away from all windows and doors? 3. Inspect electrical outlets: Are multi-sockets overloaded with irons or heaters? - Fire Safety Drill: Teach your child how to operate a fire extinguisher using the P.A.S.S. technique (Pull the pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze the handle, Sweep from side to side). - Chemical Relabeling: Remove any toxic chemicals stored in beverage or soft-drink containers immediately.
