The "Tricky Person" Rule vs The "Monster" Myth

Children often imagine that kidnappers look like frightening, ugly movie villains with eye patches and scars. In reality:

Predatory adults usually look clean, well-spoken, respectable, and polite.
The Universal Adult Rule: Normal, responsible adults never ask a strange child for help with adult tasks (e.g., asking a 10-year-old to help find lost puppies, carry boxes into a house, or guide them through lonely alleys). Responsible adults ask other adults for help!
If an unfamiliar adult asks you for help, that is an immediate Red Flag! Step away immediately.

The "No, Go, Yell, Tell" Protocol

When confronted with an aggressive, predatory, or suspicious situation, execute these four actions in rapid sequence:

1.
NO: Shout a firm, loud, and confident "NO!" Do not apologize or hesitate.
2.
GO: Turn and run immediately in the opposite direction towards safety. (Vehicles cannot reverse as fast as a person can run back around a corner).
3.
YELL: Scream loudly: "HELP! THIS IS NOT MY PARENT! HE IS TRYING TO TAKE ME!" Screaming specific words alerts the public that a crime is occurring, rather than ordinary childish tantrums.
4.
TELL: Run directly into a Safe Haven and report the incident immediately to an adult in uniform (police officer, security guard, teacher, or bank teller).

Identifying Safe Havens Along Daily Routes

Students should map out reliable Safe Havens between home and school:

Commercial bank premises guarded by security personnel.
Busy petrol filling stations with staff and bright lighting.
Registered supermarkets, pharmacy stores, and government offices.
Active police stations or civil defence posts.
Never seek shelter in: Uncompleted buildings, abandoned parked vehicles, dense thickets, or dark dead-end alleys!
