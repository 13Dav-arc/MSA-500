Understanding Bullying: The Core Mechanisms

Bullying differs from ordinary playful teasing in three distinct ways:

1.
Imbalance of Power: The bully uses physical strength, popularity, age, or access to embarrassing information to intimidate or control the victim.
2.
Repetition: Bullying behaviors happen more than once or have the potential to happen repeatedly over time.
3.
Intentional Malice: The perpetrator acts deliberately to cause emotional distress, physical pain, or social isolation.

The Four Faces of Bullying

Physical Bullying: Slapping, pushing, extortion of pocket money, vandalizing exercise books or school bags.
Verbal Bullying: Spreading cruel nicknames, mocking a student's speech impediment, height, weight, skin color, or family background.
Social / Relational Bullying: Telling classmates not to sit near a certain student, maliciously organizing class boycotts against someone, spreading defamatory lies.
Cyberbullying: Posting doctored pictures of a classmate online, creating abusive social media group chats, sending threatening anonymous text messages.

The Role of the School Safety Committee

Every junior secondary school should maintain an active School Safety Committee composed of:

School Principal / Vice Principal (Administration): Formulates and enforces school safety regulations.
Guidance Counselor: Offers confidential psychological counseling to victims and rehabilitates aggressive students.
Teacher Safety Officer: Conducts daily corridor and playground supervision.
PTA Representative (Parents): Partners with community elders, vigilante groups, and local law enforcement.
Student Safety Prefects: Serves as the eyes and ears of the student body, reporting hidden hotspots where bullying or smoking occurs.

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Warning Signs to Watch For: Regularly observe your child for signs of bullying: unexplained bruises, torn clothes, missing items, sudden refusal to attend school on Monday, dropping grades, or unusual silence during family dinners. - Establish Open Communication: Regularly ask specific, non-threatening questions: "Who did you sit with at lunch today?", "Did anyone say something that made you feel uncomfortable or scared this week?" - Escalate Promptly: If your child reports bullying, do not storm the school to confront the bully's parents personally. Contact the school principal and guidance counselor in writing, presenting documented incidents and dates. ---
