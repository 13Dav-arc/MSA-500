Why This Matters to You

If you were an architect constructing a soaring skyscraper, what two steel foundation pillars would hold the entire structure upright against storms and earthquakes? In the French language, those two invincible pillars are the verbs ÊTRE (To Be) and AVOIR (To Have)! Almost every sentence you will ever speak, read, or write in French relies on these two royal verbs. Want to say who you are, what your profession is, describe your character, or say where you are right now? You need Être (Je suis élève, Il est intelligent, Nous sommes à Lagos)! Want to state your age, describe what is inside your schoolbag, or talk about what you own? You need Avoir (J'ai douze ans, Elle a une règle, Ils ont des livres)! Because both verbs are irregular, you cannot just guess their forms—you must master them by heart like multiplication tables. In this lesson, you will master the present tense conjugations of Être and Avoir, explore their vital expressions, and unlock the power of French sentence construction!

1. Conjugation of the Verb ÊTRE (To Be) in the Present Tense

Les Verbes Fondamentaux: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

The verb Être is completely irregular:

Core Real-World Uses of Être:

1.
Nationality & Origin: Je suis nigérian. (I am Nigerian) / Elle est béninoise. (She is Beninese).
2.
Professions & Identity: Il est professeur. (He is a teacher) / Nous sommes élèves. (We are students).
3.
Descriptions & Adjectives: Tu es intelligent. (You are intelligent) / La classe est grande. (The classroom is large).
4.
Location: Je suis à l'école. (I am at school) / Ils sont à Abuja. (They are in Abuja).

Core Real-World Uses of Avoir:

1.
Expressing Age (Mandatory): J'ai douze ans. (I am 12 years old) / Quel âge as-tu? (How old are you?).
2.
Possession of Objects: Tu as un stylo. (You have a pen) / Nous avons des cahiers. (We have notebooks).
3.
Physical Sensations (Idiomatic expressions):
Avoir faim = To be hungry (e.g., J'ai faim = I am hungry).
Avoir soif = To be thirsty (e.g., Il a soif = He is thirsty).
Avoir chaud / froid = To be warm / cold.

3. The Dangerous Trap: "Ils sont" versus "Ils ont"

Every year, thousands of French students stumble on this critical distinction:

*Ils sont (From Être = They ARE):*
Pronounced with a clean, unvoiced soft 'S': Eel-son.
Example: Ils sont nigérians. (They are Nigerians.)
*Ils ont (From Avoir = They HAVE):*
The silent 's' of ils links with the vowel 'o' of ont to create a buzzing 'Z' sound: Eel-zon!
Example: Ils ont des livres. (They have books.)
