Why This Matters to You

Imagine walking through the colorful Dantokpa Market in Cotonou, Republic of Benin, or visiting a bustling bakery in Paris. The aroma of warm, crusty baguettes fills the air. You want to buy three fresh loaves of bread, two bottles of juice, and find out the price. What is the very first tool you need? Numbers! And what is one of the most exciting questions new JSS 1 students ask each other on the first day of school? "How old are you?" In English, we say: "I am twelve years old" using the verb to be. But in French, you do not be your age—you possess your age! A French speaker says: "J'ai douze ans"—literally, "I have twelve years!" In this lesson, you will master counting from zero all the way to thirty with authentic French pronunciation, decode number patterns, and learn how to ask and state age with total confidence!

Core Concept

1. Counting from 0 to 10: The Foundation Stones

Les Nombres de 0 à 30 et l'Âge: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

Every number in French builds upon the first ten digits:

```

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│ NUMBERS 0 TO 10 IN FRENCH │

├────────┬─────────────────┬──────────┬────────┬────────────────┬────────┤

│ DIGIT │ FRENCH WORD │ PHONETIC │ DIGIT │ FRENCH WORD │PHONETIC│

├────────┼─────────────────┼──────────┼────────┼────────────────┼────────┤

│ 0 │ zéro │ zay-ro │ 6 │ six │ seess│

│ 1 │ un / une │ uhn/oon│ 7 │ sept │ set │

│ 2 │ deux │ deuh │ 8 │ huit │ weet │

│ 3 │ trois │ twa │ 9 │ neuf │ nuff │

│ 4 │ quatre │ kat-ruh│ 10 │ dix │ deess│

│ 5 │ cinq │ sank │ │ │ │

└────────┴─────────────────┴──────────┴────────┴────────────────┴────────┘

```

Pronunciation Tips for 0–10:

Sept (7): The letter 'p' is completely silent! Say set, not "sept."
Huit (8): The letter 'h' is silent. Say weet.
Six (6) and Dix (10): The final 'x' sounds like an "s" (seess, deess).

2. The Teens: Counting from 11 to 20

The numbers from 11 to 16 follow a musical rhyme ending in -ze:

11: onze
12: douze
13: treize
14: quatorze
15: quinze
16: seize

From 17 to 19, French builds composite numbers using ten (dix) joined by a hyphen:

17: dix-sept ($10 + 7$, pronounced deess-set)
18: dix-huit ($10 + 8$, pronounced deez-weet)
19: dix-neuf ($10 + 9$, pronounced deez-nuff)
20: vingt (pronounced van; both the 'g' and 't' are silent!).

---

3. Counting from 21 to 30

Notice the special rule for numbers ending in "one":

21: vingt et un (notice the conjunction et meaning "and"; there are no hyphens!).
From 22 to 29, the numbers are connected with a simple hyphen without the word et:
22: vingt-deux
23: vingt-trois
24: vingt-quatre
25: vingt-cinq
26: vingt-six
27: vingt-sept
28: vingt-huit
29: vingt-neuf
30: trente (pronounced tront).

4. Asking and Stating Age in French

In French grammar, age is treated as a possession, not a state of being:

```

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│ AGE IN ENGLISH VERSUS FRENCH │

├──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤

│ ENGLISH MINDSET (TO BE) │ FRENCH MINDSET (AVOIR = TO HAVE) │

├──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤

│ "I AM twelve years old." │ "J'AI douze ans." (Literally: I have 12 yrs)│

│ (Uses the verb to be) │ (Uses the verb avoir = to have) │

├──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤

│ NEVER SAY IN FRENCH: │ "Je suis douze ans" (INCORRECT!) │

│ ALWAYS SAY IN FRENCH: │ "J'ai douze ans" (CORRECT!) │

└──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

```

The Formula for Stating Age:

$$\mathbf{J'ai} + [\text{Number}] + \mathbf{ans}$$

J'ai dix ans. (I am 10 years old.)
J'ai onze ans. (I am 11 years old.)
J'ai douze ans. (I am 12 years old.)
J'ai treize ans. (I am 13 years old.)

Asking Age:

Informal (to a friend/peer):
Quel âge as-tu? (How old are you?)
Tu as quel âge? (You have what age?)
Formal (to an elder or superior):
Quel âge avez-vous? (How old are you, Sir/Ma'am?)
Talking about Others:
Quel âge a-t-il? $\rightarrow$ Il a onze ans. (He is 11 years old.)
Quel âge a-t-elle? $\rightarrow$ Elle a douze ans. (She is 12 years old.)

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Age Practice at Home: Ask your child: "Quel âge as-tu?" Guide them to answer in a complete French sentence: "J'ai [number] ans!" Then ask them to state the ages of their brothers, sisters, or cousins in French using Il a... ans or Elle a... ans. - Market Counting Game: When buying fruits or groceries (e.g., oranges, eggs), challenge your child to count the items from 1 to 20 aloud in French (un, deux, trois, quatre... vingt). ---
