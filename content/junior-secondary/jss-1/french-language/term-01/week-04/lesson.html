Why This Matters to You

Imagine standing in front of an international youth conference in Abuja, or welcoming a foreign exchange student from Dakar, Senegal or Cotonou, Benin into your school compound. All eyes are on you. They smile warmly and ask: "Comment tu t'appelles?" What do you say? In English, you would say: "My name is Amina, I am 12 years old, and this is my friend Emeka." But how do you express that in French with charm, elegance, and perfect grammar? The secret lies in a beautiful reflexive verb: s'appeler—which literally means "to call oneself". In French, you do not just state your name as an object; you announce to the world: "Je m'appelle..." (literally, "I call myself...")! In this lesson, you will master the complete art of introducing yourself, presenting your friends and family members to others, exchanging polite courtesies like Enchanté(e) (Pleased to meet you), and commanding the fundamental French subject pronouns: Je, Tu, Il, Elle!

Core Concept

1. The Conjugation of the Verb *S'appeler* (To Be Called)

Se Présenter et Présenter Quelqu'un: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

To state your name or ask someone else's name, French uses the reflexive verb s'appeler:

```

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│ CONJUGATION OF THE VERB S'APPELER │

├────────────────────┬───────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┤

│ PRONOUN & VERB │ PRONUNCIATION │ ENGLISH MEANING │

├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ Je m'appelle │ zhuh mah-pell │ I am called / My name is │

├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ Tu t'appelles │ too tah-pell │ You are called (informal) │

├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ Il s'appelle │ eel sah-pell │ He is called / His name is│

├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ Elle s'appelle │ ell sah-pell │ She is called / Her name │

├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ Nous nous appelons │ noo nooz ah-puh-lon │ We are called │

├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ Vous vous appelez │ voo vooz ah-puh-lay │ You are called (formal/pl)│

├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ Ils s'appellent │ eel sah-pell │ They are called (masc/mix)│

├────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ Elles s'appellent │ ell sah-pell │ They are called (feminine)│

└────────────────────┴───────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘

```

Note the spelling rule: In the singular forms (je, tu, il, elle) and third-person plural (ils, elles), the letter 'l' is doubled (appelle). In the nous and vous forms, there is only a single 'l' (appelons, appelez).

2. Asking Names: Informal versus Formal

Informal (Classmates and peers):
Comment tu t'appelles? (What is your name?)
Comment t'appelles-tu? (Inverted form)
Tu t'appelles comment? (Colloquial / spoken form).
Formal (Elders, teachers, officials):
Comment vous vous appelez? (What is your name, Sir/Ma'am?)
Comment vous appelez-vous? (Polite inverted form).

3. The 5-Point Complete Self-Introduction

When a student introduces themselves in French, they weave together five foundational elements:

```

1.
SALUTATION:

"Bonjour, Monsieur / Bonjour, mes amis!" (Good morning, Sir / friends!)

1.
NAME:

"Je m'appelle Chukwudi Okonkwo." (My name is Chukwudi Okonkwo.)

1.
AGE:

"J'ai douze ans." (I am twelve years old.)

1.
NATIONALITY:

"Je suis nigérian." (Masc.) / "Je suis nigériane." (Fem.) (I am Nigerian.)

1.
RESIDENCE & STATUS:

"J'habite à Abuja et je suis élève en classe de JSS 1."

(I live in Abuja and I am a student in JSS 1.)

```

4. Introducing Someone Else (*Présenter Quelqu'un*)

To introduce a companion, classmate, or family member to others:

*Using Voici (Here is / Meet...):*
Voici mon ami. Il s'appelle Tunde. (Here is my male friend. His name is Tunde.)
Voici mon amie. Elle s'appelle Fatima. (Here is my female friend. Her name is Fatima.)
Voici ma mère. (Here is my mother.)
*Using C'est (This is...):*
C'est mon frère, Samuel. (This is my brother, Samuel.)
C'est ma sœur, Blessing. (This is my sister, Blessing.)
*The Polite Response: Enchanté(e):*
When introduced, you smile and say: Enchanté!
Spelling Agreement:
If a male writes it: Enchanté (one 'e').
If a female writes it: Enchantée (adds feminine 'e'). Both sound identical: on-shon-tay!

5. French Subject Pronouns (*Les Pronoms Sujets*)

Pronouns replace nouns and tell us who performs the action:

```

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│ FRENCH SUBJECT PRONOUNS │

├───────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┤

│ PRONOUN │ MEANING │ EXAMPLE │

├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤

│ Je (or J') │ I (first person singular) │ Je m'appelle Ada. │

├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤

│ Tu │ You (singular informal) │ Tu as douze ans. │

├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤

│ Il │ He / It (masculine singular) │ Il s'appelle Musa. │

├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤

│ Elle │ She / It (feminine singular) │ Elle s'appelle Ngozi. │

├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤

│ Nous │ We (first person plural) │ Nous sommes nigérians. │

├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤

│ Vous │ You (formal singular / plural)│ Vous êtes le professeur│

├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤

│ Ils │ They (all-male or mixed group)│ Ils sont étudiants. │

├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤

│ Elles │ They (strictly all-female) │ Elles sont amies. │

└───────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┘

```
