Core Concept

1. Greetings Based on the Time of Day

Les Salutations et Prendre Congé: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

In the French language, choosing the right greeting depends on the time of day:

Bonjour (Dawn to Sunset):
Formed from two words: bon (good) and jour (day).
Used from sunrise until dusk to mean "Good morning" or "Good afternoon."
Polite additions: Always add the title of respect when speaking to adults:
Bonjour, Monsieur (Good morning, Sir / Mr.)
Bonjour, Madame (Good morning, Ma'am / Mrs.)
Bonjour, Mademoiselle (Good morning, Miss)
Bonsoir (Evening / Dusk onwards):
Formed from bon (good) and soir (evening).
Used from late afternoon (around 5:00 PM) throughout the night when arriving at a gathering:
Bonsoir, Monsieur (Good evening, Sir)
Bonsoir, mes amis (Good evening, my friends)
Bonne nuit (Bedtime only):
Used ONLY when parting at night to go to sleep:
Bonne nuit, maman! (Good night, mother!)
Bonne nuit et faites de beaux rêves (Good night and sweet dreams).

2. Formal versus Informal Registers: *Vous* versus *Tu*

Just as in Nigerian cultures where Yoruba uses the respectful pronoun E or Hausa uses Ku when addressing parents and teachers, French has two distinct registers:

3. Asking and Responding to "How Are You?"

Mastering conversational exchanges of wellbeing:

Questions:
Comment ça va? (How are you doing? - neutral/everyday)
Ça va? (Doing okay? - short/casual)
Comment vas-tu? (How are you? - informal, addressing a friend)
Comment allez-vous? (How are you? - formal, addressing an elder or group)
Responses:
Ça va très bien, merci! (I am doing very well, thank you!)
Ça va bien. (I am fine.)
Pas mal. (Not bad.)
Comme ci, comme ça. (So-so / manageable.)
Ça ne va pas. (Things are not going well.)
Returning the Courtesy:
Et toi? (And you? - informal)
Et vous? (And you? - formal / respectful)

4. Taking Leave (*Prendre Congé*)

When departing from a conversation or class, polite French speakers say:

Au revoir: The standard polite "Goodbye" (used with everyone).
À bientôt: "See you soon!" (when expecting to meet again within a few days).
À demain: "See you tomorrow!" (used leaving school in the afternoon).
À tout à l'heure: "See you in a little while!" (later today).
Salut: Can also be used among close friends to mean "Bye!"
Bonne journée: "Have a good day!" (wished when departing in the morning).
Bonne soirée: "Have a good evening!" (wished when departing in the evening).
