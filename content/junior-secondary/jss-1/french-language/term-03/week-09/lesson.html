Core Concept

1. Describing the Weather: The Impersonal Formula "Il Fait..."

Le Climat, Les Saisons et L'Importance de la Francophonie: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

To describe the weather in French, we do not say "The weather is...". Instead, we use the impersonal pronoun Il fait... followed by an adjective or noun:

```

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│ EXPRESSIONS DU TEMPS QU'IL FAIT │

├─────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┤

│ WEATHER CONDITION │ FRENCH PHRASE │ PRONUNCIATION │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ The weather is fine │ Il fait beau. │ eel fay bo │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ It is hot │ Il fait chaud. │ eel fay sho │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ It is cold │ Il fait froid. │ eel fay frwah │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ It is sunny │ Il fait du soleil. │ eel fay doo so-lay │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ It is windy │ Il fait du vent. │ eel fay doo vahn │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ It is bad weather │ Il fait mauvais. │ eel fay mo-vay │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ It is raining │ Il pleut. │ eel pluh │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ There are clouds │ Il y a des nuages. │ eel ya day noo-ahzh │

├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤

│ There is a storm │ Il y a de l'orage. │ eel ya duh lo-rahzh │

└─────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘

```

> [!IMPORTANT]

> Never Say "Le temps est chaud" or "Il est chaud"!

> In French, stating that the weather is hot requires Il fait chaud. Saying Il est chaud means a specific object or person is hot to the touch or feverish!

2. The Seasons: West Africa vs The Temperate World

In West Africa and Nigeria, our tropical climate features two primary seasons, alongside the distinct harmattan period:

1.
La saison des pluies (Wet / Rainy Season): From April to October in southern and central Nigeria (Pendant la saison des pluies, il pleut souvent et la terre est très verte.)
2.
La saison sèche (Dry Season): From November to March (Pendant la saison sèche, il fait très chaud et il ne pleut presque pas.)
3.
L'harmattan: A unique dry, hazy atmospheric season (Pendant l'harmattan, il fait frais le matin et la poussière du Sahara souffle partout.)

The Four Temperate Seasons (Les Quatre Saisons Tempérées):

In France and temperate regions, the year has four seasons:

Le printemps: Spring (warm, flowers bloom).
L'été: Summer (hot, long sunny days).
L'automne: Autumn / Fall (leaves turn orange and drop).
L'hiver: Winter (cold, snow and frost).

3. Why French is a Strategic Superpower for Nigeria

Why does the Federal Government of Nigeria and the NERDC national curriculum make French compulsory for junior secondary students?

1.
Geographical Reality: Nigeria shares thousands of kilometers of land borders with four Francophone nations:
Le Bénin (capital: Porto-Novo) to the west.
Le Niger (capital: Niamey) to the north.
Le Tchad (capital: N'Djamena) to the northeast.
Le Cameroun (capital: Yaoundé) to the east.
1.
Economic & Trade Power: Over 120 million people in neighboring nations conduct business in French. Nigerian traders, manufacturers, bankers, and tech entrepreneurs who speak French can sell products across the entire ECOWAS common market.
2.
Regional Diplomacy & Security: Joint military operations around Lake Chad (the Multinational Joint Task Force) and diplomatic missions at ECOWAS headquarters in Abuja require fluent bilingual communication between Nigerian commanders and Francophone allies.
3.
Global Career Advantage: French is an official language of the United Nations, African Union, International Olympic Committee, FIFA, and the World Trade Organization. A bilingual Nigerian professional is twice as employable worldwide!
