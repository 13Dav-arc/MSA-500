Core Concept

1. The Three Verb Groups in French

Les Verbes du Premier Groupe (-ER au Présent): Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

French verbs are divided into three major categories based on their infinitive endings:

1.
First Group (-ER): Over 80% of all French verbs (parler, habiter, aimer, chanter, danser, étudier). They are completely regular! (Note: The only irregular verb ending in -er is aller - to go).
2.
Second Group (-IR): Regular verbs ending in -ir that take -issant in the plural (finir, choisir).
3.
Third Group (-RE, -OIR, irregular -IR): Irregular verbs (être, avoir, faire, prendre).

---

2. The Golden 2-Step Rule for Conjugating -ER Verbs

To conjugate any regular -er verb in the present tense (le présent de l'indicatif), follow this simple two-step rule:

1.
*Drop the -er from the infinitive to find the radical* (stem).
parler $\rightarrow$ radical = parl-
habiter $\rightarrow$ radical = habit-
aimer $\rightarrow$ radical = aim-
1.
Add the magical present tense endings corresponding to each subject pronoun:

```

┌─────────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────────┐

│ SUBJECT PRONOUN │ ENDING │ PARLER (TO SPEAK)│ PRONUNCIATION │

├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤

│ Je (I) │ -e │ Je parle │ zhuh parl │

├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤

│ Tu (You - sing.)│ -es │ Tu parles │ too parl │

├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤

│ Il / Elle (He/She)│ -e │ Il / Elle parle │ eel / el parl │

├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤

│ Nous (We) │ -ons │ Nous parlons │ noo par-lohn │

├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤

│ Vous (You - pl.)│ -ez │ Vous parlez │ voo par-lay │

├─────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────────┤

│ Ils / Elles (They)│ -ent │ Ils / Elles parlent│ eel / el parl│

└─────────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────────┘

```

> [!IMPORTANT]

> The Silent 'E' Secret of French Verbs (The "Boot" Verbs):

> Notice that for Je, Tu, Il/Elle, and Ils/Elles, the endings (-e, -es, -e, -ent) are completely SILENT!

> In all four cases, parle, parles, parle, and parlent are pronounced identically as /paʁl/ (parl)!

> Only Nous (-ons) and Vous (-ez) have endings that are sounded out (par-lohn and par-lay).

---

3. Elision with Verbs Starting with a Vowel or Silent 'H'

When a verb begins with a vowel or silent h (like aimer, habiter, écouter):

The subject pronoun Je contracts to J' (élision):
J'aime (NOT Je aime) - I like / I love.
J'habite (NOT Je habite) - I live.
J'écoute (NOT Je écoute) - I listen.
A liaison occurs with plural pronouns:
Nous habitons $\rightarrow$ pronounced noo-zah-bee-tohn (the silent 's' links as a 'z' sound).
Vous aimez $\rightarrow$ pronounced voo-zay-may.
Ils écoutent $\rightarrow$ pronounced eel-zay-koot.

4. Common -ER Verbs in Daily School and Home Life

Here is your essential vocabulary of first-group action verbs:

Parler: To speak (Je parle français et anglais - I speak French and English).
Habiter: To live (J'habite à Abuja au Nigéria - I live in Abuja, Nigeria).
Aimer: To like/love (J'aime le football et la musique - I love football and music).
Regarder: To watch (Nous regardons un match - We are watching a match).
Écouter: To listen (Tu écoutes la radio - You listen to the radio).
Chanter: To sing (Elle chante une belle chanson - She sings a beautiful song).
Danser: To dance (Ils dansent le bata - They dance the Bata dance).
Étudier: To study (J'étudie les sciences - I study science).
Manger: To eat (Nous mangeons du riz - We eat rice).
Travailler: To work (Mon père travaille à Lagos - My father works in Lagos).

---

5. Making Sentences Negative: *Ne ... Pas*

To say that you do NOT do an action, sandwich the conjugated verb between ne and pas:

$$\text{Sujet} + \mathbf{ne} + \text{Verbe Conjugé} + \mathbf{pas} + \text{Complément}$$

Examples:

Je parle yoruba. $\rightarrow$ Je ne parle pas yoruba. (I do not speak Yoruba).
Il regarde la télévision. $\rightarrow$ Il ne regarde pas la télévision. (He is not watching television).
Before a vowel or silent 'H', ne becomes n':
J'aime le poisson. $\rightarrow$ Je n'aime pas le poisson. (I do not like fish).
Il habite à Kano. $\rightarrow$ Il n'habite pas à Kano. (He does not live in Kano).

Home Application & Guardian Sync

- Home Practice Prompt: Ask your child to tell you three things they like (J'aime...) and three things they do not like (Je n'aime pas...) in French. Examples: J'aime les mathématiques (I like mathematics), Je n'aime pas le bruit (I don't like noise). - The "Silent Boot" Pronunciation Check: Have your child read aloud: Je parle, tu parles, il parle, ils parlent. Verify that they pronounce all four words identically (parl) and do not pronounce the -s or -ent! - Real-World Relevance: When travelling across West Africa, being able to say J'habite au Nigéria (I live in Nigeria) and Je parle un peu français (I speak a little French) immediately creates friendly rapport with border officials, merchants, and hotel staff.
