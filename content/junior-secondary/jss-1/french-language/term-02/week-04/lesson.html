Why This Matters to You

When your alarm clock rings on Monday morning, you leap out of bed knowing that a brand-new school week has begun! When Friday afternoon arrives, students cheer because the weekend is finally here! And when December rolls around, families across Nigeria celebrate the holidays with festive feasts, church services, and the cool breezes of the harmattan season. Humans have always organized their lives around the rhythmic dance of the calendar. But did you know that in French, the seven days of the week were named after the ancient planets of our solar system (the Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, and Saturn)? And unlike English where Monday and December are always capitalized, in French, the days of the week and months of the year are never capitalized! In this lesson, you will master the seven days of the week, the twelve months of the year, learn how to state today's date, and celebrate your birthday in French!

1. The Seven Days of the Week (*Les Jours de la Semaine*)

Les Jours de la Semaine et les Mois de l'Année: Core Concepts and Pedagogical Illustration

In the French calendar, the week begins on lundi (Monday):

```

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│ THE SEVEN DAYS OF THE WEEK │

├──────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────┬───────────────────────┤

│ FRENCH DAY │ PRONUNCIATION │ ENGLISH DAY │ ANCIENT ORIGIN │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ lundi │ luhn-dee │ Monday │ Moon's day (Lune) │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ mardi │ mar-dee │ Tuesday │ Mars' day │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ mercredi │ mair-kruh-dee │ Wednesday │ Mercury's day │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ jeudi │ zhuh-dee │ Thursday │ Jupiter's day │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ vendredi │ von-druh-dee │ Friday │ Venus' day │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ samedi │ sahm-dee │ Saturday │ Saturn's day │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ dimanche │ dee-monsh │ Sunday │ Lord's day (Dominus) │

└──────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────┴───────────────────────┘

```

The Capitalization Rule:

In French grammar, the days of the week are written in LOWERCASE letters (e.g., lundi, mardi), unless they appear as the first word of a sentence! Writing "Je vais à l'école le Lundi" with a capital 'L' is an English capitalization error.

2. The Twelve Months of the Year (*Les Mois de l'Année*)

The twelve months are all grammatically masculine and also written in lowercase:

```

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│ THE TWELVE MONTHS OF THE YEAR │

├──────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────┬───────────────────────┤

│ FRENCH MONTH │ PRONUNCIATION │ ENGLISH │ SPECIAL NOTES │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ janvier │ zhon-vyay │ January │ First month of year │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ février │ fay-vree-yay │ February │ Has acute accents (é) │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ mars │ marss │ March │ Final 's' is sounded! │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ avril │ ah-vreel │ April │ Easter season │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ mai │ may │ May │ Workers' Day (1er mai)│

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ juin │ zhwahn │ June │ Mid-year month │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ juillet │ zhwee-yay │ July │ French National Day │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

│ août │ oot │ August │ Pronounced oot! │

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

├──────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┼───────────────────────┤

└──────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

3. Stating the Date in French

To state the date, French strictly follows the order: le + [Number] + [Month]:

The Question:
Quelle est la date aujourd'hui? (What is the date today?)
The Response Formula:

$$\mathbf{Aujourd'hui,\ c'est\ le} + [\text{Numéro}] + [\text{Mois}]$$

Aujourd'hui, c'est le 15 mars. (Today is March 15th.)
C'est le 25 décembre. (It is December 25th.)
Demain, c'est le 16 mars. (Tomorrow is March 16th.)

The Special Rule for the First Day of the Month (*Le Premier*):

For the first day of any month, French does NOT use the cardinal number "un". You must use the ordinal number le premier (written as le 1er):

Nigeria's Independence Day: Le premier octobre (October 1st) - NEVER "le un octobre".
New Year's Day: Le premier janvier (January 1st).
For all other days (2 to 31), standard numbers are used: le deux, le trois, le quatre....

4. Birthday Celebrations (*L'Anniversaire*)

Asking Birthday:
Quelle est la date de ton anniversaire? (When is your birthday?)
C'est quand, ton anniversaire? (When is your birthday? - informal)
Answering:
Mon anniversaire est le 10 juin. (My birthday is June 10th.)
Je suis né le 14 février. (I was born on February 14th.)
Wishing Someone Well:
Joyeux anniversaire! (Happy Birthday!).
