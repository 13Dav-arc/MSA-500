Foundational curriculum module in French Language covering "La Description Physique et Morale: Physical and Character Adjectives, Gender and Number Agreement in French" for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Look around your school assembly ground or classroom. You see people of all shapes, sizes, and personalities! Perhaps your deskmate is tall and athletic, while your friend in the front row is short, energetic, and wears glasses. Your English teacher might be patient and smiling, while your coach is energetic and demanding. How do you describe these wonderful traits in French? In English, adjectives are completely rigid: whether you talk about a tall boy, a tall girl, or ten tall men, the word "tall" never changes. But in French, adjectives are like dynamic dancers—they change their clothing to match the person they describe! A boy is grand, but a girl is grande! A hardworking boy is travailleur, but a hardworking girl is travailleuse! This fascinating grammar rule is called L'Accord des Adjectifs (Adjective Agreement). In this lesson, you will master physical and moral adjectives, conquer gender and plural agreement rules, and paint vivid word portraits of people in French!

3. Rules for Forming the Feminine of Adjectives

To convert a masculine adjective to its feminine form, follow these five golden rules:

1.
The Universal Rule (Add '-e'):
Simply add an unaccented -e to the masculine singular form:
grand $\rightarrow$ grande
petit $\rightarrow$ petite
intelligent $\rightarrow$ intelligente
poli $\rightarrow$ polie.
Phonetic Change: Adding '-e' makes the final consonant (which was previously silent in grand or petit) voiced! You pronounce the 'd' in grande (gron-duh) and the 't' in petite (puh-teet).
1.
Invariable Adjectives (Already ending in '-e'):
If an adjective already ends with an unaccented -e in the masculine, do not add another 'e'! It remains identical in both genders:
mince (masc.) $\rightarrow$ mince (fem.)
jeune (masc.) $\rightarrow$ jeune (fem.)
timide (masc.) $\rightarrow$ timide (fem.)
sympathique (masc.) $\rightarrow$ sympathique (fem.).
1.
Adjectives ending in '-eur' $\rightarrow$ '-euse':
travailleur $\rightarrow$ travailleuse (hardworking).
1.
Adjectives ending in '-eux' $\rightarrow$ '-euse':
paresseux $\rightarrow$ paresseuse (lazy)
heureux $\rightarrow$ heureuse (happy).
1.
Special Irregular Feminines:
beau $\rightarrow$ belle
vieux $\rightarrow$ vieille
gros $\rightarrow$ grosse
gentil $\rightarrow$ gentille.

4. Rules for Forming the Plural of Adjectives

Adjectives must also match the number of the noun (singular vs. plural):

The Standard Plural Rule:
Add an -s to the singular form (masculine or feminine):
Il est grand. $\rightarrow$ Ils sont grands.
Elle est grande. $\rightarrow$ Elles sont grandes.
The Invariable Plural Rule:
Masculine singular adjectives already ending in -s or -x do not change in the plural:
un garçon gros $\rightarrow$ des garçons gros (no change!)
un homme vieux $\rightarrow$ des hommes vieux (no change!).
