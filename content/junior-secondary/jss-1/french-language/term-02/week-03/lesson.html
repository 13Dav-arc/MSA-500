Why This Matters to You

Look at the Nigerian national flag waving proudly above your school: vibrant green, pure white, and vibrant green (vert, blanc, vert)! Look at your school uniform: perhaps a cobalt blue shirt with navy trousers, or a bright yellow dress with neat black shoes. Colors give life, mood, and beauty to everything in our world! But how do you describe colors in French? In English, you always put the color before the noun: you say "a blue pen" or "a green leaf." But in French, the word order is completely flipped: you say un stylo bleu and une feuille verte! The noun comes first, and the color follows behind! Even more intriguing: just like living beings, colors in French change their clothes to match the gender and number of the noun they describe—except for two curious colors derived from fruits and nuts that refuse to change for anyone! In this lesson, you will master the French color palette, learn where to place colors in a sentence, and conquer the rules of color agreement!

2. The Golden Rule of Word Order

In English, the color comes before the noun. In French, the color almost always comes AFTER the noun:

English: a blue pen $\rightarrow$ French: un stylo bleu
English: a green ruler $\rightarrow$ French: une règle verte
English: a black board $\rightarrow$ French: un tableau noir
English: a red shirt $\rightarrow$ French: une chemise rouge
Never say: "un bleu stylo" (This is an English word-order error!).

3. Rules of Gender and Number Agreement for Colors

Colors must match the gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural) of the noun they describe:

1.
Regular Feminine (+ 'e'):
vert $\rightarrow$ verte (e.g., la règle est verte)
bleu $\rightarrow$ bleue (e.g., la porte est bleue)
noir $\rightarrow$ noire (e.g., la chaise est noire)
gris $\rightarrow$ grise (e.g., la trousse est grise).
1.
Colors Ending in '-e' (Invariable in Gender):
If a color already ends in unaccented -e, it remains the same in the feminine:
un livre rouge $\rightarrow$ une table rouge
un stylo jaune $\rightarrow$ une gomme jaune
un sac rose $\rightarrow$ une chemise rose.
1.
Irregular Feminines:
blanc $\rightarrow$ blanche (e.g., une feuille blanche = a white sheet of paper).
violet $\rightarrow$ violette (e.g., une robe violette = a purple dress).
1.
Plural Formation (+ 's'):
Standard colors add an -s in the plural:
des stylos bleus (blue pens)
des règles vertes (green rulers)
des chaises noires (black chairs).

---

4. The Two Royal Invariable Colors: Orange and Marron

French grammar has a special rule for colors named after natural fruits and nuts:

Orange (named after the fruit orange) and Marron (named after the chestnut) are COMPLETELY INVARIABLE!
They NEVER take an '-e' in the feminine!
They NEVER take an '-s' in the plural!
une table orange (feminine singular, no '-e' added)
des cahiers orange (plural, NO '-s' added!)
des chaussures marron (plural feminine, NO '-es' added!).
