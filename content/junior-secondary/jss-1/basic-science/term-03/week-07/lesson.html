Week 07: Space Travel — Exploration, Benefits, and Hazards

Foundational curriculum module in Basic Science covering Space Travel — Exploration, Benefits, and Hazards for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

For hundreds of years, humans looked up at the stars and dreamed of what it would be like to leave our atmosphere and walk among them. That dream became a reality when powerful rockets were invented, allowing humans to travel into outer space, orbit the Earth, and even walk on the surface of the Moon. But venturing into the dark, silent vacuum of space is not easy—it requires advanced science, enormous financial cost, and incredible courage to face severe dangers.

Key Terms

Space Travel The journey of humans and spacecraft from the Earth into outer space.
Space The vast environment surrounding the Earth, Moon, and other planets.
Astronaut (Cosmonaut) A specially trained human traveller who journeys into outer space aboard a spacecraft.
Rocket A high-powered vehicle propelled by engines that carries spacecraft and astronauts through the atmosphere into space.
Weightlessness The physical sensation experienced by astronauts in orbit where they do not feel the pulling force of gravity.

Core Concept

Meaning and History of Space Travel

Space travel is the art and technology of travelling from the Earth into outer space. Space is the environment that surrounds the Earth, Moon, and all other planets.

For generations, space travel was only a dream until the invention of huge, high-powered rockets (such as the American Saturn V rocket, the largest rockets ever built) made space travel a reality.
On April 12, 1961, Russian cosmonaut Major Yuri Gagarin blasted into orbit, becoming the first human ever to travel into space and complete an orbit of the Earth.
On July 21, 1969, American astronaut Neil Armstrong became the first human to step onto the surface of the Moon, followed by Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin. Later missions, such as Apollo 15 in 1971 (with astronauts Eugene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt), allowed astronauts to stay for nearly 75 hours on the lunar surface.

Purpose and Advantages of Space Travel

Space travel is expensive to run, but it provides immense benefits and serves several major purposes:

1.
Advancing Scientific Research: Experiments conducted in space show that certain materials and proteins form more perfectly in space than they do on Earth, making space-grown proteins valuable for designing new medicines.
2.
Weather Forecasting and Agriculture: Orbiting weather satellites help meteorologists monitor storms, track hurricanes, and forecast weather to protect farmers and communities.
3.
Telecommunications and Navigation: Space satellites make global telephone communication, television broadcasting, internet connections, and GPS location tracking fast and affordable.
4.
Predicting Natural Disasters: Satellites help monitor and predict natural disasters before they strike.
5.
Technological Spin-offs: Space research has led to the production of advanced photographic equipment, X-ray and gamma-ray machines, and alternative power sources like space solar power.
6.
Broader Human Goals: Space exploration aims to gain knowledge useful to humanity, unite different nations through joint projects like the International Space Station, and ensure the future survival of human civilization.

Dangers and Challenges of Space Travel

Exploring outer space is extremely risky and comes with severe hazards:

Inability to Live Permanently: Human beings cannot live permanently in outer space due to harsh physical constraints. Astronauts who stay in space for long periods develop weak bones caused by the loss of calcium.
Risk to Life: Space flight involves extreme danger that can lead to tragic loss of life, such as the disappearance of the American space flight Avenger with 11 crew members in 1980.
Penetrating Radiation: Dangerous, invisible radiation exists in space, making long-term space travel hazardous to human health.
Unknown Pathogens: Bringing extraterrestrial materials back to Earth risks introducing unknown diseases.

Comparing the Benefits and Hazards of Space Travel

Scientific Advances

Medicine & Research vs Weather Forecasting

Space research allows scientists to grow perfect protein crystals for new medicines, while weather satellites track storms and predict natural disasters to save lives.

Global Communication

Telecommunications vs GPS Navigation

Satellites make long-distance phone calls and internet connections cheap and instant, while GPS networks help ships, aircraft, and individuals pinpoint their exact location anywhere on Earth.

Physiological Hazards

Bone Weakness vs Space Radiation

Long-term weightlessness causes astronauts to lose bone calcium and grow weak, while cosmic space radiation poses severe risks to human health.

Operational Risks

Mission Failures vs Unknown Diseases

Space travel carries extreme mechanical risks that can lead to tragic mission losses, alongside theoretical risks of bringing unknown alien diseases back to Earth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Believing that human beings can easily pack up and move to live permanently in outer space today.

Scientific Fact

Human biology is adapted to Earth; prolonged exposure to weightlessness causes bone weakening, and humans cannot live permanently in space without specialized life support.

Common Mistake

Thinking that space travel is purely for entertainment and has no practical use on Earth.

Scientific Fact

Space travel drives vital practical technologies including weather forecasting, GPS navigation, global communication, and medical research.

Worked Examples

Example A: The First Human in Space

Context: A science class is discussing historical milestones in space exploration.

Problem: Who was the first human to travel into space, and what country did he represent?

Fix: Major Yuri Gagarin, a Soviet (Russian) cosmonaut, blasted into orbit on April 12, 1961, becoming the first person in space.

Example B: Tracking Severe Weather with Satellites

Context: Meteorologists in the Caribbean need to track a swirling hurricane before it hits populated coastal towns.

Problem: How do scientists monitor dangerous storms across vast oceans?

Fix: They use specialized weather satellites orbiting in space to photograph and track storm clouds (such as Hurricane Mitch), allowing them to forecast weather and warn the public.

Example C: Evaluating Bone Health in Astronauts

Context: An astronaut returns to Earth after spending six months living and working aboard an orbiting space station.

Problem: Why must doctors examine the astronaut's bones carefully after a long space mission?

Fix: Because human bones do not experience normal gravity in space, long-term space travel causes a dangerous loss of calcium, leading to weak bones.
