Week 06: The Earth in Space — The Solar System, Rotation, and Eclipses

Foundational curriculum module in Basic Science covering The Earth in Space — The Solar System, Rotation, and Eclipses for JSS 1.

Why This Matters to You

Right now, even as you sit still reading this lesson, our planet Earth is speeding through outer space around the Sun! Why don't we feel the wind rushing past us or get thrown off as the Earth moves? We do not notice this rapid movement because the blanket of air surrounding the Earth is carried right along with it. Let us journey into space to explore our Solar System, how day turns into night, and why the sky sometimes goes dark in the middle of the afternoon.

Key Terms

Solar System The Sun together with the eight planets, their moons, dwarf planets, comets, and asteroids that revolve around it.
Rotation of the Earth The spinning movement of the Earth on its own axis through the North and South poles, which takes 24 hours (one day) and causes day and night.
Revolution of the Earth The journey of the Earth completely around the Sun, which takes 365 days (one year) and brings about the seasons.
Satellite A natural or artificial body in space that revolves around the Earth or another planet, such as the Moon.
Eclipse A natural shadow event that occurs when one heavenly body moves between the Sun and another body, blocking direct sunlight.

Core Concept

The Solar System and the Eight Planets

The Sun, along with all the heavenly bodies that revolve around it, makes up our Solar System. The Solar System consists of the Sun at the centre, eight planets and their moons, dwarf planets, comets, and asteroids.

In order of their distance from the Sun, the eight planets are:

Mercury

4.

5.

7.

Scientists divide the Solar System into two main sections separated by a ring of rocky objects called the asteroid belt:

The Inner Part: Contains the Sun, Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars.
The Outer Part: Contains the giant planets Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.
The Solar System: Inner Planets, Asteroid Belt, and Outer Planets

Rotation and Revolution of the Earth and Moon

The Earth and the Moon carry out two distinct movements in space:

1. Rotation of the Earth (24 Hours)

The Earth spins continuously around an imaginary line called its axis, which runs through the North and South poles. It takes 24 hours (one day) for the Earth to complete one full rotation. As the Earth rotates, different parts of its surface turn toward the Sun's light. The side of the Earth receiving light from the Sun experiences day, while the side turned away from the Sun is in darkness and experiences night.

2. Movement and Phases of the Moon (29 Days)

The Moon is the natural satellite of the Earth and revolves around our planet once in about 29 days. As the Moon travels around the Earth, we see five main phases of the Moon in the night sky:

New Moon
Quarter Moon
Half Moon
Three-Quarter Moon
Full Moon

3. Revolution of the Earth (365 Days / 1 Year)

While spinning on its axis, the Earth also travels in a large path around the Sun. This movement is called revolution, and it takes 365 days (1 year) to complete one full journey around the Sun. The revolution of the Earth, combined with the tilting of the Earth's axis, causes seasons (such as summer and winter). When the northern axis tilts toward the Sun, that half of the world has summer, while the southern axis tilts away from the Sun and experiences winter.

Eclipse of the Sun and the Moon

An eclipse happens when a heavenly body moves directly into the path of sunlight, casting a shadow in space. Because light travels in straight lines, blocking the Sun's rays creates darkness on the body behind it. There are two types of eclipses:

1.
Eclipse of the Sun (Solar Eclipse): Occurs when the Moon comes directly between the Sun and the Earth (`Sun -> Moon -> Earth`), blocking sunlight from reaching parts of the Earth.
2.
Eclipse of the Moon (Lunar Eclipse): Occurs when the Earth comes directly between the Sun and the Moon (`Sun -> Earth -> Moon`), blocking sunlight from reaching the Moon.
Alignments of the Sun, Earth, and Moon During Solar and Lunar Eclipses

Comparing Space Movements and Solar System Regions

Planetary Regions

Inner vs Outer Solar System

The inner part lies inside the asteroid belt and holds Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars, while the outer part lies beyond the asteroid belt and holds Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

Earth's Movements

Rotation vs Revolution

Rotation is the Earth spinning on its axis every 24 hours to cause day and night, whereas revolution is the Earth orbiting the Sun every 365 days to cause the seasons.

Shadow Events

Eclipse of the Sun vs Eclipse of the Moon

An eclipse of the Sun happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth, whereas an eclipse of the Moon happens when the Earth passes between the Sun and Moon.

Orbiting Bodies

Planet Earth vs The Moon

The Earth is a planet that revolves around the Sun in 365 days, while the Moon is a natural satellite that revolves around the Earth in about 29 days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Thinking that day and night happen because the Sun moves around the Earth every day.

Scientific Fact

The Sun stays at the centre of the Solar System; day and night happen because the Earth rotates on its own axis every 24 hours, turning different sides toward the Sun.

Common Mistake

Confusing the positions of the Earth and Moon during Solar and Lunar eclipses.

Scientific Fact

Remember **SME** (Sun–Moon–Earth) for a Solar eclipse, where the **M**oon is in the middle; and **SEM** (Sun–Earth–Moon) for a Lunar eclipse, where the **E**arth is in the middle.

Worked Examples

Example A: Explaining Why We Don't Feel the Earth Moving

Context: During a science class, a student asks why nobody gets blown over by wind if the Earth is constantly flying through space around the Sun.

Problem: Explaining why Earth's motion through space feels completely calm to us on the ground.

Fix: The Earth does not travel alone—gravity holds the entire atmosphere (the air around the Earth) so that it is carried along at the exact same speed as the ground beneath our feet.

Example B: Identifying an Eclipse in the Daytime

Context: At 1:00 PM on a sunny afternoon, a dark disk slowly covers the Sun until the sky becomes dark for a few minutes.

Problem: What type of eclipse is taking place, and which heavenly body is in the middle?

Fix: Because the Sun itself is being blocked from our view on Earth, this is an **Eclipse of the Sun (Solar Eclipse)**. It happens because the **Moon** has moved directly between the Sun and the Earth.

Example C: Sorting Planets by the Asteroid Belt

Context: A student is building a model of the Solar System and places Mars, Jupiter, and Earth on the same side of the asteroid belt.

Problem: One of those three planets is on the wrong side of the asteroid belt.

Fix: The asteroid belt divides the inner planets from the outer planets. Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars belong in the **inner part**, while **Jupiter** must be moved across the asteroid belt into the **outer part** along with Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.
