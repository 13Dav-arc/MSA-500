Why This Matters to You

On a cold harmattan morning, have you ever rubbed your two palms together quickly and felt them become warm within seconds? Or have you wondered how a metal electric pressing iron, cold to the touch when unplugged, gets hot enough to smooth out your wrinkled school uniform once switched on? Energy does not just sit still—it travels and constantly changes from one form to another. In this lesson, you will discover the golden rule of energy and learn how everyday machines change energy to do amazing work.

Key Terms

Energy Transformation The process of changing energy from one form into another form to perform useful work.
Law of Conservation of Energy A fundamental scientific law stating that energy cannot be created or destroyed, but can only be changed from one form to another.
Potential Energy (PE) The stored energy an object possesses because of its position, height, or state, calculated using the formula $PE = mgh$.
Kinetic Energy (KE) The energy possessed by an object due to its physical motion, calculated using the formula $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.
Energy Converter Any appliance, machine, or biological organism that changes energy from one form into another.

Core Concept

The Law of Conservation of Energy

Energy is never made from nothing, and it never vanishes into thin air. The Law of Conservation of Energy states:

> Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, but it can only be transformed from one form to another.

This means the total amount of energy in the entire universe always remains the same. When an appliance stops working or an engine runs out of petrol, the energy was not destroyed—it was simply converted into other forms, such as heat, sound, or light.

Energy Transformations in Everyday Life

Every day, we use different appliances and devices that act as energy converters:

Electric Pressing Iron & Boiling Ring: Electrical energy $\rightarrow$ Heat (thermal) energy.
Electric Bulb / Fluorescent Tube: Electrical energy $\rightarrow$ Light energy (and some heat).
Electric Standing Fan / Blender: Electrical energy $\rightarrow$ Mechanical (kinetic) energy.
Torchlight with Dry Cells (Batteries): Chemical energy $\rightarrow$ Electrical energy $\rightarrow$ Light energy.
Ringing a School Bell / Beating a Drum: Mechanical energy $\rightarrow$ Sound energy.
Microphone: Sound energy $\rightarrow$ Electrical energy (which travels through sound wires).
Loudspeaker: Electrical energy $\rightarrow$ Sound energy (vibrating the speaker cone).
Motor Car / Generator Engine: Chemical energy (petrol or diesel) $\rightarrow$ Thermal energy $\rightarrow$ Mechanical (kinetic) energy.
Rubbing Palms Together: Mechanical energy $\rightarrow$ Heat energy.
Photosynthesis in Green Plants: Radiant (solar light) energy $\rightarrow$ Stored chemical energy (food).
Common Energy Transformations in the Home and Society

Basic Mechanical Energy Calculations

In physics, we can easily calculate how much mechanical energy an object holds using two simple formulas.

#### 1. Gravitational Potential Energy ($PE$)

When an object is lifted off the ground, it stores potential energy due to gravity:

$$\text{Potential Energy } (PE) = m \times g \times h$$

$m$ = Mass of the object in kilograms ($\text{kg}$)
$g$ = Acceleration due to gravity on Earth (approximately $10\text{ m/s}^2$)
$h$ = Height above the ground in metres ($\text{m}$)
The final answer is always written in Joules (J).

#### 2. Kinetic Energy ($KE$)

When an object is actively moving with speed, it possesses kinetic energy:

$$\text{Kinetic Energy } (KE) = \frac{1}{2} \times m \times v^2$$

$m$ = Mass of the object in kilograms ($\text{kg}$)
$v$ = Speed or velocity of the object in metres per second ($\text{m/s}$)
$v^2$ means multiplying the speed by itself ($v \times v$).
The final answer is also written in Joules (J).

Comparing Common Energy Converters in Society

Heating Devices

Electric Iron & Gas Cooker

An electric iron transforms electrical energy into heat energy, whereas a gas cooker burns cooking gas to convert chemical energy into heat energy.

Sound Devices

Microphone & Loudspeaker

A microphone converts spoken sound vibrations into electrical signals, whereas a loudspeaker takes electrical signals and turns them back into audible sound energy.

Motion Devices

Electric Fan & Car Engine

An electric fan converts electric current into rotational kinetic energy, while a car engine burns fuel to convert stored chemical energy into kinetic motion.

Natural Converters

Plant Photosynthesis & Human Body

Green leaves transform solar light energy into chemical energy stored in food, while our bodies burn that chemical food energy to produce motion and body warmth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Believing that energy is completely destroyed when a rolling ball comes to a stop.

Scientific Fact

Energy cannot be destroyed; friction between the ball and the ground slowly transforms the kinetic energy into invisible heat and faint sound energy.

Common Mistake

Thinking that an electric bulb or flashlight creates light out of nothing.

Scientific Fact

Bulbs cannot create energy; they only convert the electrical or battery chemical energy supplied to them into light and heat energy.
