Core Concept

1. Living Systems and Biological Diversity (Weeks 01–02)

The Ten Signs of Life (MR NIGERCLAD): Living organisms carry out Movement, Respiration, Nutrition, Irritability, Growth, Excretion, Reproduction, Competition (struggling for food, water, and space), Life span/Death, and Adaptation (features that help an organism survive in its habitat).
The Five Kingdoms: Living things are classified into Monera (bacteria without true nuclei), Protista (Amoeba), Fungi (mushrooms and moulds that absorb dead organic matter), Plantae (green photosynthetic plants), and Animalia (multicellular animals).
Plant Diversity:
Monocotyledons: One seed-leaf, narrow leaves with parallel veins, and fibrous roots (e.g., maize, rice).
Dicotyledons: Two seed-leaves, broad leaves with net-like veins, and a deep taproot system (e.g., cowpea beans, groundnuts).
Animal Diversity:
Invertebrates: Animals without backbones (insects, earthworms, snails).
Vertebrates: Animals with backbones, divided into Pisces (fish), Amphibia (frogs, toads), Reptilia (lizards, snakes), Aves (birds), and Mammalia (mammals that have fur and nurse young with milk).

2. Matter and Physical Phase Changes (Week 03)

The Three States of Matter:
Solids: Particles packed in tight, regular rows vibrating in fixed positions; fixed shape and volume; cannot be compressed.
Liquids: Particles close together but free to slide past one another; fixed volume, takes the shape of its container; cannot be easily compressed.
Gases: Particles spaced far apart and moving rapidly in all directions; no fixed shape or volume; easily compressed.
Phase Changes: Melting (solid $\rightarrow$ liquid), Freezing (liquid $\rightarrow$ solid), Evaporation/Boiling (liquid $\rightarrow$ gas), Condensation (gas $\rightarrow$ liquid), and Sublimation (solid directly to gas, like camphor).
Metals vs. Non-Metals: Metals are shiny, malleable, ductile, and conduct heat and electricity well (e.g., copper, iron). Non-metals are dull, brittle, and act as electrical and thermal insulators (e.g., wood, rubber, sulphur).

3. Energy, Transformations, and Calculations (Weeks 04 & 06)

Forms and Sources: Energy is the capacity to do work (measured in Joules, $\text{J}$). Sources are either renewable (solar, wind, hydroelectric from Kainji Dam) or non-renewable (crude oil, coal, natural gas).
Law of Conservation of Energy: Energy is never created or destroyed, only transformed.
Electric Iron: Electrical $\rightarrow$ Heat energy.
Electric Fan: Electrical $\rightarrow$ Mechanical kinetic energy.
Torchlight: Chemical (battery) $\rightarrow$ Electrical $\rightarrow$ Light energy.
Microphone: Sound $\rightarrow$ Electrical energy; Loudspeaker: Electrical $\rightarrow$ Sound energy.
Mechanical Energy Formulas:
Gravitational Potential Energy: $PE = mgh$ (where $m$ is mass in $\text{kg}$, $g \approx 10\text{ m/s}^2$, and $h$ is height in metres).
Kinetic Energy: $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ (where $m$ is mass in $\text{kg}$ and $v$ is speed in $\text{m/s}$).

4. Pure and Impure Substances & Separation (Weeks 07–08)

Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures:
Element: Simplest pure substance made of one kind of atom (e.g., Carbon $\text{C}$, Sodium $\text{Na}$, Potassium $\text{K}$, Oxygen $\text{O}$, Calcium $\text{Ca}$).
Compound: Pure substance of two or more elements chemically bonded in fixed proportions (e.g., Water $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, Table Salt $\text{NaCl}$).
Mixture: Two or more substances physically blended in any proportion without chemical bonds (e.g., air, muddy water, soaked garri).
Separation Techniques:
Decantation: Pouring off liquid after heavy solids settle.
Filtration: Separating an insoluble solid from a liquid using filter paper (retains the residue, passes the clear filtrate).
Evaporation to Dryness: Boiling off the liquid to recover a dissolved solid.
Simple Distillation: Boiling a solution and condensing the vapour in a Liebig condenser to collect the pure liquid (distillate) while leaving the solid behind.
Magnetic Separation: Using a magnet to extract magnetic metals (iron) from non-magnetic substances.
Second Term Basic Science Core Themes Summary

Second Term Core Scientific Distinctions

Plant Architecture

Monocots vs Dicots

Monocots feature single cotyledon seeds, parallel leaf veins, and fibrous roots (e.g., maize), whereas dicots have two cotyledons, net-veined leaves, and taproots (e.g., cowpea).

States of Matter

Solids vs Gases

Solids possess tightly packed particles vibrating in place with fixed shape and volume, while gases have widely spaced particles moving rapidly with variable shape and volume.

Chemical Classification

Compounds vs Mixtures

Compounds consist of elements chemically joined in fixed ratios that require chemical reactions to separate, while mixtures are physically combined in any ratio and separated by physical techniques.

Mechanical Energy

Potential vs Kinetic Energy

Gravitational potential energy is stored energy determined by mass and height ($PE = mgh$), while kinetic energy is energy of motion determined by mass and speed ($KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common Mistake

Believing that dissolving sugar in water forms a brand new chemical compound.

Scientific Fact

Dissolving forms a physical mixture (solution). No chemical bonds are created, and the water can be evaporated away to recover the dry sugar crystals.

Common Mistake

Confusing excretion with egestion (defecation).

Scientific Fact

Excretion removes chemical waste made inside body cells (like urine, sweat, and carbon dioxide), while egestion expels undigested food matter through the anus.

Common Mistake

Assuming that a moving object creates energy out of nothing.

Scientific Fact

Energy can never be created; a moving car simply transforms stored chemical energy from petrol into mechanical kinetic energy, heat, and sound.
